# What research finds about ad effects

## Sponsored search and organic listings

A platform experiment that blocked sponsored search for 3% of an e-commerce site's visitors found that sponsored search "steals business from organic listings" and reduced total transactions. (C12)
A marketplace study found shoppers preferring top-ranked organic listings to sponsored listings of the same product and position. (C12)

## Noise

Across 25 large ad field experiments the median confidence interval on ROI was over 100 percentage points wide, because individual sales are highly variable relative to advertising's effect. (C13)
Across 15 Facebook experiments, observational methods "often fail to produce the same effects as the randomized experiments". (C13)
Ordinary p-values and intervals are unreliable when a test is stopped on watching its results. (C16)

## Positions and bids

Outcome differences between ad positions won by bidding are biased by selection, which "cannot be eliminated by randomizing the bids for the focal advertiser" because competitors bid too. (C14)
Narayanan and Kalyanam's regression discontinuity finds naive estimates overstate position effects, which are "much smaller" and "exist only in some of the positions". (C14)
In Narayanan and Kalyanam's study, position effects are weaker when the phrase carries brand or product information, and larger for smaller advertisers and less experienced consumers. (C14)
Agarwal, Hosanagar and Smith find profit can be higher below the top position. (C14)
The four studies of conversion by position disagree. (C14)
A report shows what happened at the bid that ran, never what a target would have done at another bid. (C27)
Amazon's AuctionGym authors state that "an advertiser does not observe counterfactual outcomes of bid amounts that were not submitted", and that competitors' adaptation complicates evaluating bidding strategies from logged data alone. (C27)

## Short term and long term

Short-term ad effects are "not always predictive of the long-term effect". (C17)
User learning about ads took months to develop on Google search, and an ad shutoff for a mobile game reduced organic installs by 20–30% through store rankings. (C17)

## Regression to the mean

Units picked because their last measurement was extreme tend to move back toward their mean with no intervention. (C18)
So a campaign handed to automation after an unusually good or bad period shows a change that is partly regression to the mean. (C18)
