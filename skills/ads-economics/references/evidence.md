# What published research measures

## Advertising, price and margin

- In economics the Dorfman–Steiner condition sets the profit-maximising advertising-to-sales ratio at the price-cost margin times the advertising elasticity of demand, for a firm raising sales by price or by advertising. (G17)
- Meta-analyses put brand advertising elasticity at about 0.12 short term and 0.24 long term, and price elasticity at −2.62. (G17)
- TV advertising showed negative marginal ROI for more than 80% of 288 brands. (G17)
- Click curves against bid are concave beyond a point. (G17)
- From sales ranks of about 20,000 books in 2001, Chevalier and Goolsbee estimated Amazon's own-price elasticity around −0.6, against close to −4 at Barnes and Noble's site. (G18)
- Bach et al. find strong product-level heterogeneity in price elasticity among toy cars on Amazon.com. (G18)

## Attributed against experimental returns

- In published field experiments, observational or attributed estimates of advertising returns exceeded the experimental ones. (G19)
- At eBay, paid-search returns were a fraction of non-experimental estimates, with no measurable short-term benefit from brand-keyword ads and effects only on new and infrequent users (Blake, Nosko and Tadelis). (G19)
- A geographic Google-ads shutoff lost only 63% as many customers as a non-experimental estimate implied. (G19)
- On Meta, observational methods often failed to reproduce experimental lifts even with over 5,000 user features (29% experimental against 83% DML and 173% SPSM). (G19)
- On Meta, 7-day last-click attribution predicted experimental incrementality with R² 0.19, against 0.88 for a model trained on experiments. (G19)
- Advertising is not randomly assigned in observational data; with an identification strategy, Shapiro, Hitsch and Tuchman found mean and median long-run TV elasticities of 0.023 and 0.014. (G19)
- Customers acquired through Google search ads at one firm were worth more than first-purchase measures showed. (G19)

## Sponsored listings and organic outcomes

- On marketplaces and app stores, field experiments found sponsored listings' effect on organic outcomes negative in some settings and positive in others, and none measures a seller's Sponsored Products against its Amazon organic sales. (G25)
- On a brand search, the brand's own ad added little without competitors and more with them. (G25)
- On Amazon, one position up raised sales quantity by about a third of a percent. (G25)
- A mobile game's app-install ad shutoff cut organic installs by 20–30%. (G25)
- On 2023 Amazon.com first pages the median ad appeared 17 ranks above the same product's organic listing, with 48.3% of ads having an organic duplicate there. (G30)
- On a shopper panel, being sponsored moved a product up 7 positions on average. (G30)
- Over 4,800 simulated searches in four stores, items beyond organic rank 100 appeared as sponsored above the top organic results, top sponsored results mostly costlier and of poorer quality than top organic ones. (G30)
- On the Kindle Daily Deal page, Amazon-published ebooks ranked 23.6% better conditional on observables and sold 50% less conditional on rank, quantities inferred from sales ranks. (G30)

## Amazon-authored studies

- Amazon Ads' Qin (2023), on 15 US brands that asked for a carry-over study (weekly 2021–22 data, a seven-week VARMAX impulse response), found that "SP does not show significant carry-over effects beyond three weeks for any brand". (G31)
- Qin found that lower-funnel products realise 60–90% of their effect within two weeks, against about 40% of DSP display and about 30% of Sponsored Display effect in week 5 and beyond. (G31)
- The paper states the sample is "only … brands that asked for a long-term effects assessment" and that the estimate nets out company feedback effects and competitive response. (G31)
- Pauwels and co-authors' Amazon-authored Sponsored Brands video estimates rest on brands' own adoption choices, adjusted by matching and causal Gaussian processes over 25,364 advertisers for one month and by propensity score stratification on 50+ observed features for one year. (G32)
- In that study nothing was randomised, and the authors did not analyse ROAS. (G32)
