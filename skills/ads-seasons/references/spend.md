# Spend and performance around events

## Amazon's holiday guidance

For the 2025–2026 holiday Amazon advises launching Sponsored Products campaigns by early October. (E5)
It advises raising daily budgets 20–30% around Black Friday and Cyber Monday, starting three to five days ahead of each peak, as guidance figures, not limits. (E5)
Amazon describes the season in three phases: build (October), peak (November through Cyber Monday) and extend (December and beyond). (E5)
It defines the extend phase by last-minute gift shoppers, post-holiday deal seekers, visitors who browsed but did not buy during peak events, and seasonal buyers to turn into repeat customers; its New Year guide adds January resolution shoppers. (E5)
No Amazon page read on 2026-11-09 uses the term "Q5" or describes CPC after December 25. (E5)
In Amazon's post-event teaching scenario, "Extend holiday deals into January and maintain Sponsored Brands investment" is the answer marked incorrect. (E5)
The answer marked correct moves budget to high-performing branded terms and repositions seasonal stock with non-holiday messaging. (E5)

## Event CPC and budgets

Marketplace Ad Pros measured Prime Day 2026 median Sponsored Products CPC up 10% ($1.19 to $1.31) and Sponsored Brands CPC up 2% against matched pre-event days, across several hundred US advertisers. (E6)
In Marketplace Ad Pros' data, 51% of advertisers cut SP budgets and 13.7% raised them by 20% or more, and raisers and holders showed the same 1.30x ad-sales lift, which the authors call correlational. (E6)
Skai measured Prime Day 2025 CPC at $1.80, down 10.4% from 2024, in a same-store cohort. (E6)
Ad Badger recommends 1.5–2.5× budgets in the two days before a Prime event and 2–3× on event days. (E6)
Against Ad Badger's figures, Amazon's holiday guide and Florian Nottorf (Adference) give +20–30% starting 3–5 days ahead. (E6)

## Conversion during an event

Amazon's holiday "myths" page says conversion rates often drop during major sales events while shoppers browse. (E7)
The same page says a temporary ACOS spike is read against TACOS, which "reveals whether your total sales (organic and paid) are growing in relation to your ad spend". (E7)
Ad Badger reports impressions rising while clicks and conversions stay flat in the 48 hours before a Prime event. (E7)
ACOS, TACOS and the other metrics, defined: ../ads-review/references/metrics.md.

## After the event

Practitioners disagree on post-event spend. (E16)
Pare holds ad velocity for 48–72 hours and retargets detail-page viewers at 20–30 days. (E16)
Ad Badger returns bids to normal the next day and runs Sponsored Display retargeting for days 1–7. (E16)
Acadia finds "consistently thin post-event lift" in its going-dark tests and trims lead-out media. (E16)
Acadia reports brands running no deals lifting 46% over their 30-day baseline at Prime Day 2025 and 116% at Prime Day 2024, and calls non-participation "a penalty" in 2026. (E16)
Acadia treats year-over-year growth rather than period-over-period lift as comparable across events, because 2025's baseline week was unusually low. (E16)
Amazon's 137% (Prime Day 2025) and 145% (holiday 2025) "increase in sales" figures compare advertisers with average category growth over tentpole windows against the adjacent preceding weeks. (E16)

## December 26 to mid-January

Practitioners call December 26 to mid-January "Q5" and disagree on it. (E20)
Evolve AMZ says CPMs "commonly fall 28% or more against the Q4 peak" as seasonal advertisers exit and gift-card redemption concentrates there, and Pattern says CPCs go down. (E20)
Ad Badger's app data puts January 2026 at the year's highest ACOS (32.5%, against 28% in October 2025). (E20)
Intentwise's data has conversion falling from 13.4% in the week ending December 23 to 11.03% the week after. (E20)
Teikametrics reported, across more than 700 sellers in 2019, ad conversion rising and cost per conversion falling from Cyber Monday to the holiday shipping cutoff before Christmas, which it attributed to brands having spent their seasonal budgets. (E20)
