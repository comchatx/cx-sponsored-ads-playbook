# Reading an event

## Attribution

Sponsored Products attributes seller conversions over 7 days and vendor conversions over 14. (E8)
Sponsored Brands and Sponsored Display attribute over 14 days, click or view. (E8)
Conversions are dated to the ad interaction and restated 1, 7 and 28 days after the conversion. (E8)
Amazon advises checking 7–14-day attribution before changing campaigns after a peak, because peak-event sales often occur days after the click. (E8)
Marketplace Ad Pros found the clicks of Prime Day 2026's last day fully mature about two weeks later. (E8)
Attribution and metrics at large: ../ads-review/references/metrics.md.
Report types and columns: ../ads-review/references/reports.md.

## Benchmarks across a moving event

Data Kiosk's week-level `yearOverYear` benchmark compares the same ISO week number a year earlier, and its month-level benchmark the same calendar month. (E15)
Data Kiosk has no benchmark at day level, so a moving event such as Prime Day lands in different benchmark weeks. (E15)
Search Query Performance runs on Sunday–Saturday weeks, calendar months or quarters, and a request cannot span periods. (E15)

## Promotions over time

Store-level scanner research by Van Heerde, Leeflang and Wittink (2000) puts the post-promotion dip at 4–25% of the promotion's current sales effect, usually missed by simple models and visual inspection. (E18)
Panel research by Mela, Gupta and Lehmann (1997) found consumers growing more price- and promotion-sensitive as promotions increase and advertising falls. (E18)
Amazon's own guide says regular promotions "can condition customers to wait for deals". (E18)
A review by Kaul and Wittink (1995) found price advertising raising price sensitivity and non-price advertising lowering it. (E18)
Across 70 French brands, Ataman, van Heerde and Mela (2010) found the total sales elasticity of discounting at .04 and of advertising at .13, and discounting's long-term effect one third of its short-term effect. (E18)
In Erdem, Keane and Sun (2007), advertising raised marginal consumers' willingness to pay for 17 of 18 brands in four categories. (E18)
