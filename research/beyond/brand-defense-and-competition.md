## What this covers

**The part of a seller's ad work.** Everything a seller's ads do about other brands on the same results page and the same detail page, and everything other brands' ads do about the seller's. In order:

- **Bidding on one's own brand terms** — what Amazon calls the *Protect* strategy and "brand defense": why a seller pays for a query whose shopper already typed the seller's name, what Amazon says about it, and what the controlled experiments outside Amazon measured about how much of that traffic is incremental.
- **Defending one's own detail pages** — targeting one's own ASINs with Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands and Sponsored Display so that the paid slots on the seller's own page carry the seller's catalogue, and the negatives that keep a seller's ads off pages it does not want.
- **Conquesting** — targeting competitors' brand names as keywords and competitors' ASINs as product targets; and the policy line Amazon draws between a keyword (a targeting choice the shopper never sees) and creative (what the shopper reads).
- **Share metrics** — search term impression share and rank, top-of-search impression share, the console's Brand Impression Share dashboard, the share-of-voice insight for partners, and the brand-share columns of Brand Analytics' Search Query Performance.
- **Brand Analytics as a competitive signal** — Top Search Terms (the three most-clicked ASINs per query) and Search Query Performance (the brand's share of a query's impressions, clicks, cart adds and purchases).
- **Sponsored Brands and the Store as brand tools** — the "Grow brand impression share" goal, Reserve share of voice (a fixed-price reservation of the Sponsored Brands top-of-search slot on branded keywords), theme targeting on "keywords related to your brand", Store Spotlight, branded-search metrics and peer benchmarks.
- **Competitive response and price wars in the auction** — how Amazon describes its auction and reserve prices, the 2026 FTC case about them, and what the research on bidding cycles and competitive poaching says about escalation.
- **Stock-outs and the Featured Offer as competitive moments** (gap fill) — what Amazon says happens to a product's ads when it goes out of stock or its SKU loses the Featured Offer; what that means for a rival's ads and for one's own when a reseller holds one's Featured Offer; the Keepa and SP-API reads of a rival's stock and Featured Offer state; and the practitioners who change bids on those events (§K).
- **Sponsored Brands video and Store Spotlight in defence and conquest** (gap fill) — where Amazon says each format serves, how each is targeted, the creative rules that bound a video shown on a rival's term or page, and what practitioners report about video on competitor terms (§L).
- **The research on competition in sponsored search** — brand-keyword experiments (Blake, Nosko & Tadelis; Simonov, Nosko & Rao; Simonov & Hill; Coviello, Gneezy & Goette), competitor-side spillovers (Golden & Horton), game-theory models of buying a rival's keyword (Desai, Shin & Staelin; Sayedi, Jerath & Srinivasan), and trademark-policy natural experiments (Bechtold & Tucker; Chiou & Tucker).

**Why a senior Amazon advertising lead cares.** Three facts shape this whole area. First, Amazon allows any advertiser to target any brand's name as a keyword and any ASIN as a product target, so a seller's brand query and a seller's detail page are open auctions in which competitors buy space (§A, §F). Second, Amazon never names who those competitors are: every share metric it publishes is the seller's own share, or a rank, against an unnamed field (§D); competitor identity comes only from observing the page or from Brand Analytics' top-clicked products (§G). Third, the controlled evidence says that paying for one's own brand query buys few extra clicks when nobody else is bidding, and protects a measurable share when others are (§Research); so the value of brand defence depends on the competitive state of that query, which moves.

## Amazon's own rules and guidance

Each item is marked **RULE** (a constraint Amazon enforces: rejection, removal, a field that cannot change) or **GUIDANCE** (Amazon's advice, which a seller may ignore). Quotes are verbatim where the exact words carry weight.

### A. Branded keywords: what Amazon says about bidding on one's own brand

1. **GUIDANCE — Amazon names brand defence as a strategy.** *A guide to targeting with Sponsored Products* (advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/targeting-with-sponsored-products, read 2026-11-09) lists six targeting strategies: "Expand", "Promote", "**Protect** : focus on branded keywords and targeting", "**Conquest** : target competitors' products", "Upsell" and "Cross-sell". On branded keywords it says: "Branded keywords are directly associated with your brand or a brand you sell… Consider bidding on your branded keyword as a brand defense strategy since your competitors may be bidding on your branded keywords, too." [official]

2. **GUIDANCE — what counts as a branded keyword.** *What is Keyword Targeting? How It Works, Benefits, Examples* (advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/keyword-targeting, read 2026-11-09): "Branded keywords include brand names, product lines, or trademark terms… Consider including variations of your brand and product names." [official] For reserve share of voice the help page defines the class more tightly (§B.2): misspellings and variations are rejected there.

3. **GUIDANCE — Amazon's own figures on branded search (human readers only).** The unBoxed 2025 announcement (advertising.amazon.com/library/news/unboxed-2025-sponsored-brands-reserve-share-of-voice, dated November 11, 2025, byline Kelly Lui, read 2026-11-09): "70% of Amazon shoppers begin their journey with search, and 1 in 5 searches include a brand name", and "advertisers only achieve about 62% Sponsored Brands top-of-search impression share for branded queries on average." [official] *Your complete guide to Sponsored Brands* (advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/sponsored-brands-what-to-know, read 2026-11-09): "More than a quarter of shoppers shop across multiple brands, so it's important to be competitive against both category-level shopping queries and branded shopping queries." [official]

4. **GUIDANCE — Sponsored Brands for brand up-selling.** The same Sponsored Brands guide: "Use individual product targeting if you are interested in brand up-selling and cross-selling." [official]

5. **GUIDANCE — "keywords related to your brand" as a theme.** In Sponsored Brands theme targeting, `KEYWORDS_RELATED_TO_YOUR_BRAND` is "keywords that are often used to search for brands and promote brand impressions", beside `KEYWORDS_RELATED_TO_YOUR_LANDING_PAGES` (guides/sponsored-brands/targeting/theme-based-targeting.md, read 2026-11-09) [official]. The merged v1 OpenAPI model's `ThemeMatchType` enum (d1y2lf8k3vrkfu.cloudfront.net/openapi/en-us/dest/AmazonAdsAPIALLMerged_prod_3p.json, read 2026-11-09) describes it as "Search terms related to your brand", and carries a second brand-facing theme: `KEYWORDS_RELATED_TO_PEER_BRANDS_PRODUCT_CATEGORY` — "Search terms that shoppers often use when searching for and interacting with products from other brands in the category of your advertised products. **The peer brands are selected automatically.**" [official] `ThemeMatchType` is one enum shared across ad products (it also carries `KEYWORDS_CLOSE_MATCH`, `INTERESTED_AUDIENCE` and the like), and neither the model nor the two theme-targeting guides read (SB theme-based targeting; SP theme-based bid suggestions) say which ad product accepts the peer-brands theme; the SB theme guide names only `KEYWORDS_RELATED_TO_YOUR_BRAND` and `KEYWORDS_RELATED_TO_YOUR_LANDING_PAGES`. [seat] So Amazon defines an automated conquesting theme whose competitor list it chooses and does not show; where it can be used is open (Open question 15).

6. **GUIDANCE — Amazon labels a recommended keyword as branded or not.** The recommendations API's `BRAND_PRODUCT_KEYWORD` type (Sponsored Brands, `POST /adsApi/v1/query/recommendations`, scoped by `brandId`/`brandName`) returns a `keywordTheme` per keyword, with values such as `Generic`, `Branded` and `Seasonal` (guides/recommendations/recommendations-api/recommendation-types.md, read 2026-11-09). [official] This is the only Amazon-side branded/non-branded label on a keyword that the seat found in the API; reports carry no such flag on a search term.

7. **RULE — branded-search metrics match brand names, trademarks and variations, and do not exist for Sponsored Products.** *Branded searches metric enhancements* (advertising.amazon.com/resources/whats-new/branded-searches-metric-enhancements, read 2026-11-09): from October 1, 2025, "Enhanced brand matching" counts searches containing a "brand name, trademark, or common variations like abbreviations and misspellings"; "These metrics are not available for Sponsored Products"; they are available for Sponsored Brands, Sponsored Display, Sponsored TV and DSP. [official] The v1 field `metric.brandedSearches` is "The number of times shoppers searched for a brand promoted in the campaign, attributed to an ad interaction", eligible for SB, SD, STV, DSP and Full-Funnel (guides/reporting/ads-v1/metrics/amazon-retail-conversions/branded-searches.md, read 2026-11-09); its family includes `metric.costPerBrandedSearch`, `metric.brandedSearchRate` (branded searches divided by impressions) and `metric.brandedSearchesFromClicks` (only the P25/P50/P75 benchmark variants such as `metric.costPerBrandedSearchP25` list Sponsored Products among their programs). The v3 column `brandedSearches` is described differently: "The number of searches that included the name of your brand occurring within 14 days of an ad click or view" (guides/reporting/v3/columns.md, read 2026-11-09). The 2026-08-26 release note adds new-to-brand branded searches and Brand Store page views to v1 (release-notes/index.md, read 2026-11-09). [official] Detail in `beyond/metrics-and-attribution.md`.

### B. Reserve share of voice: renting the Sponsored Brands top slot on one's own brand

Reserve share of voice (RSOV) is a Sponsored Brands campaign goal that buys a fixed share of the Sponsored Brands top-of-search placement on the seller's own branded keywords, at a price fixed before the flight. It is the only Amazon product that sells a guaranteed share of a results-page slot to self-service advertisers. `areas/keyword-and-product-targeting.md` §K covers its targeting side; this section carries the rules that make it a brand-defence tool.

1. **RULE — three Sponsored Brands goals, one per campaign, fixed.** *Campaign goals for Sponsored Brands* (help GXXPFXLSVX9VD3U9, lastModified 2026-08-11, read 2026-11-09) lists "Drive page visits" (CPC, all placements: search pages, detail pages, Amazon homepage), "Grow brand impression share" (vCPM, top-of-search) and "Reserve share of voice" (fixed price, top-of-search); one goal per campaign, and it cannot be changed after launch. The SB v4 OpenAPI (`openapi/en-us/sponsored-brands/4-0/openapi.json`, read 2026-11-09) names the API values: `BRAND_IMPRESSION_SHARE` ("This goal will allow you grown [sic] your brand impression share on top of search placement") and `PAGE_VISIT` (the default), with `ACQUIRE_NEW_CUSTOMERS` and `AD_VIEWS` marked "PREVIEW ONLY", and "Goal-based campaigns are not supported in the Ireland (IE) marketplace." (in IE, supplying `goal` and `costType` returns `GOAL_BASED_CAMPAIGNS_DISABLED`). [official]

2. **RULE — only one's own registered brand terms can be reserved.** *Branded keyword guidelines and keyword suspension* (help G2QZJUGUT4RGLJ6N, lastModified 2025-11-05, read 2026-11-09): reserved keywords must be branded ("Glow moisturizer" is accepted; "moisturizer" is not). Rejection reasons, verbatim: the brand or trademark is not in Brand Registry; the keyword is too broad; "common misspellings and variations"; non-branded keywords; "A keyword contains both your and another company's brand name or trademark or your keyword has a brand name or trademark shared by more than 2 brands"; "You used keywords that contain another company's brand name or trademark"; the brand is not mapped to the account. [official] The library guide *How to set up Amazon Sponsored Brands reserve share of voice* (advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/sponsored-brands-reserve-share-of-voice, read 2026-11-09) answers the direct question: "Can I reserve generic or competitor keywords? No. Only exact match branded keywords… Generic terms, misspellings, competitor brands, and over-broad shared trademarks are excluded." [official] So the guaranteed slot can be bought only for defence, never for conquest.

3. **RULE — keyword hygiene in RSOV.** Same help page: "You cannot reserve the same keyword on multiple campaigns or ad groups" (moving one means archiving it in the old ad group first); keywords must be mapped to a relevant ad within 14 days or they are removed, with charges for up to 14 days; "Once your keyword is removed, you cannot add it back to the same campaign"; if the account is suspended for non-payment, the ads stop. [official] Figures human readers only.

4. **RULE — what is frozen at launch.** *Reserve keywords in a Sponsored Brands campaign* (help G86SD7HK6NHHRB9B, lastModified 2026-07-07, read 2026-11-09): "Once the campaign starts, you can't change your keywords, campaign duration, or budget." Suggested keywords are "listed in order of keyword search volume". Moderation usually finishes within 24 hours and can take up to 3 business days. "Note: Reserve share of voice is not available in Spain." (the same note is on the goals page GXXPFXLSVX9VD3U9, re-read 2026-11-09). [official]

5. **RULE, figures conflicting across Amazon's pages (human readers only).** Flight length: 15–92 days, start 3 days to 3 months ahead (help G86SD7HK6NHHRB9B); "between 30 to 92 days" (goals page GXXPFXLSVX9VD3U9); "e.g., 14–90 days" (library guide). Minimum: "A minimum budget of $3,000 is required" (help G86SD7HK6NHHRB9B); "minimum spend of $6K per campaign for the U.S. marketplace" (library guide FAQ). Keyword count: 5–1,000, the one figure on which all three help pages agree (G2QZJUGUT4RGLJ6N: "You must reserve a minimum of 5 keywords and a maximum of 1,000"; G86SD7HK6NHHRB9B: "Reserve 5 to 1,000 keywords"; GXXPFXLSVX9VD3U9: "5 to 1,000 branded keywords for your own brands"; all re-read 2026-11-09); the v1 pricing schema accepts 1–1,000 keywords per pricing request (`SBBrandedKeywordsPricingCreate.keywords`, minItems 1, maxItems 1000), and the API guide requires at least five deal targets. Marketplaces: the library guide lists 20 (US, CA, MX, BR, DE, FR, IT, UK, BE, PL, NL, SE, TR, IE, AU, JP, IN, SG, AE, SA) while the SB v4 model says goal-based campaigns are not supported in IE and two help pages (G86SD7HK6NHHRB9B and GXXPFXLSVX9VD3U9) say RSOV is not in Spain (which the 20 do not include). The library guide's page metadata reads datePublished 2025-11-11, dateModified 2026-10-09, the same modification date as the what's-new page, so neither is the later word. The what's-new launch page *Drive top-of-search visibility and engagement by reserving branded share of voice through Sponsored Brands* (advertising.amazon.com/resources/whats-new/sponsored-brands-reserve-share-of-voice, published 2025-10-22, dateModified 2026-10-09, read 2026-11-09) also lists 20, but with Egypt in place of Ireland: US, CA, MX, BR, DE, FR, IT, UK, BE, PL, NL, SE, TR, EG, SA, AE, AU, JP, IN, SG; it names sellers, vendors, partners and agencies as users, through the console and the API. [official] See Open questions 1–3.

6. **GUIDANCE — what the reservation delivers.** Goals page: "We strive to deliver 95% SOV at the campaign level…"; top-of-search impression share for a reserved keyword is "the amount of impressions the advertiser received for the reserved keyword divided by the total amount of impressions across all ads for the reserved keyword", with the worked example Kitchen Smart, 600 of 1,000, = 60%. Library guide FAQ: it "guarantees coverage… at a fixed cost per mille (CPM)"; formats are "Sponsored Brands video, store spotlight, and collections" (the help page's builder steps name only Product collection and Video). A further RSOV-only format, Brand Gallery — "a media block, brand messaging, a call-to-action linking to the Brand Store, and a carousel of three to five Brand Store categories or collections" — is announced on *Drive branded search engagement with Sponsored Brands Brand Gallery* (advertising.amazon.com/resources/whats-new/sponsored-brands-brand-gallery, published 2026-05-28, read 2026-11-09; beta "with 4 advertisers, March – May, 2026"). The 2025-10-15 release note and the launch page say the ad will appear "most of the time". [official] "Strive" and "most of the time" are not a contractual floor on the help pages read; the help pages point to separate "additional terms that apply to Reserve Share of Voice campaigns", which this seat did not read.

7. **GUIDANCE — Amazon's beta results (human readers only; Amazon internal, not independent).** Library guide: beta with 15+ advertisers, May–August 2025: Sponsored Brands top-of-search impression share on reserved keywords rose from 62.7% to 99.3%, CTR from 3.5% to 4.1%, click-attributed sales +143%, lost top-of-search sales from 10.3% to 0.3%. Samsung case study (advertising.amazon.com/library/case-studies/sponsored-brands-reserve-sov-samsung, read 2026-11-09): SB top-of-search impression share 44.9% → 99.8%, click-attributed sales +187%, conversion rate 13.2% → 14.3%, lost top-of-search sales 7.4% → 0.04%, footnoted "Amazon Marketing Cloud, US, 2025… a single advertising campaign for an early-stage or newly launched product… Results may not be typical". Bayer case study (…/sponsored-brands-reserve-sov-bayer, read 2026-11-09): eight OTC brands in Germany, SB top-of-search impression share 77% → 97%, 7.8× impressions, 5.6× click-attributed top-of-search sales; the Bayer quote reads "Sponsored Brands reserve share of voice broke the CPC auction spiral exactly where we can't afford to lose visibility". [official] These are before/after comparisons of attributed sales, not holdout tests of incrementality.

8. **RULE — the API lifecycle of a reservation.** *Reserve share of voice* API guide (guides/sponsored-brands/reserve-share-of-voice.md, read 2026-11-09) and the merged v1 model (read 2026-11-09): keyword recommendations `POST /adsApi/v1/create/recommendations/sb`; reservability check `POST /adsApi/v1/create/keywordReservationValidations/sb` (up to 1,000 keywords per call, `SBCreateKeywordReservationValidationRequest` maxItems 1000; each answer carries `isReservable`, a required `keywordReservationValidationId` and, when false, `reservationRejectedReason`, e.g. "No valid brand or trademark was found in the keyword."); pricing `POST /adsApi/v1/create/brandedKeywordsPricings/sb` (returns `keywordsPricing` and `rejectedKeywords`; `advertisingDealId` is omitted when pricing a new deal); the deal `POST /adsApi/v1/create/advertisingDeals/sb`; deal targets `POST /adsApi/v1/create/advertisingDealTargets/sb` (at least five), `…/query/…` and `…/delete/…`; `POST /adsApi/v1/update/advertisingDeals/sb` moving the state from `DRAFT` to `PROPOSED` (the only two states in `SBAdvertisingDealState`), which triggers pricing; `POST /adsApi/v1/query/advertisingDeals/sb`; the campaign itself on `POST /sb/v4/campaigns` with `targetPGDealId`; and `POST /adsApi/v1/delete/advertisingDeals/sb`. "A deal in PROPOSED state cannot be modified"; a deal can be deleted only before its start time minus 12 hours, and deletion archives its campaigns; only the day part of start and end is used, in the marketplace's time zone. [official] Detail in `method/ads-api-surface.md`.

### C. Grow brand impression share: buying top-of-search share in the auction

1. **RULE — the goal's shape.** "Grow brand impression share" is the vCPM Sponsored Brands goal that serves top-of-search (goals page GXXPFXLSVX9VD3U9; SB v4 `BRAND_IMPRESSION_SHARE`). Formats on top-of-search require a brand Store (goals page table). The goal predates the 2024-06-13 release note that lists it among SB v4 features: the 2023-11-06 note already calls "grow brand impression share" campaigns, "transacted on `vCPM` basis", "recently launched", and the 2023-12-26 note adds video-only creatives under it (release-notes/index.md, read 2026-11-09). [official]

2. **GUIDANCE — bid recommendations come from history.** Goals page: bid recommendations are "built from historical winning bids…". [official]

3. **RULE — the v1 KPI enum carries top-of-search share as a target.** The merged v1 model's `KPI` enum includes `TOP_OF_SEARCH_IMPRESSION_SHARE` beside `NEW_TO_BRAND_PURCHASES`, and the `BidStrategy` enum includes `NEW_TO_BRAND` ("Optimizes bidding to maximize new-to-brand customer acquisitions") and `PRIORITIZE_KPI_TARGET` (read 2026-11-09). [official] Which ad products accept which KPI is in `beyond/goals-and-economics.md` and `beyond/automation-and-amazon-tools.md`.

4. **RULE — budget rules can key on impression share.** The 2023-04-06 release note: Sponsored Brands performance-based budget rules can use "top-of-search impression share (`metricName` is `IS`)" and "percent of sales new-to-brand (`metricName` is `NTB`)" (release-notes/index.md, read 2026-11-09). [official] Detail in `areas/budgets-and-pacing.md`.

### D. Share metrics: what Amazon publishes about a seller's position against others

Amazon publishes five share measures. None names a competitor.

1. **RULE — search term impression share and rank are account-wide, per search term.** *Downloadable reports in sponsored ads* (help GBYSPTSLR337JMLH, lastModified 2026-08-19, read 2026-11-09): the Search Term Impression Share report gives "for each search term, the percentage share and numeric rank of your impression share of the total impressions relative to other advertisers", for Sponsored Products and Sponsored Brands. The v1 metric pages (guides/reporting/ads-v1/metrics/impression-share/search.md, read 2026-11-09): `metric.impressionShare` and `metric.impressionShareRank` ("numeric rank of your account-wide impression share compared to all other advertisers") require `adProduct.value`, `advertiserAccount.id` and `searchTerm.value`, and are **incompatible** with campaign, ad group, ad, advertised product, target and placement classification; v3 names "Search Term Impression Share" and "Search Term Impression Rank". The what's-new page *Search term impression share report for Sponsored Products* (March 22, 2021; …/whats-new/search-term-impression-report-sponsored-products, read 2026-11-09) gives the reading within one ad product: a 20% share "means that you won 20% of all Sponsored Products ad impressions for that search term on that date"; rank 3 means "you received the third most Sponsored Products ad impressions". [official] So the denominator is the ad impressions of one ad product on that term, not every ad on the page. Perpetua's explainer (perpetua.io/blog-amazon-search-term-impression-share-rank-report/, read 2026-11-09) adds a working note: "Impression share is account-wide; however, search terms and impressions appear separately for each ad group/campaign in the report", so impressions for a term have to be summed across rows. [opinion, Perpetua]

2. **RULE — top-of-search impression share has the seller's own eligibility as its denominator.** v1 `metric.topOfSearchImpressionShare`: "percentage of impressions that appeared in top search results compared to the number of times they were eligible"; compatible with campaign; incompatible with ad group, target, search term and placement classification; v3 "Top-of-search Impression Share" (same metric page, read 2026-11-09). The 2021-09-22 what's-new page *Top-of-search impression share (top-of-search IS) to measure brand awareness* (…/whats-new/top-of-search-impression-share-metric, read 2026-11-09) frames it as share of the top-of-search impressions the campaign was "eligible to serve on", with a 90-day lookback, and suggests one can "adjust bids by placement". [official] In v3, the columns page's entry reads "**Report types**: spCampaigns, sbCampaigns, sbTargeting"; the *Targeting reports* page lists it in the base metrics of both the Sponsored Products and the Sponsored Brands targeting reports; and the *Campaign reports* page lists it under Sponsored Products "Group by `campaign`" and "Group by `campaignPlacement`" and under Sponsored Brands "Group by `campaign`" (guides/reporting/v3/columns.md, report-types/targeting.md and report-types/campaign.md, all re-read 2026-11-09), while v1 holds it at campaign level only (`areas/bids-and-placements.md` §J). So the column is documented for SB as well as SP in v3; a list naming SP alone is incomplete against these three pages. The RSOV definition (§B.6) is different again: there the denominator is "all ads for the reserved keyword". [seat] Three Amazon metrics called "impression share" have three different denominators: one ad product's impressions on a search term across all advertisers (STIS), the campaign's own eligible top-of-search impressions (TOS IS), and all ads in a reserved keyword's Sponsored Brands top slot (RSOV).

3. **GUIDANCE — the Brand Impression Share dashboard is a console view, brand-level, relative to peers.** *Brand impression share* (help GNU4AUAUHS76DNDR, lastModified 2026-02-25, read 2026-11-09): in Insights and Planning → Brand impression share, it shows "top-of-search impression share on their brand terms across their Sponsored Products and Sponsored Brands campaigns over time"; it identifies brand terms automatically, rolls up across all advertising accounts of the brand, and shows the share "relative to peers"; a second section lists campaigns and keywords with filters such as "raising bids and budgets for terms with low impression share but high impression volume". Note on the page: "It represents the advertising share of voice that you have on brand terms." [official] The RSOV library guide places the "Lost Top-of-Search Sales metric… in Amazon Ads Agent under the Insights and Planning tab within the Brand Impression Share report". [official] The seat found no API operation that returns this dashboard (Open question 4).

4. **RULE — keyword recommendations carry share and rank.** Sponsored Products keyword recommendations version 4 add "search term impression share" ("percentage share of all ad-attributed impressions you received on that keyword in the last 30 days") and rank, as `searchTermImpressionShare` and `searchTermImpressionRank`, on `POST /sp/targets/keywords/recommendations` (guides/sponsored-products/keywords/suggestions.md, read 2026-11-09); the same guide says version 5 "includes all the features of version 4" and asks integrators on version 3 or 4 to move to version 5 "as soon as possible". Sponsored Brands keyword recommendations gained the same pair on 2023-05-25, and the SB campaign insights API on 2023-03-07 (release-notes/index.md). [official]

5. **RULE — the partner share-of-voice insight is gated and brand-level.** *Share of voice insights* (guides/recommendations/partner-opportunities/catalog/share-of-voice-insights.md, read 2026-11-09), for Amazon Ads Partner Network members only: share of voice "is the percentage of impressions achieved for a brand and browse node as a result of any keyword compared to total Sponsored Products impressions for the same brand or similar brands"; four opportunity types: brands losing or gaining share of voice, in Sponsored Products and in Sponsored Brands. ### F. Conquesting and the policy line between a keyword and creative

1. **RULE — no Amazon page read forbids targeting another brand's name as a keyword or another brand's ASIN as a target.** The *Targeting* policy page (help GD6SPCK5RJZL73PU, lastModified 2026-08-03, read 2026-11-09) has no competitor or trademark rule; the ad-policy change log (help G8X8M375UPCN4UUC, lastModified 2026-04-27, read 2026-11-09) has entries from October 2025 through April 2026 and none on competitors or trademarks. Amazon's targeting guide names "Conquest : target competitors' products" as a strategy (§A.1), and *What is keyword targeting?* suggests targeting "products from other brands where your offer adds strong value, such as when other brands' products have lower star ratings, fewer reviews, and higher price points." [official] The one place Amazon refuses another company's trademark as a keyword is reserve share of voice (§B.2). This matches `areas/keyword-and-product-targeting.md` §P, re-read for this file.

2. **RULE — creative may not name or disparage a competitor.** *Prohibited content and products for Amazon O&O supply* (help GTEEH3E3J2BDELBA, lastModified 2026-04-10, read 2026-11-09) prohibits "Disparaging or criticizing a named competitor". The Sponsored Brands and display moderation guide (advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/sponsored-brands-display-ads-moderation, re-read 2026-11-09): "Don't use competitive language"; "Ads on Amazon cannot explicitly call out your competitors by name. You may include references to a 'leading brand' or other generic phrasing."; "Don't use superiority claims (the best, the biggest, better than ) or mention competitors by name." Superiority claims "will be rejected unless they can be substantiated". [official]

3. **RULE — comparative claims need substantiation; for health products, in the ad itself.** *Claims and substantiation* (help G6UU66YLSCUYDULQ, lastModified 2026-01-28, read 2026-11-09), for healthcare products "(excluding personal care products)": "Comparative claims: Claims that compare a product or service to a competitor's product (for example, 'Provides 50% more pain relief than the leading brand'). If the claim is on the product packaging, no further substantiation is needed. Otherwise, substantiation, including source and date (within 24 months), is required in the ad creative, on product packaging, or landing page." [official]

4. **RULE — no competitive pricing claims.** *Pricing and Promotions* (help GKP5TCJWVN5W7AFU, lastModified 2026-03-26, read 2026-11-09): ads "must not contain superlatives relating to pricing… or contain pricing claims that are competitive, misleading, or can't be proven", with examples "Competitive price" and "Unbeatable price". [official]

5. **RULE — rights to every element of the creative.** *General requirements* (help G4U9JBZH3664JWX2, lastModified 2026-07-22, read 2026-11-09): advertisers must "Obtain and maintain all necessary rights and permissions for all content in ads, such as logos…", follow the Brand Usage Guidelines for Amazon marks, and "must not use unauthorized content that uses 'Amazon's Choice,' 'Best Seller'". The O&O page also says "Brand Stores can't be titled 'Sale,' 'Discount,' 'Deals,' or other terms that imply a promotion." [official]

6. **Mechanism — why a keyword is not creative.** [seat] In Sponsored Products the shopper sees the advertised product's own title, image, price and rating; the keyword that matched is never shown. In Sponsored Brands and Display the advertiser writes a headline and supplies a logo, which moderation reads. The policy line Amazon draws follows that split: targeting by another brand's name is unrestricted outside RSOV; naming that brand in what the shopper reads is forbidden.

7. **[legal] — the US case law around Amazon's own use of a competitor's mark in search.** *Multi Time Machine v. Amazon.com* (9th Cir., No. 13-55575, decided October 21, 2015; read through the FindLaw case summary, 2026-11-09, which affirms summary judgment for Amazon): the search "mtm special ops" on Amazon returned "a list of other watches not sold by Multi Time Machine"; the court held that "no reasonably prudent customer accustomed to shopping online would likely confuse the source of the products", because Amazon labels each result with its name, manufacturer and photo. *1-800 Contacts v. FTC* (2d Cir., June 11, 2021; read through the AIPLA summary): the FTC had found anticompetitive 1-800 Contacts' settlements requiring rivals to "refrain from engaging in search advertising using specific keywords" and to "employ 1-800 Contacts' trademarks as negative keywords"; the Second Circuit vacated the order under the rule of reason, saying "we owe significant deference to arm's length use agreements" (FTC matter page, ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/141-0200-1-800-contacts-inc-matter: agreements with "at least 14 competing online contact lens retailers", ALJ initial decision October 20, 2017, Commission opinion and final order 2018-11-14, FTC docket 9372; 18-3848 is the Second Circuit appeal number; the page's last entry is the FTC's petition for rehearing of August 6, 2021). In the EU, *Google France* (CJEU C-236/08, 2010) is the leading keyword case; EUR-Lex did not answer and it is cited here only through Bechtold & Tucker (below). [legal] These are US and EU reference points, not advice; trademark law is by jurisdiction (Open question 13).

### I. The auction, reserve prices and the 2026 dispute over them

The auction rules decide what a bidding war costs, so the current state of the dispute belongs here. Mechanics of bidding are in `areas/bids-and-placements.md`.

1. **GUIDANCE — Amazon's description of its auction.** *Understand Amazon Ads auctions* (help GEXGZNBBWPZZK7P8, lastModified 2026-04-27, read 2026-11-09): relevance factors decide winners as well as bids, and "ads with lower bid amounts, but higher relevance… can and do win", over "billions of auctions each day". On reserves: "Some reserves help allocate ad space by setting a bid threshold… Other reserves influence your final CPC or vCPM based on factors including the likelihood that the ad will lead to a sale, predicted return on your ad spend, the value of any competing bids, placement, context… As a result, the price charged to you may exceed the runner-up bid but will never exceed the maximum bid you authorize." And: "Rather than trying to optimize for specific auctions, focus on setting bids that align with your overall goals." [official]

2. **[official, regulator — allegations, untested] — the FTC complaint.** Press release *FTC, States Sue Amazon over Secret Ad Surcharge Scheme*, 2026-08-31 (ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/08/ftc-states-sue-amazon-over-secret-ad-surcharge-scheme, read 2026-11-09): the FTC (vote 2-0) and 22 states sued in the U.S. District Court for the Western District of Washington. Allegations: Amazon told advertisers winners pay "one cent more than the next highest bidder"; since 2019 a "soft reserve price", described internally as a "proxy 2nd price that we calculate", acted as an "invented auction participant"; Sponsored Products advertisers paid "their own winning bid close to 80% of the time"; Amazon "effectively converted its nominally 'second price' auction into a first price auction". Scope: Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands and "Display Ads". Update, 2026-11-05 (…/2026/11/new-details-emerge-amazon-ads-auction-rigging-case, read 2026-11-09): alleged surcharges up to 50% on ordinary days and up to 250% on Prime Day and Black Friday; 2021 holiday CPC +20% with some advertisers over 200%; a 2022 holiday figure of $87M; a 2023 Prime Day food advertiser over 100%; Amazon's internal assessment of that 2023 case quoted as "destroying campaign performance"; an account manager quoted: "we were informed there is not a bug, or a change in auction style, so no worries there" and "This year the marketplace is even more crowded than any year previous". Figures human readers only; these are allegations in a pending case.

3. **[official, Amazon's response] — Amazon's answer.** *Amazon's response to the FTC's lawsuit regarding Sponsored Ads* (aboutamazon.com/company-news/amazon-ftc-sponsored-ads-lawsuit-response, published 2026-08-31, read 2026-11-09): "The case centers on generalized second price auction dynamics"; "In no scenario does an advertiser pay more than their bid"; a hard reserve is "the minimum a bid must surpass to enter an auction"; a soft reserve is "a real-time minimum value that seeks to better reflect what each placement is actually worth". The mechanism in Amazon's own words: "When the winning advertiser's bid exceeds both the hard and soft reserve, they pay the soft reserve, which is less than they were willing to pay. When the winning advertiser's bid exceeds the hard reserve but doesn't meet the soft reserve, we still grant the placement to that advertiser and they pay their bid." Figures: "Average winning bids fell 50% from 2019 to 2025 on Sponsored Products search ads"; "in 2024, approximately 92% of selected Sponsored Products ads were not the highest bid"; "The mean winning advertiser's bid is typically about the 128th bid by amount"; average SP search CPC "remained flat adjusted for inflation" 2019–2024 while "conversion rates grew 24% from 2021 to 2025"; advertisers "saved over $8 billion from 2021 to 2025"; the suit is "misguided". Figures human readers only.

4. **[seat] — what the dispute changes for a bidding war.** Both parties agree that a soft reserve exists, that a winner can pay more than the runner-up, and (in Amazon's own description) that a winner whose bid falls between the hard and the soft reserve pays its own bid; they disagree on how often that happens and on whether it was disclosed. Under either account, raising a max bid in a contested brand auction can raise the price paid even when the rival does not move, because the price is not set by the rival's bid alone. That makes "bid one cent above the competitor" an unreliable model of cost on Amazon.

### K. Stock-outs and the Featured Offer as competitive moments (gap fill 2026-11-09)

A product's ads depend on its stock and on which seller's offer is featured. Amazon states both rules for one's own ads. The same rules hold for a rival's ads, which makes a rival's stock-out or Featured Offer loss a change in who is bidding.

1. **RULE — Sponsored Products and Display serve only from the SKU that holds the Featured Offer; Sponsored Brands does not need it.** *Understand Featured Offer eligibility* (help GYVDGT5WNNW23LJ5, lastModified 2026-10-16, re-read 2026-11-09): "Failing to hold the Featured Offer directly prevents Sponsored Products and Sponsored Display ads from serving impressions, regardless of campaign status shown in the campaign manager." "Sponsored Products and Display campaigns generate 0 impressions when the advertised ASIN or SKU doesn't hold the Featured Offer, even when the campaign shows "Delivering" status in Amazon Ads Agent." "Sponsored Brands campaigns, including Sponsored Brands video, aren't affected and continue to serve even when another seller holds the Featured Offer, as long as the advertised product is available to buy on Amazon." "When multiple SKUs exist for 1 ASIN, only the SKU that holds the Featured Offer is eligible to serve ads." And: "Featured Offer status can shift to a competitor when their offer becomes more competitive. When status shifts, impressions for the previous holder stop immediately." The eligibility factors it lists include "Product availability : Sufficient stock levels to fulfill orders" and "Price competitiveness : Your total price (item + shipping) compared to other sellers". An Amazon staff reply (Manny_Amazon, Seller Forums thread *Sponsored Products and Buy Box*, re-read 2026-11-09): "Amazon will only display your Sponsored Products ads when your product is the Featured Offer". [official] `beyond/ad-products-and-eligibility.md` §D and `areas/diagnosis.md` carry this gate as a delivery question.

2. **RULE — an out-of-stock product's ads stop.** *How to improve your product detail page for advertising* (advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/improve-your-products-for-advertising, re-read 2026-11-09): "Your products need to be in-stock in order to be the featured offer and be eligible for inclusion in a Sponsored Products campaign. If a product you are advertising goes out of stock, it will be automatically paused within your campaign." Amazon Ads *FAQs* (advertising.amazon.com/resources/faq, re-read 2026-11-09): "If your product isn't the Featured Offer or is out of stock, your ad will not display." [official]

3. **Mechanism — what those two rules mean for a rival's ads.** [seat] The rules are stated for the advertiser, and they bind every advertiser alike. So when a rival's ASIN goes out of stock, the rival's Sponsored Products and Display ads for that ASIN stop serving; and when a reseller's offer takes the Featured Offer on the rival's ASIN, the rival brand's own SKU stops serving SP and SD impressions "immediately", while the reseller's SKU becomes the one that may serve. The rival's Sponsored Brands ads for that product keep serving only "as long as the advertised product is available to buy on Amazon". Each event therefore takes a bidder out of the SP and SD auctions on the queries and pages where the rival advertised that ASIN. No page read says what Amazon shows in the paid slots of a detail page whose product is out of stock or has no Featured Offer, or whether a product target aimed at such a page keeps serving (Open question 18).

4. **Mechanism — the reverse case: a reseller holding one's own Featured Offer.** [seat, from §K.1] When another seller's offer holds the Featured Offer on one's own ASIN, one's own SP and SD ads for that ASIN serve 0 impressions; one's SB ads, including SB video, keep serving and keep sending clicks to a detail page whose default purchase is the other seller's offer. Amazon's page gives the fix for the case where the featured SKU is one's own other SKU ("Add the SKU that holds the Featured Offer. Impressions should resume within 24-48 hours."), and routes the case of another seller to Seller Support ("Advertising Support can't directly restore Featured Offer eligibility"). [official, for the quoted sentences] Removing an unauthorised seller is a Brand Registry and Seller Central matter outside the Ads API (Brand Registry's *Report a Violation*, §Pages read).

5. **GUIDANCE — Amazon's own tools name offer state, not rivals' stock.** The 2024-02-01 release note on campaign diagnostics covers "featured offer competitiveness" (§J.2). No Amazon Ads page or API read reports a rival's stock or Featured Offer state; Amazon's APIs report a rival's offers only through SP-API Product Pricing (§*What cx must read at run time*). [official, seat]

### Brand-term bidding: defend, but measure what it adds

1. **Perpetua** (*How to use branded traffic to strategically drive incremental sales on Amazon*, perpetua.io/blog-branded-traffic-strategy-amazon-ads/, undated, no author shown, read 2026-11-09) frames two "truths": "Truth #1: Branded ads can cannibalize organic sales" and "Truth #2: On Amazon, rivals can bid on your brand terms". It says "Branded search is non‑negotiable when your brand queries are materially contested" ("If competitors are appearing above your organic tile or aggressively targeting your PDPs"), names "ASIN mirroring" as a trap to avoid — "when the same product shows up both in the top organic slot and in your top‑of‑search ad", paying "for a click you might have earned for free, without meaningfully increasing your shelf space" — and suggests using branded traffic to "expand coverage rather than duplicate it": complements (refills, accessories, bundles), high-margin and premium lines, launches and overstock rather than the item already ranking first. It gives a "Rule of thumb: If branded spend is >40%, you're likely paying for traffic you'd get organically", says "Branded terms convert at the highest rate and carry the lowest CPCs, which can make advertising cost of sale (ACOS) look artificially strong", and recommends TACoS and brand-halo conversions as the reading instead. ### Where practitioners disagree

- **How much brand spend is too much.** Perpetua offers a 40%-of-spend rule of thumb; SalesDuo and Haus say the answer depends on measured competition on the query, not on a share of spend; Amazon's guidance says to defend because competitors "may be bidding". The research below sides with "depends on competition".
- **What the defence target costs.** AMALYZE says own-ASIN targets win at "the lowest" CPC in the auction and calls the auction second-price; Amazon's own help page says the price "may exceed the runner-up bid" (§I.1), and the FTC alleges winners often pay their own bid (§I.2). No page read measures own-ASIN CPC against competitor CPC on the same page.
- **Where the paid slots are on a detail page.** AMALYZE puts the Sponsored Products carousel "immediately under the main image block"; Helium 10 puts it "above the customer question-and-answer section"; JumpFly (2022) puts Sponsored Display at the top and Sponsored Products further down. Amazon publishes no layout (Open question 11); the layout varies by device and over time.
- **Whether own-brand targets perform.** AMALYZE and JumpFly recommend own-ASIN defence targets; Amazon's SB API description says "targeting your own brands usually results in lower performance than targeting competitors' brands" (§E.8a), with no measure stated.
- **Whether conquest pays.** Perpetua calls competitor terms more incremental but expensive; Amazon suggests conquest where the advertiser's offer is stronger (ratings, reviews, price); the research (Simonov & Hill, on Bing) finds stolen clicks bounce back far more often.
- **Whether SP keeps running without the Featured Offer.** Trellis's Qore page says Sponsored Products ads "keep running" after a Buy Box loss; Amazon's help page says SP and Display "generate 0 impressions" without it and that only Sponsored Brands keeps serving (§K.1). Perpetua, Canopy and Pattern describe the loss as wasted or leaking spend without separating ad types.
- **How to react to a rival's stock-out.** Perpetua raises bids "across your campaigns" for the stock-out period; Jarvio and Netpeak aim product targets at the stocked-out rival's page; Eva treats the stock-out as a window of "typically 2 to 6 weeks" and puts one's own inventory cover first. None publishes a measured effect, and Amazon's pages do not say what serves in the paid slots of an out-of-stock page (§K.3).
- **Where the cheapest defence is.** AMALYZE calls an SB banner on one's own term "the cheapest defence"; Perpetua (2021) calls the Store the only place rivals cannot buy space; Amazon sells a reserved slot (RSOV, §B) and a top-of-search goal (§C). No page read compares their costs on the same terms.

## Published research

All items [research]. For each: what was measured, on what data, and how it transfers to Amazon. Abstracts were read on IDEAS/RePEc, EconPapers, arXiv or the publisher's page on 2026-11-09; where only the abstract was read, the claim is limited to it.

### Own-brand keyword advertising: how incremental is it?

1. **Blake, Nosko & Tadelis (2015), "Consumer Heterogeneity and Paid Search Effectiveness: A Large-Scale Field Experiment", *Econometrica* 83(1), 155–174.** "A series of large‑scale field experiments done at eBay" on paid search. Finding (abstract): "brand keyword ads have no measurable short‑term benefits"; for non-brand keywords, average returns are negative because frequent users, not influenced by ads, account for most spend. Coviello, Gneezy & Goette's abstract summarises the eBay result as "when the company suspended paid search, most of the traffic still ended up on its website". (Which engines were involved, and how many rivals bid on eBay's brand terms, are in the paper's body, not in the abstracts read.) **Transfer:** [seat] general search engines where the brand's own site took the traffic back when ads stopped; read beside the next paper, it looks like the low-competition case, though the abstracts read do not state the competitive state. On Amazon, the organic result for a brand query is the brand's own listing, but rivals' Sponsored Products and Sponsored Brands ads can sit above it, which is the condition the next paper isolates.

2. **Simonov, Nosko & Rao (2018), "Competition and Crowd-Out for Brand Keywords in Sponsored Search", *Marketing Science* 37(2), 200–215.** Experiments on Bing across thousands of brands, varying whether the brand's own ad and competitors' ads were shown. Findings: with no competitors, the brand's own ad adds 1%–4% of clicks, less for larger brands, at a "cost per incremental click" significantly higher than on other keywords; when the focal ad is present, competitors in positions 2–4 "steal" 1%–5%; brands that do not advertise while facing competition lose 18%–42% of clicks; the authors find the "return on investment on defensive advertising to be strongly positive". **Transfer:** the closest published analogue to Amazon brand defence; the magnitudes are for a web engine's text ads and a click outcome, not Amazon purchases, but the mechanism — defence pays in proportion to competitive presence — is the one Amazon's guidance invokes. (human readers only)

3. **Simonov & Hill (2021), "Competitive Advertising on Brand Search: Traffic Stealing and Click Quality", *Marketing Science* 40(5), 923–945.** A large-scale quasi-experimental ad allocation on Bing. Competitors steal 1%–2% of traffic, rising to 6%–15% "if the focal brand's link is exogenously removed from the top paid position"; most stealing is by "a competitor in the top paid link (6%–9% of traffic)". Stolen clicks are lower quality: a "quick back" within 30 seconds happens on 20%–47% of stolen clicks against 7% of the focal brand's clicks. **Transfer:** the conquest side: on brand queries, poached shoppers mostly return. On Amazon the analogue is a rival's ad on a brand query drawing clicks that then fail to convert; Amazon reports conversion for the advertiser, so the conquesting seller sees this directly in its own conversion rate. (human readers only)

4. **Coviello, Gneezy & Goette (2017), "A Large-Scale Field Experiment to Evaluate the Effectiveness of Paid Search Advertising", CESifo WP 6684 / CEPR DP 12333.** The abstract opens on "paid links to branded search terms" and reports a similar experiment at Edmunds.com, "arguably a more representative company": "More than half of the paid traffic is lost when we shut off paid-links search" (EconPapers abstract). **Transfer:** a counter-example to Blake et al. on branded search; the abstract does not state the competitive state of Edmunds' queries. Together with item 1, it shows the answer is site-specific. (human readers only)

### Competition and spillovers among advertisers

5. **Golden & Horton (2021), "The Effects of Search Advertising on Competitors: An Experiment Before a Merger", *Management Science* 67(1), 342–362, doi 10.1287/mnsc.2019.3534.** "Firm Vary" temporarily suspended its Google sponsored search in randomly selected US advertising markets; it later merged with its closest competitor, "Firm Fixed", whose data the authors then used (the firms are unnamed in the abstract). Firm Vary lost customers, but "only 63% as many as a nonexperimental estimate" would say; spillovers to the rival ("Firm Fixed") were "surprisingly small, even in the market for Firm Vary's brand name", the two being "effectively duopsonists". **Transfer:** [seat] in this one experiment a nonexperimental estimate overstated the customer loss from pausing (the abstract does not say what kind of nonexperimental estimate), and a rival did not automatically take what the paused firm gave up. `method/change-measurement.md` does not cite this paper; the directional finding it carries on pauses is the opposite one, from Ju, Zhao & Aral (2025, mobile apps), that a short pause understates a loss that builds through rankings over weeks. The two concern different horizons and mechanisms (a market-level customer count on Google; organic ranking feedback in an app store), so the sign of a naive pause read's error is not settled by either for Amazon.

6. **Lewis & Nguyen (2015), "Display advertising's competitive spillovers to consumer search", *Quantitative Marketing and Economics* 13(2), 93–115.** A natural experiment randomising ad delivery on 500 million Yahoo! homepage visits. In three advertisers' campaigns, display ads raised searches for the advertised brand by 30–45% and for competitors' brands by up to 23%; "the total number of incremental searches for competitors is 2–8 times the increase for advertisers' brands". **Transfer:** upper-funnel ads raise category search that rivals can harvest on their brand and generic terms; on Amazon, the branded-search metrics (§A.7) count only searches for the advertiser's own brand, so this spillover is invisible to the advertiser. (human readers only)

7. **Moshary (2025), "Does Sponsored Search Advertising Augment Organic Search? Evidence from an E-Commerce Platform", *Management Science* 71(11), 9687–9709.** A field experiment blocking all sponsored search for 3% of visitors on an unnamed e-commerce platform: sponsored search "both steals business from organic listings and also reduces the total volume of transactions", yet stays profitable for the platform because ad revenue outweighs lost commissions. **Transfer:** [seat] a platform-level result on one platform: there, sponsored search shifted business away from organic listings and lowered total transactions. For a brand, this is the near-zero-sum frame in which defence and conquest operate. Detail on organic substitution is in `areas/ads-organic-and-total.md`.

8. **Joo, Shi & Abhishek (2024), "Do Sellers Benefit from Sponsored Product Listings? Evidence from an Online Marketplace", *Marketing Science* 43(4), 817–839.** A field study on a mobile marketplace app with experimental and natural variation: consumers prefer top-ranked organic listings to sponsored listings of the same product and position, and become indifferent at lower positions; top organic listings are "perceived as more credible"; a simulation shows a seller "may benefit financially if a lower-ranked organic listing can be replaced with a top-ranked sponsored listing". **Transfer:** [seat] a seller already first organically on its brand query gains little from adding a sponsored copy of the same item there; a seller pushed down by rivals' ads can gain by buying back a top sponsored slot.

9. **Dash et al. (2024), "Sponsored is the New Organic: Implications of Sponsored Results on Quality of Search Results in the Amazon Marketplace", arXiv 2407.19099.** 4,800 simulated searches on Amazon in four countries, 2M organic and 638K sponsored results: items with poor organic rank (beyond 100th) appear as sponsored results "even before the top organic results on the first page", and "in majority of the cases, these top sponsored results are costlier and are of poorer quality than the top organic results" (abstract). **Transfer:** direct Amazon evidence that the results page lets a weaker product buy its way above a stronger one, which is the opening for conquest.

10. **Farronato, Fradkin & MacKay (2025), "Vertical Integration and Consumer Choice: Evidence from a Field Experiment", NBER WP 34135 (2025-08-18).** A field experiment on Amazon that hides Amazon's own brands, and studies their placement in search (NBER page: issued August 2025, revised March 2026): consumers chose similar products, with no extra search effort or cross-platform switching; hiding cost consumers about 5.5% in welfare, and demotion did not raise welfare. **Transfer:** on Amazon, shoppers substitute readily among similar products shown to them, so position on the page is a large part of brand competition. (human readers only)

### Models of buying a rival's keyword

11. **Desai, Shin & Staelin (2014), "The Company That You Keep: When to Buy a Competitor's Keyword", *Marketing Science* 33(4), 485–508.** Game-theory model. A firm "may buy its own brand name only to defend itself"; by buying it, the brand owner "precludes the competitor"; mutual bidding on each other's brands creates "a prisoner's dilemma… the search engine captures the lost profits"; the quality gap between the firms moderates both decisions. **Transfer:** the theoretical frame for a brand-defence standoff: both sellers may be worse off and the platform better off.

12. **Sayedi, Jerath & Srinivasan (2014), "Competitive Poaching in Sponsored Search Advertising and Its Strategic Impact on Traditional Advertising", *Marketing Science* 33(4), 586–608.** Model: "as budget asymmetry increases, the smaller-budget firm poaches more"; the search engine benefits from limiting poaching, which explains relevance handicaps on trademarked keywords. **Transfer:** a smaller rival on a large brand's terms is the predicted pattern. On Amazon, the relevance factors in the auction (§I.1) play the role of the handicap; whether Amazon weighs them specifically against conquest is not stated anywhere read.

13. **Jerath, Ma, Park & Srinivasan (2011), "A 'Position Paradox' in Sponsored Search Auctions", *Marketing Science* 30(4), 612–627.** Model plus Korean search-engine data: a superior firm may bid lower and sit below an inferior firm yet still get more clicks, because informed consumers look for it; the inferior firm has a stronger incentive to be at the top under pay-per-click. **Transfer:** a strong brand need not hold position 1 on a contested generic query; on its own brand query the logic is stronger still, since shoppers came for it. Pairs with Ghose & Yang.

14. **Ghose & Yang (2009), "An Empirical Analysis of Search Engine Advertising: Sponsored Search in Electronic Markets", *Management Science* 55(10), 1605–1622.** A six-month panel of several hundred keywords of a nationwide retailer on Google, modelled with a hierarchical Bayesian simultaneous-equations model (Crossref abstract, re-read 2026-11-09): "conversion rates are highest at the top and decrease with rank", but "profits are often higher at the middle positions than at the top or the bottom ones". **Transfer:** top-of-search share is not automatically the profitable target. This is one of four studies that disagree on how conversion moves with position; `areas/bids-and-placements.md` owns the reconciliation (its P-51): Rutz, Bucklin & Sonnier (2012, a lodging chain, latent instrumental variables) find higher positions raise both click-through and conversion; Agarwal, Hosanagar & Smith (2011, a retailer's field experiment) find "conversion rate increases with position", that is, rises lower down; and Narayanan & Kalyanam (item 14a) show that auction-set positions bias such comparisons. [seat] This file uses Ghose & Yang only for its profit finding, not as the answer on conversion by position.

14a. **Narayanan & Kalyanam (2015), "Position Effects in Search Advertising and their Moderators: A Regression Discontinuity Approach", *Marketing Science* 34(3), 388–407, doi 10.1287/mksc.2014.0893.** Crossref abstract, read 2026-11-09: "Because positions are determined through an auction, there are significant selection issues in measuring position effects. A simple mean comparison of outcomes at two positions is likely to be biased due to these selection issues", and randomising the focal advertiser's bids cannot remove the selection that "competitors' bidding behavior" induces; with a regression discontinuity on a focal advertiser's and its major competitors' daily data, "our RD estimates of these effects are much smaller, and exist only in some of the positions". Moderators: "Position effects are stronger when the advertiser is smaller, and when the consumer has low prior experience with the keyword for the advertiser. They are weaker when the keyword phrase has specific brand or product information, when the ad copy is more specific as in exact matching options, and on weekends compared to weekdays." **Transfer:** [seat] for brand defence, the moderator that matters is the last: on a query that already names the brand, position moved outcomes less in this study, which points the same way as the brand-keyword experiments (items 1–3). The setting is web search text ads, not Amazon product ads.

### Bidding wars and price cycles

15. **Zhang & Feng (2011), "Cyclical Bid Adjustments in Search-Engine Advertising", *Management Science* 57(9), 1703–1719.** Model plus data from two search engines: competing advertisers' bids follow Edgeworth cycles — "price-escalating phases interrupted by price-collapsing phases" — under both first- and second-price auctions. **Transfer:** bid escalation on a contested term followed by collapse is a documented equilibrium pattern, not only a sign of one rival's aggression. Automated bidders on both sides are the setting in which it arises.

16. **Edelman, Ostrovsky & Schwarz (2007), "Internet Advertising and the Generalized Second-Price Auction: Selling Billions of Dollars Worth of Keywords", *American Economic Review* 97(1), 242–259.** GSP is not truthful: truth-telling is not an equilibrium, and bidders shade bids. **Transfer:** the theoretical base under Amazon's "generalized 'second-price' auction" (§I.3); with soft reserves (§I.1) the price rule departs from textbook GSP, so bid-shading logic transfers only loosely.

17. **Brown & MacKay (2023), "Competition in Pricing Algorithms", *American Economic Journal: Microeconomics* 15(2), 109–156.** High-frequency online retail price data and a model: firms with faster, rival-reactive pricing algorithms can sustain higher prices, more dispersion and larger merger effects. **Transfer:** about product prices, not ad bids; relevant because a competitor's price cut on the detail page changes who wins the click and the conversion (Pacvue's case above), and the repricers on Amazon are this kind of algorithm.

### Stock-outs and substitution (gap fill 2026-11-09)

17a. **Conlon & Mortimer (2013), "Demand Estimation Under Incomplete Product Availability", *American Economic Journal: Microeconomics* 5(4), 1–30, doi 10.1257/mic.5.4.1.** Crossref abstract, read 2026-11-09: vending machines with a wireless inventory system tracking availability every four hours; the data "provide valuable variation for identifying substitution patterns when products stock out", and "the corrected model predicts significantly larger impacts of stock-out events on profitability" than a model ignoring availability. **Transfer:** [seat] a stock-out moves buyers to substitutes, and a demand read that ignores availability misjudges the effect; on Amazon the rival's stock-out is a change in the choice set on the rival's queries, and a seller's sales lift in that window is partly substitution that ends when the rival restocks. Physical retail, not search ads.

17b. **Musalem, Olivares, Bradlow, Terwiesch & Corsten (2010), "Structural Estimation of the Effect of Out-of-Stocks", *Management Science* 56(7), 1180–1197, doi 10.1287/mnsc.1100.1170.** Crossref abstract, read 2026-11-09: a structural demand model "that endogenously captures the effect of out-of-stocks on customer choice by simulating a time-varying set of available alternatives", from store-level sales and "partial information on product availability"; it quantifies "the lost sales induced by out-of-stock products" and suggests "price promotion policies that can be used to help mitigate their negative economic impact, which run counter to simple commonly used heuristics". **Transfer:** [seat] the same choice-set logic; neither paper measures advertising during a rival's stock-out, and the seat found no study that does.

### Trademarks in keyword advertising

18. **Bechtold & Tucker (2014), "Trademarks, Triggers, and Online Search", *Journal of Empirical Legal Studies* 11(4), 718–750.** After the CJEU's *Google France* decision, Google relaxed its trademark-keyword policy in continental Europe in September 2010. On 5.38M site visits: little average change; navigational searches became less likely to reach the trademark owner and non-navigational searches more likely; the effect was stronger for lesser-known marks. **Transfer:** opening a brand's name to rivals' bidding mostly hurts small brands on navigational queries — on Amazon, the brand query of a small brand with a loyal searcher base.

19. **Chiou & Tucker (2012), "How Does the Use of Trademarks by Third-Party Sellers Affect Online Search?", *Marketing Science* 31(5), 819–837.** Google's June 2009 change allowing any third-party reseller of a product to use its trademark in ad text, hotel industry: this weakly reduced clicks on the trademark holder's paid ads, a decrease "outweighed by a large increase" in clicks on its unpaid links; when a reseller focuses on the trademark, "the ads become less distinct, and customers are more likely to ignore the advertised offers and buy from the direct channel". **Transfer:** [seat] concerns resellers of the same product using the owner's mark in creative, not rival brands; on Amazon, naming a competitor in creative is forbidden anyway (§F.2). It suggests a mark in someone else's ad text can send shoppers to the owner, but transfers only loosely to Amazon conquest.

### Platform design

20. **Long, Jerath & Sarvary (2022), "Designing an Online Retail Marketplace: Leveraging Information from Sponsored Advertising", *Marketing Science* 41(1), 115–138**, and **Choi & Mela (2019), "Monetizing Online Marketplaces", *Marketing Science* 38(6), 948–972.** Models (Choi & Mela with marketplace data) of a platform trading ad revenue against commissions; Long et al. show bids reveal seller information that the platform can use in organic ranking ("strategic listing"), which intensifies ad competition. **Transfer:** background for why a platform keeps conquest open: ad competition on brand queries is revenue, and in Long et al.'s model it also feeds organic ranking. No claim here about what Amazon's ranking uses.

17. **Amazon's SB API recommends a seller's own brands for negative targeting, stating that targeting one's own brands "usually results in lower performance than targeting competitors' brands".** Source: SB v4 OpenAPI, `GET /sb/negativeTargets/brands/recommendations`.
18. **Sponsored Products and Sponsored Brands prompts are on by default in eligible US campaigns, appear in shopping results and on detail pages, and have been charged under the campaign's CPC bidding since general availability on March 25, 2026; prompts with clicks appear in existing SP/SB reporting.** Sources: *Sponsored Products prompts and Sponsored Brands prompts* (what's new); v3 *Prompt Ad Extension reports*.
19. **Sponsored Products and Display ads for an ASIN serve only from the SKU holding the Featured Offer and only while the product is in stock; when the Featured Offer shifts, the previous holder's impressions stop at once; Sponsored Brands ads, video included, keep serving while the product is available to buy. These rules bind a rival's ads as they bind the seller's.** Sources: *Understand Featured Offer eligibility*; *How to improve your product detail page for advertising*; Amazon Ads *FAQs*; staff reply in *Sponsored Products and Buy Box*.
20. **When another seller holds the Featured Offer on the seller's own ASIN, the seller's SP and SD ads for it stop and its SB ads continue, sending clicks to a page whose default offer is the other seller's.** Source: *Understand Featured Offer eligibility*.
21. **Sponsored Brands formats are collections (the older product collection is deprecated; automatic and manual collections replace it), Store Spotlight, video to a detail page (`VIDEO`) and brand video to a Store (`BRAND_VIDEO`), plus RSOV-only Brand Gallery; under Drive page visits every format can serve on search pages, detail pages and the homepage, and under the two top-of-search goals only formats with a Store serve, at top of search.** Sources: SB 4.0 OpenAPI; SB *Ad types overview* and *Video ad* guides; *Campaign goals for Sponsored Brands*; *Understand Sponsored Brands*; *Drive branded search engagement with Sponsored Brands Brand Gallery*.

## What cx must read at run time

All Ads API calls go through the door `cx raw amazon-ads --origin <origin> --method <M> --path <P>` with the profile's scope header, per `method/ads-api-surface.md`; report mechanics per `method/reporting.md`. SP-API and Keepa reads are by name; this seat called none of them.

### Who the brand is, and which terms are "branded"

- **The brand and its ids.** SB `GET /brands` (SB 3.0 YAML; `brandId`, `brandEntityId` "available exclusively for seller advertisers", `brandRegistryName`; referenced from the SB v4 `CreateCampaign.brandEntityId` description); Brand Home `POST /brand/stores/v1/stores/list` (`stores[]` of `brandEntityId`, `storeId`, `storeName`, `storeStatus`) and `POST /brand/stores/v1/storePages/list` (a Store's pages); not `GET /v2/stores`, which the *Deprecations* page shut off on June 1, 2026 even though the Brand Store overview guide still shows it (`method/ads-api-surface.md` owns this); `/adsApi/v1/query/brandStores`; SB brand recommendations `POST /sb/recommendations/targets/brand` for the brand ids used in `asinBrandSameAs` refinements and negatives.
- **Amazon's own label on keywords.** `POST /adsApi/v1/query/recommendations` with `BRAND_PRODUCT_KEYWORD` (field `keywordTheme`: `Branded`, `Generic`, `Seasonal`, …) and `BRAND_PRODUCT_SEGMENT`.
- **Reservability as a brand test.** `POST /adsApi/v1/create/keywordReservationValidations/sb` returns `isReservable`, `reservationRejectedReason` and a `keywordReservationValidationId` for each keyword; it is Amazon's own answer to "is this term the seller's brand, someone else's, or generic". It is a create-family call on the v1 surface, its request carries no deal id, and its answer carries a record id; no page read says whether it creates anything billable (Open question 2).
- **Amazon's own-brand list for negatives.** SB v4 `GET /sb/negativeTargets/brands/recommendations` (on the SB 4.0 model and the JSON "Sponsored Brands 3.0" file, not on the SB 3.0 YAML, whose negative paths are `/sb/negativeKeywords` and `/sb/negativeTargets`; sellers and vendors; US, CA, MX, UK, DE, FR, ES, IT, IN, JP), which Amazon says includes the advertiser's own brands.
- **The seller's own definition.** Brand names, product lines and misspellings from the listing and the Brand Registry record (SP-API Catalog Items `brand` attribute; Listings Items); the brand list a seller states.

## Open questions

1. **Reserve share of voice flight length and minimum.** Amazon's own pages give 15–92 days, 30–92 days and "e.g., 14–90 days", and $3,000 versus $6K minimum (US). Which governs today is not stated; a run-time read of the help page and the pricing response is the only check.
2. **Is `keywordReservationValidations` side-effect-free?** The request carries no deal id and the answer carries validity, a reason and a `keywordReservationValidationId`; the API guide calls it a check ("call this API to check whether they can be reserved") and marks it optional, but it sits in the v1 `create` family and returns a record id, and no page says whether it creates anything billable.
3. **RSOV marketplaces.** The library guide lists 20 including IE; the what's-new launch page (modified 2026-10-09) lists 20 with EG instead of IE; the SB v4 model says goal-based campaigns are not supported in IE; two help pages (G86SD7HK6NHHRB9B and GXXPFXLSVX9VD3U9) exclude Spain (absent from both lists). The library guide and the what's-new page carry the same modification date, 2026-10-09, so neither is the later word.

5. **Partner share-of-voice insights for a seller's own app.** The insight is for Partner Network members; whether a seller-authorised tool like cx qualifies is not stated.
6. **Seller campaign-level negative products on manual SP campaigns.** The API guide's warning (vendors only) conflicts with the 2025-05-22 release note (every campaign, US) and the SP 3.0 model (AUTO only), as recorded in `areas/search-terms-and-negatives.md`. Which is current is open.
7. **Negative broad.** A library guide mentions negative broad; the help page lists only negative phrase and exact.
8. **Is the soft reserve still applied, and how?** Amazon's help page (lastModified 2026-04-27) still describes reserves that set price; the FTC case is pending. Whether anything changed after 2026-08-31 is not stated on the pages read.
9. **Prompts, Rufus and Alexa+ in share metrics.** No page read says whether prompt or Rufus impressions count in STIS, TOS IS or brand impression share. (The billing date is settled: Amazon's prompts launch page gives general availability and CPC charging from 2026-03-25; the prompt report page says "Prompts with clicks will show in your existing Sponsored Products or Sponsored Brands reporting", without saying which metrics.)
10. **Expanded targets on own-ASIN defence.** Expanded product targeting is default; whether an own-ASIN target with expansion on spends materially on competitors' pages is measurable only from the seller's own reports ("Product-expanded" rows in the targeting column; the search term report for SP; the matched target report for SD only — see question 17). No public measurement found.
11. **The detail-page ad layout.** Amazon publishes no map of where Sponsored Products and Sponsored Display ads sit on a detail page; practitioner maps disagree and change by device and over time.
12. **Brand query definition.** Amazon's branded-search metrics use "enhanced brand matching" including misspellings, RSOV rejects misspellings, and reports carry no branded flag on a search term. A single Amazon definition that a seller's reports can be split by does not exist on the pages read.
13. **Trademark law by jurisdiction.** US case law read here (MTM v. Amazon; 1-800 Contacts) and the EU's Google France decision (unreached) cover keyword use; other jurisdictions where cx sellers operate (UK, JP, CN-based sellers on US marketplaces) were not researched.
14. **Incrementality of brand defence on Amazon specifically.** The controlled studies are on Bing, Google, eBay and Edmunds; the Amazon-specific evidence read is Amazon's own before/after case studies of attributed sales and vendor meta-analyses. No independent Amazon holdout study of brand-term ads was reached (the Search Engine Land article on Amazon incrementality was blocked).
15. **Which ad product takes the peer-brands theme.** `KEYWORDS_RELATED_TO_PEER_BRANDS_PRODUCT_CATEGORY` is in the v1 model's shared `ThemeMatchType`, `MatchType` and `TargetMatchTypeFilter` enums; no guide read says whether Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands or both accept it.

## Positions

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-1.** Amazon lets any advertiser target any brand's name as a keyword and any ASIN as a product target, and names brand defence ("Protect") and competitor targeting ("Conquest") among its targeting strategies. Sources: [official] *A guide to targeting with Sponsored Products*; *What is keyword targeting?*; *Targeting* policy page (no competitor rule).

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-2.** Amazon's guidance is to consider bidding on one's own branded keywords "as a brand defense strategy since your competitors may be bidding on your branded keywords, too". Source: [official] *A guide to targeting with Sponsored Products*.

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-3.** Ad creative on Amazon may not name or disparage a competitor, may not make unsubstantiated superiority claims or competitive price claims, and may refer to a "leading brand" generically; health-product comparative claims need dated substantiation. Sources: [official] *Prohibited content and products for Amazon O&O supply*; *Sponsored Brands and display ads moderation guide*; *Claims and substantiation*; *Pricing and Promotions*.

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-4.** Reserve share of voice reserves the Sponsored Brands top-of-search slot at a fixed price for the advertiser's own exact-match branded keywords only; competitor brands, generic terms, misspellings and trademarks shared by more than two brands are rejected. Sources: [official] *Branded keyword guidelines and keyword suspension*; *Sponsored Brands reserve share of voice* guide.

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-5.** Once a reserve share of voice campaign starts, its keywords, duration and budget cannot change; a proposed deal cannot be modified, and it can be deleted only until twelve hours before its start, which archives its campaigns. Sources: [official] *Reserve keywords in a Sponsored Brands campaign*; RSOV API guide.

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-6.** Amazon's pages state different reserve share of voice limits — flights of 15–92, 30–92 or "e.g., 14–90" days, and a US minimum of $3,000 or $6K — while all three help pages agree on 5 to 1,000 reserved keywords (figures as of 2026-11-09). Sources: [official] *Reserve keywords in a Sponsored Brands campaign*; *Campaign goals for Sponsored Brands*; *Branded keyword guidelines and keyword suspension*; *Sponsored Brands reserve share of voice* guide.

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-7.** A Sponsored Brands campaign has one goal — drive page visits (CPC, all placements), grow brand impression share (vCPM, top-of-search) or reserve share of voice (fixed price) — set at creation and not changeable. Sources: [official] *Campaign goals for Sponsored Brands*; SB v4 OpenAPI.

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-8.** Amazon reports its reserve share of voice beta (15+ advertisers, May–August 2025) raised Sponsored Brands top-of-search impression share on reserved keywords from 62.7% to 99.3%; these are Amazon's own before/after figures, not holdout tests. Sources: [official] *Sponsored Brands reserve share of voice* guide; Samsung and Bayer case studies.

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-9.** Search term impression share is the account's share of one ad product's ad impressions on a search term across all advertisers (Amazon's example: 20% means "you won 20% of all Sponsored Products ad impressions for that search term on that date"), and its rank is the account's position among advertisers; both are account-wide and cannot be split by campaign, ad group, target or placement. Sources: [official] v1 impression-share metric pages; *Downloadable reports in sponsored ads*; *Search term impression share report for Sponsored Products* (what's new).

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-10.** Top-of-search impression share is a campaign's top-of-search impressions divided by the top-of-search impressions it was eligible for, so its denominator is the campaign's own eligibility, not the whole field. Sources: [official] v1 impression-share metric pages; *Top-of-search impression share metric* (what's new); [opinion] Trellis.

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-11.** The console's Brand impression share dashboard shows top-of-search impression share on brand terms Amazon identifies automatically, across the brand's SP and SB campaigns and accounts, relative to peers; no API for it was found. Sources: [official] *Brand impression share* help; [opinion] Intentwise.

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-12.** None of Amazon's share metrics names competitors; the Brand Analytics Top Search Terms report names the three most-clicked ASINs on a query, with click and conversion shares, and Sponsored Brands brand recommendations return suggested brand ids by keyword without shares. Sources: [official] `sellingPartnerSearchTermsReport` schema; v1 impression-share metric pages; SB negative product targeting guide; [opinion] Trellis.

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-13.** Search Query Performance reports the requested ASINs' share of a query's impressions, clicks, cart adds and purchases on the search results page, by week, month or quarter, for Brand Registry sellers with the Brand Analytics role; through SP-API the report requires an `asin` list of at most 200 characters. Sources: [official] `sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport` schema; SP-API report type values (analytics); sell.amazon.com *Amazon Brand Analytics*. Trellis reads its counts as organic and paid combined [opinion, Trellis].

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-14.** Amazon recommends targeting one's own product pages and bidding higher on them to keep one's range visible there. Source: [official] *Best practices for your Sponsored Products ads*.

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-15.** Expanded product targeting is the default on a product target and also serves on products Amazon judges closely related; such matches are labelled "Product-expanded" in reports. Source: [official] *Set up product targeting*.

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-16.** Negative product and brand targeting keeps Sponsored Products ads off named detail pages, the advertiser's own or a competitor's, with a buffer of 72 hours for negative keywords and 96 hours for negative products (figures as of 2026-11-09). Sources: [official] *Add negative keywords or negative products targeting*; SP negative targeting API guide.

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-17.** Amazon's API guide says campaign-level negative product targeting on manual Sponsored Products campaigns is for vendors only, while a 2025 release note widens it; the two pages conflict. Sources: [official] SP negative product and brand targeting guide; release notes (as recorded in `areas/search-terms-and-negatives.md`).

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-18.** The matched target report, listing the product detail pages an ad appeared on that drew at least one click, exists for Sponsored Display only; Sponsored Products and Sponsored Brands search term reports include only clicked impressions and show `*` where a detail-page placement had no search keyword. Sources: [official] *Downloadable reports in sponsored ads*; v1 *Matched target* dimension page; v3 *Search term reports*.

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-19.** Sponsored Display can target audiences who viewed or bought products of a given brand (`asinBrandSameAs`) and detail pages of chosen products, own or other. Sources: [official] SD audience targeting guide; *Understand targeting in Display campaigns*.

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-20.** The Amazon Ads v1 theme `KEYWORDS_RELATED_TO_PEER_BRANDS_PRODUCT_CATEGORY` is defined as search terms shoppers use for "products from other brands in the category of your advertised products", with "The peer brands are selected automatically"; the pages read do not say which ad product accepts it. Source: [official] Amazon Ads API merged OpenAPI, `ThemeMatchType`.

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-21.** Branded-search metrics count searches for the advertised brand, including abbreviations and misspellings, attributed to an ad interaction, for Sponsored Brands, Display, TV and DSP, and are not available for Sponsored Products. Sources: [official] *Branded searches metric enhancements*; v1 branded-searches metric pages.

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-22.** Amazon reports no branded/non-branded flag on a search term; its keyword recommendations carry a `keywordTheme` label such as `Branded` or `Generic`, and practitioners define the branded list themselves. Sources: [official] Recommendation types guide; [opinion] Ad Badger, *How to Segment Branded Keywords*.

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-23.** Amazon states that the price a winning advertiser pays can exceed the runner-up's bid because reserve prices can set it, and never exceeds the advertiser's maximum bid; in its own description, a winner whose bid clears both reserves pays the soft reserve, and a winner whose bid clears the hard reserve but not the soft reserve still wins and pays its own bid. Sources: [official] *Understand Amazon Ads auctions*; Amazon's response to the FTC lawsuit.

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-24.** The FTC and 22 states allege (August 2026, pending) that Amazon's soft reserve made Sponsored Products winners pay their own bid close to 80% of the time; Amazon answers that about 92% of selected Sponsored Products ads in 2024 were not the highest bid and that no advertiser pays more than its bid (figures as of 2026-11-09, human readers only). Sources: [official, Amazon response] aboutamazon.com; [official, regulator — allegations] FTC press releases of 2026-08-31 and 2026-11-05.

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-25.** In Bing experiments across thousands of brands, a brand's own ad on its brand query added 1%–4% of clicks when no competitor advertised, while brands that did not advertise against competitors lost 18%–42%. Source: [research] Simonov, Nosko & Rao (2018).

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-26.** In eBay's field experiments, brand-keyword ads had "no measurable short-term benefits" (when eBay suspended paid search, most of the traffic still reached its site); at Edmunds.com, shutting off paid links to branded search lost more than half the paid traffic. Sources: [research] Blake, Nosko & Tadelis (2015); Coviello, Gneezy & Goette (2017).

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-27.** On Bing, competitors' ads on a brand query took 1%–2% of its traffic, rising to 6%–15% when the brand's own link was removed from the top paid position, and 20%–47% of stolen clicks returned to Bing within 30 seconds against 7% of the brand's own. Source: [research] Simonov & Hill (2021).

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-28.** In an experiment before a merger, a firm that paused its Google search ads in random US markets lost only 63% as many customers as a non-experimental estimate implied, and spillovers to its closest rival were "surprisingly small", even on the paused firm's brand name. Source: [research] Golden & Horton (2021).

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-29.** Game-theory models find that mutual bidding on each other's brand names can be a prisoner's dilemma in which the platform gains, and that the smaller-budget firm poaches more as budget asymmetry grows. Sources: [research] Desai, Shin & Staelin (2014); Sayedi, Jerath & Srinivasan (2014).

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-30.** Competing advertisers' bids on a keyword can follow Edgeworth cycles of escalation and collapse under both first- and second-price auctions. Source: [research] Zhang & Feng (2011).

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-31.** An audit of 4,800 simulated Amazon searches in four countries found products with organic rank beyond 100 shown as sponsored results above the top organic results, and in most cases top sponsored results costlier and "of poorer quality" than top organic ones. Source: [research] Dash et al. (2024).

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-32.** When Google opened trademarks to rivals' keyword bidding in continental Europe in 2010, navigational searches became less likely to reach the trademark owner, more so for lesser-known marks. Source: [research] Bechtold & Tucker (2014).

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-33.** The Ninth Circuit held in 2015 that Amazon showing other brands' watches for a search of "mtm special ops" was not likely to confuse shoppers, because Amazon labels each result with its name, manufacturer and photo; the Second Circuit in 2021 vacated the FTC's order against 1-800 Contacts' settlement agreements requiring rivals to refrain from search advertising on its trademarks and to use them as negative keywords. Sources: [legal] FindLaw summary of *Multi Time Machine v. Amazon.com*; AIPLA summary of *1-800 Contacts v. FTC*; [official, regulator] FTC matter page.

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-34.** Perpetua's rule of thumb is that branded spend above 40% of total ad spend likely pays for traffic the brand would get organically, and that branded terms make ACOS look "artificially strong". Source: [opinion] Perpetua, *How to use branded traffic*.

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-35.** Haus reports, from Google branded-search tests, that 82% of brands facing high competition on their brand terms measured significant lift against 35% facing low competition. Source: [opinion] Haus, *When is branded search worth the investment?*.

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-36.** Store insights carry a peer dwell-time comparison, Brand Metrics indices compare a brand to category peers, and the cross-program benchmarks give peers' 25th, 50th and 75th percentiles; none names a peer. Sources: [official] Brand Store insight metrics guide; Brand Metrics overview; Benchmarks report guide.

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-37.** Amazon's Sponsored Brands API recommends the advertiser's own brands for negative targeting, stating that "targeting your own brands usually results in lower performance than targeting competitors' brands"; the statement names no metric and shows no data. Source: [official] SB v4 OpenAPI, `GET /sb/negativeTargets/brands/recommendations`.

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-38.** Sponsored Products and Sponsored Brands prompts are enrolled automatically, can appear in shopping results and on product detail pages, have been charged under the campaign's CPC bidding in the US since general availability on March 25, 2026, and can be paused one by one in the console. Sources: [official] *Sponsored Products prompts and Sponsored Brands prompts* (what's new); v3 *Prompt Ad Extension reports*; [opinion] Ad Badger (agreeing on the date).

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-39.** Amazon's RSOV pages list different marketplaces: the library guide includes Ireland, the what's-new launch page (modified 2026-10-09) includes Egypt instead, the SB v4 model excludes goal-based campaigns in Ireland, and two help pages exclude Spain (lists as of 2026-11-09). Sources: [official] *How to set up Amazon Sponsored Brands reserve share of voice*; RSOV what's-new page; SB v4 OpenAPI; *Reserve keywords in a Sponsored Brands campaign*.

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-40.** Sponsored Products and Sponsored Display campaigns "generate 0 impressions when the advertised ASIN or SKU doesn't hold the Featured Offer", only the SKU holding the Featured Offer serves ads for an ASIN, and "When status shifts, impressions for the previous holder stop immediately"; Sponsored Brands, including video, keeps serving while the product "is available to buy on Amazon". Sources: [official] *Understand Featured Offer eligibility* (help GYVDGT5WNNW23LJ5); staff reply in *Sponsored Products and Buy Box*.

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-41.** Amazon states that an advertised product that goes out of stock "will be automatically paused within your campaign" and that an ad does not display if the product "isn't the Featured Offer or is out of stock"; these rules apply to every advertiser, so a rival's stock-out or Featured Offer loss stops that rival's SP and SD ads for the ASIN, and no Amazon page read says what then serves in the paid slots of the rival's page. Sources: [official] *How to improve your product detail page for advertising*; Amazon Ads *FAQs*; *Understand Featured Offer eligibility*.

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-42.** When another seller holds the Featured Offer on a brand's own ASIN, the brand's SP and SD ads for that ASIN serve no impressions while its Sponsored Brands ads keep serving and send shoppers to a page whose default offer is the other seller's; Amazon routes that case to Seller Support, saying "Advertising Support can't directly restore Featured Offer eligibility". Sources: [official] *Understand Featured Offer eligibility*; [opinion] Canopy Management (Chuck Kessler), Pattern UK and a seller in the Seller Forums (Europe), on spend reaching the other seller's offer.

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-43.** Keepa's data structure records the Buy Box holder as a time series (`buyBoxSellerIdHistory`, "-1" when no seller qualified, "-2" for an unknown new seller, set only when offers are requested), per-seller Buy Box win shares (`stats.buyBoxStats.percentageWon`), price histories in which "-1" means "no offer at the given timestamp (e.g. out of stock)", and 30-day, 90-day and interval out-of-stock percentages. Source: [opinion, vendor data structure] Keepa `Product.java`, `Stats.java`, `Offer.java` (github.com/keepacom/api_backend), read through `cx-keepa`'s product-file reference.

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-44.** SP-API's `getItemOffers` returns an ASIN's lowest-priced offers with each `SellerId` and `IsBuyBoxWinner`, and `getCompetitiveSummary` returns its featured buying options (whose `offerIdentifier` "may not belong to the requester"), lowest-priced offers, reference prices and "up to 20 ASINs which are similar to the item". Source: [official] Product Pricing v0 and 2022-05-01 models (amzn/selling-partner-api-models).

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-45.** Perpetua's Competitor Signals "detects when a competitor goes out of stock and automatically raises bids across your campaigns for the period you specify, then normalizes them once the competitor restocks", and Perpetua says a competitor that loses the Buy Box will "convert less efficiently, which creates an opening"; Jarvio and Netpeak advise product-targeting a rival's page during its stock-outs; Eva calls a rival's stock-out a window of "typically 2 to 6 weeks" and warns that conquest-driven sales can stock out the conquering ASIN. None of the four publishes a measured effect. Sources: [opinion] Perpetua (Brendan Smith, 2026-07-28); Jarvio (Connor Mulholland, 2026-02-28); Netpeak (Aliaksandr Vlasenka, 2025-03-31); Eva (Hai Mag, modified 2026-11-03).

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-46.** Trellis's Qore page says Sponsored Products ads "keep running" after a Buy Box loss, while Amazon's Featured Offer page says SP and SD serve 0 impressions without the Featured Offer and only Sponsored Brands keeps serving. Sources: [official] *Understand Featured Offer eligibility*; [opinion] Trellis, *Stop paying for a lost buy box*.

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-47.** Sponsored Brands formats are collections (product collection is marked deprecated; automatic and manual collections replace it), Store Spotlight (a Store with a home page and at least three subpages, each with a unique product), video linking one product to its detail page (`VIDEO`) and brand video linking to the Store (`BRAND_VIDEO`, one to three products under Drive page visits, none to three under Grow brand impression share); Brand Gallery serves only with reserve share of voice. Sources: [official] SB *Ad types overview*, *Video ad* and *Store spotlight* API guides; SB 4.0 OpenAPI `CreativeType`; *Understand Sponsored Brands*; *Your complete guide to Sponsored Brands*; *Drive branded search engagement with Sponsored Brands Brand Gallery*.

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-48.** Under the Drive page visits goal every Sponsored Brands format can serve on search pages, detail pages and the Amazon homepage, and under Grow brand impression share and reserve share of voice only formats with a Store serve, at top of search; Amazon's video guide and ad-specs page place SB video on product detail pages as well as in search results, while the API video guide names only shopping-results placements. Sources: [official] *Campaign goals for Sponsored Brands*; *Get started with Sponsored Brands video*; *Sponsored Brands video - Ad specs and guidelines*; SB *Video ad* API guide.

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-49.** Amazon says Sponsored Brands video "has its own ad placement and auction" and "won't compete against other self-service ad campaigns you may be running", targets "keywords, categories, and products", and suggests as video keywords "even brand names that are similar to yours"; SB bid recommendations take an `adFormat` of `productCollection` or `video`. Sources: [official] *Your complete guide to Sponsored Brands*; *Get started with Sponsored Brands video*; *Sponsored Brands video - Ad specs and guidelines*; SB keyword targeting API guide.

**P-brand-defense-and-competition-50.** Every Sponsored Brands and display video must follow chapters 1 to 5 of Amazon's moderation guide, whose chapter 4 says "Ads on Amazon cannot explicitly call out your competitors by name" while allowing "a "leading brand" or other generic phrasing" and forbids unsubstantiated superiority claims; Sponsored Brands targets "must be relevant to the product being promoted". Source: [official] *A complete guide to Sponsored Brands and display ads moderation*.

### Seat's positions (enter no skill)

- [seat] Three Amazon metrics called "impression share" use three denominators (all advertisers on a term; the campaign's own eligibility; all ads on a reserved keyword's slot), so a reader comparing them across reports compares different things.
- [seat] The research converges on "brand defence pays in proportion to competitive presence on the brand query"; on Amazon the presence can be observed from search term impression share and rank on the brand term and from Top Search Terms' clicked ASINs, so the competitive state is a run-time read, not a constant.
- [seat] A defence campaign on own ASINs with expanded targeting on may be partly a conquest campaign; the "Product-expanded" rows of the seller's reports (and, for Sponsored Display, the matched-target report) are the evidence.
- [seat, practitioner knowledge not on an Amazon page read] ASIN-valued rows in the Sponsored Products search term report are read by practitioners as the detail pages a product target matched.
- [seat] Under either side's account of the auction, a bid raise on a contested term can raise the price paid without a rival moving, which weakens "outbid the competitor by a cent" as a cost model on Amazon.
- [seat] The conquest-side research (Simonov & Hill's bounce-backs; loosely, Chiou & Tucker on resellers' trademark ads) and Amazon's conquest guidance (target where your offer is stronger) point the same way: conquest's return is set by the comparison the shopper makes after the click.
- [seat] A rival's stock-out or Featured Offer loss changes the auction twice over: the rival's SP and SD ads for that ASIN leave it (Amazon's rule), and the rival's shoppers face a smaller choice set (the stock-out studies). The first is a fact of Amazon's serving rules; the second, and any sales lift a seller sees in that window, ends when the rival restocks or regains the offer, so a lift read across the window is not a lift the seller keeps.
- [seat] A brand whose own Featured Offer is held by a reseller still pays for Sponsored Brands clicks to that page; its own SP and SD stop by themselves. The facts that bear on the SB spend are which ad types serve and whose offer the click lands on.
- [seat] In Sponsored Brands video on a rival's term or page, the targeting may name the rival (its brand keyword, its ASIN) and the creative may not; "leading brand" phrasing and substantiated claims are what the moderation guide leaves to a comparison video.
- [seat] Store Spotlight and brand video send the click to the seller's own Store; a detail-page video sends it to a detail page, which AMALYZE describes as carrying rivals' sponsored carousels (practitioners item 11). Whether a Store page carries no other brands' ads is a practitioner claim, not an Amazon one (Open question 19).
