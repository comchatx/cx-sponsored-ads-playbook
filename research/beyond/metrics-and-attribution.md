## What this covers

**The part of a seller's ad work.** Every number a seller reads about their ads, and the rules that make it. On the traffic side: impressions, gross and invalid impressions, viewable impressions, clicks, CTR, CPC, CPM and vCPM, cost. On the conversion side: purchases (formerly "orders"), units, sales, ROAS and ACOS, the click-based conversion rate and Amazon's new impression-based "purchase rate", the promoted / halo split (formerly "advertised SKU" / "other SKU" / "brand halo"), new-to-brand metrics, detail page views, add-to-cart, branded searches, Brand Store page views, video metrics, long-term sales, and the KDP metrics (KENP read, estimated KENP royalties, Kindle Unlimited qualified borrows). Around them: the attribution rule set (lookback window by ad product and by account type, eligible products, click against view, the last-touch ranking, one credit per conversion), how a conversion is dated (to the day of the ad interaction), when figures settle (restatement, invalid-traffic filtering, cancellations), the time zone and currency of a report's day, the change of every name between Reporting v3 and the Reporting API v1 (Amazon's "unified reporting"), the multi-touch attribution metrics that sit beside last-touch, Amazon Attribution for off-Amazon traffic, Amazon Marketing Cloud as far as a seller's own account reaches, the differences between console, API and bulksheet figures, and the places where Amazon's own pages define a metric backwards. Since the gap fill of 2026-11-09 it also holds the yardsticks a senior lead reads these rules against: the IAB/MRC retail-media measurement guidelines and the MRC's accreditation scope for Amazon (§R, D7a), what makes a long-term-sales forecast a valid long-term effect (F8–F11), the evidence on whether view-attributed purchases are incremental (C8–C9), and the attribution-method literature on why credit is not marginal value (Published research 9–17).

**Why a senior Amazon advertising lead cares.** Every judgement in the other files of this set is a reading of these numbers. Four facts change the reading more than any optimisation does:

1. **The day is the click's day, not the sale's.** A sale reported for 1 October can have happened on any day up to the end of the lookback window. The latest days of any report are incomplete by construction, and they fill in for weeks.
2. **The same word means different things by surface and by date.** "Sales" for a Sponsored Products seller is a 7-day click-attributed figure that includes other products the seller sells; for a vendor it is a 14-day figure that includes the brand halo; for Sponsored Brands it is 14 days and, under vCPM, includes views. In unified reporting "Purchase rate" is purchases per *impression*, while the console search-term page still calls the per-*click* rate "Purchase rate". Amazon Attribution, alone, dates conversions to the day they happened.
3. **The rules moved in 2025–2026.** View attribution for vCPM Sponsored Brands and Sponsored Display changed on 2026-01-01; Reporting API v1 went generally available on 2026-09-30 with new names, no window suffixes and halo folded into the base metrics; the console's old report centres shut on 2026-12-31; Reporting v3 stops on 2027-06-30. A year-over-year comparison that crosses these dates compares two measurements.
4. **Attributed is not caused.** Amazon's metrics are last-touch credit, not incrementality. That question belongs to `areas/ads-organic-and-total.md` and `method/change-measurement.md`; this file states what the numbers are.

**Lane.** Report mechanics (create, poll, retention, rate limits, file formats) are `method/reporting.md`'s; this file names the report types and fields only where a metric's meaning depends on them. Ads against total sales (TACoS, the SP-API Sales and Traffic join, SQP, incrementality evidence) is `areas/ads-organic-and-total.md`'s. Break-even ACOS, margins and targets are `beyond/goals-and-economics.md`'s. Why a number moved is `areas/diagnosis.md`'s; this file supplies the definitional and settling causes it checks first. The Ads API surface itself is `method/ads-api-surface.md`'s. Placement and impression-share use in bidding is `areas/bids-and-placements.md`'s.

## Amazon's own rules and guidance

RULE marks something Amazon enforces or a shape its API, report or attribution system fixes (a definition, a field, a window, a limit, a date). GUIDANCE marks a recommendation. Help pages carry a `lastModified` stamp in their own data; it is given where it dates a rule.

### A. What an attributed conversion is

A1. RULE. Amazon's frame, verbatim: "Attribution measures conversion events that occur after shoppers interact with ads." "Conversions may take up to 12 hours to appear in reports and are reflected on the date of the shopper's ad interaction, which may be different from the conversion date. Attribution metrics for a given report date remain incomplete until the lookback window has ended." The page names the conversion types: purchases ("formerly known as Orders on sponsored ads"; "A purchases can include multiple sold units of product"), new-to-brand purchases, detail page views, branded searches and add to cart. [official] (Ad campaign attribution, https://advertising.amazon.com/help/GX7KDKHMWQYMJ385, lastModified 2026-10-20, read 2026-11-09)

A2. RULE. How a conversion is assigned, in Amazon's three terms: "Product relevance: Campaigns can receive attribution for products beyond those advertised. Advertised products are always eligible, and campaign type determines which additional relevant products may qualify." "Ad interaction type: Some campaign types will only attribute to ad clicks. Others will attribute to clicks and views, depending on the selected cost type." "Lookback window: Maximum number of days that a conversion is considered relevant to an ad click or view." Amazon's worked example: "A 14-day lookback means that a purchase on October 17 can be attributed to ad interactions between October 3 and October 17. An ad interaction on October 2 would be ineligible for attribution." [official] (Ad campaign attribution, above)

A3. RULE. The table by ad product and advertiser type, verbatim in the load-bearing cells:

| Ad product, advertiser | Lookback | Eligible products | Eligible interactions | Model |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sponsored Products, Seller | 7 days | "Products featured in the ads as well as any other products sold by the Seller Central merchant." | "Clicks only" | "Last click" |
| Sponsored Products, Vendors and Authors | 14 days | "Products featured in ads as well as any other products from the same brands ("brand halo"). Only products sold by Amazon are attributed. Book products don't have a brand halo." | "Clicks only" | "'Last touch' attribution that prioritizes product relevance and clicks over views. Competes with other Amazon Ads products." |
| Sponsored Brands and Display, all advertisers ("must be registered brand owners") | 14 days | "Products on the landing page as well as any other products from the same brands ("brand halo"). Attributed purchases may include sales by Amazon or third-party sellers, in New or Used condition." | "Clicks (CPC) or clicks and views (vCPM)" | as the vendor row |
| Streaming TV, all advertisers | 14 days | products advertised plus the brand halo; Amazon or third-party sellers, New or Used | "Clicks (including QR scans) and views" | as the vendor row |
| Amazon DSP, all advertisers | 14 days | "Products tracked by the campaign as well as any other products from the same brands" | "Clicks and views" | as the vendor row |

The 7 and 14 are **(human readers only)** as numbers; a skill reads the page. [official] (Ad campaign attribution, above)

A4. RULE. One sale, one credit: "Amazon will attribute a conversion to no more than 1 ad interaction across all campaigns and all advertisers. A sale won't, for example, be attributed to 2 campaigns in both a promoted and brand halo context, or as both a view-through and click-through conversion. It's attributed once to the most relevant ad interaction." The ranking: "Promoted ASIN ad clicks", then "Brand halo ad clicks", then "Promoted ASIN ad views", then "Brand halo ad views", where brand halo here means "Products within the same brand and subcategory as a promoted ASIN" (example: "Category = Electronics", "Subcategory = Headphones, Home Audio, Video Game Consoles & Accessories, etc."). "If the customer interacted with multiple ads, attribution considers each ad's relevance to the product, then uses a last-touch model that prioritizes the last click over the last view. Clicks are prioritized over views because they're a stronger engagement signal." [official] (Ad campaign attribution, above)

A5. RULE. Who competes with whom: "All Amazon Ads products that promote brands (Display, Video, and Audio campaigns, Display, Streaming TV, Sponsored Brands, and Sponsored Products for vendors) compete for attribution." "Sponsored Products for sellers is a merchant-based ad program that doesn't normally compete for attribution with brands. However, if the same advertiser wins attribution as both a merchant and a brand, attribution will choose the more relevant ad, ensuring the same conversion is attributed only once per advertiser." "Seller Central merchants who also use Display, Video, and Audio campaigns should ensure that their merchant token is entered in their Display, Video, and Audio campaigns account configuration." [official] (Ad campaign attribution, above)

A6. RULE. Why attributed sales differ from retail sales, Amazon's own list (verbatim): "In campaign reports, sales are reported on the ad interaction date, which may differ from the actual sale date. Sales reported on a given date may have occurred at any time between that date and the end of the lookback window." "Attributed sales don't reflect any discounts applied in the cart." "Attributed sales don't include tax. Inclusive tax such as VAT is subtracted from sales totals." "Depending on the campaign type, attributed sales may include items sold by you, Amazon, or another seller. They can also be items sold in Used condition at a different price." "Depending on the campaign type, attributed sales may include products that weren't promoted in the campaign but that are from the same brands as products that were promoted." "Payment failures and purchases that are cancelled within 72 hours of order creation will be deducted from attribution metrics. These deductions may take up to 7 days to process after the cancellation." "Ads that feature a specific product may have conversions attributed to them for different products. In reports grouped by "Advertised ASIN," the ASIN is the product featured in the ad. Attributed sales for the ad may include other ASINs." [official] (Ad campaign attribution, above). The 72-hour and 7-day figures are **(human readers only)**.

A7. RULE. Returns are not among the deductions Amazon lists for sponsored ads: the page names payment failures and cancellations within 72 hours, nothing else. Amazon Attribution, by contrast, says its restatements take "into account returns or refunds" (§Q). [official] (Ad campaign attribution; Amazon Attribution API how-to, both read 2026-11-09). Whether a sponsored-ads sale returned on day 20 is ever removed is not stated on any page read (Open questions). [seat]

A8. RULE. The help page's own metric frame for brand advertisers, which is the reason halo is in base metrics: "The totals metrics that you see in campaign management data tables, downloadable reports, and reporting APIs contain conversions for promoted products (tracked ASINs) and conversions for brand halo products (additional ASINs within your promoted brands). We recommend using totals conversions metrics as the best way to measure the full value delivered by your brand's advertising." (GUIDANCE in its second sentence.) [official] (Campaign success metrics, https://advertising.amazon.com/help/G9EMRNUYZPP58HLX, lastModified 2026-08-14, read 2026-11-09)

### B. How a conversion is dated

B1. RULE. Sponsored ads date conversions to the day of the ad interaction (A1, A6). The Reporting API v1 makes this universal: "All conversion metrics are now reported on "traffic date," meaning conversions are reported on the date of the ad interaction rather than the date the conversion occurred." Example: "If a shopper clicked an ad on Oct 1 and purchased on Oct 3, the purchase conversion is now reported on Oct 1". "Previously, DSP reported conversions on the conversion date." [official] (Overview of the Reporting v1 API, https://d3a0d0y2hgofx6.cloudfront.net/en-us/guides/reporting/ads-v1/overview.md, read 2026-11-09)

B2. RULE. The console's version of the same change names a sponsored-ads exception that ended: "Previously, Display, Video, and Audio campaigns and legacy Sponsored Brands campaigns reported conversions on the conversion date." "Tip: You may observe higher conversion volumes earlier in the campaign flight for Display, Video, and Audio campaigns and legacy Sponsored Brands campaigns that previously reported on conversion date." "Both types of reporting reflect the same number of conversions overall, they are just distributed differently across dates." [official] (Understand unified reporting, https://advertising.amazon.com/help/GMH8A8AJSH4ATV6T, lastModified 2026-10-20, read 2026-11-09)

B3. RULE. Two Amazon measures do *not* date to the click:
- KENP read: "We track clicks associated with your advertised titles, and attribute them to the reading events for the same titles up to 14 days after customers click the ad. The estimated number of pages read are reported to the specific date when readers actually read the pages." "Pages read from offline devices can take 1 week to be reported." [official] (Kindle Edition Normalized Pages (KENP) metrics, https://advertising.amazon.com/help/GSJCXUYD4UGQP2MX, lastModified 2023-03-30, read 2026-11-09)
- Amazon Attribution (off-Amazon traffic): "In the report, each metric's value reflects the events which took place on the row's date; in other words, events are reported on the date they occurred, not on the date of the attributed click. Attribution is calculated using a 14-day last-click model." [official] (Amazon Attribution OpenAPI model, `ReportRequestBody.metrics`, https://dtrnk0o2zy01c.cloudfront.net/openapi/en-us/dest/AmazonAttribution_prod_3p.json, read 2026-11-09)

The unified-reporting page's statement that "All conversion metrics are now reported on "traffic date"" was read beside these two. Whether KENP in unified reporting has moved to traffic date is not stated (Open questions). [seat]

B4. RULE. AMC exposes both datings as separate tables. "In the `amazon_attributed_events_by_conversion_time` table, the conversion events all happened within the time window the query is run over, while the traffic events the conversions were attributed to may happen up to 14 days before"; "The `amazon_attributed_events_by_traffic_time` table has the opposite behavior … the conversion events may have occurred up to 30 days after the time window", and "the output of a workflow that uses this table may change over time." "Note that these windows are not the same thing as an attribution window, as the attribution window is decided based on the campaign and cannot be changed in AMC." [official] (Amazon attributed events by conversion time table, https://d3a0d0y2hgofx6.cloudfront.net/en-us/guides/amazon-marketing-cloud/datasources/amazon_attributed_events_by_conversion_time.md, read 2026-11-09)

B5. [seat] Consequence a skill can reason from: for any traffic-dated metric, the last `lookback` days of a report are structurally incomplete before any restatement; ROAS, ACOS and conversion rate for those days are biased toward worse (cost is nearly complete, sales are not). The settling schedule is §L.

### C. Clicks, views, viewability and the 2026 view model

C1. RULE. Which campaigns count views: "View attribution is enabled for: All Display, Video, and Audio campaigns … All Streaming TV campaigns … Sponsored Brands and Display campaigns using vCPM billing." "Amazon Ads records view conversions for impressions that meet viewability standards (50% of pixels visible for 1+ second for display ads, or 2+ seconds for video ads). When direct viewability measurement isn't available, viewability is inferred based on historical data and placement characteristics." "Standard conversion metrics such as Purchases and ROAS include the ad interaction types supported by that campaign. … Click-only breakouts are labeled clearly, such as ROAS from clicks or Purchases from clicks. The standard lookback window for views is 14 days." [official] (Ad campaign attribution, above)

C2. RULE. "Campaigns that do not measure view-through attribution (e.g., Sponsored Products or Sponsored Brands CPC) will continue to report 0 for conversions from views." [official] (Overview of the Reporting v1 API, above; the console page says the same for "Sponsored Brands campaigns using cost per click, or CPC, billing", Understand unified reporting, above)

C3. RULE. The view model changed on 2026-01-01. Amazon's launch page: a "shopping-signal enhanced last-touch attribution model" whose metrics (Purchases, Sales, ROAS) become standard reporting; it applies to "Sponsored Brands and Sponsored Display campaigns billed on a viewable impression (vCPM) basis, including branded keyword campaigns" and "Amazon DSP campaigns that serve ads in the Store"; the model gives "credit to ad views that reach shoppers during early discovery moments such as exploratory browsing or general category searches" and "applies a shorter attribution window"; "Conversion reporting for all other campaign types and inventory types remains unchanged. Click-based attribution remains unchanged."; legacy measurement stays available as "conversion metrics that include all ad views for eligible campaigns within a 14-day window" under the name "all views". The page does not give the new window's length. [official] (View attribution updates for Amazon Store ads, https://advertising.amazon.com/resources/whats-new/view-attribution-updates-for-amazon-store-ads, read 2026-11-09). The attribution help page's sentence: "Beginning January 1, 2026, view attribution for some placements will incorporate additional signals, such as shopping behavior patterns, when determining which ad views are eligible for attribution. This may result in changes to attribution trends for campaigns that attribute to views." [official] (Ad campaign attribution, above)

C4. RULE. The "all views" family in v1 has 54 fields, e.g. `metric.allViewsPurchases`, `metric.allViewsSales` (requires `budgetCurrency.value`), each described as "calculated using the original attribution model, which includes all ad views for eligible campaigns. Metrics labeled "all views" are provided for comparison with the updated attribution model. For ongoing performance assessment, we recommend using the standard purchase metrics." Program eligibility lists Sponsored Products among others. [official] (Purchases (all views), https://d3a0d0y2hgofx6.cloudfront.net/en-us/guides/reporting/ads-v1/metrics/amazon-retail-conversions/purchases-all-views.md, read 2026-11-09)

C5. RULE. Viewability fields and who has them. `metric.viewableImpressions`: "The number of times the ad was viewed. This excludes impressions that were deemed invalid by traffic quality filters. An ad is counted as viewed when at least 50% of the ad shows on screen for at least 1 second for display ads and 2 seconds or longer for video ads." Program eligibility: Sponsored Brands, Sponsored Display, Sponsored TV, Amazon DSP, Full-Funnel, **not Sponsored Products**. Same eligibility for `metric.vcpm` ("(cost / viewable impressions) * 1,000"; v3 name "cost per 1,000 viewable impressions (VCPM)"), `metric.viewableDistribution` ("viewable impressions / impressions"; v3 `viewabilityRate`) and `metric.viewableRate` ("vieweable impressions / measurable impressions", Amazon's spelling). `metric.measurableImpressions` and `metric.measurableRate` do list Sponsored Products. [official] (Viewability (Delivery), https://d3a0d0y2hgofx6.cloudfront.net/en-us/guides/reporting/ads-v1/metrics/delivery/viewability.md, read 2026-11-09)

C6. RULE. vCPM in the console: "This metric is available when you use the bidding option to optimize for viewable impressions. … This means you only pay when shoppers actually viewed your ad. In Display, Video, and Audio campaigns, this metric is available when you choose Optimize for reach as your bid optimization strategy. In Sponsored Brands, this metric is available when you choose Grow brand impression share as your campaign goal." "An image is counted as viewable when at least 50% is visible for at least 1 second. A video is counted as viewable when at least 50% is visible while playing for at least 2 seconds. This is the Media Rating Council's (MRC) definition of viewability." [official] (Performance metrics, https://advertising.amazon.com/help/GG44RFW942U9F6F5, lastModified 2026-11-05, read 2026-11-09). Amazon's library guide adds that larger desktop ads need only 30% of pixels in view. [official] (Viewability guide, https://advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/viewability, read 2026-11-09)

C7. RULE. v3's naming collides with v1's: v3 `viewabilityRate` is "View-through rate (vtr). Views divided by impressions"; v3 `viewClickThroughRate` is "Click-through rate for views (clicks divided by views)"; v3 `impressionsViews` and `viewableImpressions` are "Number of impressions that met the Media Ratings Council (MRC) viewability standard." The console page uses "View-through rate (VTR)" for video as "dividing the views by the impressions". [official] (Reporting columns (version 3), https://d3a0d0y2hgofx6.cloudfront.net/en-us/guides/reporting/v3/columns.md; Performance metrics, above; both read 2026-11-09). In v1 the same concept is `metric.viewableDistribution`; "VTR" is not a v1 name. [seat]

C8. RULE (what Amazon says, and does not say, about view credit; gap fill 2026-11-09). Amazon's stated basis for the 2026 view model is its own research, not a published test: "We are introducing a shopping-signal enhanced last-touch attribution model on January 1, 2026, based on our continuous research on shopping journeys in the Amazon store." The page's list of affected campaigns reads "Sponsored Brands , Sponsored Display campaigns billed on a viewable impression (vCPM) basis, including branded keyword campaigns" and "Amazon DSP campaigns that serve ads in the Store", and "Click-based attribution remains unchanged." [official] (View Attribution Updates for Amazon Store ads, datePublished 2026-01-01, dateModified 2026-10-09, read 2026-11-09). Where viewability cannot be measured Amazon infers it: "When direct viewability measurement isn't available, viewability is inferred based on historical data and placement characteristics." [official] (Ad campaign attribution, lastModified 2026-10-20, read 2026-11-09). No Amazon page read publishes an incrementality figure for view-attributed purchases, under the old model ("all views") or the new one; both are attribution rules. [seat, as searched]

C9. [seat] The evidence on whether views cause purchases sits in Published research (items 16–17, with Gordon et al. in items 3 and 5): randomized display experiments outside Amazon found that most of a retailer's measured lift came from shoppers who never clicked (Lewis & Reiley 2014) and that display retargeting raised purchases among exposed users (Johnson, Lewis & Nubbemeyer 2017), so click-only credit can miss real effects; observational exposed-versus-unexposed comparisons missed the experimental lift, mostly by overstating it on Facebook (Gordon et al. 2019, 2023) and once by understating it (Lewis & Reiley's cross-sectional estimator), so view-attributed credit can also count purchases that would have happened anyway. For the choice between vCPM and CPC on Sponsored Brands or Sponsored Display, the reporting consequence is fixed by C1–C3 (vCPM adds view-attributed purchases to the base metrics, CPC reports 0 from views); whether those purchases are incremental is not answered by any Amazon metric a seller can read, and the IAB/IAB Europe guidelines class "baseline vs. exposed analysis" and "platform-reported incrementality" as "Weak" in causal strength (§R7).

### D. Traffic and cost metrics

D1. RULE. Impressions: "The number of times the ad was displayed. This excludes impressions that were deemed invalid by traffic quality filters. Impressions = gross impressions - invalid impressions" (`metric.impressions`, v3 `impressions`). Gross: "The total number of times the ad was displayed. This can include valid as well as invalid impressions." Invalid impression rate: "invalid impressions / gross impressions" (`metric.invalidImpressionRate`). [official] (Impressions (Delivery), https://d3a0d0y2hgofx6.cloudfront.net/en-us/guides/reporting/ads-v1/metrics/delivery/impressions.md; Traffic metrics, https://advertising.amazon.com/help/GMCTGJGY2M438E2R, lastModified 2026-11-02; both read 2026-11-09)

D2. RULE. Clicks: "The number of times the ad was clicked. This excludes clicks that were deemed invalid by traffic quality filters. Clicks = gross clicks - invalid clicks" (`metric.clicks`; v3 `clicks`). `metric.grossClicks` (v3 `grossClickThroughs`), `metric.invalidClicks` (v3 `invalidClickThroughs`), `metric.invalidClickRate` ("invalid clicks / gross clicks"; v3 `invalidClickThroughRate`). `metric.ctr`: "CTR = clicks / impressions" (v3 `clickThroughRate`, "Clicks divided by impressions."). `metric.vctr`: "vCTR = clicks / viewable impressions" (v3 `viewClickThroughRate`). `metric.cpc`: "Cost per click (CPC) = cost / clicks", requires `budgetCurrency.value` (v3 `costPerClick`, "Total cost divided by total number of clicks."). [official] (Clicks (Delivery), https://d3a0d0y2hgofx6.cloudfront.net/en-us/guides/reporting/ads-v1/metrics/delivery/clicks.md; Reporting columns (version 3); both read 2026-11-09)

D3. RULE. Clicks and every traffic metric are incompatible with the converted-product and product-relevance dimensions: "Impressions, clicks, and other traffic metrics are not specific to any 1 conversion. Because a single ad interaction can lead to multiple product conversions, traffic metrics cannot be meaningfully reported at the converted product level and are not available when this dimension is selected." [official] (Data availability in unified reporting, https://advertising.amazon.com/help/G8A5Z6UD9ME5W3GZ, lastModified 2026-10-12, read 2026-11-09; the v1 Clicks page lists "Converted product" and "Product relevance" among its incompatible dimensions)

D4. RULE. Cost. `metric.totalCost`: "The total amount spent on running the campaign. This excludes the third-party fees paid by the agency." v3 Sponsored Ads name `spend` (also `cost`, "Total cost of ad clicks."). "Cost is assessed per click or per 1,000 views depending on cost type." The console renamed the column: "Total Cost (formerly known as Spend on sponsored ads)". `metric.totalCostReconciled`: "The total amount spent on media, fees, and technology costs after reconciliation", with `metric.roasReconciled` ("sales / total cost reconciled"); unified reporting lists "Total cost (reconciled), ROAS (reconciled)" as available only from 8/4/2024. [official] (Costs (Costs and fees), https://d3a0d0y2hgofx6.cloudfront.net/en-us/guides/reporting/ads-v1/metrics/costs-and-fees/costs.md; Performance metrics; Data availability in unified reporting; all read 2026-11-09)

D5. RULE. Cost is provisional for days: "The metrics in the campaign manager are updated every hour. Sales data takes up to 48 hours to populate and might be incomplete in the "Yesterday" view in campaign manager. In addition, consider the Cost (formerly known as Spend on sponsored ads) column to be an estimate as it can be updated until 3 days after the click or viewable impression occurs, while we validate your click traffic." [official] (Performance metrics, above). Figures **(human readers only)**.

D6. RULE. CPM and its siblings: `metric.cpm` "(cost / impressions) * 1000", listed for Sponsored Products too; benchmark quartiles `metric.cpcP25/P50/P75`, `metric.ctrP25/…`, `metric.cpmP25/…` describe "peer brands" and require benchmark dimensions (`benchmarkBrand.id`, `benchmarkBrowseCategory.id`, …). [official] (Impressions (Delivery); Clicks (Delivery); both above). Amazon's help page defines CPC (p50, converted) as "the value where 25% of peer brands fall at or below" — a copy error beside the correct p50 wording for the unconverted field. [official, the page's own inconsistency] (Traffic metrics, above)

D7. RULE. Measurement accreditation is narrow. The Media Rating Council's letter of 2026-07-08 continues accreditation of "Amazon Advertising's first-party measurement and reporting of Sponsored Products ("the Service") Display and Search Clicks inclusive of Sophisticated Invalid Traffic (SIVT) filtration for Amazon placements. This applies to total reporting and does not include any environment (Desktop, Mobile Web, In-app) segmentations." Accredited statistics: "Gross Clicks" and "Clicks (Total Net of SIVT)". Impressions and conversions are not on the list. [third party: MRC] (MRC accreditation letter to Amazon Ads, July 8, 2026, https://www.mediaratingcouncil.org/sites/default/files/Accreditation%20Letters/Amazon/GI070826_Amazon%20Sponsored%20Products%20Accreditation%20Letter.pdf, read 2026-11-09). The console can filter to it: "The MRC accreditation table setting provides the ability to filter sponsored ads user interfaces and reports to only show MRC accredited data." [official] (MRC accredited data for Sponsored Products campaigns, https://advertising.amazon.com/help/GVLUHLCD2Y6C364M, lastModified 2026-02-25, read 2026-11-09)

D7a. RULE. Where Amazon's accreditation stops, across ad products (gap fill 2026-11-09). F11. [seat] What makes such a forecast a valid long-term *effect*, stated in the terms of the surrogate literature (Published research, item 14): LTS uses short-term engagements (detail page view, branded search, add to cart, purchase) as surrogates for 12-month sales. Reading LTS as the campaign's causal 12-month value needs (a) the short-term engagements credited to the campaign to be caused by it (attribution does not establish this; §A, M3), (b) the Prentice surrogacy condition, that 12-month sales are independent of the ad given the surrogates (an ad that also changes later behaviour by another path breaks it), and (c) comparability, that the engagement-to-sales relation learned from the brand's historical shoppers holds for the shoppers this campaign reached. `metric.cohortAccumulatedSales*` checks the prediction step for the attributed cohort (did these shoppers go on to spend what LTS forecast?); it does not check (a), because the cohort has no unexposed comparison. The IAB/MRC guidelines require of any 12-month forecast that "The basis for such forecasts must be disclosed and the methodology for extending shorter period results to 12 months or longer periods should be re-evaluated periodically based on empirical evidence" (§R4); the accumulated-sales fields are the means of that re-evaluation on a seller's own data, available in the US.

J1. J2. RULE. The profile carries the account's zone and currency: `GET /v2/profiles` returns `countryCode`, `currencyCode` and `timezone` per profile, and Amazon's own example shows `America/Los_Angeles` for US, CA and MX profiles. v3 exposes `advertiserTimezone` on DSP report types only (`dspCampaign`, `dspInventory`, `dspAudience`). J3. RULE. Currency. ### K. Console, API and bulksheet: where the same metric differs

K1. RULE. Bulksheets define two metrics backwards. Amazon's bulksheet search-term page, column R "Click-through Rate": "This is calculated as impressions divided by clicks."; column W "Conversion Rate": "The total number of orders divided by sales within 7 days from the date that your ad was clicked." [official] (Bulksheets search term report, above; the cx-listing-playbook research recorded the same text earlier, so the error has stood for months). [seat] The computed column in a downloaded bulksheet is not checked here; the definitional text is plainly inverted against every other Amazon page (D2, E7). K2. RULE. The bulksheet search-term sheet has its own window: "The lookback window in bulksheets search term reports is always 7 days for both vendors and sellers", and the date range "does not affect the search term report metrics"; it "includes only the terms that resulted in at least one ad click". [official] (Bulksheets search term report, above). [seat] A vendor's bulksheet search-term sales (7-day) and the same vendor's console search-term sales (14-day) differ by construction.

K3. RULE (and an Amazon inconsistency). Search-term coverage. ### L. When figures settle: restatement, invalid traffic, cancellations

L1. RULE. Invalid traffic is filtered and removed from what a seller pays and sees. Amazon's definition covers non-human and fraudulent traffic and also "duplicate clicks" and accidental clicks; filtering runs pre-bid and post-impression, with monitoring "for up to 30 days"; invalid traffic is removed from invoices and reports and reports may be restated. [official] (Traffic validation, https://advertising.amazon.com/help/GBYPYH79NGJJ5JPS, lastModified 2026-11-02, read 2026-11-09). "Most" detection completes within 6 hours and invalid clicks are usually removed the same day; Amazon recommends "waiting at least 5 days" before judging; detection continues up to 30 days and invoice adjustments follow the billing cycle. [official] (Adjustments to invalid spend, https://advertising.amazon.com/help/GWQ7F2EL4E2Y7H4B, read 2026-11-09). Figures **(human readers only)**.

L2. RULE. Amazon on reading invalid-traffic rates: "IVR level on its own doesn't indicate undetected IVT"; "if you notice a significant drop in IVR, with an anomalous increase in select campaign metrics like click-through rate (CTR) or Impressions or Clicks, it could suggest undetected IVT was billed to your campaign. For Sponsored Ads campaigns, a drop in Conversion Rate (CVR) along with the above changes in metrics, is a higher confidence indicator for undetected IVT." For Display, Video, and Audio campaigns, "a small discrepancy (up to 5%) between Amazon and third-party reported IVR is expected". [official] (Traffic validation, above). Diagnosis use is `areas/diagnosis.md` §K.

L3. RULE. API restatement schedule as Amazon states it: data available within 12 hours; clicks can change for up to 3 days; conversions appear within 24 hours and are restated at 1, 7 and 28 days, up to 42 days for a 14-day window; reports take up to 3 hours. [official] (Reporting v3 FAQ, above). Amazon Ads reporting overview adds: invalid-traffic adjustment for up to 30 days, "waiting at least 5 days", and conversions can change for up to 60 days, so re-retrieve monthly; DSP invalid traffic is monitored for 72 hours. [official] (Amazon Ads reporting overview, https://d3a0d0y2hgofx6.cloudfront.net/en-us/guides/reporting/overview.md, read 2026-11-09). The DSP freshness page: invalid-traffic decreases appear within 72 hours; ASIN changes trigger restatement within 28 days; reach and frequency are recomputed within 96 hours. [official] (Data availability and freshness, above). All figures **(human readers only)**.

L4. RULE. Marketing Stream restates by re-delivery: restated records arrive again with a new total, and the consumer keeps the record with the highest `streamBatch.version`. [official] (Amazon Ads advanced tools release notes, 2026-09-30 entry, above). [seat] cx does not consume Stream (the door is request and response); the fact matters only for a seller comparing their own Stream-fed tool with cx's pull.

L5. RULE. Cancellations within 72 hours and payment failures are deducted, taking up to 7 days (A6). Downloadable reports "exclude canceled purchases". [official] (Ad campaign attribution; Downloadable reports in sponsored ads; both above)

L6. [seat] Combining A1, B1, L1 and L3: a day's figures are near-final for cost after about 3 days, and for conversions only after the lookback window plus the restatement schedule, which for a 14-day product runs past four weeks. `method/change-measurement.md` owns how this enters a before-and-after read; the numbers here are read from the pages at run time.

### M. Multi-touch attribution beside last touch

M1. RULE. Amazon's multi-touch attribution (MTA), "Announced at unBoxed 2024", is a beta for US advertisers that divides credit across ad touches "in proportion to their value", using experiments, machine learning and shopping signals; its metrics, "Orders (multi-touch)", "Sales (multi-touch)" and "ROAS (multi-touch)", sit beside the last-touch metrics, and they "can differ from last-touch attribution at all reporting grains". The page also names a multi-touch ACoS and breakouts for promoted and brand halo, same and other SKU, and click and view. Its two surface sentences, verbatim (re-read 2026-11-09; page metadata datePublished 2024-10-15, dateModified 2026-10-09): "Multi-touch metrics are available alongside familiar last-touch metrics in Ad console, Amazon DSP campaign manager, sponsored ads downloadable reports (Campaign, Targeting, Keyword, Placement, Search term, Advertised product, and Purchased product), Amazon DSP downloadable custom reports (Campaign, Inventory, Geography, Technology, Product) for all US advertisers using Amazon DSP, Sponsored Display , Sponsored Brands , or Sponsored TV ." and "Multi-touch metrics can also be accessed in most report types for version 3 of the Amazon Ads API, as well as Amazon Marketing Stream conversion datasets for Amazon DSP, Sponsored Products , Sponsored Brands, and Sponsored Display." [official] (Uncover ad value across the funnel with multi-touch attribution (beta), https://advertising.amazon.com/resources/whats-new/uncover-ad-value-across-funnel-with-multi-touch-attribution, read 2026-11-09). [seat] The first sentence's advertiser list omits Sponsored Products; the second names Sponsored Products in a list that may govern the Stream datasets only or the v3 report types too. M1a. RULE (settled 2026-11-09, re-opened for a cross-file contradiction). [official] (Reporting columns (version 3), https://d3a0d0y2hgofx6.cloudfront.net/en-us/guides/reporting/v3/columns.md; Purchases (Amazon retail conversions) (v1); Amazon Marketing Stream data guide, https://d3a0d0y2hgofx6.cloudfront.net/en-us/guides/amazon-marketing-stream/data-guide.md; Amazon Marketing Stream v2 overview, https://d3a0d0y2hgofx6.cloudfront.net/en-us/guides/amazon-marketing-stream/v2/overview.md; all read 2026-11-09). ### R. The industry standard Amazon's figures are read against (gap fill 2026-11-09)

These are the IAB's and the MRC's words, not Amazon's. They bind an accredited measurer only through an MRC audit; for Amazon's sponsored ads they are the benchmark a buyer applies, and Amazon Ads staff are named among the contributors ("Aly Anzalone, Senior Program Manager, Measurement Standards Compliance, Amazon Ads"; "Celeste Donahoe, Director, Retail Media Measurement, Amazon Ads"; "Amazon" among the company participants). [third party: IAB/MRC] (IAB/MRC Retail Media Measurement Guidelines, January 2024, https://www.iab.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/IAB_Retail_Media_Measurement_Guidelines_January2024.pdf, pp. 4–6, read 2026-11-09). Page numbers below are the document's printed page numbers.

R1. GUIDANCE (industry). Attribution windows must be supported, disclosed in advance and consistent: "such windows must be empirically supported, but they must also be logical with regard to campaign objectives and length as well as category sales cycles"; "These windows must be disclosed before campaign execution and measurement and as part of disclosures corresponding to reported results"; "typical attribution windows for digital measurement are often 3, 7, 14, 28, or 30 days"; "a reasonable maximum attribution window should be established with support and applied consistently, as well as disclosed to measurement users"; and, for reconciliation, "consistent granularity of component days (data providing day-level granularity on the basis of when an ad was viewed or clicked relative to when a conversion occurred should be made available to a measurement user by a retail measurement organization) is encouraged". [third party: IAB/MRC] (Guidelines, §3.6 "Attribution windows", p. 27). [seat] Against Amazon: the windows are disclosed per ad product and advertiser type (A3); the day-lag granularity exists for Sponsored Products in v3 (`purchases1d/7d/14d/30d`, E2) and at event level in AMC (O2), and v1 removes the suffixes (E1).

R2. GUIDANCE (industry). Weights and halo must be disclosed: "Attribution approaches should assign value or weight to different exposures or touchpoints, with the process being suited to the campaign objective, supported by evidence, and disclosed transparently"; "At minimum, SKU-level reporting is recommended where relevant …; reporting of any halo products being counted for is encouraged to be transparent"; and, in the metric table, "Attributable/assisted halo sales/revenue/ROAS must provide transparency on methodology, SKUs that are being attributed, online and offline distinction, and deterministic vs. probabilistic outcomes as well as the attribution window and session period." The guideline also warns that "Many attribution approaches may not account for elements such as price, promotion, influencers, PR, competition, weather, economy, and other factors that can influence consumer actions. Therefore, the weights assigned to exposures may not reflect the broader context." [third party: IAB/MRC] (Guidelines, §3.6, pp. 26–27; §5.3 metric table, p. 48)

R3. GUIDANCE (industry). Viewability and attribution: "The MRC requires viewable impressions for attribution of outcomes to ad exposures"; attribution "based in part or in whole on impressions that are non-viewable (including cases in which viewability cannot be measured due to technical limitations such as may occur for CTV) may still be reported. However, they must be properly labeled with a clear disclaimer (these would not be considered fully compliant) alongside fully compliant attribution metrics"; "'strong user interaction' such as a click or tap can serve as a proxy for viewability". The metric table's "Attributable Direct Sales/ ROAS/Conversions" are "Outcomes that can be attributed to a viewable ad based on MRC viewability standards", with "Need to disclose if attribution is based on viewability or extrapolation". [third party: IAB/MRC] (Guidelines, §3.5, p. 25; §5.3, p. 47). [seat] Against Amazon: Sponsored Products and CPC Sponsored Brands credit clicks only (the click proxy); vCPM view credit uses the MRC thresholds but "viewability is inferred based on historical data and placement characteristics" where it cannot be measured (C1, C8), and no Amazon page read labels the inferred share.

R4. GUIDANCE (industry). Forecasts beyond the window: "Marketers will sometimes need to make decisions based on a shorter time period of measured sales. In these cases, it is common for the measurement provider to forecast the full 12-month effect of the campaign. The basis for such forecasts must be disclosed and the methodology for extending shorter period results to 12 months or longer periods should be re-evaluated periodically based on empirical evidence." Time-decay curves are to be "revisited and validated regularly (at least annually)", and post-campaign adstock modelled so a later campaign's sales are not credited to an earlier one. [third party: IAB/MRC] (Guidelines, §3.6, pp. 27–28). [seat] This is the standard Amazon's long-term sales (F6, F8–F11) meets or misses; the accumulated-sales fields are the periodic check.

R5. GUIDANCE (industry). Modelled and extrapolated conversions: measurers are "recommended to disclose … The percentage of attributed outcome metrics (sales, units, visits, etc.) that are extrapolated within each measurement deliverable (e.g., 20% of attributed sales in ROAS calculation are extrapolated) as required by MRC Minimum Standards … and MRC Outcomes and Data Quality Standards". [third party: IAB/MRC] (Guidelines, §3.6 "Modeled attribution for unknown data", p. 29). [seat] Against Amazon: AMC's NTB columns include modeled conversions from 2026-11-02 (N1) and modeled conversions show NULL below ad-line level (O2); no sponsored-ads page read states a modelled share for the standard metrics.

R6. GUIDANCE (industry). Invalid traffic: "All digital measurement for retail media organizations should at least meet MRC GIVT requirements … and are strongly encouraged to also meet SIVT requirements. The MRC requires SIVT filtration for measuring outcomes"; "IVT decision rates should be disclosed as required by MRC and the inability to collect sufficient signals to make IVT determinations … should result in disclosed unknown IVT (not valid or invalid by default)"; crawling and scraping "should be filtered from reported/monetized measurement and disclosed". [third party: IAB/MRC] (Guidelines, §3.3, pp. 23–24). [seat] Against Amazon: SIVT filtration is MRC-accredited for Sponsored Products clicks only (D7, D7a).

R7. GUIDANCE (industry). Incrementality. "Incrementality measures the true value created by any business strategy … In other words, incrementality is the potential causal impact of marketing"; "MRC Standards highly recommends using RCTs, where individuals or units are randomly assigned to a test group or a control group"; an RCT is to separate the treated from the intention-to-treat group ("it may be necessary to utilize intent-to-treat (ITT) analysis methods … or leverage approaches centered around other implementations such as Ghost Ads or PSA methods"); synthetic controls and matched markets are accepted where "the matching variables account for all confounding variables". [third party: IAB/MRC] (Guidelines, Chapter 4, pp. 31–34). The Amazon MTA paper quotes this sentence: the guidelines "'highly recommends using RCTs' to measure advertising incrementality". [research, Amazon-authored] (Lewis, Zettelmeyer, Gordon et al. 2025, arXiv 2508.08209 HTML, read 2026-11-09). The later IAB and IAB Europe guidelines (November 2025) define incrementality against attribution in so many words: "Incrementality differs from attribution and ROAS: those methods show what happened, not whether marketing caused the result", and grade four method families: "Experiment-based" (RCTs, "Hold out / Ghost Ads", matched markets) "Strong"; "Model-based Counterfactual" (synthetic control, ML propensity models) "Strong to Moderate"; "Econometric" (MMM, time-series regression) "Moderate to Weak"; "Hybrid Proxies" ("New-to-brand percentage, baseline vs. exposed analysis, platform-reported incrementality, Simple Multi-Touch Attribution") "Weak", "relying on platform-specific attribution / modeling rather than an independent, true control group". [third party: IAB/IAB Europe] (Guidelines for Incremental Measurement in Commerce Media, November 2025, https://iabeurope.eu/wp-content/uploads/IAB_and_IAB_Europe_Guidelines_Incremental_Measurement_Commerce_Media_November_2025.pdf, pp. 2–3, read 2026-11-09)

R8. GUIDANCE (industry). Disclosure and definitions: new-to-brand "defines a shopper who has not purchased any products from the specified brand within a defined time frame that needs to be disclosed by the retailer"; the incrementality chapter's limitation is that it measures "the direct incremental impact within a retailer's ecosystem, rather than considering the broader 'halo effect' it may have on the rest of the market"; and a measurer's methodology "should be fully described and accessible to users of the data", including "data editing procedures or other types of data adjustment or projection". [third party: IAB/MRC] (Guidelines, §5.3, p. 47; §4.3 "Limitations", p. 40; "Disclosure Guidance", p. 55). [seat] Amazon discloses its NTB look-back (12 months / 365 days, F1); "halo" in the guideline's incrementality chapter means off-retailer effects, a different use of the word from Amazon's same-brand or same-merchant halo (E5).

## Published research

1. **Qin, "Lengthen your attribution window: which digital ads have the most long-term impact?"**, Applied Marketing Analytics 9(1):73–83, 2023, by an Amazon Ads scientist (https://cdn.amazon.science/79/6e/915822bb4663a28c97c869a75931/lengthen-your-attribution-window-which-digital-ads-have-most-long-term-impact.pdf, read 2026-11-09). 3. **Gordon, Moakler and Zettelmeyer, "Close Enough? 4. **Gordon, Moakler and Zettelmeyer, "Predicted Incrementality by Experimentation (PIE) for Ad Measurement"**, arXiv 2304.06828, v2 2026-04-01 (read 2026-11-09). 9. **Danaher and van Heerde, "Delusion in Attribution: Caveats in Using Attribution for Multimedia Budget Allocation"**, Journal of Marketing Research 55(5):667–685, 2018 (doi 10.1177/0022243718802845; abstract via Crossref, read 2026-11-09). [research] They define attribution formally as "the relative incremental contribution that each medium makes to a purchase, taking into account advertising carryover and interaction effects", and show that "attribution is proportional to the marginal effectiveness of a medium times its number of exposures. This means that often-used media will have high attribution weights. However, the profit-maximizing allocation for a fixed budget is a function of advertising effectiveness, but not a function of past exposure levels." Their simulated and empirical data show "how attribution can offer misleading insights on how to allocate resources across media", and "substantial gains in purchase probability can be made using profit-maximizing allocation compared with attribution-based allocation". *Transfer:* even an attribution that is causal per touch credits an ad product in proportion to how much of it ran; a seller's budget split read from attributed sales by campaign, ad product or MTA credit inherits that scale effect. [seat]

10. **Li and Kannan, "Attributing Conversions in a Multichannel Online Marketing Environment: An Empirical Model and a Field Experiment"**, Journal of Marketing Research 51(1):40–56, 2014 (doi 10.1509/jmr.13.0050; abstract via Crossref). [research] Individual-level touch data model "consideration of online channels", "visits through these channels over time", and "subsequent purchases", estimating "the carryover and spillover effects of prior touches at both the visit and purchase stages"; channels' relative contributions "are significantly different from those found by other currently used metrics"; "A field study validates the proposed model's ability to estimate the incremental impact of a channel on conversions", and the estimates "help identify cases in which retargeting strategies may actually decrease conversion probabilities". *Transfer:* last-touch credit and a channel's incremental contribution diverge, and the divergence can change sign for retargeting-like touches (Sponsored Display remarketing is the Amazon analogue). [seat]

11. **Anderl, Becker, von Wangenheim and Schumann, "Mapping the customer journey: Lessons learned from graph-based online attribution modeling"**, International Journal of Research in Marketing 33(3):457–474, 2016 (doi 10.1016/j.ijresmar.2016.03.001; abstract via EconPapers). [research] "a novel attribution framework reflecting the sequential nature of customer paths as first- and higher-order Markov walks", applied "to four large customer-level data sets from various industries, each entailing at least seven distinct online channels"; "The results show substantial differences from currently applied heuristics such as last click attribution", with "idiosyncratic channel preferences (carryover) and interaction effects both within and across channel categories (spillover)". *Transfer:* a path model on conversion-path data (Amazon's conversion path report, F7; AMC's event tables, O2) credits touches differently from last touch, but a Markov removal effect is still observational credit, not a holdout. [seat]

12. **Dalessandro, Perlich, Stitelman and Provost, "Causally motivated attribution for online advertising"**, Proceedings of the Sixth International Workshop on Data Mining for Online Advertising (ADKDD 2012), doi 10.1145/2351356.2351363 (abstract via OpenAlex). [research] "we position attribution as a causal estimation problem and then propose two approximation methods as alternatives for when the full causal estimation can not be done. These approximate methods derive from our causal approach and incorporate prior attribution work in cooperative game theory. We argue that in cases where causal assumptions are violated, these approximate methods can be interpreted as variable importance measures." *Transfer:* Shapley-style splits (Berman, item 6; Amazon's MTA, M1) are causal only under the assumptions the authors state; otherwise they rank touches by importance. [seat]

13. **Zantedeschi, Feit and Bradlow, "Measuring Multichannel Advertising Response"**, Management Science 63(8):2706–2728, 2017 (doi 10.1287/mnsc.2016.2451; abstract via Crossref). [research] Observational exposure data are hard to read "because of concerns that advertisers target customers with a higher propensity to buy or increase advertising during periods of peak demand"; a firm "randomly held out customers from each campaign", but "the estimated effect for any individual campaign is imprecise, because of the small size of the holdout"; a hierarchical Bayesian ad-stock model pooled across campaigns estimates "separate decay rates for each advertising medium" and finds "catalogs have substantially longer-lasting impact on customer purchase than emails"; targeting the most responsive customers "increases the predicted returns on advertising by approximately 70% versus traditional recency, frequency, and monetary value–based targeting". *Transfer:* decay differs by medium, which is the same question Qin (item 1) asks across Amazon ad products; single small holdouts are noisy, pooled ones informative. [seat]

14. **Athey, Chetty, Imbens and Kang, "The Surrogate Index: Combining Short-Term Proxies to Estimate Long-Term Treatment Effects More Rapidly and Precisely"**, NBER Working Paper 26463, November 2019, revised August 2024 (doi 10.3386/w26463; NBER page read 2026-11-09; arXiv 1603.09326 v5, 2024-08-22, abstract read). [research] Short-term outcomes are combined into a "surrogate index"; "Under the Prentice surrogacy assumption, which requires that the primary outcome is independent of the treatment conditional on the surrogates, we show that the average treatment effect on the surrogate index equals the treatment effect on the long-term outcome." They "characterize the bias that arises from violations of each of the key assumptions" and "provide simple methods to validate these assumptions using additional observed outcomes". The arXiv version's setting is "two samples, an experimental sample containing data about the treatment indicator and the surrogates and an observational sample containing information about the surrogates and the primary outcome", and it notes "The validity of the surrogacy condition is often controversial." In their job-training application, six quarters of outcomes estimated the nine-year effect with "a 35% reduction in standard errors". *Transfer:* this is the frame for Amazon's long-term sales (F10–F11): the engagements are surrogates, the brand's historical shoppers are the observational sample, and the treatment-to-surrogate step is attributed credit rather than an experimental sample. [seat]

15. **Dekimpe and Hanssens, "The Persistence of Marketing Effects on Sales"**, Marketing Science 14(1):1–21, 1995 (doi 10.1287/mksc.14.1.1; abstract via Crossref). [research] "persistence modeling to derive long-term marketing effectiveness from time-series observations on sales and marketing expenditures": "we use unit-root tests to determine whether sales are stable or evolving (trending) over time. If they are evolving, we examine how strong this evolution is (univariate persistence) and to what extent it can be related to marketing activity (multivariate persistence)"; for a home-improvement chain "some, but not all, advertising has strong trend-setting effects on sales", which "traditional modeling approaches would not pick up". Qin (item 1) applies this persistence method to Amazon brands and reports that "All tests reject the null hypothesis on the presence of a unit root", so all KPIs are stationary, which in Qin's words "implies that no marketing effects last forever" (Qin's own reference is the companion paper, Dekimpe and Hanssens, "Empirical Generalizations about Market Evolution and Stationarity", Marketing Science 14(3):G109–G121, 1995). *Transfer:* on the stationary Amazon series Qin studied, ad effects decay to zero, so a long-term value is a finite sum of decaying effects, not a permanent shift; a 12-month figure such as LTS sums such effects for new-to-brand shoppers. [seat]

16. **Lewis and Reiley, "Online ads and offline sales: measuring the effect of retail advertising via a controlled experiment on Yahoo!"**, Quantitative Marketing and Economics 12(3):235–266, 2014 (abstract at https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/qmktec/v12y2014i3p235-266.html, read 2026-11-09). [research] "A randomized experiment with 1.6 million customers"; "The advertising profitably increases purchases by 5%. 93% of the increase occurs in brick-and-mortar stores; 78% of the increase derives from consumers who never click the ads." The experiment "provides a specification check for observational difference-in-differences and cross-sectional estimators; the latter exhibits a large negative bias three times the estimated experimental effect." *Transfer:* exposure without a click moved purchases, so click-only credit (Sponsored Products, CPC Sponsored Brands; C2) can miss effects; and observational bias can run in either direction. The setting is display ads for a retailer with mostly offline sales, not Amazon. [seat]

17. **Johnson, Lewis and Nubbemeyer, "Ghost Ads: Improving the Economics of Measuring Online Ad Effectiveness"**, Journal of Marketing Research 54(6):867–884, 2017 (doi 10.1509/jmr.15.0297; abstract via OpenAlex, read 2026-11-09). [research] "To measure the effects of advertising, marketers must know how consumers would behave had they not seen the ads"; Ghost Ads identify "the control-group counterparts of the exposed consumers in a randomized experiment" and "work with modern ad platforms that optimize ad delivery in real-time"; in "an online retailer's display retargeting campaign", "the ads lifted website visits by 17.2% and purchases by 10.5%", measured "just as precisely while spending at least an order of magnitude less" than intent-to-treat or PSA designs. *Transfer:* the lift is measured on exposed users against their randomized ghost counterparts, clicks or not; it is the design the IAB/MRC guidelines name (§R7) and the kind of evidence that would settle whether Amazon's view-attributed purchases are incremental; no Amazon page read offers it for sponsored ads. [seat]

**Carry-over: weighing the three Amazon-relevant sources (gap fill; the owner of the window question is `method/change-measurement.md`).** (a) Qin 2023 [research, Amazon-authored] is the only Amazon-data measurement broken out for Sponsored Products: "SP does not show significant carry-over effects beyond three weeks for any brand", but only two of the fifteen brands carry an SP row in the appendix table, and in those rows week 1 holds 37% and 69% of the DPV effect (48% and 62% of revenue), week 2 27% and 22% (23%, 26%), week 3 36% and 9% (29%, 12%), weeks 4 and beyond 0%. So within three weeks, but with 63% and 31% of the measured SP effect on DPV (52% and 38% on revenue) arriving after week 1, i.e. after a seller's 7-day window (A3); the paper does not say whether its revenue KPI is ad-attributed or total, and the brands are ones "that asked Amazon Ads to quantify ad carry-over effects". (b) Lily Tong's ~20% after the window [Amazon staff via Digiday] pools all Amazon ad products and windows "typically seven to 30 days", with no published method, so it neither confirms nor contradicts (a) for Sponsored Products. (c) Hershaff's daily adstock decay of ~0.35 [opinion, one seller, about a month of daily on/off] implies carry-over measured in days. The non-Amazon studies `method/change-measurement.md` cites (Ju, Zhao & Aral on app-store rank feedback; Hohnhold, O'Brien & Tang on Google user learning; not re-opened by this seat) describe mechanisms that build over weeks or months; Qin's two SP rows show no such build on Amazon data. [seat] Read together: on Amazon data a Sponsored Products effect has been measured to fade within about three weeks, with a material part arriving in weeks 2–3; a reading window or a pause shorter than about three weeks can miss part of it; no Amazon-data study read shows a Sponsored Products effect lasting months.

## Stable enough for a skill

Items 1–17 are Amazon's documented definitions or rules, stable across the pages read; item 18 is the MRC's, item 19 the IAB/MRC's, item 20 published research's. Numbers inside them are read at run time (next section).

1. Sponsored ads date every attributed conversion to the day of the ad interaction, not the day of the sale; Amazon Attribution and KENP read date to the event. (Ad campaign attribution; Overview of the Reporting v1 API; Understand unified reporting; KENP metrics; Amazon Attribution OpenAPI model)
2. Attribution is per conversion, once, across all advertisers, ranked promoted clicks, halo clicks, promoted views, halo views, then last touch with clicks over views. (Ad campaign attribution)
3. Lookback, eligible products and interactions differ by ad product and by seller against vendor; seller Sponsored Products halo is "any other products sold by the Seller Central merchant", vendor and brand halo is same-brand products. (Ad campaign attribution)
4. Sponsored Products and CPC Sponsored Brands count clicks only; vCPM Sponsored Brands, Sponsored Display, Streaming TV and DSP count views too, under the MRC viewability standard; from 2026-01-01 a shopping-signal model governs vCPM view credit and "all views" metrics keep the old model for comparison. (Ad campaign attribution; View attribution updates for Amazon Store ads; Purchases (all views))
5. Impressions and clicks are net of invalid traffic; gross and invalid counts are separate metrics. (Traffic metrics; Impressions and Clicks (v1))
6. Unified reporting and v1 have no window suffixes; base metrics use the product's single window and include halo; promoted and halo are breakouts. (Understand unified reporting; Overview of the Reporting v1 API; Conversion metrics)
7. "Purchase rate" in unified reporting and v1 is per impression; the per-click rate is "Purchase rate over clicks"; older pages and bulksheets use other meanings. (Conversion metrics; Purchases (v1); Search term report for Sponsored Products; Bulksheets search term report)
8. ACOS is not a unified-reporting or v1 field; it is total cost over sales, the inverse of ROAS; v3 offers click-window ACOS on SP targeting, search-term and advertised-product reports and on the prompt and video ad-extension report types, not on `spCampaigns`. (What is ACOS; Reporting columns (version 3); Bulksheets search term report)
9. Attributed sales exclude tax and cart discounts and can include items sold by other sellers or used; cancellations within the stated hours are deducted. (Ad campaign attribution)
10. Traffic metrics do not exist at the converted-product level. (Data availability in unified reporting)
11. v1 money metrics require the budget-currency dimension and are in the campaign budget currency unless converted; v1 days follow the campaign time zone. (Helpful concepts (v1); Dates and historical data retention (v1))
12. Figures restate: clicks and cost for days, conversions for weeks, invalid traffic for up to a month. (Reporting v3 FAQ; Amazon Ads reporting overview; Traffic validation; Adjustments to invalid spend)
13. Bulksheet search-term metrics use a fixed 7-day window for sellers and vendors, and the bulksheet page defines CTR and conversion rate inverted. (Bulksheets search term report)
14. MRC accreditation of Amazon's Sponsored Products measurement covers clicks (gross and net of SIVT) on Amazon placements, in total, not impressions or conversions. (MRC letter of 2026-07-08; MRC accredited data for Sponsored Products campaigns)
15. Multi-touch metrics are a separate, modelled family beside last touch, in beta; their API names are v1's `metric.multiTouch*` fields, which map to no v3 column. (Uncover ad value … multi-touch attribution (beta); Purchases (v1); Reporting columns (version 3))
16. Amazon's NTB help page states that new-to-brand data is not available for Sponsored Products; v3 carries NTB on no `sp*` report type. (Sponsored ads new-to-brand metrics; Reporting columns (version 3))
17. Long-term sales is an ad-attributed forecast of 12-month value from new-to-brand engagements, machine-learning based in the US since 2026-09-25, with cohort accumulated-sales fields to check the forecast against what the cohort spent. (Long-term sales help page; Long-term sales (v1); release notes of 2025-06-05 and 2026-09-25)
18. Of Amazon's first-party measurement, only Sponsored Products clicks (gross and net of SIVT, total) and parts of Amazon DSP's impressions, clicks and viewability are MRC-accredited; Sponsored Brands and Sponsored Display are not on the MRC list. (MRC Accredited Services — Digital; MRC letters of 2026-05-29 and 2026-07-08)
19. The IAB/MRC retail media guidelines ask that attribution windows be empirically supported and disclosed, that view-based attribution use MRC-viewable impressions or carry a disclaimer, that 12-month forecasts disclose their basis and be re-evaluated, and that incrementality be measured preferably by RCTs. (IAB/MRC Retail Media Measurement Guidelines, January 2024)
20. Attribution credit is not marginal value: published models show credit scaling with exposure volume while the profit-maximising budget depends on effectiveness alone, and observational credit diverging from experimental lift. (Danaher & van Heerde 2018; Li & Kannan 2014; Gordon et al. 2019, 2023)

## Positions

Each position is a fact with its source. Numbers inside a position are the value read on 2026-11-09; the skill reads the current one.

**P-metrics-and-attribution-1.** Sponsored ads report an attributed conversion on the date of the shopper's ad interaction, which may differ from the conversion date; attribution metrics for a report date stay incomplete until the lookback window has ended. (Ad campaign attribution; Overview of the Reporting v1 API; Understand unified reporting) [official]

**P-metrics-and-attribution-2.** Amazon attributes each conversion to no more than one ad interaction across all campaigns and all advertisers, ranked promoted-product clicks, brand-halo clicks, promoted-product views, brand-halo views, and then by last touch with the last click before the last view. (Ad campaign attribution) [official]

**P-metrics-and-attribution-3.** Amazon's lookback and eligible products differ by ad product and advertiser: Sponsored Products for sellers is 7 days, clicks only, crediting the advertised products and any other product the Seller Central merchant sells; Sponsored Products for vendors and authors is 14 days, clicks only, crediting the advertised products and same-brand products, only those sold by Amazon, with no brand halo for books; Sponsored Brands, Sponsored Display, Streaming TV and DSP are 14 days and credit the brand halo, including sales by third parties and used items. (Ad campaign attribution) [official]

**P-metrics-and-attribution-4.** Sponsored Products and CPC Sponsored Brands attribute to clicks only and report zero conversions from views; vCPM Sponsored Brands and Sponsored Display, Streaming TV and DSP also attribute to views that meet the MRC standard of 50% of pixels for 1 second (display) or 2 seconds (video). (Ad campaign attribution; Overview of the Reporting v1 API; Performance metrics) [official]

**P-metrics-and-attribution-5.** From 2026-01-01 Amazon credits views for vCPM Sponsored Brands and Sponsored Display (including branded keyword campaigns) and DSP Store ads with a shopping-signal enhanced last-touch model with a shorter window, and keeps the original 14-day all-views model as separate "all views" metrics for comparison. (View attribution updates for Amazon Store ads; Ad campaign attribution; Purchases (all views)) [official]

**P-metrics-and-attribution-6.** Amazon's impressions and clicks are net of invalid traffic (impressions = gross − invalid; clicks = gross − invalid), with gross and invalid counts reported as separate metrics. (Traffic metrics; Impressions (Delivery); Clicks (Delivery)) [official]

**P-metrics-and-attribution-7.** Amazon defines CTR as clicks / impressions, CPC as cost / clicks, CPM as cost / impressions × 1,000, vCPM as cost / viewable impressions × 1,000 and ROAS as sales / total cost; its ACOS guide defines ACOS as ad spend / ad revenue × 100, the inverse of ROAS. (Clicks (Delivery); Impressions (Delivery); Viewability (Delivery); Purchases (v1); What is ACOS) [official]

**P-metrics-and-attribution-8.** Unified reporting and the Reporting API v1 have no ACOS field; Reporting v3 has `acosClicks7d` and `acosClicks14d` on the Sponsored Products targeting, search-term and advertised-product report types and on the prompt and video ad-extension report types, not on `spCampaigns`. (Reporting columns (version 3); Purchases (v1); Conversion metrics) [official]

**P-metrics-and-attribution-9.** In unified reporting and v1, "Purchase rate" is purchases / impressions and "Purchase rate over clicks" is purchases from clicks / clicks (formerly "14 day conversion rate"); Amazon's Sponsored Products search-term help page (modified 2026-05-18) still calls the per-click rate "Purchase rate". (Conversion metrics; Purchases (v1); Search term report for Sponsored Products) [official]

**P-metrics-and-attribution-10.** Amazon's bulksheet search-term page defines CTR as "calculated as impressions divided by clicks" and conversion rate as "The total number of orders divided by sales within 7 days from the date that your ad was clicked", the inverse of its definitions everywhere else, and fixes the bulksheet search-term lookback at 7 days for sellers and vendors regardless of the date range. (Bulksheets search term report; Clicks (Delivery); Conversion metrics) [official]

**P-metrics-and-attribution-11.** Unified reporting and v1 drop window suffixes: Sponsored Products base metrics are 7-day for sellers and 14-day for vendors, other campaign types 14-day except DSP's flexible windows, and base metrics include halo, with promoted and halo as breakouts that replace "advertised SKU", "other SKU" and "brand halo". (Understand unified reporting; Overview of the Reporting v1 API; Conversion metrics) [official]

**P-metrics-and-attribution-12.** Reporting v3 carries Sponsored Products 1-, 7-, 14- and 30-day click-attributed purchases, sales and units, and same-SKU and other-SKU variants; v1 carries no day-count variant; v3 is to be shut off after 2027-06-30. (Reporting columns (version 3); Purchases (v1); Amazon Ads reporting overview) [official]

**P-metrics-and-attribution-13.** A purchase is an order event that can include several units; units sold is a separate metric. (Ad campaign attribution; Purchases (v1)) [official]

**P-metrics-and-attribution-14.** Attributed sales exclude tax (VAT subtracted) and cart discounts, can include items sold by Amazon or other sellers and in used condition, and have payment failures and cancellations within 72 hours of order creation deducted, which can take up to 7 days. (Ad campaign attribution) [official]

**P-metrics-and-attribution-15.** Amazon does not report impressions, clicks or other traffic metrics at the converted-product level, because one ad interaction can lead to several product conversions. (Data availability in unified reporting) [official]

**P-metrics-and-attribution-16.** Amazon counts a purchase as new-to-brand when the customer has not bought from the brand within a 12-month lookback, from purchase history based on all shopping activity within the brand on Amazon, not limited to attributed purchases; its NTB help page says NTB data is not available for Sponsored Products (P-metrics-and-attribution-35 sets this against v1's eligibility list). (Sponsored ads new-to-brand metrics; Performance metrics; Purchases (new to brand) (v1)) [official]

**P-metrics-and-attribution-17.** Detail page views, add to cart, viewable impressions and vCPM are not offered for Sponsored Products in v1; detail page views are not unique per customer, and in unified reporting include halo. (Detail page views (v1); Add to cart (v1); Viewability (Delivery); Performance metrics; Understand unified reporting) [official]

**P-metrics-and-attribution-18.** KENP read is attributed to reading up to 14 days after an ad click and reported on the day the pages were read; offline reads can take a week to be reported; estimated KENP royalties multiply KENP read by the KENP rate at the time of reading, using the latest published rate when that day's rate is not yet published; KENP metrics appear once at least one Sponsored Products campaign promotes a book with at least one attributed KENP read. (KENP metrics; Ad campaign attribution) [official]

**P-metrics-and-attribution-19.** Amazon Attribution reports events on the date they occurred, not on the date of the attributed click, with a 14-day last-click model in which the last-clicked tag gets full credit; it restates at 1, 7 and 28 days taking returns and refunds into account, and masks rows with fewer than 10 lifetime clicks. (Amazon Attribution OpenAPI model; Amazon Attribution how-to) [official]

**P-metrics-and-attribution-20.** v1 aggregates report days in each campaign's time zone and expresses money in the campaign budget currency, requiring `budgetCurrency.value` with money metrics; converted-currency fields require `convertedCurrency.value`; console downloads use the account's registered time zone and AMC defaults to UTC. (Dates and historical data retention (v1); Helpful concepts (v1); Downloadable reports in sponsored ads; AMC data sources) [official]

**P-metrics-and-attribution-21.** Amazon's stated settling schedule: reporting data within 12 hours, clicks changing for up to 3 days, conversions restated at 1, 7 and 28 days (up to 42 days for a 14-day window) and changing for up to 60 days, invalid-traffic detection for up to 30 days, with Amazon advising to wait at least 5 days. (Reporting v3 FAQ; Amazon Ads reporting overview; Adjustments to invalid spend; Traffic validation) [official]

**P-metrics-and-attribution-22.** In the console, metrics update hourly, sales take up to 48 hours to populate, and cost is an estimate that can change until 3 days after the click or viewable impression. (Performance metrics) [official]

**P-metrics-and-attribution-23.** Amazon says an invalid-traffic rate (IVR) level on its own does not indicate undetected invalid traffic; for Sponsored Ads campaigns, a significant drop in IVR with an anomalous rise in CTR, impressions or clicks, together with a drop in conversion rate, is in Amazon's words "a higher confidence indicator for undetected IVT". (Traffic validation) [official]

**P-metrics-and-attribution-24.** The MRC accreditation renewed on 2026-07-08 covers Amazon's Sponsored Products gross clicks and clicks net of SIVT on Amazon placements, in total reporting without environment segmentation; impressions and conversions are not accredited statistics. (MRC accreditation letter; MRC accredited data for Sponsored Products campaigns) [third party: MRC; official for the console filter]

**P-metrics-and-attribution-25.** Amazon's multi-touch attribution is a US beta that splits credit across ad touches in proportion to their value using experiments, machine learning and shopping signals, reported as separate multi-touch purchases, sales and ROAS beside last-touch metrics. (Uncover ad value … multi-touch attribution (beta); Purchases (v1); Lewis, Zettelmeyer, Gordon et al. 2025) [official; research]

**P-metrics-and-attribution-26.** The console's legacy sponsored ads and Display, Video and Audio report centres shut on 2026-12-31, with saved reports, schedules and historical data deleted; new legacy reports and edits stop on 2026-12-17; Amazon says these dates are subject to change. (Migrate your scheduled reports to unified reporting; Understand unified reporting) [official]

**P-metrics-and-attribution-27.** Before unified reporting, Display, Video and Audio campaigns and legacy Sponsored Brands reported conversions on the conversion date; they now report on the traffic date, which moves conversions earlier in a flight without changing their total. (Understand unified reporting; Overview of the Reporting v1 API) [official]

**P-metrics-and-attribution-28.** AMC cannot change a campaign's attribution window; its `conversions` table marks a conversion ad-attributed if the user had a traffic event in the prior 28 days, and it offers conversion-time and traffic-time tables whose traffic-time output can change for up to 30 days. (AMC conversions table; Amazon attributed events by conversion time) [official]
