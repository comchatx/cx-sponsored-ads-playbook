## What this covers

This file maps what Amazon itself automates for an advertiser as of November 2026, what Amazon exposes to an AI agent, and what the paid tools sellers use automate and how they say they do it. It covers:

- Amazon's automated bidding and its rules: the bidding strategies (named here, owned by `areas/bids-and-placements.md`), rule-based bidding, schedule bid rules, and the newer optimization-rules model that carries performance conditions;
- Amazon's budget automation: schedule and performance budget rules, recommended events, portfolio unspent-budget sharing, budget recommendations (the mechanics of pacing belong to `areas/budgets-and-pacing.md`);
- Amazon's recommendation surfaces and their "apply" flows: the consolidated Sponsored Products recommendations, theme-based bid recommendations, target promotion groups (Amazon's own harvesting), the cross-product Recommendations API launched in August 2026, and the Partner Opportunities catalog;
- campaign types and settings where Amazon decides on the advertiser's behalf: automatic targeting, keyword groups, Sponsored Brands theme targeting and optimization rules, Sponsored Display's optimization objective, the v1 campaign model's `autoCreateTargets`, `autoManageCampaign`, automatic budget allocation and automatic global scaling, Full-Funnel Campaigns and DVA+;
- creative automation: Creative Agent, Video Generator, image generation, auto-assembled videos (opted in by default), Sponsored Brands headline optimization, AI-powered prompt ads and the AI shopping surfaces (Alexa for Shopping, into which Rufus was merged in May 2026, and Alexa+);
- Amazon Ads Agent (from 2026-09-29 the name of the unified ads platform, with a conversational experience in closed beta inside it);
- Seller Central's Seller Assistant (formerly Project Amelia), which drafts Sponsored Products and Sponsored Brands campaigns, and the SP auto-optimization announced at Accelerate 2026;
- how Amazon measured the figures it publishes for its own automation;
- the Amazon Ads MCP server: its tools, its Lite form, its skills library and what that library says about Amazon's agent-facing method;
- Amazon Marketing Stream as the data feed automation runs on (hourly and 15-minute data, AWS destinations);
- the third-party tools: Helium 10 Ads (formerly Adtomic), Perpetua, Teikametrics, Scale Insights, m19, Quartile, Intentwise, Ad Badger, BidX, Adbrew, Pacvue and others, with their stated method and, as context only, their pricing model;
- and, last, what an agent holding `cx-amazon-ads`'s open door can already do that these tools do.

Why a senior Amazon advertising lead cares. Almost every lever a seller pulls has an Amazon-run twin: a bid the seller sets is moved by Amazon's bidding strategy, a budget the seller sets is raised by a budget rule or borrowed from the portfolio, a target the seller does not add is added by an auto campaign, a keyword group, a Sponsored Brands optimization rule or `autoCreateTargets`, and creative the seller never uploaded (auto-assembled videos, prompts) runs on the seller's campaigns unless the seller opts out. A lead reading an account first asks which of these are switched on, because each one changes what a report means and what a manual change will do. Third-party tools stack a second layer of automation on top (bid algorithms, harvest rules, dayparting), which writes through the same Ads API that `cx raw amazon-ads` reaches. Several of these tools, and Amazon itself, now sell an MCP connection or "skills" for Claude and other agents, which is the exact form the ads playbook takes.

Other files own the levers in depth: `areas/bids-and-placements.md` (strategies, adjustment arithmetic, rule-based bidding's 5× ceiling), `areas/budgets-and-pacing.md` (budget rules as a pacing tool), `areas/search-terms-and-negatives.md` (harvesting method), `areas/keyword-and-product-targeting.md` (auto targeting's four expressions, keyword recommendations), `areas/campaign-structure.md`, `beyond/ad-products-and-eligibility.md` (Full-Funnel, DVA+, prompts as ad products), `method/ads-api-surface.md` (the endpoint inventory), `method/reporting.md` (Reporting v1 and Stream as data surfaces), `method/change-measurement.md` (why Amazon's automation confounds a before/after reading). This file states each automation once, at the level of "what Amazon decides, on what input, with what switch", and points to them.

### B. Bidding automation

The full treatment is `areas/bids-and-placements.md`; the lines here are what this file needs to describe Amazon's automation as a whole.

- RULE: Sponsored Products bidding strategies in the v3 model. `LEGACY_FOR_SALES` is "Dynamic bids - down only": "Lowers your bids in real time when your ad may be less likely to convert to a sale. Campaigns created before the release of the bidding controls feature used this setting by default." `AUTO_FOR_SALES` is "Dynamic bids - up and down": "Increases or decreases your bids in real time by a maximum of 100%." `MANUAL` is "Fixed bid": "Uses your exact bid and any placement adjustments you set, and is not subject to dynamic bidding." The `BiddingStrategy` enum also carries `RULE_BASED`. [official] (Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI, schemas `BiddingStrategy` and `BiddingStrategyRecommendation`, https://d1y2lf8k3vrkfu.cloudfront.net/openapi/en-us/dest/SponsoredProducts_prod_3p.json, read 2026-11-09)
- RULE: the v1 model's `BidStrategy` enum: `MANUAL`, `NEW_TO_BRAND` ("Optimizes bidding to maximize new-to-brand customer acquisitions"), `PRIORITIZE_KPI_TARGET` ("Optimizes bidding to achieve the KPI target specified"), `RULE_BASED` ("Applies bidding rules defined by the advertiser"), `SALES_DOWN_ONLY` ("Bids will never increase beyond your set bid"), `SALES_UP_AND_DOWN` ("by a maximum of 100%"), `SPEND_BUDGET_IN_FULL` ("Prioritize spending full budget, while maximizing performance") and `USE_CAMPAIGN_STRATEGY` ("Inherit the bid strategy from the parent campaign"). A campaign's `goalSettings` carries `goal` (`AWARENESS`, `CONSIDERATION`, `CONVERSIONS`, `ENGAGEMENT`), `kpi` and `kpiValue` ("The value of the KPI that the campaign is working to optimize"). Which strategies each ad product accepts is in the per-product view of the reference, not in the enum. [official] (Amazon Ads API v1 merged OpenAPI, schemas `BidStrategy`, `GoalSettings`, `Goal`, `KPI`, https://d1y2lf8k3vrkfu.cloudfront.net/openapi/en-us/dest/AmazonAdsAPIALLMerged_prod_3p.json, read 2026-11-09)
- RULE: how far up and down goes. "When you select dynamic bids - up and down, we will raise or lower your bid based on performance ... We will increase or decrease your bids by up to 100% for all placements based on performance." "Ensure you are prepared to potentially spend double the amount of the bid you input." [official] (*Guide to dynamic bidding - up and down with Sponsored Products*, Amazon Ads library, https://advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/dynamic-bidding-sponsored-products, read 2026-11-09)
  - RULE, the dissenting Amazon page: *An author's guide to Sponsored Products* (library guide, https://advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/authors-guide-to-sponsored-products, re-opened 2026-11-09 in the gap fill) still reads "Bids will be increased by up to 100% for placements at the top of the first page of shopping results and by up to 50% for all other placements." [official]
  - [seat] So four current Amazon sources (this library guide, which says "Currently, we can increase this bid by up to 100%, so that would be a maximum of $2.00 for all placements opportunities"; the v3 model; the v1 model; and the help page *Bidding strategies for Sponsored Products*, GCU2BUWJH2W3A8Z7, https://advertising.amazon.com/help/GCU2BUWJH2W3A8Z7: "We may increase or decrease your bids by up to 100% for all placements based on performance") say 100% for all placements, and one Amazon page, written for KDP authors, says 100% top of search and 50% elsewhere; several practitioners (BidX, Perpetua, Trellis) still use 100/50. No Amazon page dates a change from one to the other, so "the 50% is the older rule" is a reading (this seat's, PPC Land's), not Amazon's statement. `areas/bids-and-placements.md` owns this fact (P-bids-and-placements-8), and this file defers to it.
- GUIDANCE: the same guide lists when to use up and down ("Your key objective is sales", winning top-of-page placements, "a high-performing campaign", "ASINs with excess inventory", "deal ASINs"), says "You should review your bids often, about once every two weeks", and "We recommend that you limit your changes while testing strategies, so that you can attribute the difference in performance to a specific change." [official] (same page)
- RULE: rule-based bidding. "For any existing campaign running for at least 10 days, you can apply a rule with a guardrail of ROAS and Amazon Ads may then adjust your base bids up and down to increase conversions while maintaining your guardrails. Amazon Ads does not guarantee the ROAS guardrail. However, if your campaign is not meeting the guardrail and the return on ad spend (ROAS) of the campaign for the last 20 days drops to less than 30% of your ROAS over a 20-to-40-day period, we will disable the rule on your behalf and will enable your previous bidding strategy and targeting level bids." Eligibility: "Your campaign must be running for at least 10 days prior to setting a rule and have a minimum of 10 conversions in the last 30 days", any targeting type, and a minimum daily budget by marketplace (US, UK, DE, FR, IT, ES, CA, AU, AE, NL, BE, SG 10; IN 300; JP 600; MX 50; BR 20; EG 50; SA 20; SE 50; PL 30; TR 50, in local currency) *(for human readers only)*. "The guardrail is applied at the campaign level and the ROAS guardrail is based on the previous 21 days." When Amazon disables a rule, "you will receive an additional recommendation from AMR with an updated suggestion for your ROAS guardrail." [official] (*Sponsored Products rule-based bidding overview*, Ads API guide, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/rules/bidding-rules/overview, markdown at https://d3a0d0y2hgofx6.cloudfront.net/en-us/guides/rules/bidding-rules/overview.md, read 2026-11-09)
- GUIDANCE: "We suggest that you use the provided recommendation to set your ROAS guardrail. This recommendation is based on your historical campaign information and a group of similar advertisers. Additionally, if you set your ROAS guardrails too aggressively, you may see a decrease in the number of conversions compared to your historical performance." [official] (same page)
- RULE: the rule-based bidding API. `POST /sp/rules/campaignOptimization` creates a rule (`recurrence` `DAILY` only; `ruleAction` `ADOPT` only; `ruleType` `BID`; with `metricName` `ROAS` the operators are `GREATER_THAN` or `EQUAL_TO`); `GET`, `PUT`, `DELETE` manage it; `POST /sp/rules/campaignOptimization/state` reads per-campaign state ("We recommend that you execute this query a minimum of once per day"); `POST /sp/rules/campaignOptimization/eligibility` returns, for an eligible campaign, "a ROAS and bid guardrail guidelines ... based on the historical campaign performance of advertisers similar to you". `ruleStatus` is `ACTIVE` or `ARCHIVED`. The create request takes at most 20 `campaignIds`; the stored rule lists up to 100. [official] (*Getting started with the Sponsored Products rule-based bidding API*, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/rules/bidding-rules/getting-started; SP 3.0 OpenAPI, `CreateSPCampaignOptimizationRulesRequest`, `CampaignOptimizationRule`; read 2026-11-09)
- RULE: schedule bid rules. They "allow advertisers to automatically increase bids at specific times of the day, days of the week, date ranges, or during high-traffic events like Black Friday." A rule is created with `POST /sp/rules/optimization` and attached with `POST /sp/campaigns/{campaignId}/optimizationRules` (header `application/vnd.spoptimizationrules.v1+json`); `POST /sp/rules/optimization/search` finds rules (the SP 3.0 model has no `GET` on a campaign's optimization rules; the association path is `POST` only). "Whenever a new schedule bid rule is created or an existing rule is updated, the changes will take effect within 24 hours. A maximum of 20 schedule bid rules can be specified for a campaign. Schedule bid rules are applied on top of 'Placement adjustment' and 'Bidding strategies'." Increases from rules whose conditions hold are added: a 10% daily rule, a 20% Black Friday rule and a 20% 5–9 PM rule give 30% all Black Friday and 50% from 5 to 9 PM. Event ids come from `POST /sp/v1/events`. [official] (*Schedule Bid Rules*, Ads API guide, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/rules/bidding-rules/scheduled-bid-rules, read 2026-11-09)
- RULE: the shape of a schedule rule's action. `actionType` is `ADOPT` only; `actionOperator` is `INCREMENT` only; `actionUnit` is `PERCENT`; `value` is "An integer between 1 & 100, representing the percent increase on base bid". `timesOfDay` takes at most one window with `HH:00` start and end; `recurrence.type` is `DAILY` or `WEEKLY`; `status` is `ENABLED`, `ENDED`, `PAUSED` or `SCHEDULED` ("Only ENABLED and PAUSED are accepted in requests"). The association request takes at most 25 rule ids, and the schema says "Maximum 100 rules can be associated to each campaign". [official] (SP 3.0 OpenAPI, `OptimizationRulesAPISwagger*` schemas, read 2026-11-09)
  - [seat] The guide's "maximum of 20 schedule bid rules" and the schema's "Maximum 100 rules ... to each campaign" are different counts (schedule bid rules vs all optimization rules); the model reads the live error when a write is refused.
- RULE: a newer optimization-rule model with performance conditions. The same endpoint accepts `application/vnd.spoptimizationrules.v2+json`. In it, `ruleCategory` is `BID`, `BUDGET` or `TARGETING`; `ruleSubCategory` is `PERFORMANCE` or `SCHEDULE`; the required fields are `ruleCategory`, `ruleName`, `ruleSubCategory` and `status`, and `conditions`, `targeting` and `action` are optional; when present, `conditions` holds 1 to 10 entries, each an `attributeName` of `ACOS`, `CLICKS`, `CPC`, `CTR`, `CVR`, `IMPRESSIONS`, `ORDERS`, `ROAS`, `SALES` or `SPEND` with a value criterion (`comparisonOperator` and `value`; range criteria "NOT SUPPORTED right now"); and, when present, exactly one `targeting` block with `targetingType` `KEYWORD` or `PRODUCT`, 1 to 3 `expressionTypes` (`BROAD`, `EXACT`, `EXPANDED`, `PHRASE`) and `lookbackDays` from 3 to 65. A create request takes up to 300 rules, and its description says "Budget rules are not supported for this operation." The action remains `ADOPT` / `INCREMENT` / `PERCENT`. [official] (SP 3.0 OpenAPI, `OptimizationRulesAPISwaggerOptimizationRuleWithoutRuleIdV2`, `...RuleAttributeV2`, `...RuleCategoryV2`, `...RuleSubCategoryV2`, `...RuleTargeting`, `...CreateOptimizationRulesRequestV2`, read 2026-11-09)
  - [seat] No Amazon guide page found on 2026-11-09 describes these v2 performance rules; the shape is readable only from the model. As modelled, every SP optimization rule (schedule or performance, v1 or v2) can only add a percentage to a bid. That is not true of Amazon's bid automation as a whole: rule-based bidding and Sponsored Brands optimization rules move bids down as well as up (next item and section E). Pausing a target on performance is what the third-party rule engines below do with direct writes.
- RULE: the console help page on bidding rules. Rule-based bidding: "Amazon then adjusts your base bids for each ad impression, increasing or decreasing them as needed to try to achieve your performance target. Amazon does not guarantee that we'll hit the performance target that you set." "rule-based bidding can increase your bids by up to 5 times your adjusted bid amount (if any adjustments are applied to your original bid)", with a worked table ($1.00 base, 10% top-of-search adjustment: $1.10 adjusted, $5.50 max). The console eligibility reads differently from the API guide above: campaigns must meet the minimum daily budget; "You can specify a ROAS target in campaign settings. We recommend you should wait at least two weeks for the campaign to get enough data before specifying a ROAS target. If not specified, Amazon Ads will automatically determine an appropriate ROAS target to maximize sales." Schedule bid rules: "You can't apply schedule bid rules to campaigns that have Rule-based bidding active. Schedule bid rules will be on hold if you change your bidding strategy to Rule-based bidding"; they apply to "Dynamic bids - up and down", "Down only" and "Fixed"; "Schedule bid rules are add-on" (a 20% and a 10% rule give 30%), and "Schedule bid rules will be applied on top of your placement adjustment" (down only, 10% top-of-search, $1.00 bid, 20% Monday rule: $1.30 top of search and $1.20 elsewhere on Mondays). The hourly campaign report "is available for the last 30 days" *(for human readers only)*. [official] (*Bidding rules for Sponsored Products*, help GDLQ5D2BNFCAU3TW, https://advertising.amazon.com/help/GDLQ5D2BNFCAU3TW, read 2026-11-09 by the verifier from the page's embedded content on a plain GET)
  - [seat] The API guide's "at least 10 days ... minimum of 10 conversions in the last 30 days" and the help page's optional, Amazon-set ROAS target are two Amazon pages that disagree on eligibility; the eligibility call's live answer is the reading that holds for a given campaign.

Sponsored Products carries three Amazon-built audiences ("Purchased brand products", "Clicked or added brand product to cart," "Affinity for similar products") and Sponsored Brands carries "New-to-brand shoppers" plus "Purchased brand product" and "Clicked or Added brand product to cart"; the ids come from `ListTargetableEntities` with `targetTypeFilter` `AUDIENCE`, used with `audienceSegmentType` `BEHAVIOR_DYNAMIC`. ### C. Budget automation

The pacing method is `areas/budgets-and-pacing.md`; these are the Amazon-run mechanisms.

- RULE: budget rules exist for Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands and Sponsored Display. "Schedule-based rules enable you to set a rule to increase daily budget based on scheduled events such as Black Friday or a custom time period." "Performance-based rules enable you to set rules to increase campaign budgets when your campaigns meet a certain performance threshold ... These metrics are calculated using data from the previous 7 days for sellers and 14 days for vendors." [official] (*Sponsored ads budget rules overview*, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/rules/budget-rules/overview, read 2026-11-09)
- RULE: performance-rule metrics by ad type: `ACOS`, `CTR`, `CVR` (SP, SD); `IS`, top-of-search impression share, "not available as a metric for any campaign that contains a video creative" (SB); `NTB` (SB); `ROAS` (SB, SP, SD). Operators: `LESS_THAN`, `GREATER_THAN`, `EQUAL_TO`, `LESS_THAN_OR_EQUAL_TO`, `GREATER_THAN_OR_EQUAL_TO`. Endpoints: `POST /sp/budgetRules`, `POST /sb/budgetRules`, `POST /sd/budgetRules`, then `POST /{sp|sb|sd}/campaigns/{campaignId}/budgetRules` to associate. [official] (*Performance-based rules*, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/rules/budget-rules/performance-based, read 2026-11-09)
- RULE: schedule rules take `DAILY`, `WEEKLY` or `intraDaySchedule` recurrence; "`intraDaySchedule` is currently only available for campaigns in the following marketplaces: US, CA, UK, IN, JP" *(for human readers only)*; hours-of-day rules need a minimum budget to become active. "Recommended events are only available for Sponsored Products and Sponsored Brands": `POST /sb/campaigns/budgetRules/recommendations` (and the SP equivalent `POST /sp/campaigns/budgetRules/recommendations`) returns events with `suggestedBudgetIncreasePercent`; events listed before their dates are announced get their dates filled in later automatically. [official] (*Schedule-based rules*, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/rules/budget-rules/schedule-based; SP 3.0 OpenAPI path list; read 2026-11-09)
- RULE: evaluation. "Budget rules are evaluated on your campaigns at the start of the day to calculate campaign budget for the day." A rule is also evaluated "immediately" when created or updated, and whenever the campaign's daily budget changes. "Multiple rules can be specified for a campaign to a maximum of 250." Since February 2023, "If more than one budget rule satisfies your set conditions, all budget rules are applied and their increases are added to daily budget independently of other budget rules." Statuses: Active, On Hold (criteria not met, superseded, recurrence not met), Paused, Budget threshold not met (performance rules only), Pending Start, Expired. [official] (*Budget rule evaluation*, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/rules/budget-rules/rule-evaluation, read 2026-11-09)
  - [seat] The page's own worked example contradicts itself: with campaign ROAS 4, it applies the 40% rule (ROAS > 3) and says the 20% rule (also ROAS > 3) "was not applied because its condition was not met". The additive rule above is the stated logic; the example is not a reliable reading of edge cases.
- RULE: portfolio unspent-budget sharing. "when some campaigns do not use their full daily budget, that unspent campaign budget ... can automatically flow to other campaigns in your portfolio that consistently reach their daily spending limits ... high-performing campaigns [can] spend up to twice their daily budget by tapping into unspent campaign budget from previous days in the month ... while staying within your total monthly portfolio budget." Switch: `budgetControls.campaignUnspentBudgetSharing` `ENABLED` or `DISABLED`. [official] (Ads API release notes, "Automatically share unspent campaign budget with budget exhausted campaigns in a portfolio - updated", 2025-07-19, read 2026-11-09)
- RULE: budget recommendations. `POST /sp/campaigns/budgetRecommendations` returns, per campaign, a "Recommended daily budget" ("Estimated daily budget needed to keep the campaign in budget for the full 24-hour period"), "Percent time in budget" ("value -1 means we don't have enough information to compute the campaign's percent time in budget"), and "Estimated missed impressions, clicks and sales" ranges ("estimates based on historical traffic and the campaign's past performance ... but not guaranteed"). `POST /sp/campaigns/initialBudgetRecommendation` "Creates daily budget recommendation along with benchmark metrics when creating a new campaign." `POST /sp/campaigns/budget/usage` (and SB, SD equivalents) reads budget usage. [official] (SP 3.0 OpenAPI, operation descriptions, read 2026-11-09). The Partner Opportunities catalog says these "are refreshed once per day". [official] (*Partner Opportunities - Campaign Optimization*, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/recommendations/partner-opportunities/catalog/campaign-optimization, read 2026-11-09)

### D. Recommendations and the "apply" flows

- RULE: consolidated Sponsored Products recommendations. `GET` or `POST /sp/campaign/recommendations` "Gets the top consolidated recommendations across bid, budget, targeting for SP campaigns given an advertiser profile id. The recommendations are refreshed everyday." Each campaign entry may carry `biddingStrategyRecommendation` (`action` `UPDATE`, `suggestedBiddingStrategy` `AUTO_FOR_SALES` / `LEGACY_FOR_SALES` / `MANUAL`), `budgetRecommendation` (`INCREASE` or `DECREASE`, `suggestedBudget`), up to 50 `keywordTargetingRecommendations` (`ADD`, `DECREASE`, `INCREASE`, `REMOVE`, `UPDATE`; `keywordText`, `matchType` including `GROUP`, `suggestedBid`), up to 50 `placementBiddingRecommendations` (`PLACEMENT_TOP`, `PLACEMENT_REST_OF_SEARCH`, `PLACEMENT_PRODUCT_PAGE`; `suggestedBidAdjustment`; `incrementalImpressionsLowerPercent` and `...UpperPercent`), up to 50 `targetingGroupBidRecommendations` for the four auto expressions, and `sevenDaysEstimatedOpportunities` (`estimatedIncrementalClicksLower`, `...Upper`, "if all optimizations are made"). Placement and targeting-group recommendations carry an `action` of `ADD`, `DECREASE`, `INCREASE` or `REMOVE`. That shape is the `GET` response (`application/vnd.spgetcampaignrecommendationsresponse.v1+json`). ### M. How Amazon's own automation figures were measured

Added in the gap fill. Every lift figure in sections D to L is Amazon's report on its own product. None is a figure a skill uses as an expected effect for one seller.

- RULE, what the footnotes say. Ads Agent 40% faster first sales and 25% more unique customers: "Based on beta estimates; results are not predictive of future results" (product page). Full-Funnel 67% higher Long-Term ROAS: "against the blended average of other campaign performance across beta advertisers ... Early beta advertiser data; individual results may vary" (*Grow total sales with Full-Funnel Campaigns*). Keyword auto-optimization 40% and 13%: "In beta testing, campaigns that adopted keyword auto-optimization" (small-business page). Creative tools in Ads Agent: "up to 71% more product coverage and up to 15% sales growth versus their results before adopting the suite" (unBoxed 2026). Campaign presets 29%: "compared to similar \* advertisers", "Amazon internal data, WW, September 2022 – August 2023" (Partner Opportunities). SB headline optimization 1.6%: "Based on internal testing, headline text optimization can drive up to 1.6% `CTR` lift vs. non-optimized campaigns" (release notes, 2025-04-17). Ads data manager: "advertisers using first-party data with Amazon Ads see a 30% increase in ROAS compared to those who don't" (small-business page). Prompts: shoppers who click "convert to a sale 48% more often ... than those who don't" (same page). [official] (pages as named, read 2026-11-09)
  - [seat] The comparison groups are adopters against non-adopters, adopters against their own past, or a blended average of other campaigns. None of these pages describes a randomised holdout. `method/change-measurement.md` P-42 records the same for DCO and Full-Funnel; Improvado's Ivo Petrovic reads the Full-Funnel footnote as "not a holdout test on your account" [opinion] (*Amazon unBoxed 2026: What Changed for Retail Media Metrics*, https://improvado.io/blog/amazon-unboxed-2026-retail-media-measurement, read 2026-11-09).
- RULE, a selection rule built into one automation. Rule-based bidding is open only to a campaign that has been "running for at least 10 days prior to setting a rule and ha[s] a minimum of 10 conversions in the last 30 days". [official] (*Sponsored Products rule-based bidding overview*, section B)
  - [seat] So every campaign switched to rule-based bidding through the API was already converting, and a comparison of those campaigns with campaigns that were not eligible mixes the rule's effect with the gate's. A campaign switched on after a strong month is also exposed to regression to the mean (next item), which makes a before/after look worse than the rule's effect; a campaign switched on after a weak month is exposed the other way.
- [research] Regression to the mean: "unusually large or small measurements tend to be followed by measurements that are closer to the mean", and the effect "becomes more noticeable with increasing measurement error and when follow-up measurements are only examined on a sub-sample selected using a baseline value". (Barnett, van der Pols and Dobson, *Regression to the mean: what it is and how to deal with it*, International Journal of Epidemiology 34, 2005, pp. 215–220, doi 10.1093/ije/dyh299; abstract read on PubMed 2026-11-09)
- [research] Interference in an auction. In Baidu's AiAds study the 670 treated advertisers were chosen by Baidu from 13,350 that had set up conversion tracking ("we choose 670 advertisers to use the AiAds system as the experimental group, and the others can be treated as the controlled group"), and the authors write: "Since the advertising platform is an auction market, the performance of AiAds will gradually abate as the increase of more advertisers who choose the AiAds". That is the authors' expectation, not a measured decline. (Yang et al., *AiAds*, KDD 2019, https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.12118, full text read 2026-11-09)
- [research] Test-control interference measured. Blake and Coey found test-control interference in an eBay email-marketing experiment, and "naive estimates of the TATE ignoring test-control interference exaggerate the treatment's effectiveness by about a factor of 2"; Fradkin's simulation of marketplace search experiments overstates true effects "by over 100%". (Blake and Coey, *Why marketplace experimentation is harder than it seems: the role of test-control interference*, ACM EC 2014, pp. 567–582, doi 10.1145/2600057.2602837, Crossref record read 2026-11-09; their result as summarised by Holtz and Aral, *Limiting Bias from Test-Control Interference in Online Marketplace Experiments*, 2020, https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12162, full text read 2026-11-09. The ACM page answered a browser challenge and the paper itself was not read.)
  - [seat] Transfer to Amazon: when a beta's treated campaigns bid in the same auctions as untreated ones, part of the measured gain can be taken from the comparison group, so the gain shrinks when every advertiser in the auction has the same automation. ### What practitioners agree on, and where they split

- [opinion] Shared method across Helium 10, Scale Insights, Teikametrics, Ad Badger and Perpetua: the bid is derived from a value per click (revenue per click, or conversion rate times average order value) times a target ACoS, recomputed on a schedule; targets with too little data get a starting bid and a discovery period; search terms that convert are moved to exact match and terms that spend without orders become negatives.
- [opinion] They split on frequency (daily for Perpetua's ACoS mode and Ad Badger; several times a day for Teikametrics; hourly for dayparting tools), on the lookback (Scale Insights' user-set days to ignore and to analyse, its example 2 and 7; Pacvue's 7 skipping 3, per snippet only), and on whether harvesting runs inside the bidder (Perpetua's conversion mode does not).
- [opinion] Several vendors have moved from "AI does it" to "AI proposes, you approve" (BidX, Ads Agent's approval step), and from a dashboard to an MCP connection (Helium 10, Intentwise, Ad Badger, Adbrew).
- [seat] Every third-party tool writes through the same Ads API endpoints section D and the door use. What a tool adds is the schedule (it runs without being asked), a store of history longer than Amazon's report retention, and its bid formula; none of them has an Amazon write the door lacks.

### What an agent with the open door already does that these tools do

[seat] The door is `cx raw amazon-ads --origin <origin> --method <m> --path <p> [--header ...] [--body ...]` against `advertising-api.amazon.com`, `-eu` or `-fe`, with the profile scope header or `Amazon-Ads-AccountId` for v1 and cross-product calls (`cx-amazon-ads` skill and its `references/door.md`). The door adds no wait to a 429, reads a lost reply to a POST list as "sent, outcome unknown", and a report's download url is fetched outside it. Against that, job by job:

| Job a tool sells | Reachable through the door | What the door does not give |
|---|---|---|
| Read every campaign, ad group, target, bid, budget, strategy and automation flag | yes: v3 list calls and `POST /adsApi/v1/query/*` | nothing missing |
| Set or move a bid, a placement adjustment, a strategy, a budget | yes: v3 update calls, `POST /adsApi/v1/update/*` | a scheduler: the change happens when cx is asked or run |
| Create and read Amazon's own rules (rule-based bidding, schedule and v2 optimization rules, budget rules) | yes: `/sp/rules/*`, `/sp|sb|sd/budgetRules` | SP optimization rules and budget rules only raise; rule-based bidding moves bids both ways toward Amazon's reading of the ROAS goal; a seller-chosen decrease is a direct write |
| Dayparting | yes, by writing Amazon's schedule rules (Amazon then runs them) | down-moves inside the day need hourly writes, which needs a scheduler |
| Read Amazon's recommendations and apply one | yes: `/sp/campaign/recommendations`, bid, budget, keyword recommendations, `/adsApi/v1/query/recommendations`; apply is the write the recommendation names | Ads Agent's chat proposals have no API |
| Harvest search terms into manual targets | yes: Reporting v1 or v3 search term reports, then target writes; or Amazon's `/sp/targetPromotionGroups` | the report download step is outside the door |
| Add negatives | yes: negative keyword and target writes | same report step |
| Build a campaign set from a template | yes: v1 create calls or the v3 sequence | none |
| Opt out of auto-assembled videos | yes: `POST /adsApi/v1/update/campaigns` with `optOuts` | none |
| Hourly data | partly: a Stream v2 subscription can be created | the S3 bucket and SQS queue are the seller's AWS, not reachable through the door |
| Keep a long history of bids and results | no store of its own | report retention is Amazon's; a change log is Amazon's change history API (`POST /history`), which covers SP and SB only and "doesn't return information about who made the change" |
| Organic rank inputs for rank-based rules | not on the Ads API | rank comes from other sources (SP-API, listing tools) |
| Amazon's MCP skills | the `/skills` path sits on the Ads API origin | not called by this seat; whether the door's caller is allowed to fetch it is open |

The difference between cx and a bid tool is therefore the clock and the store, not the reach: a tool runs every hour without being asked and keeps its own history; cx acts when a seller's conversation or a scheduled run invokes it, and reads history from Amazon each time. Amazon's own rule engines (schedule bid rules, budget rules, rule-based bidding, target promotion groups) are the part of "automation without a clock" the door can hand to Amazon to run. [seat]

## Published research

Only what the seat opened is cited; for most items that is the abstract or the bibliographic record, and the line says so.

- [research] Platform-provided bid recommendations, as evidence. In Zillow's switch from negotiated contracts to auctions, many bidders did not use the platform's recommended bid; inferring values from bid histories, "for half of the agents not following the recommendation, the increased effort of experimenting with alternate bids results in increased regret", and the share not following "slowly declines as markets mature, but it remains large"; the authors attribute it to "the lack of trust in the platform-provided tool". (Jalaly, Nekipelov and Tardos, *Learning and Trust in Auction Markets*, 2017, https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.10672, abstract read 2026-11-09)
- [research] Autobidders under budget and ROI constraints. A gradient-based autobidder that uses only bandit feedback can satisfy budget and return-on-investment constraints with vanishing regret, and when such autobidders compete, "the resulting expected liquid welfare over all rounds is at least half of the expected optimal liquid welfare", whether or not bidding converges. (Lucier, Pattathil, Slivkins and Zhang, *Autobidders with Budget and ROI Constraints: Efficiency, Regret, and Pacing Dynamics*, 2023, https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13306, abstract read 2026-11-09). [seat] This is the theory behind a target-ROAS or target-ACoS strategy like `PRIORITIZE_KPI_TARGET` or rule-based bidding: the constraint is met by pacing a multiplier, not by a per-bid rule.
- [research] Amazon's own work on learning to bid. Amazon scientists frame bidding as a bandit or reinforcement-learning problem, because "an advertiser does not observe counterfactual outcomes of bid amounts that were not submitted, and successful advertisers will adapt their own strategies based on bids placed by competitors", and release AuctionGym, a simulator for offline evaluation of bidding strategies. (Jeunen, Murphy and Allison, *Learning to bid with AuctionGym*, AdKDD 2022, https://www.amazon.science/publications/learning-to-bid-with-auctiongym, abstract read 2026-11-09; code https://github.com/amazon-science/auction-gym, last pushed 2026-02-11). [seat] The counterfactual point is why neither a third-party bidder nor cx can read "what would this target have done at another bid" from a report; it can only change the bid and watch, which is `method/change-measurement.md`'s problem.
- [research] A platform's automated bidding at scale. Baidu's AiAds system replaced manual keyword bidding with automated bidding ("the advertisers adopt the target CPA bidding language ... their keyword-level manual bidding is invalid"), broad match with intelligent targeting, and creative generation. In an online A/B test against manual optimisation, AiAds raised conversions 56.91% and the platform's revenue 43.51% (table 2; the text says "more than 47% improvement in the revenue"); in a year-long grouping experiment (last week of 2018 against last week of 2017), the 670 treated advertisers, chosen by Baidu from 13,350 with conversion tracking, grew revenue 27.22% and budget 30.20%, against 10.85% and 8.83% for the rest. The authors expect that "the performance of AiAds will gradually abate as the increase of more advertisers who choose the AiAds", because the platform "is an auction market". (Yang et al., *AiAds: Automated and Intelligent Advertising System for Sponsored Search*, KDD 2019, https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.12118; full text read 2026-11-09 in the gap fill, section 7 and tables 2–3) *(figures for human readers only)*. [seat] "Revenue" in table 2 is the sponsored search system's revenue, that is Baidu's; the grouping experiment is not randomised; section M carries what this means for Amazon's own beta figures.
- [research] Test-control interference and regression to the mean, the two reasons a beta figure overstates or misstates one seller's expected effect: Blake and Coey (ACM EC 2014; via Holtz and Aral 2020) and Barnett, van der Pols and Dobson (2005). They are quoted in section M with their records.
- [research] Platform-side objectives. Large e-commerce ad platforms run the sell side, the buy side and the exchange themselves, so "the goal of the auto-bidding algorithms is not only to maximize the advertisers' conversions, but also the total revenue of the platform"; the benchmark tests bidders under homogeneous, heterogeneous and promotion-event competition such as Black Friday budget surges. (Yang et al., *PlatformBid: An Auto-Bidding Benchmark from a Unified Advertising Platform's Perspective*, 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27265, abstract read 2026-11-09). [seat] Amazon is such a platform; its "spend double" warning on up and down and its unspent-budget sharing are consistent with a bidder that serves both sides, which is a reason a seller's own view of value per click matters.
- [research] Human reliance on algorithms. People "erroneously avoid algorithms after seeing them err" (Dietvorst, Simmons and Massey, *Algorithm aversion*, Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 2015, doi 10.1037/xge0000033), while in other tasks "people prefer algorithmic to human judgment" (Logg, Minson and Moore, *Algorithm appreciation*, Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes, 2019, doi 10.1016/j.obhdp.2018.12.005). (Crossref records, titles read 2026-11-09). [seat] The two findings bracket why sellers both hand campaigns to tools and turn them off after one bad week.
- [research] Not found on 2026-11-09: a peer-reviewed or working paper that measures the effect of Amazon's own dynamic bidding, rule-based bidding, or a third-party Amazon bid tool on seller outcomes. Practitioner tests on Google Ads disagree on manual versus automated bidding (Inflow and Marketing Syrup posts, seen only as search summaries), and are not cited as evidence here. An Osmos survey (search summary only) reports that more than four in five advertisers use automated bidding, an adoption figure, not an effect.
- [research] Paid search effects in general (Blake, Nosko and Tadelis, *Consumer Heterogeneity and Paid Search Effectiveness: A Large-Scale Field Experiment*, Econometrica, 2015, doi 10.3982/ECTA12423) are `areas/ads-organic-and-total.md`'s subject; listed here only because automation that maximises attributed sales inherits that paper's caution about incrementality.

1. Amazon runs several automations on an advertiser's campaigns, each with its own switch: dynamic bidding strategies, rule-based bidding, schedule and optimization rules, budget rules, portfolio unspent-budget sharing, automatic targeting, keyword groups, Sponsored Brands theme targeting and optimization rules, the v1 flags `autoCreateTargets`, `autoManageCampaign`, automatic budget allocation and automatic global scaling, auto-assembled videos, headline optimization and prompt ads. 2. Under "up and down" Amazon's current help, library guide and API models say it moves a bid by up to 100% in either direction for all placements, while Amazon's author's guide still says up to 100% for top of the first page and up to 50% elsewhere (the owner is `areas/bids-and-placements.md`, P-8); under "down only" it never raises it; under "fixed" it does not move it. 4. (*Sponsored Products rule-based bidding overview*; *Getting started with the Sponsored Products rule-based bidding API*; *Bidding rules for Sponsored Products*)
5. Budget rules only raise a daily budget, are evaluated at the start of the day and on change, and add up when several apply. (*Sponsored ads budget rules overview*; *Budget rule evaluation*; *Performance-based rules*; *Schedule-based rules*)
6. With unspent-budget sharing enabled, a campaign in a portfolio can spend up to twice its daily budget from its portfolio's unspent budget, within the monthly portfolio budget. (release notes, 2025-07-19)
7. Amazon's recommendations are estimates refreshed about daily, and applying one is the ordinary write to the object it names. (SP 3.0 OpenAPI, Consolidated Recommendations and budget recommendation descriptions; *Recommendations API overview*; *How to call the API*; *Partner Opportunities - Campaign Optimization*)
8. The cross-product Recommendations API returns types the reader may not know, and a reader is told to tolerate them. (*Recommendations API overview*; *Recommendation types*)

10. Auto-assembled videos, assembled by Amazon from existing uploaded video clips, are on for every US Sponsored Products campaign unless the advertiser opts out, and the opt-out does not pause videos already delivering. (*Auto-assembled videos*)
11. Amazon Ads Agent is the name, from 2026-09-29, of Amazon's unified ads platform (sponsored ads, Display, Video, and Audio, Full-Funnel Campaigns); its conversational experience, a closed beta, proposes changes and applies them after the advertiser approves, and has no public API; the help centre's "formerly known as Amazon DSP" is the same platform name written into DSP-specific passages. ## What cx must read at run time

The figures and states a skill must fetch, not remember:

- Each campaign's bidding strategy, placement adjustments, `goalSettings` and automation flags (`autoCreateTargets`, `autoManageCampaign`, `budgetAllocation`, `marketplaceBudgetAllocation`, global scaling), and `optOuts`: `POST /adsApi/v1/query/campaigns` or the v3 list calls.
- Rules attached to each campaign and their status: `POST /sp/rules/optimization/search` (with a campaign filter; there is no `GET` on `/sp/campaigns/{campaignId}/optimizationRules`), `POST /sp/rules/campaignOptimization/state`, `GET /{sp|sb|sd}/campaigns/{campaignId}/budgetRules`, `POST /sb/rules/optimization/list`, and `POST /sp/rules/campaignOptimization/eligibility` with its suggested guardrail.
- Portfolio `budgetControls` and unspent-budget sharing.
- Recommendations as of today: `GET` or `POST /sp/campaign/recommendations` (the two answer different media types and shapes), budget recommendations, theme-based bid recommendations, target promotion recommendations, `POST /adsApi/v1/query/recommendations` in discovery mode with its live type list (and `GET /adsApi/v1/recommendations`, the `ListRecommendation` path in the v1 model, which the guide writes `/adsApi/v1/list/recommendations`).
- Budget usage: `POST /sp|sb|sd/campaigns/budget/usage`.
- Event ids and dates for schedule rules: `POST /sp/v1/events` and budget-rule recommended events.
- Marketplace availability lists (intraday budget rules, keyword groups, auto-assembled videos, Video Generator, Ads Agent countries), minimum daily budgets for rule-based bidding, rule count limits (20 schedule bid rules, 100 rules per campaign, 250 budget rules, 25 ids per association, 300 rules per create), Stream limits, Reporting v1's concurrency (whose scope, per advertiser or per client id, Amazon's pages state both ways; a `429` or queued answer is the live reading): all from the live pages or the live error, since each is dated *(for human readers only)* above.
- What changed on a campaign and when: the change history API (`POST /history`, `Changehistory_prod_3p.json`, see `method/ads-api-surface.md`; `THEME` events need `Accept: application/vnd.historyresponse.v1.1+json`). It covers Sponsored Products and Sponsored Brands, not Sponsored Display, and "doesn't return information about who made the change"; who changed it (seller, tool, Amazon) is read from the automation switches above, not from this API.
- `optOuts` and the `videoType` of each SP ad extension (`POST /adsApi/v1/query/adExtensions`), to see which auto-assembled videos are delivering.
- The MCP specification and skills library, if cx is to compare with or borrow Amazon's skills: their content changes release to release.
- Whether auto-optimization or a Seller Assistant draft is behind a campaign: no Ads API field found on 2026-11-09. What can be read is its result: targets on the campaign (`POST /adsApi/v1/query/targets`, or the v3 keyword and target lists), their states, the campaign's `autoCreationSettings`, and the change history (`POST /history`), which does not name who made a change. Whether the switch is visible in the API after the January 2027 US release is read from the reference and the release notes then.
- The current wording of Amazon's own figures and their footnotes, if one is quoted to a seller: from the page itself, since footnotes change with each beta (Ads Agent product page, *Grow total sales with Full-Funnel Campaigns*, the small-business tools page).
- Deprecation dates (v3 reporting and Stream v1 on 2027-06-30; SB benchmark APIs 2026-12-17; SB v2 impression share returning NULL from 2026-11-10): from *Deprecations* at run time.

## Positions

**P-automation-and-amazon-tools-1.** Amazon changes a seller's bids, budgets, targets and creative through switches on the campaign, portfolio or ad extension (and, for Alexa for Shopping, Alexa+ and prompts, through automatic eligibility with no documented campaign switch), so an account's results are not explained by the seller's own edits alone. Sources: [official] SP 3.0 OpenAPI; Amazon Ads API v1 merged OpenAPI; *Sponsored Products auto targeting*; *Auto-assembled videos*; *Managing Sponsored Brands optimization rules*; release notes 2025-07-19. Tags: bidding, budgets, targeting, creative, diagnosis.

**P-automation-and-amazon-tools-2.** Under "dynamic bids - up and down" Amazon's current help, library guide and API models say Amazon may raise or lower a bid by up to 100% for all placements ("Ensure you are prepared to potentially spend double"), while Amazon's author's guide still says up to 100% for top of the first page and up to 50% for other placements; `areas/bids-and-placements.md` P-8 owns this fact. Sources: [official] *Bidding strategies for Sponsored Products*; *Guide to dynamic bidding - up and down with Sponsored Products*; SP 3.0 OpenAPI `BiddingStrategy`; v1 merged OpenAPI `BidStrategy`; *An author's guide to Sponsored Products*. Tags: bidding, placements.

**P-automation-and-amazon-tools-3.** Sponsored Products schedule and performance optimization rules and sponsored ads budget rules can only add a percentage to a bid or a budget, while rule-based bidding and Sponsored Brands optimization rules let Amazon lower bids as well as raise them; no Amazon rule pauses a target, and a decrease or pause the seller chooses is a direct write (Amazon's announced auto-optimization, section L, is not a rule and does pause keywords). Sources: [official] SP 3.0 OpenAPI optimization-rule schemas; *Schedule Bid Rules*; *Sponsored ads budget rules overview*; *Budget rule evaluation*; *Bidding rules for Sponsored Products*; *Managing Sponsored Brands optimization rules*. Tags: rules, bidding, budgets.

**P-automation-and-amazon-tools-4.** Rule-based bidding holds a campaign-level ROAS guardrail that Amazon does not guarantee, may raise a bid up to 5 times the adjusted bid, puts any schedule bid rules on hold, and is switched off by Amazon, restoring the earlier strategy and bids, when the campaign's recent ROAS collapses against its earlier level. Sources: [official] *Sponsored Products rule-based bidding overview*; *Getting started with the Sponsored Products rule-based bidding API*; *Bidding rules for Sponsored Products*. Tags: bidding, rules.

**P-automation-and-amazon-tools-5.** When several schedule bid rules or several budget rules hold at once, Amazon adds their increases together, and schedule bid rules sit on top of placement adjustments and the bidding strategy. Sources: [official] *Schedule Bid Rules*; *Budget rule evaluation*. Tags: rules, bidding, budgets, seasons.

**P-automation-and-amazon-tools-6.** With unspent-budget sharing on, a campaign in a portfolio can spend beyond its own daily budget, up to twice it, from budget its sibling campaigns left unspent earlier in the month. Sources: [official] Ads API release notes, 2025-07-19. Tags: budgets, portfolios.

**P-automation-and-amazon-tools-7.** Amazon's bid, budget and targeting recommendations are dated estimates refreshed about daily, and applying one is the same write to the named campaign, keyword or placement that any other change uses. Sources: [official] SP 3.0 OpenAPI Consolidated Recommendations and budget recommendation descriptions; *Recommendations API overview*; *Partner Opportunities - Campaign Optimization*. Tags: recommendations.

**P-automation-and-amazon-tools-8.** Amazon's cross-product Recommendations API serves the types its *Recommendation types* page lists (four on 2026-11-09, among them campaigns paused by the system and a free-trial expansion of Sponsored Products campaigns into other countries), Amazon adds types over time, and its guide tells readers to tolerate `recommendationType` values they do not recognise. Sources: [official] *Recommendation types*; *How to call the API*; *Recommendations API overview*. Tags: recommendations, global, diagnosis.

**P-automation-and-amazon-tools-9.** Amazon runs its own harvesting: target promotion groups move converting search terms and ASINs from an auto ad group into linked manual keyword and product ad groups, on Amazon's reading of their historical performance. Sources: [official] *Target Promotion*; SP 3.0 OpenAPI `TargetPromotionGroups`; *Partner Opportunities - Campaign Optimization*. Tags: harvesting, search terms, campaign structure.

**P-automation-and-amazon-tools-10.** Several Amazon targeting modes choose and refresh targets themselves: automatic targeting, keyword groups, Sponsored Brands theme targeting, Sponsored Brands cost-control rules that may add targets, and the v1 `autoCreateTargets` flag. Sources: [official] *Sponsored Products auto targeting*; *Sponsored Product keyword groups targeting*; *Sponsored Brands theme-based targeting*; *Managing Sponsored Brands optimization rules*; v1 merged OpenAPI `AutoCreationSettings`. Tags: targeting.

**P-automation-and-amazon-tools-11.** In the US every Sponsored Products campaign is opted in to auto-assembled videos, which Amazon assembles from existing uploaded video clips, unless the seller opts out on the campaign, and the opt-out leaves videos already delivering running until they are paused through the ad extension. Sources: [official] *Auto-assembled videos*; release notes 2026-08-09. Tags: creative, Sponsored Products.

**P-automation-and-amazon-tools-12.** In May 2026 Amazon brought Rufus and Alexa+ together as Alexa for Shopping on the US Amazon Shopping app, website and Echo Show, while Alexa+ continues on Alexa devices and Alexa.com; existing sponsored ads campaigns are automatically eligible to serve in Alexa for Shopping and to be optimized as Sponsored Products and Sponsored Brands prompts, and are automatically enrolled in Alexa+ Conversational Ads on Alexa-enabled devices; no campaign switch for these surfaces is documented, and individual prompts can be paused in the console. Sources: [official] *Meet Alexa for Shopping, your personalized, agentic AI assistant on Amazon* (2026-05-13); *What agentic shopping means for advertising* (2026-06-11); *Prompt Ad Extension reports*; release notes 2024-09-05 and 2025-07-15. Tags: placements, reporting, creative.

**P-automation-and-amazon-tools-13.** Amazon Ads Agent is, from 2026-09-29, "the new name of the unified platform" that holds sponsored ads, Display, Video, and Audio, and Full-Funnel Campaigns; inside it, the conversational experience is a closed beta that proposes keyword, bid and budget changes and applies them after the advertiser approves, with no public API; the help centre's "Amazon Ads Agent (formerly known as Amazon DSP)" is the same platform name written into passages about DSP settings and accounts. Sources: [official] *Introducing Amazon Ads Agent*; *Ads Agent: An AI marketing assistant from Amazon Ads*; *The 11 biggest takeaways from unBoxed 2026*; *Understand delivery alerts and recommendations* (help GR6VEAG2EQWG56RD); *Understand Advertiser Accounts* (help GXKXR56ZCMJ3WCS4). Tags: Ads Agent, recommendations.

**P-automation-and-amazon-tools-14.** Full-Funnel Campaigns and DVA+ take a goal, products, creative and budget and leave planning, targeting and optimisation to Amazon, with advanced settings for advertisers who keep control. Sources: [official] *Introducing Amazon Ads Agent*; *The 11 biggest takeaways from unBoxed 2026*. Tags: ad products, Ads Agent.

**P-automation-and-amazon-tools-15.** Amazon's creative tools (Creative Agent, Video Generator, one-click image generation) produce ad creative from the seller's product pages and assets at no added cost, with availability set by country, and Sponsored Brands headline optimization, with the advertiser's consent, tests headlines and serves the best performer. Sources: [official] *Transform campaign and asset development with Creative Agent*; *Amazon Ads launches new agentic AI tool that creates professional-quality ads*; *Video Generator is now available for Sponsored Brands video campaigns in nine marketplaces*; *AI creative solutions*; release notes 2025-04-17. Tags: creative.

**P-automation-and-amazon-tools-16.** The Amazon Ads MCP server is a host separate from the Ads API, with deterministic tools over the same campaign, report and account objects, and Amazon's published MCP skills cover reporting, accounts, eligibility, AMC and DSP campaign creation, not Sponsored Products bid or budget optimisation. Sources: [official] *Overview* (Amazon Ads MCP Server); *Connecting to the Amazon Ads MCP Server*; MCP tool specifications; MCP skills library pages. Tags: MCP, skills.

**P-automation-and-amazon-tools-17.** Marketing Stream v2 delivers hourly sponsored-ads performance to an S3 bucket and SQS queue the advertiser owns, and Stream v1's recommendation and messaging datasets end on 2027-06-30 without a v2 equivalent.
