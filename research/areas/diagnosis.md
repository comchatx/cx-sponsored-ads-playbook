## What this covers

This file covers how a change in an advertiser's spend or attributed sales can be traced to its source, using what Amazon documents and what named practitioners and researchers add. It covers:

- the two identities that split a movement into parts: spend = impressions × click-through rate × cost per click, and attributed sales = clicks × conversion rate × average order value; and Amazon's own definitions of each part, including the definitions that trap a reader;
- the documented causes that stop or change serving: the Featured Offer gate, out of stock, suppressed or ineligible listings, moderation, budget caps at campaign, portfolio and account level, payment failure, account eligibility, end dates, pausing and archiving; with the serving status and delivery reason values that the Sponsored Products v3 API and the Amazon Ads API v1 return;
- data that moves after the fact: reporting latency, invalid-traffic removal, conversion restatement, null values, report warnings and incidents on the Amazon Ads status page;
- time zones and currencies as sources of apparent movement;
- Amazon changes dated 2025–2026 that move spend or sales without any change by the advertiser: new placements (prompts, off-Amazon and creator placements, Alexa+, Rufus, Amazon Business), bid and budget mechanics, deprecations of recommendation endpoints, and the reserve-price mechanics Amazon described in its response to the FTC lawsuit of 2026-08-31;
- competitor entry and cost-per-click inflation, as Amazon's impression share and benchmark metrics expose them;
- demand and seasonality as Brand Analytics Search Query Performance exposes them, and the offer-side data in the Sales and Traffic report;
- price as a cause of an impression drop: the competitive external price, Pricing Health and the Featured Offer price rules, the 2026 removal of seller eligibility, and the Product Pricing reads that expose them;
- conversion-side causes on the listing and the offer: coupons and deals ending, price changes, ratings and review counts (including the 2026 review-sharing change across variations), main images, variation merges and splits, browse-node changes and stock, each with its SP-API or Keepa read;
- which Amazon surfaces are push-only (SP-API notifications, Marketing Stream) and the polling reads that stand in for them;
- the change history API and its limits;
- differences between console figures and API figures;
- named practitioners' diagnostic sequences, stated as theirs;
- research on multiplicative decomposition, root-cause attribution in advertising systems (including Amazon's own, public in DoWhy), tests of whether a movement exceeds noise (control charts for counts, CUSUM, EWMA, changepoints, seasonal anomaly detection), the noise floor of advertising measurement, and reserve prices.

### A. The parts of spend and sales, as Amazon defines them

RULE [official]. In unified reporting (the Amazon Ads API v1 reporting surface; "Metrics library", https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/reporting/ads-v1/metrics/library, is an index, and the definitions below are on its metric family pages "Clicks (Delivery)", "Impressions (Delivery)", "Costs" and "Purchases", all read 2026-11-09 through their markdown sources on the docs host):

- `metric.impressions` is "The number of times the ad was displayed. This excludes impressions that were deemed invalid by traffic quality filters. Impressions = gross impressions - invalid impressions". The 12-field Impressions family carries `metric.invalidImpressions` and `metric.invalidImpressionRate` but no gross-impressions field; gross impressions are the v3 column `grossImpressions` (section K).
- `metric.clicks` is gross clicks minus invalid clicks. The family carries `metric.grossClicks`, `metric.invalidClicks` and `metric.invalidClickRate`.
- `metric.ctr` is clicks divided by impressions.
- `metric.cpc` is cost divided by clicks. `metric.cpcConverted` is the same figure in the converted currency.
- `metric.totalCost` is the field whose v3 name is `spend` (v3 column `cost` for most report types).
- `metric.purchases`, `metric.sales` and `metric.unitsSold` are the attributed conversion counts and value. `metric.sales` requires `budgetCurrency.value`.
- `metric.purchaseRate` is purchases divided by **impressions**. The per-click rate is a separate field, `metric.purchaseRateOverClicks` (purchases from clicks divided by clicks), whose v3 name is "14 Day Conversion Rate".
- `metric.salesPromoted` and `metric.salesHalo` split attributed sales between the advertised product and other products (the `productRelevance.value` dimension carries the same split as promoted or halo).
- `metric.roas` is sales divided by cost.

RULE [official]. "Understand unified reporting" (Amazon Ads help, https://advertising.amazon.com/help/GMH8A8AJSH4ATV6T, read 2026-11-09) states: "While underlying measurement logic remains unchanged, metrics may appear under different names than in previous reports." It gives one example of a definition that widened: "Detail page views now includes promoted and halo conversions, while Detail page views (promoted) captures promoted only." It also says: "Conversion metrics now report conversions on traffic date and reflect the lookback window applicable to each campaign type." It states a shutdown date for console reports: "We are shutting down sponsored ads reports and Display, Video, and Audio campaigns reports by December 31, 2026."

RULE [official]. The v1 reporting overview ("Reporting overview" for the new reporting API, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/reporting/ads-v1/overview, read 2026-11-09) states that Sponsored Products base conversion metrics use a 7-day window for sellers and a 14-day window for vendors, and that other ad products use 14 days. It states that Sponsored Products and Sponsored Brands cost-per-click campaigns "will continue to report 0 for conversions from views". It defines reporting on the **traffic date**: a conversion is reported on the date of the ad interaction it is attributed to, not on the date of the purchase.

RULE [official]. "Placement report for Sponsored Products" (Amazon Ads help, https://advertising.amazon.com/help/G89VFUTQUWFFN2VU, read 2026-11-09) defines Clicks as "The number of times the ad was clicked, excluding general and sophisticated invalid traffic", Impressions likewise, Cost as "Cost is assessed per click or per 1,000 views depending on cost type", and CPC as "dividing total Cost ... by the total number of clicks". For sellers, Purchases and Sales are "within 7 days from the date that your ad was clicked"; for vendors, 14 days.

RULE [official]. Sponsored Display campaigns carry a `costType` of `cpc` or `vcpm`: "vcpm | The performance of this campaign is measured by the viewed impressions triggered by the ad. $1 is the minimum bid for vCPM." (Sponsored Display OpenAPI, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/sponsored-display/3-0/openapi, read 2026-11-09). On a vCPM campaign, spend is not clicks × CPC.

[seat] On these definitions, the two identities hold exactly for cost-per-click campaigns, row by row:

- spend = impressions × (clicks / impressions) × (cost / clicks) = impressions × CTR × CPC;
- attributed sales = clicks × (purchases / clicks) × (sales / purchases) = clicks × CVR × AOV, where CVR is `metric.purchaseRateOverClicks` and AOV is sales per attributed purchase.

They do not hold where a row has zero clicks or zero purchases (the ratio is undefined), where conversions from views are included, or where the purchase rate read is `metric.purchaseRate` (per impression). A halo sale is part of `metric.sales`, so CVR × AOV on the advertised product alone differs from total attributed sales. The sum of row-level products is not the product of account-level ratios: a change in the mix of campaigns, placements or search terms moves account CTR, CPC and CVR even when no row's ratio moves.

### B. The Featured Offer and stock gate

RULE [official]. Amazon Ads "FAQs" (https://advertising.amazon.com/resources/faq, read 2026-11-09): "If you create an ad for a product listing that is not eligible for the Featured Offer, your ad will not display ... Ads that are not eligible are flagged in the campaign manager." And: "If your product isn't the Featured Offer or is out of stock, your ad will not display."

RULE [official]. An Amazon staff reply ("Manny_Amazon") in the Seller Forums thread "Sponsored Products and Buy Box" (https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/b55bc259-e982-43bb-a660-58ad413c95ea, public page, read 2026-11-09; the reply's timestamp is 2026-02-13): "Amazon will only display your Sponsored Products ads when your product is the Featured Offer at the time the customer views the ad." The same reply: "the advertising itself doesn't directly influence Featured Offer eligibility." [seat] On that wording the gate is evaluated at view time, so a product that holds the Featured Offer part of the day can serve part of the day.

RULE [official]. "How to improve your product detail page for advertising" (Amazon Ads library guide, https://advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/improve-your-products-for-advertising, read 2026-11-09): "Your products need to be in-stock in order to be the featured offer and be eligible for inclusion in a Sponsored Products campaign. If a product you are advertising goes out of stock, it will be automatically paused within your campaign."

RULE [official]. "Best practices for your Sponsored Products ads" (Amazon Ads library guide, https://advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/sponsored-products-best-practices, read 2026-11-09): "Sponsored Products only appear when your advertised items are in stock". It defines the Featured Offer as "an automated window that appears at the top right of a product detail page above the Buy Now button."

GUIDANCE [official]. The same two guides advise competitive pricing, Prime eligibility and positive reviews as factors in winning the Featured Offer and in ad performance.

RULE [official]. The offer side is measurable outside the ads API. The Selling Partner API "Sales and Traffic" report schema (amzn/selling-partner-api-models, `schemas/reports/sellerSalesAndTrafficReport.json`, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/main/schemas/reports/sellerSalesAndTrafficReport.json, read 2026-11-09) carries `buyBoxPercentage`, "The percentage of page views where the buy box (the add to shopping cart link) appeared on the page for customers to add your product to their cart", together with `sessions`, `pageViews`, `unitSessionPercentage`, `orderedProductSales`, `unitsOrdered`, `averageSellingPrice` and `averageOfferCount`, at DAY, WEEK or MONTH granularity and PARENT, CHILD or SKU ASIN granularity, for one marketplace per request, with a start date no more than two years back. Its `refundRate` description reads "calculated by dividing unitsOrdered by unitsRefunded", which is the inverse of a refund rate as the field name implies.

### E. Product eligibility, as the Eligibility API reports it

RULE [official]. The Eligibility OpenAPI (https://d1y2lf8k3vrkfu.cloudfront.net/openapi/en-us/dest/Eligibility_prod_3p.json, read 2026-11-09) defines `POST /eligibility/product/list`. Its description: "Requests are permitted only for products sold by the merchant associated with the profile." A response can return several SKUs for one ASIN. Each result carries an `EligibilityStatus` with a `name` and a `severity`:

- `name` values: ADULT_PRODUCT, CLOSED_CATEGORY, INELIGIBLE_CONDITION, INELIGIBLE_OFFER, INELIGIBLE_PRODUCT_COST, LISTING_SUPRESSED (spelled so in the model), MISSING_IMAGE, MISSING_TITLE, NOT_IN_BUYBOX, OUT_OF_STOCK, RESTRICTED_CATEGORY, VARIATION_PARENT;
- `severity` values: INELIGIBLE ("cannot be included in an advertisement") and ELIGIBLE_WITH_WARNING ("may not receive impressions").

[seat] ELIGIBLE_WITH_WARNING is the documented state for a product that is in a campaign and allowed, but may draw no impressions. A NOT_IN_BUYBOX or OUT_OF_STOCK warning on an ASIN that was serving is a documented mechanism for a fall in impressions without any campaign change. The product-level detail is in `areas/launch.md` section C; this file uses it as one layer of a diagnosis.

RULE [official]. The same model defines `POST /eligibility/programs` (header `Amazon-Ads-AccountId`), which answers whether the account may run an ad program. Its reason codes include ACCOUNT_SUSPENDED, ADS_TERMS_NOT_ACCEPTED, BILLING_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND, EXPIRED_PAYMENT_METHOD, NOT_BRAND_REPRESENTATIVE, PAYMENT_METHOD_NOT_FOUND, PAYMENT_METHOD_NOT_VALID, PREPAY_BALANCE_TOO_LOW, TAX_INFO_NOT_COMPLETE and VETTING_FAILURE, at the levels INELIGIBLE and INELIGIBLE_WITH_RESOLUTION.

RULE [official]. Listing state on the selling side is a separate system. The Listings Items API model 2021-08-01 (`models/listings-items-api-model/listingsItems_2021-08-01.json`, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/amzn/selling-partner-api-models/main/models/listings-items-api-model/listingsItems_2021-08-01.json, read 2026-11-09) gives a listing summary `status` of BUYABLE and/or DISCOVERABLE, and an `Issue` object with `code`, `message`, `severity` (ERROR, WARNING, INFO), `categories` and `enforcements.actions`, whose actions are `LISTING_SUPPRESSED` ("takes down the current listing item's buyability"), `ATTRIBUTE_SUPPRESSED`, `CATALOG_ITEM_REMOVED` ("all offers against it in the applicable Amazon store are non-buyable") and `SEARCH_SUPPRESSED` ("the catalog item is hidden from search results"). The Selling Partner API notification schemas in the same repository include `ListingsItemStatusChangeNotification`, `ListingsItemIssuesChangeNotification`, `AnyOfferChangedNotification`, `FBAInventoryAvailabilityChangeNotification` and `PricingHealthNotification`, each a pushed event for one of these layers, delivered only to an Amazon SQS queue or an Amazon EventBridge destination (section U).

### F. Moderation

RULE [official]. The Moderation OpenAPI (https://d1y2lf8k3vrkfu.cloudfront.net/openapi/en-us/dest/Moderation_prod_3p.json, read 2026-11-09) defines `POST /moderation/results` (content type `application/vnd.moderationresultsrequest.v4.1+json`, permission `advertiser_campaign_view`, `maxResults` up to 10) for the ad programs SB_PRODUCT_COLLECTION, SB_STORE_SPOTLIGHT, SB_VIDEO, SPONSORED_DISPLAY and SPONSORED_PRODUCTS.

- `moderationStatus` values: APPROVED, FAILED, IN_PROGRESS, REJECTED.
- When IN_PROGRESS, `etaForModeration` gives an expected completion time in UTC.
- When REJECTED, `policyViolations` lists `policyDescription`, `policyLinkUrl` and the violating text, image, video or ASIN contents, with evidence positions.

### J. Change history

RULE [official]. The Change history OpenAPI (https://d1y2lf8k3vrkfu.cloudfront.net/openapi/en-us/dest/Changehistory_prod_3p.json, read 2026-11-09) defines `POST /history`. Its model text: "This API only returns change history for Sponsored Products and Sponsored Brands campaigns. Changes for Sponsored Display campaigns are not returned. Also, it doesn't return information about who made the change."

- Event types: AD, AD_GROUP, CAMPAIGN, KEYWORD, NEGATIVE_KEYWORD, PRODUCT_TARGETING, THEME. THEME events need the Accept type `application/vnd.historyresponse.v1.1+json`.
- Request filters: BUDGET_AMOUNT, IN_BUDGET, END_DATE, START_DATE, PLACEMENT_GROUP and SMART_BIDDING_STRATEGY on campaigns; BID_AMOUNT on ad groups; NAME on campaigns and ad groups; STATUS on campaigns, ad groups, ads, keywords, product targets and negative keywords.
- Up to 10 event type IDs and 10 parents per event type per request.
- `fromDate`: "Max 90 days of history". `fromDate`, `toDate` and each event's `timestamp` are Unix times (the model's example uses milliseconds, such as `1704195640000`); the model describes them as UTC.
- Each returned event has `entityType`, `entityId`, `changeType`, `previousValue`, `newValue`, `timestamp` and `metadata`. The model's table of change types: on CAMPAIGN, STATUS, IN_BUDGET, PORTFOLIO, BUDGET_AMOUNT, NAME, START_DATE, END_DATE, SMART_BIDDING_STRATEGY, PLACEMENT_GROUP, CREATED, IS_PREMIUM_BID_ENABLED, PORTFOLIO_ID, BUDGET_DOCUMENT_VERSION, IS_PROCESSING, RULE_BASED_BUDGET_VALUE, APPLICABLE_RULE_NAME, APPLICABLE_RULE_ID, CREATION_DATE and AUTO_TARGETED; on AD_GROUP, STATUS, NAME and DEFAULT_BID_AMOUNT; on AD and NEGATIVE_KEYWORD, STATUS; on KEYWORD and PRODUCT_TARGETING, STATUS and BID_AMOUNT.
- Values: STATUS moves among CREATED, ENABLED, PAUSED and ARCHIVED; IN_BUDGET is "true or false"; amounts are "in marketplace currency"; SMART_BIDDING_STRATEGY is OPTIMIZE_FOR_SALES, LEGACY or MANUAL; PLACEMENT_GROUP is a "number as percent", with `metadata.placementGroupPosition` of DETAIL_PAGE or TOP_OF_SEARCH. `metadata` also carries `campaignBudgetType` (DAILY or LIFETIME_BUDGET), the keyword text and match type, and the product-targeting expression.
- `count` defaults to 100, minimum 50, maximum 200. `pageOffset` is capped at 10,000 and cannot be combined with `nextToken`.

[opinion] The python-amazon-ad-api documentation for this operation ("Change History", https://python-amazon-ad-api.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api/history.html, read 2026-11-09) maps `POST /history` to a `get_history` wrapper at media types `vnd.historyresponse.v1.0` and `v1.1`, and notes that THEME is the only event type that needs 1.1 (at 1.0 the service answers 400).

[opinion] Luis Rijo, PPC Land, "Amazon rolls out a new history feature for Sponsored Products and Sponsored Brands campaigns" (2020-01-20, https://ppc.land/amazon-rolls-out-a-new-history-feature-for-sponsored-products-and-sponsored-brands-campaigns/, read 2026-11-09): the console History tab shows "a summary of all changes made to campaign settings in the past 90 days", at campaign level and in the Sponsored Products ad group view.

[seat] Three limits shape what the change history can explain. It holds no Sponsored Display changes. It does not say who changed something, so it does not separate a seller's edit from an agency's, a rule's or a tool's. And it holds only changes to the advertiser's own entities: none of the external causes in sections B, K, N and O appear in it. IN_BUDGET is a campaign change type with the values true and false, which puts budget-state transitions (a campaign running out of budget and coming back) into the same feed as edits. The placement-adjustment change type names top of search and detail page positions only; this survey found no rest-of-search position in that table, although rest-of-search adjustments exist (see `areas/bids-and-placements.md`).

[seat] A day in a report is a day in the campaign's time zone, a budget resets at that time zone's midnight, and a change-history timestamp is UTC. An account that spans marketplaces sums days that begin at different instants and figures in different currencies; a converted total moves with the exchange rate even when nothing in any marketplace moves.

### O. Auction price: reserves, relevance and the FTC case

RULE [official]. "Amazon's response to the FTC's lawsuit regarding Sponsored Ads" (aboutamazon.com, read 2026-11-09 through its markdown copy at https://www.aboutamazon.com/md/company-news/amazon-ftc-sponsored-ads-lawsuit-response; the page is dated to the day the FTC filed, 2026-08-31). These are Amazon's statements in a contested case:

- Amazon began to test "soft reserve prices," "a real-time minimum value that seeks to better reflect what each placement is actually worth", and a "hard reserve," "the minimum a bid must surpass to enter an auction".
- "When the winning advertiser's bid exceeds both the hard and soft reserve, they pay the soft reserve, which is less than they were willing to pay. When the winning advertiser's bid exceeds the hard reserve but doesn't meet the soft reserve, we still grant the placement to that advertiser and they pay their bid. In no scenario does an advertiser pay more than their bid."
- "reserves are determined in real time and aren't predictable in advance by anyone, including Amazon or the advertiser."
- The ranking "best balances relevancy and bid value": "in 2024, approximately 92% of selected Sponsored Products ads were not the highest bid"; "The mean winning advertiser's bid is typically about the 128th bid by amount."
- "From 2019 through 2024, the average cost-per-click for Amazon's Sponsored Products search ads remained flat adjusted for inflation, while conversion rates grew 24% from 2021 to 2025." "Average winning bids fell 50% from 2019 to 2025 on Sponsored Products search ads".
- "from 2019 to 2024, eighty percent of bid changes on clicked Sponsored Products search ads occurred within one day of a prior change."
- "in one study, when one advertiser stopped bidding on specific keywords, average bids for those keywords from competitors dropped about 83%, from $15.96 to $2.74."
- Amazon says it "updated our Amazon Ads Help content to explicitly explain the use of reserve prices." This seat did not locate that help text (see *Open questions*).

[opinion] The Federal Trade Commission and 22 states, "FTC, States Sue Amazon Over Secret Ad Surcharge Scheme" (press release dated 2026-08-31, https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/08/ftc-states-sue-amazon-over-secret-ad-surcharge-scheme, read 2026-11-09 through a fetch tool; a plain curl GET returned 404): the complaint, filed in the U.S. District Court for the Western District of Washington, alleges that "beginning in 2019" Amazon added an undisclosed surcharge it called a "soft reserve price", and that the share of the time Sponsored Products advertisers paid "the amount of their bid" rose from "between 30% and 40%" (2021) to "70%" (2022) and "approximately 80%" (2024); the auctions named are "Sponsored Product ads, Sponsored Brands ads and Display Ads". These are allegations. Search-result summaries of press coverage (not opened) say the complaint dates a Sponsored Brands soft reserve to 2018 and that Florida joined the suit in September 2026.

[seat] On Amazon's own description, the price paid per click is the larger of the second-price outcome and a soft reserve that Amazon sets in real time, capped at the bid. A change in reserves moves CPC with no change in the advertiser's bids and no change in competitors' bids, and nothing in the reporting surfaces read here separates a reserve-driven price from a competition-driven one. Amazon's 83% example shows the reverse channel: competitors' bids respond within days to one advertiser's exit.

### S. Price as a cause: Pricing Health, the competitive external price and the 2026 eligibility change

Section B establishes that a Sponsored Products ad serves only when the advertised product is the Featured Offer. This section covers the price rules that can withhold the Featured Offer from an offer that is in stock and enabled, which makes price a documented cause of an impression drop, and not only of a conversion drop.

RULE [official]. The withholding rule, in Amazon's developer documentation ("Product Pricing API and Notifications FAQ", https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/pricing-faq.md, page `updatedAt` 2026-09-09, read 2026-11-09; the docs host `developer-docs.amazon.com` now redirects to `developer-docs.amazon`):

- On a Pricing Health notification: "You receive a notification when your offer becomes ineligible to be a Featured Offer on the product detail page. This happens when your total price (price + shipping) is higher than the Competitive External Price, if available, or when your total price is higher than recent prices."
- "The `PRICING_HEALTH` notification ... doesn't mean that the offer has lost placement as the Featured Offer because of competitors. It means that Amazon cannot select the offer as the Featured Offer at the current price, regardless of changes to price or stock of competitors."
- "The Competitive Price Threshold (`competitivePriceThreshold`) is based on competitive prices from other retailers (excluding other Amazon sellers). Your offer might be ineligible for the Featured Offer if your total price (price + shipping) is greater than this competitive price."
- "The Competitive External Price is the lowest price for an item, or items that customers might consider being equivalent, recently found at another reputable retailer outside the Amazon store. Retailer names are not disclosed." The page's examples: a per-unit price of a 6-pack "multiply[ied] ... by 12" for a 12-pack, and the price of an equivalent product "with a different brand name" that has "near-identical attributes".
- "The average selling price (`averageSellingPrice`) ... is calculated based on the sum of the prices at which customers have bought a product in the Amazon store in the last 60 days divided by units sold. Prices and quantity sold during promotions (for example, Best Deals, Lightning Deals) are excluded." "The Price by Amazon (`retailOfferPrice`) is the highest price listed for the product in the last 14 days that is shipped and sold by Amazon."
- A `PRICING_HEALTH` notification "May contain `referencePrices` as the `averageSellingPrice`, `msrpPrice`, or `competitivePriceThreshold`. If you set your price to any of these, your offer will regain Featured Offer eligibility."

RULE [official]. "Notification Type Values" (https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/notification-type-values.md, `updatedAt` 2026-10-28, read 2026-11-09), `PRICING_HEALTH`: "sent whenever a seller offer is ineligible to be the Featured Offer (Buy Box offer) because of an uncompetitive price. Sellers can take steps to restore featured offer eligibility by adjusting an offer's total price (price plus shipping minus points) so that it matches or is lower than the competitive price, or is in line with the reference prices provided. These include the Featured Offer price, 60-day average selling price, highest 14-day price (shipped and sold by Amazon), and the list price." The schema `PricingHealthNotification.json` (amzn/selling-partner-api-models, read 2026-11-09) carries `issueType`, `merchantOffer`, `summary` (`numberOfOffers`, `buyBoxEligibleOffers`, `buyBoxPrices`, `salesRankings`) and `referencePrice` with `averageSellingPrice`, `competitivePriceThreshold` ("The competitive price threshold from external competitors of Amazon"), `retailOfferPrice` ("The 14 day maximum of retail offer price") and `msrpPrice`. "Price Adjustment Automation Workflows Guide" (https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/price-adjustment-automation-workflows-guide.md, read 2026-11-09) names the payload value: "the `issueType` is `BuyBoxDisqualification`, which means the selling partner's offer is disqualified from being the Featured Offer."

RULE [official]. The same rule surfaces as a listing issue. "Manage listings issues" (https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/manage-listings-issues.md, `updatedAt` 2026-10-30, read 2026-11-09), "Pricing validation issues", lists issue `100708`, severity `WARNING`, category `INVALID_PRICE`: "Not eligible to be the Featured Offer due to uncompetitive price. Set your price at or below the Competitive External Price to become eligible for the Featured Offer." "`WARNING` severity issues (such as `100708`) do not prevent the listing update. The update is accepted, but the offer may not be eligible for the Featured Offer." The same table lists `19038` (price above the seller's maximum), `19037` (below the minimum) and `18701` ("identified as a potential high pricing error") as `ERROR`, which reject the update. [seat] A price change can therefore be accepted by the listing and still remove the advertised product from the Featured Offer, with nothing in the Ads API's change history.

RULE [official]. The reads that expose the rule on demand, in the Product Pricing v2022-05-01 model (`productPricing_2022-05-01.json`, read 2026-11-09):

- `getFeaturedOfferExpectedPriceBatch` (`POST /batches/products/pricing/2022-05-01/offer/featuredOfferExpectedPrice`, up to 40 requests by `marketplaceId` and `sku`) returns "the computed listing price at or below which a seller can expect to become the featured offer (before applicable promotions). This is called the featured offer expected price (FOEP). Featured offer is not guaranteed because competing offers might change." Each result has `featuredOfferExpectedPrice.listingPrice`, `currentFeaturedOffer`, `competingFeaturedOffer` and a `resultStatus` of `VALID_FOEP`, `NO_COMPETING_OFFER`, `OFFER_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, `OFFER_NOT_FOUND` or `ASIN_NOT_ELIGIBLE` ("Additional values might be added in the future"). The workflows guide glosses them: `OFFER_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, "Your offer does not qualify for Featured Offer"; `OFFER_NOT_FOUND`, "Your offer might be unbuyable or you are not qualified".
- `getCompetitiveSummary` (`POST /batches/products/pricing/2022-05-01/items/competitiveSummary`) takes `includedData` of `featuredBuyingOptions` ("the current segmented featured offer information"), `referencePrices` ("reference price points for the item, such as the Competitive Price Threshold"), `lowestPricedOffers` and `similarItems`. A reference price `name` is, for example, `CompetitivePriceThreshold`, `WasPrice` or `CompetitivePrice`. Each segmented featured offer carries `featuredOfferSegments` with `customerMembership` (PRIME, NON_PRIME, DEFAULT) and `segmentDetails.glanceViewWeightPercentage` ("the glance views for this segment as a percentage of total glance views across all segments for the ASIN"). The FAQ adds that each offer "can include a `promotions` array with active promotion details (`COUPON`, `DEAL`, or `BASKET_BUILDING`), including the discount and the final discounted price"; the model's `Promotion` has `promotionType` and `promotionSavings.discountedPrice`. The model's default rate for `getCompetitiveSummary` is 0.033 requests per second, burst 1 (for human readers only).
- The v0 model (`productPricingV0.json`, read 2026-11-09) `getItemOffers` and `getListingOffers` summary carry `CompetitivePriceThreshold` ("The offer may be ineligible for the Buy Box if the seller's price + shipping (minus Amazon Points) is greater than this competitive price"), `SuggestedLowerPricePlusShipping` ("based on a range of factors, including historical selling prices, recent Buy Box-eligible prices, and input from customers"), `BuyBoxEligibleOffers`, and per offer `IsBuyBoxWinner` ("There can be up to two Buy Box winners at any time per ASIN, one that is eligible for Prime and one that is not eligible for Prime") and `IsFeaturedMerchant`.

[seat] On these definitions a Featured Offer can be lost three ways that diagnosis has to tell apart: another offer wins (the seller's offer is eligible and `currentFeaturedOffer` names someone else), the offer is ineligible on price (Pricing Health, `100708`, `BuyBoxDisqualification`), or no offer is featured at all (Keepa's -1 Buy Box seller, below). Only the second is fixed by the seller's own price, and the benchmark it is measured against is a price off Amazon that the seller cannot see by name. `glanceViewWeightPercentage` makes the gate partial: a product featured for Prime customers in some postal codes and not others serves its ads to part of the traffic.

### T. Conversion-side causes on the listing and the offer

Spend and sales split (section A) into an impressions part, a CTR part and a CVR × AOV part. The listing and offer facts below move CTR, CVR and AOV with no change in the ads account, and each has its own read outside the Ads API.

RULE [official]. What the ad shows is taken from the listing. "How to improve your product detail page for advertising" (Amazon Ads library, `dateModified` 2026-10-09, re-read 2026-11-09): "In addition to your product title, image, and other information, Sponsored Products ads include the price of your item. You can also add the list price if your price offer is below the list price"; "If your advertised product offers Prime Shipping, the Prime badge will automatically appear in your ad creative"; and GUIDANCE: "we recommend advertising products with five or more customer reviews, plus a rating of 3.5 stars or higher", four or more images, "a plain white background", the product filling "at least 80% of the image area", and images "at least 1000 pixels in height or width" for zoom. "Best practices for your Sponsored Products ads" (`dateModified` 2026-09-28, re-read 2026-11-09) repeats the review and image guidance. For display ads, "Responsive eCommerce creatives" (Amazon Ads ad specs, https://advertising.amazon.com/resources/ad-specs/ecommerce, read 2026-11-09) states that the eCommerce section is "auto-generated by Amazon with live pricing, deal badges, coupons, star ratings, and Prime eligibility pulled from your product listing", that "Active promotions are detected and badged automatically" (Lightning Deals, Deal of the Day, Best Deals, Coupons, Subscribe & Save), and that "If a product has multiple active discounts, the highest value is shown". [seat] So a price rise, a lost Prime badge, a removed coupon or a changed main image changes the ad itself on the day it happens, through CTR as well as through CVR.

RULE [official]. Coupons and deals, and the rating floors that gate them. "Guide to running Amazon seller promotions, coupons, and deals" (Sell on Amazon blog, Mickey Toogood, 2026-05-27, https://sell.amazon.com/blog/seller-promotions, read 2026-11-09): "A Lightning Deal can run from 4 to 12 hours, as determined by Amazon. A Best Deal can run for a select number of days"; "You'll need a Professional selling plan and an overall rating of at least four stars to create deals"; coupons "appear on product detail pages" and "through search results, on the Amazon Deals page, and on products they've added to their carts"; "Coupons are only available for a select amount of time"; "To be eligible for coupons, you must have a Professional selling plan and an overall rating of at least 3.5 stars." These figures are for human readers only. [seat] A rating that falls through a floor can end a promotion's eligibility, so a rating drop can arrive as a coupon or deal ending.

RULE [official]. The Promotions API (`promotions_2025-12-01.json`, read 2026-11-09) has three read operations: `searchPromotions` (`GET /promotions/2025-12-01/promotions`, filters `statuses`, `asins`, `skus`, `promotionTypes`, `startDateBefore`/`After`, `endDateBefore`/`After`, `updateDateBefore`/`After`), `getPromotion` and `getSelection` (up to 100 items, `SelectionType.ITEMS` only). `PromotionType` is BASKET_BUILDING, DEAL, PRICE_DISCOUNT or COUPON; `CouponType` STANDARD, SUBSCRIBE_AND_SAVE or REORDER_REWARDS; status PROCESSING, UPCOMING, RUNNING, EXPIRED ("passed its end date"), FAILED, CANCELLING or CANCELLED; `Schedule` has `startDate`, `endDate` and `eventId` ("For event promotions, this field contains the event name when available (for example, `Prime Day`)"); `issues` carry `code`, `message` and `severity` (ERROR, WARNING). The model's default `searchPromotions` rate is 0.1 per second, burst 4 (for human readers only). [opinion] Keepa's `Product.java` (read 2026-11-09) adds `coupon` (one-time and Subscribe & Save coupon, positive for an absolute and negative for a percentage discount), `couponHistory` ("We started tracking coupon history on June 15th 2024"), `deals` and `promotions` ("Only Amazon US promotions by Amazon (not 3rd party) are collected"), which cover competitors' coupons as well as the seller's own.

RULE [official]. Ratings and reviews across variations, 2026. "Changes to review sharing across product variations starting Feb 12" (Seller Forums, News_Amazon, 2026-01-07, https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/a572986d-9bb1-4e91-a53f-41c575874dd7, read 2026-11-09): "reviews will only be shared between variations with minor differences that don't affect functionality. Reviews will no longer appear on variations with significant differences which may affect your products' overall star ratings and review counts." Sharing continues for color or pattern, size with the same function, pack size or quantity, secondary scent on non-scent-focused products, and model fitments; "we'll implement these changes gradually by product category between February 12, 2026, and May 31, 2026." This means ratings can propagate across the variation family". [seat] A variation merge pools ratings and a split, or a family placed under the 2026 rules, un-pools them; either moves the star rating and count the ad displays on a date with no ads change, and the date falls inside February 12 to May 31, 2026 for the category rollout.

RULE [official]. Variation and browse-node structure, read from the catalogue. Catalog Items 2022-04-01 (`catalogItems_2022-04-01.json`, read 2026-11-09) `includedData` takes `attributes`, `classifications`, `dimensions`, `identifiers`, `images`, `productTypes`, `relationships`, `salesRanks`, `summaries` and `vendorDetails`. `relationships` gives `type` VARIATION or PACKAGE_HIERARCHY, `parentAsins`, `childAsins` and `variationTheme.theme` (for example "COLOR_NAME/STYLE_NAME"); `classifications` gives `ItemBrowseClassification` (`classificationId`, `displayName`, `parent`); `images` gives each image's `variant` (MAIN, PT01–PT08, SWCH); `salesRanks` gives ranks by `classificationId` and by website display group. The Customer Feedback API's `getItemBrowseNode` (`GET /customerFeedback/2024-06-01/items/{asin}/browseNode`) "returns the associated browse node of the requested ASIN". The Listings Items model 2021-08-01 summary carries `mainImage` (`link`, `height`, `width`), `status` (BUYABLE, DISCOVERABLE), `createdDate` and `lastUpdatedDate`, and `getListingsItem` takes `includedData` of `summaries`, `attributes`, `issues`, `offers`, `fulfillmentAvailability`, `procurement`, `relationships` and `productTypes`. "Notification Type Values" describes listing issues: "Issues point out that the listings item has problems which cause listing inactive, search suppression, or other quality problems. For example, if the selling partner's product image has low resolution, this issue will cause search suppression for this product", and "All problems that can occur with a listing do not have corresponding defined issues ... (neither does the Selling Partner API for Listings Items)." On the ads side, the SP v3 serving and reason enums carry AD_MISSING_IMAGE, MISSING_IMAGE and VARIATION_PARENT (section C), the Eligibility API carries MISSING_IMAGE and VARIATION_PARENT (section E), and SP product targeting has the expression types `ASIN_CATEGORY_SAME_AS` and `ASIN_EXPANDED_FROM` (SP v3 model, read 2026-11-09). [opinion] Keepa's `Product.java` records `parentAsinHistory` ("The included timestamp indicates when the previously associated parent ASIN ceased to be valid ... null if the parentAsin field never changed"), `historicalVariations`, `categories`, `rootCategory`, `categoryTree`, `salesRankReferenceHistory` and `images`.

[seat] A browse-node change moves an ASIN between the category targets other advertisers' and the seller's own category-targeted ads aim at (`ASIN_CATEGORY_SAME_AS`) and between the category benchmark sets of section P, so it can move impressions from category targeting and the category comparison without any edit in the ads account. No page read states how Amazon re-targets category ads after a reclassification.

RULE [official]. Reviews as data. The Customer Feedback API 2024-06-01 ("Customer Feedback API", https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/customer-feedback-api.md, read 2026-11-09) "provides the same information as the Product Opportunity Explorer (OX)"; it is available in the US, UK, France, Italy, Germany, Spain and Japan, "refreshed weekly" and "only available in English", and needs the Brand Analytics or Selling Partner Insights role. `getItemReviewTopics` returns the ten most positive and ten most negative topics with `numberOfMentions`, `occurrencePercentage` and `starRatingImpact` ("The effect of the topic on the star rating of the ASIN") for the ASIN, its parent and its browse node; `getItemReviewTrends` returns up to six monthly points per topic. [opinion] Keepa's `Product.java` gives `RATING` ("an integer from 0 to 50 (e.g. 45 = 4.5 stars)") and `COUNT_REVIEWS` histories, `lastRatingUpdate`, and a variation-specific `reviews` object whose "ratingCount history has not been updated since April 9th 2025 as that data point was removed by Amazon".

RULE [official]. Stock, delivery and the session denominator. The FBA Inventory API `getInventorySummaries` (`GET /fba/inventory/v1/summaries`, `fbaInventory.json`, read 2026-11-09) returns, with `details=true`, `fulfillableQuantity`, the inbound quantities, `reservedQuantity`, `researchingQuantity` and `unfulfillableQuantity`, and `lastUpdatedTime` ("The date and time that any quantity was last updated"); with `startDateTime` it returns only summaries "that have had changes after the date and time specified". ### U. Push-only surfaces: SP-API notifications and Marketing Stream

RULE [official]. SP-API notifications are delivered only to an AWS destination. The Notifications API v1 model (`notifications.json`, read 2026-11-09): `createDestination` takes a `resourceSpecification` of `sqs` (`arn`, pattern `^arn:aws:sqs:...`) or `eventBridge` (`region` and `accountId`, "The identifier for the AWS account that is responsible for charges related to receiving notifications"); "Applications should use one resource type (sqs or eventBridge) per destination." `createSubscription` "Creates a subscription for the specified notification type to be delivered to the specified destination. Before you can subscribe, you must first create the destination". The model has no operation that returns delivered notifications; `sendTestNotification` "Sends a mock notification of the specified type to your SQS" and is sandbox-only. "Notifications API" (https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/notifications-api.md, read 2026-11-09): "Instead of polling for information, your application can receive information directly from Amazon", and "As a best practice, we recommend that you have a means to retrieve needed information in the event of an unexpected outage or delay in notification delivery." "Notification Type Values" names the workflow for each type: `PRICING_HEALTH` and `FBA_INVENTORY_AVAILABILITY_CHANGES` the Amazon Simple Queue Service workflow, `LISTINGS_ITEM_STATUS_CHANGE` the Amazon EventBridge workflow. `ANY_OFFER_CHANGED` "is sent whenever there is a change to any of the top 20 offers, by condition (new or used), or if the external price (the price from other retailers) changes for an item that you sell, or if there is a change to which offer wins the BuyBox, or a change to the BuyBox price", and "You will only receive `ANY_OFFER_CHANGED` notifications for items for which you have active offers."

RULE [official]. Amazon Marketing Stream is push-only too. The Stream v2 GA release note (2026-09-30, read 2026-11-09): "You provide an S3 bucket and SQS queue, and Stream writes Parquet files"; Stream v1 "delivers the data to partner or advertiser-owned AWS destinations" and "currently supports two destinations: Amazon Data Firehose and AWS Simple Queue Service (SQS)" ("Amazon Marketing Stream overview", re-read 2026-11-09).

[seat] Through the open doors named in this pack (`cx raw amazon-ads`, `cx raw amazon-sp-api`, `cx-keepa`), cx can call `createDestination` and `createSubscription` (they are grantless or role-scoped SP-API operations, and the SP-API door carries a `sellingpartnerapi::notifications` grantless scope), but nothing in those doors reads an SQS queue, an EventBridge bus, a Firehose stream or an S3 bucket, so the events themselves (`ANY_OFFER_CHANGED`, `PRICING_HEALTH`, `FBA_INVENTORY_AVAILABILITY_CHANGES`, `LISTINGS_ITEM_STATUS_CHANGE`, `LISTINGS_ITEM_ISSUES_CHANGE`, Stream's budget and entity-change messages) are out of reach unless the seller has an AWS destination and a reader for it. The polling substitutes Amazon documents for the same facts are: `getCompetitiveSummary` and `getFeaturedOfferExpectedPriceBatch` (Featured Offer, reference prices, competitive price threshold, competitors' promotions), v0 `getItemOffers` (`CompetitivePriceThreshold`, `IsBuyBoxWinner`), `getInventorySummaries` with `startDateTime` (inventory changes since a time), `getListingsItem` with `issues` and `summaries` (issues, enforcement actions, buyability), `searchPromotions` with `updateDateAfter`, and, for history the polls cannot give, Keepa's Buy Box, price, offer-count, coupon and rating histories. A poll gives the state at the moment of the call; the notification's event time and the transitions between polls are not recoverable from it.

[opinion] Disha (Optmyzr), "Why Amazon Performance Drops Feel Random (And How to Trace the Real Cause in Minutes)" (2026-07-08, https://www.optmyzr.com/blog/amazon-ppc-performance-drops-root-cause-analysis/, read 2026-11-09). ## Published research

[research] Ang, B.W. (2005). "The LMDI approach to decomposition analysis: a practical guide." *Energy Policy* 33(7): 867–871. doi:10.1016/j.enpol.2003.10.010. Log-mean Divisia index decomposition of a product of factors V = x1 × x2 × ... × xn. In the multiplicative form, ln(V1/V0) = Σ ln(xi,1/xi,0), so each factor's share of the change is its log ratio. In the additive form, ΔV = Σ L(V1, V0) · ln(xi,1/xi,0), where L(a, b) = (a − b)/(ln a − ln b) is the logarithmic mean. The decomposition leaves no residual. Zero values are handled by substituting a small positive number. Ang, B.W. (2015), "LMDI decomposition approach: A guide for implementation", *Energy Policy* 86: 233–238, gives the implementation choices. #### Whether a movement exceeds noise

LMDI and Adtributor apportion a change; they do not say whether the change is larger than ordinary variation. The methods below are the published tests for that question on a time series.

[research] Page, E.S. (1954). "Continuous inspection schemes." *Biometrika* 41(1–2): 100–115. doi:10.1093/biomet/41.1-2.100 (Crossref record read 2026-11-09). The origin of the cumulative-sum (CUSUM) scheme. The NIST/SEMATECH *e-Handbook of Statistical Methods*, §6.3.2.3 "CUSUM Control Charts" (https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pmc/section3/pmc323.htm, read 2026-11-09), states that CUSUM charts "have been shown to be more efficient in detecting small shifts in the mean of a process ... better than Shewhart control charts when it is desired to detect shifts in the mean that are 2 sigma or less", and gives the tabular form S_hi(i) = max(0, S_hi(i−1) + x_i − μ̂0 − k), S_lo(i) = max(0, S_lo(i−1) + μ̂0 − k − x_i), signalling when either exceeds h.

[research] Roberts, S.W. (1959). "Control Chart Tests Based on Geometric Moving Averages." *Technometrics* 1(3): 239–250. doi:10.1080/00401706.1959.10489860 (Crossref record read 2026-11-09). The exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA) chart. NIST §6.3.2.4 "EWMA Control Charts" (https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pmc/section3/pmc324.htm, read 2026-11-09): EWMA_t = λY_t + (1 − λ)EWMA_{t−1}, "The equation is due to Roberts (1959)"; "By the choice of weighting factor, λ, the EWMA control procedure can be made sensitive to a small or gradual drift in the process, whereas the Shewhart control procedure can only react when the last data point is outside a control limit."

[research] Counts. NIST §6.3.3.1 "Counts Control Charts" (https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pmc/section3/pmc331.htm, read 2026-11-09): for counts following a Poisson distribution with parameter c, "both the mean and the variance of this distribution are equal to c", and the k-sigma chart is UCL = c + k√c, LCL = c − k√c ("If the LCL comes out negative, then there is no lower control limit"); "Usually k is set to 3 by many practioners" (sic). Spiegelhalter, D.J. (2005). "Handling over-dispersion of performance indicators." *Quality and Safety in Health Care* 14(5): 347–351. doi:10.1136/qshc.2005.013755 (abstract read through Europe PMC, PMID 16195568, 2026-11-09): when an indicator "shows substantially more variability than would be expected by chance alone ... ignoring such 'over-dispersion' could lead to a large number of institutions being inappropriately classified as 'abnormal'"; multiplicative and additive corrections "give intuitively reasonable results, but the additive random effects formulation appears to have a stronger conceptual foundation". Spiegelhalter, D.J. (2005). "Funnel plots for comparing institutional performance." *Statistics in Medicine* 24(8): 1185–1202. doi:10.1002/sim.1970 (Crossref abstract read 2026-11-09): control limits "form a funnel around the target outcome, in a close analogy to standard Shewhart control charts", with an example "dealing with over-dispersion due to unmeasured risk factors".

[research] Killick, R., Fearnhead, P., Eckley, I.A. (2012). "Optimal Detection of Changepoints With a Linear Computational Cost." *Journal of the American Statistical Association* 107(500): 1590–1598. doi:10.1080/01621459.2012.737745; arXiv:1101.1438 (abstract read 2026-11-09). The PELT method finds the optimal number and location of changepoints by minimising a penalised cost "with a computational cost which, under mild conditions, is linear in the number of observations", against quadratic or cubic for earlier exact methods, and "the exactness of our approach can lead to substantial improvements in the accuracy of the inferred segmentation" over Binary Segmentation.

[research] Hochenbaum, J., Vallis, O.S., Kejariwal, A. (2017). "Automatic Anomaly Detection in the Cloud Via Statistical Learning." arXiv:1704.07706 (abstract read 2026-11-09). Twitter's method for series with "inherent seasonal and trend components": "Seasonal decomposition is employed to filter the trend and seasonal components of the time series, followed by the use of robust statistical metrics -- median and median absolute deviation (MAD) -- to accurately detect anomalies, even in the presence of seasonal spikes", evaluated on production data. The public R package (twitter/AnomalyDetection, README read 2026-11-09) calls the algorithm "Seasonal Hybrid ESD (S-H-ESD)", which "builds upon the Generalized ESD test"; that test is Rosner, B. (1983), "Percentage Points for a Generalized ESD Many-Outlier Procedure," *Technometrics* 25(2): 165–172, doi:10.1080/00401706.1983.10487848 (Crossref record read 2026-11-09).

[seat] How these transfer to an advertiser's daily series. Ad-attributed orders per day are small counts; on a Poisson reading a mean of 4 orders a day has a standard deviation of 2, so a 3-sigma band runs from no lower limit to 10, and a day with 1 order is inside it. Order and click counts on Amazon also carry a weekly cycle and event spikes, which S-H-ESD's decomposition and a weekday-matched baseline address and a plain c-chart does not; and day-to-day variation in sales usually exceeds the Poisson variance (Lewis and Rao's coefficient of variation is an individual-level version of the same fact), which is the over-dispersion Spiegelhalter's corrections handle. CUSUM and EWMA detect a sustained small shift that no single day shows; PELT dates where a level changed in a longer history; none of them names the cause, which is the decomposition's and the documented-cause layers' work. Restatement (section K) means the last days of any series are not yet comparable with the rest.

#### Root-cause attribution by Amazon's own researchers

[research] The two Budhathoki et al. papers above are by Amazon researchers, and their methods are public in DoWhy's `gcm` module. Amazon Science lists "Why did the distribution change?" as a publication by Kailash Budhathoki, Dominik Janzing, Patrick Blöbaum and Hoiyi Ng, AISTATS 2021 (https://www.amazon.science/publications/why-did-the-distribution-change, read 2026-11-09); PMLR gives it as *Proceedings of AISTATS*, PMLR 130: 1666–1674 (https://proceedings.mlr.press/v130/budhathoki21a.html, read 2026-11-09). Amazon Science's "New method identifies the root causes of statistical outliers" (Kailash Budhathoki and Patrick Blöbaum, 2022-07-19, https://www.amazon.science/blog/new-method-identifies-the-root-causes-of-statistical-outliers, read 2026-11-09) presents the ICML 2022 paper as an "Amazon ICML paper" and explains that, to remove dependence on the order in which causal mechanisms are replaced, "we take the average contribution over all orderings, which is also the idea behind the Shapley value approach in game theory. The Shapley contributions sum up to the IT outlier score of the outlier event."

[research] "AWS contributes novel causal machine learning algorithms to DoWhy Python library" (Amazon Science, Peter Götz and Kailash Budhathoki, 2022-05-31, https://www.amazon.science/blog/aws-contributes-novel-causal-machine-learning-algorithms-to-dowhy, read 2026-11-09): "we are open-sourcing causal machine learning (ML) algorithms that are the result of years of Amazon research on graphical causal models"; "Using a feature we call 'distribution change attribution', we can identify the defective service. An Amazon algorithm for root-cause analysis adapts the game-theoretical concept of Shapley values". The AWS Open Source Blog, "Root Cause Analysis with DoWhy, an Open Source Python Library for Causal Machine Learning" (Patrick Blöbaum, Kailash Budhathoki and Peter Götz, 2023-01-11, https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/opensource/root-cause-analysis-with-dowhy-an-open-source-python-library-for-causal-machine-learning/, read 2026-11-09), says the algorithms "were released in DoWhy v0.8" and that AWS and Microsoft formed PyWhy, "now home of DoWhy". Its worked example is an online shop with the nodes Shopping Event, Ad Spend, Page Views, Unit Price, Sold Units, Revenue, Operational Cost and Profit; it explains a 13% first-quarter profit drop with `gcm.distribution_change(scm, data_first_quarter_2021, data_first_quarter_2022, target_node='Profit', difference_estimation_func=...)` inside `gcm.confidence_intervals`, and a single anomalous day with `gcm.attribute_anomalies(scm, target_node='Profit', anomaly_samples=...)`, where "A positive attribution score means that the corresponding node contributed to the observed anomaly". DoWhy's user guide, "Attributing Distributional Changes" (https://www.pywhy.org/dowhy/main/user_guide/causal_tasks/root_causing_and_explaining/distribution_change.html, read 2026-11-09), cites the AISTATS 2021 paper for the method and adds a "multiply-robust causal change attribution method" for changes in the mean (Quintas-Martinez, Bahadori, Santiago, Mu, Heckerman, ICML 2024, PMLR 235: 41821–41840). The library paper is Blöbaum, Götz, Budhathoki, Mastakouri, Janzing, "DoWhy-GCM: An extension of DoWhy for causal inference in graphical causal models" (arXiv:2206.06821, abstract read 2026-11-09; JMLR MLOSS 25(147), 2024, per the DoWhy README read 2026-11-09), which "addresses diverse causal queries, such as identifying the root causes of outliers and distributional changes". [seat] Each of these needs a causal graph supplied by the analyst; the AWS example's graph (event → ad spend, page views, price, units) is the kind of graph an advertiser's diagnosis would draw, and the answer is only as good as that graph.

## Positions

Amazon's sources first. Each position is a definition, an Amazon rule, a documented mechanism, a measured effect with its source, or a named party's claim stated as theirs.

**P-diagnosis-1.** [official] Amazon defines click-through rate as clicks divided by impressions and cost per click as cost divided by clicks, so on a cost-per-click row spend equals impressions × CTR × CPC. Source: v1 "Clicks (Delivery)" metric page; "Placement report for Sponsored Products".

**P-diagnosis-2.** [official] In the v1 reporting API, `metric.purchaseRate` is purchases divided by impressions; the per-click conversion rate is `metric.purchaseRateOverClicks`, whose v3 name is "14 Day Conversion Rate". Source: v1 "Purchases" metric page.

**P-diagnosis-3.** [official] Reported impressions and clicks exclude invalid traffic; v1 carries `metric.grossClicks`, `metric.invalidClicks`, `metric.invalidImpressions` and the two invalid rates (no gross-impressions field), and the v3 report types `spGrossAndInvalids`, `sbGrossAndInvalids` and `sdGrossAndInvalids` carry `grossImpressions` and `grossClickThroughs`. Source: v1 "Clicks (Delivery)" and "Impressions (Delivery)" metric pages; v3 "Gross and invalid traffic reports".

**P-diagnosis-4.** [official] Amazon states that a Sponsored Products ad does not display if the product is not the Featured Offer or is out of stock, and an Amazon staff reply states the condition is evaluated "at the time the customer views the ad". Source: Amazon Ads "FAQs"; Seller Forums "Sponsored Products and Buy Box".

**P-diagnosis-5.** [official] Amazon states that an advertised product that goes out of stock "will be automatically paused within your campaign". Source: "How to improve your product detail page for advertising".

**P-diagnosis-6.** [official] In the Sponsored Products v3 API, the serving status of a campaign, ad group, product ad, keyword or target is returned in `extendedData.servingStatus` with `servingStatusDetails` (name, message, help URL) when `includeExtendedDataFields` is true, separately from the user-set `state`. Source: Sponsored Products v3 OpenAPI.

**P-diagnosis-7.** [official] Sponsored Products v3 product-ad serving status values include AD_NOT_IN_BUYBOX, AD_OUT_OF_STOCK, AD_NO_PURCHASABLE_OFFER, AD_NOT_BUYABLE, AD_LANDING_PAGE_NOT_AVAILABLE, AD_MISSING_IMAGE, AD_INELIGIBLE and AD_POLICING_SUSPENDED, and the reason values include ASIN_QUARANTINED, BRAND_REMOVED, VARIATION_PARENT, RESTRICTED_GL, CLOSED_GL and a set of MODERATION_*_PV_DETAIL values. Source: Sponsored Products v3 OpenAPI.

**P-diagnosis-8.** [official] In the Amazon Ads API v1, `state` is user-defined (DRAFT, PROPOSED, ENABLED, PAUSED, ARCHIVED) and `status` carries `deliveryStatus` (DELIVERING, LIMITED, NOT_DELIVERING, UNAVAILABLE), up to 50 `deliveryReasons`, and per-marketplace settings. LIMITED means a global resource has different delivery status across marketplaces. Source: Amazon Ads API v1 merged OpenAPI.

**P-diagnosis-9.** [official] v1 delivery reasons name account and billing causes (including ADVERTISER_PAYMENT_FAILURE, ADVERTISER_OUT_OF_PREPAY_BALANCE, ADVERTISER_OUT_OF_POSTPAY_CREDIT_LIMIT, SPEND_LIMIT_EXCEEDED, ADVERTISER_POLICING_SUSPENDED), budget causes at campaign, portfolio and account level, date causes (CAMPAIGN_END_DATE_REACHED, CAMPAIGN_PENDING_START_DATE), offer causes (NOT_IN_BUYBOX, OUT_OF_STOCK, NO_INVENTORY, NO_PURCHASABLE_OFFER, NOT_BUYABLE) and moderation causes. ### Seat's positions (enter no skill)

- [seat] Because the identities are exact per row, a decomposition of a spend or sales change into impressions, CTR and CPC (or clicks, CVR and AOV) leaves no unexplained remainder; what it cannot do is name the cause behind the part that moved.
- [seat] Account-level ratios move with mix: a shift of spend between campaigns, placements, search terms or marketplaces changes account CTR, CPC and CVR when no row's ratio changes.
- [seat] The layers documented here fall into three kinds: causes that stop or limit serving (status values, eligibility, moderation, billing, budgets, dates); causes that change the market (new supply, reserves, competitors' bids, demand); and causes that change the record (restatement, invalid traffic, null values, time zones, currencies, renamed metrics, incidents). Only the first kind appears in serving status, and the change history holds only changes to the advertiser's own SP and SB entities (edits plus IN_BUDGET transitions).
- [seat] A recent window compared with an older one compares an unsettled record with a settled one; on Amazon's own pages the unsettled period runs from days for clicks to weeks for conversions.
- [seat] Seen through Amazon's reserve description and GSP theory, a CPC change has at least three documented channels that do not involve the advertiser's bid: competitors' bids, Amazon's real-time reserves, and Amazon's relevance ranking.
- [seat] The Featured Offer can be lost three ways with different fixes: another eligible offer wins, the offer is ineligible on price, or no offer is featured; the Product Pricing reads separate the first two (`currentFeaturedOffer`, `resultStatus`, `100708`), and Keepa's -1 Buy Box seller marks the third.
- [seat] A rating that falls through a promotion's rating floor can end a coupon or deal, so a rating drop can show up first as a promotion ending and a price rise to the shopper.
- [seat] With small daily order counts, a day-over-day drop is usually inside Poisson limits before over-dispersion is even allowed for; a test of whether a movement exceeds noise is a separate step from apportioning it, and neither names the cause.
- [seat] The 2025–2026 additions read here (prompts, off-Amazon and creator supply, Alexa+, auto-assembled videos) join existing campaigns by default (Amazon Business-exclusive campaigns, by contrast, are set by `siteRestrictions`), so a campaign's totals can change from a date with no entry in its change history.
