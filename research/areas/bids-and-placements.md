## What this covers

This file covers the price side of Amazon's sponsored ads: what a bid is, how Amazon's auction uses it to pick a winner and set a price, and every Amazon mechanism that moves the bid a seller typed before it enters an auction. In scope:

- the auction as Amazon now states it: cost per click (CPC) and vCPM pricing, the generalized second-price auction with hard and soft reserves, relevance in ranking, and the 2026 FTC case about the soft reserve;
- what "bid", "adjusted bid", "effective bid" and "maximum effective bid" mean on Amazon's pages, and the minimum and maximum bids per marketplace;
- Sponsored Products bidding strategies: dynamic bids down only, dynamic bids up and down, fixed bids, rule-based bidding with a ROAS guardrail, and the names these carry in the API;
- placement bid adjustments and their ranges: top of search, rest of search, product pages, Amazon Business, the browse placements of Sponsored Products across retailers, and the off-Amazon setting;
- audience (shopper-cohort) bid boosts, Sponsored Products video adjustments, and the order in which adjustments multiply;
- schedule bid rules (Amazon's own dayparting surface) against third-party dayparting;
- bid recommendations: suggested bid and range, theme-based recommendations, impact metrics, Performance Estimate, the campaign and tactical recommendation types that touch bids, and how Amazon says it computes them;
- top-of-search impression share and the placement reports;
- Sponsored Brands bidding (automated placement bidding, placement and audience adjustments, cost control, reserve share of voice) and Sponsored Display, now called display ads (bid optimization for reach, page visits and conversions, vCPM, cost control);
- how soon bid-relevant data arrives (hourly data, click invalidation, conversion restatement);
- the bid writes themselves in each API (operations, body fields, media types, batch ceilings, per-item refusals) and when a written bid is not the bid the auction uses;
- the AI shopping surfaces (Alexa for Shopping, into which Rufus and Alexa+ were merged in May 2026; prompts; Alexa+ Conversational Ads) as places a bid serves;
- published research on position effects in sponsored search, auction design with reserves and soft floors, and bid optimization, including Amazon Science work.

## Amazon's own rules and guidance

Each item is marked RULE (Amazon states how its system behaves or what it accepts) or GUIDANCE (Amazon advises an advertiser). Where Amazon's pages disagree, both are recorded and the disagreement is named.

### A. The auction: how the winner and the price are set

- RULE: "To help choose the ads that appear in those placements and to determine the price, we usually run auctions. We run billions of auctions each day with multiple auctions often running simultaneously on a single page because of the variety of placements and ad formats. You may win one auction and not others on that page because of the unique qualities of each placement, your ad, and what else is on the page." [official] *Understand Amazon Ads auctions* (help GEXGZNBBWPZZK7P8), https://advertising.amazon.com/help/GEXGZNBBWPZZK7P8
- RULE: ranking places "particular value on the ad's expected relevance to the customer", judged on variables including "The content surrounding where the ad will go", "The customer's search query (if applicable)", "The likelihood a customer will engage with the advertised product or service" and "The customer's shopping interests". "As a result, ads with lower bid amounts, but higher relevance to the customer, can and do win our auctions." [official] same page.
- RULE: reserves. "Some reserves help allocate ad space by setting a bid threshold. Your bid must meet this amount to be allocated, and you'll be charged at least this amount if your ad wins. Other reserves influence your final CPC or vCPM based on factors including the likelihood that the ad will lead to a sale, predicted return on your ad spend, the value of any competing bids, placement, context, and predicted performance (e.g., the same ad placement may have a different value during a peak shopping period than a non-peak period). As a result, the price charged to you may exceed the runner-up bid but will never exceed the maximum bid you authorize." [official] same page.
- RULE: "We will never charge you more than the maximum bid you authorize and you should only bid what you are willing to pay. Accordingly, as you enter your bids, make sure to account for applicable bid adjustments and dynamic bidding features that may increase or decrease your entered bid amount for some ad opportunities." [official] same page.
- GUIDANCE: "Rather than trying to optimize for specific auctions, focus on setting bids that align with your overall goals and adjust them based on the balance between performance and cost that works for you." [official] same page.
- RULE: the auction form, in Amazon's corporate statement. "Since we first introduced ads in our Store in 2006, we have priced clicks using a form of generalized “second-price” auction ... advertisers may pay less than their bid." The statement names "a “hard reserve,” which is the minimum a bid must surpass to enter an auction. The hard reserve helps cover our costs whereas soft reserves represent what we estimate to be the true market value of the ad placement." And: "Advertisers bid a maximum price for a placement. When the winning advertiser’s bid exceeds both the hard and soft reserve, they pay the soft reserve, which is less than they were willing to pay. When the winning advertiser’s bid exceeds the hard reserve but doesn't meet the soft reserve, we still grant the placement to that advertiser and they pay their bid. In no scenario does an advertiser pay more than their bid." [official] *Amazon's response to the FTC's lawsuit regarding Sponsored Ads*, aboutamazon.com, https://www.aboutamazon.com/md/company-news/amazon-ftc-sponsored-ads-lawsuit-response (undated on the page; it answers the complaint filed 2026-08-31).
  - [seat] The statement describes the soft-reserve case only for a single winner; it does not say how the price is set when the runner-up's (relevance-weighted) bid is above the soft reserve. The help page's "may exceed the runner-up bid" means the price can sit above the second price. The two pages together describe a second-price auction in which the soft reserve can lift the price up to, but never above, the winner's own maximum.
- RULE: candidate selection and ranking. Amazon says it reduces the eligible ads "down to less than a thousand candidates by removing less relevant ads. We then apply a ranking score to each eligible ad based on relevance and bid. Over time, our ranking formula has increasingly given greater weight to relevancy over bid amount." Relevance models "consider a variety of factors including the content surrounding the ad placement, the shopper’s search query and the likelihood a shopper will engage". "reserves are determined in real time and aren't predictable in advance by anyone, including Amazon or the advertiser." [official] same statement.
- RULE (Amazon's figures, for human readers only): "in 2024, approximately 92% of selected Sponsored Products ads were not the highest bid"; "The mean winning advertiser’s bid is typically about the 128th bid by amount." The same statement claims that the average winning bid fell about 50% (from 2019 to 2024 in the body; "from 2019 to 2025" in its key takeaways), that CPC was flat after inflation from 2019 to 2024, that conversion rates rose 24% from 2021 to 2025, and that "eighty percent of bid changes on clicked Sponsored Products search ads occurred within one day of a prior change". It says help content was updated "to explicitly explain the use of reserve prices", and that the console has said since 2018 that a bid is a maximum. [official] same statement. These are Amazon's claims in litigation; none is independently verified here.
- RULE: the same auction description, with retailers' reserves, applies to Sponsored Products across retailers (ads served on other retailers' sites through Amazon Retail Ad Service). Ads are ranked on "Your bid (the maximum cost per click you are willing to pay)", "How well the advertised product matches the shopper's context" and "The likelihood a customer will engage". "Retailers may set reserve pricing"; "the price charged to you may exceed the runner-up bid but will never exceed the adjusted maximum bid you authorize". "When a retailer integrates multiple demand sources, we may adjust our submitted price to compete effectively in the retailer's allocation while always respecting the adjusted maximum bid you authorize." [official] *Understand auctions for Sponsored Products ads across retailers* (help GW3CP75ZUTET4QEF), https://advertising.amazon.com/help/GW3CP75ZUTET4QEF
- RULE: "The final CPC is usually determined by an auction and is based on your adjusted bid plus additional factors ... the auction considers factors beyond bid to determine both ad placement and final CPC." "The maximum cost per click is the highest amount an advertiser could be charged for a click, which includes their base bid plus any adjustments from settings such as dynamic bidding or cost controls." [official] *What is cost per click (CPC)?*, Amazon Ads library guide, https://advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/cost-per-click
- RULE: "Your final cost-per-click will be based on your adjusted bid plus additional factors, but will never exceed your maximum adjusted bid." [official] *Sponsored Products best practices*, library guide, https://advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/sponsored-products-best-practices
- GUIDANCE, simplified: the author's guide tells KDP authors that "the amount you bid, along with the relevance of your ad to the reader's query, determines whether your ad will be shown", and gives a bid-only example ($0.75 against $0.60, "you will win the auction"). [official] *An author's guide to Sponsored Products*, library guide, https://advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/authors-guide-to-sponsored-products
  - [seat] The example leaves out relevance and reserves; the auction page and the corporate statement above supersede it.
- RULE (the regulator's allegation, not a finding): the FTC and 22 states sued on 2026-08-31 in the Western District of Washington, alleging that since 2019 Amazon has applied an undisclosed "soft reserve" so that Sponsored Products advertisers paid their own bid "close to 80% of the time": 30–40% of the time in 2021, 70% in 2022 and about 80% in 2024 (figures for human readers only). The complaint covers Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands and display ads; the year percentages are for Sponsored Products only. [opinion: the FTC, a regulator's untested allegation] *FTC, States Sue Amazon Over Secret Ad Surcharge Scheme*, FTC press release, 2026-08-31, https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/08/ftc-states-sue-amazon-over-secret-ad-surcharge-scheme (a plain curl GET returned the FTC's "Page Not Found" shell on 2026-11-09; the page was read through a fetch tool the same day). Secondary coverage gives the docket as FTC v. Amazon, W.D. Wash., 2:26-cv-03097, and reports the complaint's account that soft reserves began with Sponsored Brands in 2018, Sponsored Products in 2019 and display ads by 2023 (search-result summaries of Bloomberg Government and others, 2026-11-09; not fetched). No ruling or settlement was found by search on 2026-11-09.
  - [seat] Amazon's 92% is about who wins; the FTC's 80% is about what the winner paid. They do not contradict each other (Ad Badger says the same, below).

### B. What the bid number means, and its floor and ceiling

- RULE: "A bid is what you are willing to pay for a click or view before any bid adjustments or bidding strategy. Your bidding strategy and bid adjustments then determine the bid amount. This amount is your effective bid." "If you use bid adjustments or a bidding strategy, the effective bid, can be higher or lower than your bid. The highest it can reach is your maximum effective bid. The amount you pay is your CPC or vCPM, which will not exceed the maximum effective bid." [official] *Understand bidding on Sponsored ads* (help GDS2E64R9GQ35768), https://advertising.amazon.com/help/GDS2E64R9GQ35768
- RULE: bid types by campaign. CPC bids serve Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands with the goal "drive page visits", and Display with "page visits" or "conversions"; vCPM bids serve Sponsored Brands "grow brand impression share" and Display. [official] same page.
- RULE: "Cost-per-click bids that are set at the keyword level take precedence over the default cost-per-click bid set at the ad group or campaign level." [official] *Set bids in a Sponsored Products campaign* (help GTMXQWASBRZTHLF2), https://advertising.amazon.com/help/GTMXQWASBRZTHLF2
- RULE: in the API, an ad group's `defaultBid` is "used in sponsored ads as the maximum bid during the auction", and a target's `bid` is "The maximum bid for a target". [official] Amazon Ads API v1 merged OpenAPI (`AdGroupBid`, `TargetBid`), https://d1y2lf8k3vrkfu.cloudfront.net/openapi/en-us/dest/AmazonAdsAPIALLMerged_prod_3p.json
- RULE: cost types in the unified API are `CPC`, `CPM`, `FIXED_PRICE` and `VCPM`. [official] same specification (`CostType`).
- RULE: minimum and maximum bids per marketplace and ad product, for human readers only. Examples from the limits page: Sponsored Products US 0.02 to 1,000 USD, UK 0.02 to 1,000 GBP, JP 2 to 100,000 JPY, IN 1 to 5,000 INR; Sponsored Brands CPC US 0.10 to 49 USD and Sponsored Brands video 0.25 to 49 USD; Sponsored Brands vCPM (brand impression share) US 8 to 5,000 USD; Sponsored Display CPC US 0.02 to 1,000 USD and vCPM 1 to 1,000 USD. The page lists Egypt's Sponsored Products range as "0.15/5.50", which reads like an error. [official] *Limits, constraints, and quotas*, Ads API reference, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/reference/concepts/limits
- RULE: the help page gives a separate table of minimum vCPM bids per country (for human readers only): Sponsored Brands "Grow Brand Impressions Share" US 8 USD for image and 12 USD for video, UK 4 and 8 GBP, DE 5 and 7 EUR, JP 560 and 800 JPY; Display 1 in most currencies, with exceptions BR 2, EG 5, IN 4, JP 100, MX 5, SA 4, SG 4 and TR 1.85. "There is a minimum bid requirement for vCPM (it varies by ad type and country). Using the minimum bids may not lead to viewable impressions." [official] *Understand bidding on Sponsored ads* (help GDS2E64R9GQ35768).
- RULE: the API rejects a bid above the budget and a bid outside the marketplace range; the Sponsored Products bidding errors include `BID_GT_BUDGET`, `BID_OUT_OF_MARKET_PLACE_RANGE` and `BID_INVALID_PLACEMENT`. [official] Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI, https://d1y2lf8k3vrkfu.cloudfront.net/openapi/en-us/dest/SponsoredProducts_prod_3p.json
- RULE: in Performance Estimate, "you can’t save bids that are lower than the daily budget you’ve set for the campaign" [sic]. [official] *Performance Estimate (beta)* (help GHFYQ2D4LQC5ABPW), https://advertising.amazon.com/help/GHFYQ2D4LQC5ABPW
  - [seat] Read literally this says bids lower than the budget cannot be saved, which would be backwards; the API error `BID_GT_BUDGET` says the rule is the other way round (a bid may not exceed the budget). The help sentence is very probably inverted.

### C. Sponsored Products bidding strategies

- RULE: "In Sponsored Products campaigns, you choose between a dynamic, fixed, or rule based bidding strategy. You can also adjust your bids by audience." [official] *Bidding strategies for Sponsored Products* (help GCU2BUWJH2W3A8Z7), https://advertising.amazon.com/help/GCU2BUWJH2W3A8Z7
- RULE, down only: "Amazon will reduce your bids for clicks that may be less likely to convert to a sale ... We may decrease your bids by up to 100% for all placements based on performance. This could happen, for example, on a less relevant search query or on a placement that doesn’t perform well". The page lists it as ideal "When you want to optimize for conversion but set the maximum bid amount", for "New advertisers" and for "Strict budgets". [official] same page.
- RULE, up and down: "We may increase or decrease your bids by up to 100% for all placements based on performance. Since this strategy adjusts your bid up and down in proportion to the likelihood of a conversion, it may deliver more conversions for your ad spend compared to the other 2 strategies." "Example: Amazon can adjust your bid of $1.00 up to $2.00 for all placements." Listed as ideal for "Advertising a product with good historical performance data", "Campaigns targeting highly sought-after placements" and "Flexible budgets with room for higher bids". [official] same page.
- RULE, fixed: "Amazon will use your exact bid for all opportunities and won’t adjust your bids. Compared to dynamic bidding strategies, you may get more impressions, but fewer conversions for your ad spend with this strategy." Placement adjustments still apply to fixed bids. Listed as ideal "When you have historical campaign data and a specific target ACOS" and for "Testing new products". [official] same page.
- RULE, the up-and-down ceiling, where Amazon's pages disagree:
  - "We will increase or decrease your bids by up to 100% for all placements" (a $1 bid can reach $2), with the warning "Ensure you are prepared to potentially spend double". [official] *Dynamic bidding for Sponsored Products*, library guide, https://advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/dynamic-bidding-sponsored-products
  - The API model: `AUTO_FOR_SALES` "Increases or decreases your bids in real time by a maximum of 100%". [official] Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI (`SponsoredProductsBiddingStrategy`).
  - The help page above: "up to 100% for all placements".
  - Against these, the author's guide says up and down raises bids "up to 100% for placements at the top of the first page ... up to 50% for all other placements". [official] *An author's guide to Sponsored Products*.
  - The unified v1 model says the same of `SALES_UP_AND_DOWN`: "Increases or decreases your bids in real time by a maximum of 100%"; its `SALES_DOWN_ONLY` "Decreases your bids in real time when your ad is less likely to convert to a sale. Bids will never increase beyond your set bid." [official] Amazon Ads API v1 merged OpenAPI (`BidStrategy`).
  - [seat] Four current Amazon sources (help, library guide, SP 3.0 model, v1 model) say 100% for all placements; the 100%/50% split is the older rule, still on the author's guide and repeated by practitioners (BidX and Perpetua below; Trellis in 2025–2026, "according to a third-party summary of Amazon's announcement"). PPC Land (Luis Rijo, 2026-10-18) states that Amazon's current guide sets 100% for all placements "while earlier versions allowed 100% at top of search and 50% elsewhere". The live behaviour is not readable from any API field.
  - Re-read on 2026-11-09 for the gap fill. The library guide's example is worded in the present: "imagine you have a bid of $1.00. Currently, we can increase this bid by up to 100%, so that would be a maximum of $2.00 for all placements opportunities". The help page reads "We may increase or decrease your bids by up to 100% for all placements based on performance". The author's guide still reads "Bids will be increased by up to 100% for placements at the top of the first page of shopping results and by up to 50% for all other placements". The *Set bids in a Sponsored Products campaign* help page (GTMXQWASBRZTHLF2) describes up and down without any percentage. The worked example on *Understand bid adjustments in Sponsored ads* and *Adjust Sponsored Products bids* ($1, +50% top of search = $1.50, "can further increase the bid up to $3") is a top-of-search case and fits either ceiling. [official] pages as named, read 2026-11-09.
  - [seat] How this settles. On Amazon's own pages the count is four current sources for "100% for all placements" against one, the author's guide written for KDP authors, for 100/50. No Amazon page dates a change from 100/50 to 100/100. "The older rule" is the seat's and PPC Land's reading of the word "Currently" and of the practitioner record; Amazon does not say it. A position stating a single ceiling should name the author's guide as the dissenting Amazon page, as P-bids-and-placements-8 does.
- RULE: the API names. `LEGACY_FOR_SALES` is "Dynamic bids - down only": "Lowers your bids in real time when your ad may be less likely to convert to a sale. Campaigns created before the release of the bidding controls feature used this setting by default." `AUTO_FOR_SALES` is up and down. `MANUAL` "Uses your exact bid and any placement adjustments you set, and is not subject to dynamic bidding." `RULE_BASED` is rule-based bidding. `OTHER` appears in read responses only. A campaign created without `dynamicBidding` gets `LEGACY_FOR_SALES`. [official] Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI.
- RULE: the unified (v1) API names Sponsored Products strategies differently: its `BidStrategy` enum lists `MANUAL`, `NEW_TO_BRAND`, `PRIORITIZE_KPI_TARGET`, `RULE_BASED`, `SALES_DOWN_ONLY`, `SALES_UP_AND_DOWN`, `SPEND_BUDGET_IN_FULL` and `USE_CAMPAIGN_STRATEGY`; not every value applies to every ad product. Its KPI enum includes `ROAS` and `TOP_OF_SEARCH_IMPRESSION_SHARE`. [official] Amazon Ads API v1 merged OpenAPI.
- RULE: the history of bid controls. On the retired `/v1/sp` API, "Bid+" (a premium top-of-search adjustment) was replaced by bidding controls; Bid+ campaigns were converted to down only with a +50% top-of-search adjustment; at that time `placementTop` and `placementProductPage` accepted 0–900%; "Bid controls apply only to Sponsored Products". [official] *Bid controls* (legacy reference), https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/reference/1/bid-controls
- GUIDANCE: when to use up and down, in the library guide: the objective is sales; winning top placements; high-performing campaigns; excess inventory; deal ASINs. "review your bids often, about once every two weeks". "We recommend that you limit your changes while testing strategies, so that you can attribute the difference in performance to a specific change." [official] *Dynamic bidding for Sponsored Products*.
- GUIDANCE: for automatic campaigns, "dynamic bidding up and down ... or, if ... ROAS, then try a dynamic down only"; for manual keyword targeting, "setting the highest bid on the exact match, a lower on the phrase match, and the lowest bid on the broad match"; "Test different match types for at least 1-2 weeks"; "you won’t be bidding against yourself by adding different match types on the same keyword". [official] *Targeting with Sponsored Products*, library guide, https://advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/targeting-with-sponsored-products
  - [seat] "won’t be bidding against yourself" is a claim about the auction (one advertiser's own ads do not compete for the same slot); Amazon does not say which of the advertiser's eligible targets enters when several match one query. That question belongs to `areas/keyword-and-product-targeting.md`.
- GUIDANCE: fixed bids "may get you more impressions but fewer clicks" (author's guide wording; the help page says "fewer conversions"); "Wait about two weeks after the launch of your campaign and then try getting more advanced with your bidding strategy", then "check in every two weeks after that". [official] *An author's guide to Sponsored Products*.
- RULE: Amazon's Ad Lab video of 2026-10-15 says "With fixed bidding, your bid stays exactly where you set it" and that neither fixed nor dynamic bidding is better in every case; it published no test results. [official, through a secondary report] *Amazon Ad Lab names four bidding metrics but publishes no test results*, PPC Land (Luis Rijo), 2026-10-18, https://ppc.land/amazon-ad-lab-names-four-bidding-metrics-but-publishes-no-test-results/ , citing https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CfoPTLaOVXo (the video itself was not watched).

### D. Placement adjustments in Sponsored Products

- RULE: what each placement is. "Top of search (first page): Up to the first 5 positions in the top row of the first page search results on desktop, and up to the first 4 positions on mobile." "Rest of search: the middle or bottom of shopping results and the second page of shopping results and beyond." "Product pages: Sponsored Products ads on the product details page, and all nonshopping placements such as the add-to-cart page." "Amazon business placements: all Sponsored Products placements that appear on Amazon Business including Top of Search, Rest of Search, and Amazon Business product pages." [official] *Understand bid adjustments in Sponsored ads* (help GQ4R4FR4H56JL6V8), https://advertising.amazon.com/help/GQ4R4FR4H56JL6V8 ; the same four definitions are on *Set bids in a Sponsored Products campaign* (help GTMXQWASBRZTHLF2) and *Adjust Sponsored Products bids* (help GYYZVM7LGSRYGWV5), https://advertising.amazon.com/help/GYYZVM7LGSRYGWV5
- RULE: placement adjustments only increase. "Bid adjustments let you increase your bids when your ad is eligible to appear in specific placements, to specific audiences, or with Sponsored Product Videos. You set a percentage increase that applies on top of your base bid when certain conditions are met." "The specified increase works in addition to your selected bidding strategy (dynamic or fixed)." [official] *Understand bid adjustments in Sponsored ads*.
- RULE: the arithmetic with dynamic bidding. "You set a dynamic bid of $1 for the keyword “camera” and set a 50% bid adjustment for top of search. When your ad is eligible to appear in the top of search against “camera” your bid will increase to $1.5. Dynamic bidding (up and down) can further increase the bid up to $3." [official] *Understand bid adjustments in Sponsored ads* and *Adjust Sponsored Products bids*.
  - [seat] So dynamic bidding multiplies the placement-adjusted bid, not the base bid: the maximum effective bid under up and down is base × (1 + placement%) × 2.
- RULE: range. Sponsored Products placement adjustments are percentages from 0 to 900 (`PlacementBidding.percentage`, int32, 0–900); the campaign create and update model sets no item limit on `placementBidding` (the 3-entry maximum in the model belongs only to the `SponsoredProductsNewCampaignDynamicBidding` shape used by target promotion groups, and to the placement adjustments of a new-ad-group bid recommendation request); the placements are `PLACEMENT_TOP`, `PLACEMENT_REST_OF_SEARCH`, `PLACEMENT_PRODUCT_PAGE` and `SITE_AMAZON_BUSINESS`. The model's example: "a 50% Top of Search adjustment on a $1.00 bid would increase the bid to $1.50 ... A further 100% Amazon Business adjustment would increase the bid to $3.00 for the Amazon Business Top of Search placement and to $2.00 for all other Amazon Business placements." [official] Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI (`SponsoredProductsPlacement`, `SponsoredProductsPlacementBidding`).
- RULE: rest of search became adjustable on 2023-09-21, as a beta: "up to 900%, same as the other two placements", for all targeting types and bidding strategies. [official] *Amazon Ads advanced tools release notes* (the Ads API release notes page), 2023-09-21 (API and bulksheets, beta; previewed 2023-08-06), https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/release-notes/index ; and *Improve your campaign performance on rest of search placements using Sponsored Products rest of search bid adjustment control*, what's new, dated 2024-01-09 (the console announcement, no "beta"), https://advertising.amazon.com/resources/whats-new/improve-campaign-performance

### E. Audience bid boosts, video adjustments, and the order in which adjustments multiply

- RULE: Sponsored Products audience boosts. "Select up to 10 audiences per campaign, each with an independent bid boost percentage. ### G. Rule-based bidding with a ROAS guardrail

### H. Schedule bid rules (Amazon's dayparting surface)

- RULE: "Schedule bid rules is a feature available for Sponsored Products campaigns that allows you to schedule bid increases for specific times of the day, days of the week, date ranges or high traffic events (example: Black Friday)." They apply to "Dynamic bids - up and down", "Down only" and "Fixed". [official] *Bidding rules for Sponsored Products* (help GDLQ5D2BNFCAU3TW); *Apply Schedule bid rules for Sponsored Products* (help G7JFAU9C7PHA3Q68), https://advertising.amazon.com/help/G7JFAU9C7PHA3Q68
- RULE: they only increase. The action operator enum holds only `INCREMENT`, the unit only `PERCENT`, and the value is "An integer between 1 & 100, representing the percent increase on base bid". [official] Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI (`OptimizationRulesAPISwaggerActionDetails`, `OptimizationRulesAPISwaggerRuleActionOperator`). The guide describes schedule bid rules as rules that "automatically increase bids". [official] *Scheduled bid rules*, Ads API guide, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/rules/bidding-rules/scheduled-bid-rules
- RULE: rules add, then sit on top of placement adjustments. "Schedule bid rules are add-on. Example: On campaign A you set Rule 1 for Mondays between 9am-11am with a bid increase of 20%. Then, for the same campaign, you set a daily rule for Mondays called Rule 2 with a bid increase of 10%. This means that on Mondays between 9am-11am your bids on campaign A will increase by 30%." "Schedule bid rules will be applied on top of your placement adjustment. Example: ... down only bidding strategy, top-of-search placement adjustment of 10%, bid of $1.00, and you apply a Schedule bid rule to increase your bid by 20% on Mondays. That differs from the multiplicative stacking of placement and audience adjustments in E. The API guide's own example stacks schedule rules additively too: 10% daily + 20% Black Friday = 30%, plus 20% in a four-hour window = 50%.
- RULE: the API. Create the rule with `POST /sp/rules/optimization` (content types `application/vnd.spoptimizationrules.v1+json` and `v2`), attach it with `POST /sp/campaigns/{campaignId}/optimizationRules`, find rules with `POST /sp/rules/optimization/search`, update with `PUT`. `ruleCategory` `BID`, `ruleSubCategory` `SCHEDULE`; recurrence `DAILY` or `WEEKLY` with `daysOfWeek` and `timesOfDay` in `HH:00` (at most one window, per the model); a duration by start and end date or by `eventId` (events from `POST /sp/v1/events`). "Whenever a new schedule bid rule is created or an existing rule is updated, the changes will take effect within 24 hours. A maximum of 20 schedule bid rules can be specified for a campaign. Schedule bid rules are applied on top of 'Placement adjustment' and 'Bidding strategies'." [official] *Scheduled bid rules*, Ads API guide; the `timesOfDay` limit (`maxItems` 1) and the v2 content type are in the Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI.
- RULE: the v2 rule model also defines a `PERFORMANCE` subcategory (`OptimizationRulesAPISwaggerRuleSubCategoryV2`) with attributes `ACOS`, `CLICKS`, `CPC`, `CTR`, `CVR`, `IMPRESSIONS`, `ORDERS`, `ROAS`, `SALES` and `SPEND`, a targeting `lookbackDays` of 3 to 65 and expression types `BROAD`, `EXACT`, `EXPANDED` and `PHRASE`; its action operator is still `INCREMENT` only. [official] Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI (the guide does not describe this subcategory).
- RULE: availability when announced (2023-11-13): US, MX, CA, UK, DE, ES, FR, IT and IN; hours of the day for schedule budget rules followed on 2023-11-16. [official] Ads API release notes.
- RULE: the hourly campaign report that goes with them. "To understand campaign performance at an hourly level and adjust Schedule bid rules effectively, you can access the Report Center and download the campaign report ... Time period: Hourly ... - GUIDANCE: "Apply a schedule-based budget rule to ensure that your campaign doesn’t exceed your budget due to bid increases." [official] same page. Budget rules are `areas/budgets-and-pacing.md`'s.

Third-party "dayparting" tools that pause or cut bids by the hour do so by writing bids or states through the API on a clock; Amazon's guide says a schedule rule change takes "within 24 hours", which says nothing about how fast a direct bid write takes effect.

### M. How soon bid-relevant data arrives

- RULE: "Impression/click event to availability via API" 12 hours; "Impression/click invalidation" 72 hours. [official] *Limits, constraints, and quotas*.

Conversions "are reported based on the hour when the click they are attributed to occurred ... you should expect to receive revisions to conversion data up to sixty days after the initial click." Data "will be delivered to your queue in a burst every hour". [official] *Sponsored Products performance datasets*, Amazon Marketing Stream guide, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/amazon-marketing-stream/datasets/sp-performance . Stream v2 delivers only the records that changed; when a record is restated, you receive it again with its new total." ### O. The bid writes: operations, bodies, batch ceilings and per-item refusals

Every item below is RULE, read from the OpenAPI model named at its end on 2026-11-09 (models fetched from their public cloudfront addresses; no Ads API host called). Batch ceilings are figures **for human readers only**; the model a call is made against is the authority. Each write carries the usual `Amazon-Advertising-API-ClientId` and `Amazon-Advertising-API-Scope` headers (the `cx raw amazon-ads` door supplies the token). The call mechanics (throttling, retries, the 207 contract in general) are `method/ads-api-surface.md`'s.

Sponsored Products, version 3 (`SponsoredProducts_prod_3p.json`):

- `PUT /sp/keywords` (operation `UpdateSponsoredProductsKeywords`), media type `application/vnd.spKeyword.v3+json` on request and 207 response. Body `{"keywords": [ … ]}`, at most 1,000 items; each item requires `keywordId` and may carry `bid` (double, nullable: "Bid associated with this keyword. Applicable to biddable match types only") and `state`.
- `PUT /sp/targets` (`UpdateSponsoredProductsTargetingClauses`), `application/vnd.spTargetingClause.v3+json`. Body `{"targetingClauses": [ … ]}`, at most 1,000; each requires `targetId` and may carry `bid` (nullable: "Targets that do not have bid values in listTargetingClauses will inherit the defaultBid from the adGroup level"), `state`, `expression` and `expressionType` (`AUTO` or `MANUAL`). The automatic-targeting clauses of an auto campaign are targets of `expressionType` `AUTO` and take a `bid` here.
- `PUT /sp/adGroups` (`UpdateSponsoredProductsAdGroups`), `application/vnd.spAdGroup.v3+json`. Body `{"adGroups": [ … ]}`, at most 1,000; each requires `adGroupId` and may carry `defaultBid` ("A bid value for use when no bid is specified for keywords in the ad group"), `name` and `state`.
- `PUT /sp/campaigns` (`UpdateSponsoredProductsCampaigns`), `application/vnd.spCampaign.v3+json`. Body `{"campaigns": [ … ]}`, at most 1,000 ("Note: targetingType cannot be updated"); each requires `campaignId`. The bid fields sit in `dynamicBidding`: `strategy` (`LEGACY_FOR_SALES`, `AUTO_FOR_SALES`, `MANUAL`, `RULE_BASED` on writes; `OTHER` appears only on reads), `placementBidding[]` of `{placement, percentage}` (percentage int32 0–900; placements `PLACEMENT_TOP`, `PLACEMENT_REST_OF_SEARCH`, `PLACEMENT_PRODUCT_PAGE`, `SITE_AMAZON_BUSINESS`) and `shopperCohortBidding[]` (at most 10). `offAmazonSettings` is on the same update; `siteRestrictions` appears in the update shape but "cannot be changed post campaign creation" (D).
- The optional `Prefer: return=representation` header asks the service to "return the full object when doing create/update/delete operations instead of ids".
- The 207 body is `{"success": [ {index, keywordId|targetId|…, <entity>} ], "error": [ {index, errors: [ … ]} ]}`, where `index` is "the index of the keyword in the array from the request body". Each error carries one typed member. The bid member, `biddingError`, appears in the keyword, target, ad group and campaign error selectors with `reason`, `message`, `marketplace`, `lowerLimit` and `upperLimit`; its reasons are `BID_OUT_OF_MARKET_PLACE_RANGE`, `BID_GT_BUDGET`, `BID_INVALID_PLACEMENT`, `BID_AUDIENCES_MORE_THAN_ALLOWED`, `BID_INVALID_AUDIENCE_ID`, `BID_INVALID_AUDIENCE_SEGMENT_TYPE`, `BID_INVALID_SHOPPER_COHORT_TYPE`, `BID_MISSING_AUDIENCES` and `BID_SHOPPER_COHORTS_MORE_THAN_ALLOWED`. The state member, `entityStateError`, has reasons including `ARCHIVED_ENTITY_CANNOT_BE_MODIFIED`, `PARENT_ARCHIVED_FORBIDS_UPDATES`, `PARENT_STATUS_FORBIDS_UPDATES_AND_CREATES`, `INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` and `AUTO_TARGETING_CLAUSE_CANNOT_BE_ARCHIVED_MANUALLY`. A campaign budget lowered below the bids beneath it is refused by `budgetError` reasons `BUDGET_LT_DEFAULT_BIDS`, `BUDGET_LT_KEYWORD_BIDS` and `BUDGET_LT_PREDEFINED_TARGET_BIDS`. Whole-request failures are 400 (`KeywordMutationException` and the like), 415 for a wrong media type, and 429 for throttling.
[official] Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI, https://d1y2lf8k3vrkfu.cloudfront.net/openapi/en-us/dest/SponsoredProducts_prod_3p.json

Unified v1 (`AmazonAdsAPIALLMerged_prod_3p.json` and the per-product models):

- Every v1 write is a `POST` with media type `application/json`: `POST /adsApi/v1/update/targets` (`UpdateTarget`; body `{"targets": [ {targetId, bid: {bid} | {marketplaceSettings[]}, state, …} ]}`), `POST /adsApi/v1/update/adGroups` (`UpdateAdGroup`; `bid.defaultBid`, "The default maximum bid for ads and targets in the ad group. A keyword is a target in v1. The merged model sets each request array to at most 1,000 and notes "Batch size limits are specific to each ad product".
- The per-product models give the ceilings (for human readers only): Sponsored Products 1,000 for campaigns, ad groups and targets; Sponsored Brands 10 for campaigns and ad groups and 1,000 for targets; Sponsored Display 100 for campaigns and ad groups and 1,000 for targets.
- The per-product models also narrow the bid fields. Sponsored Products v1: `bidStrategy` `MANUAL`, `RULE_BASED`, `SALES_DOWN_ONLY` or `SALES_UP_AND_DOWN`; `placementBidAdjustments` at most 4 over `TOP_OF_SEARCH`, `REST_OF_SEARCH`, `PRODUCT_PAGE`, `SITE_AMAZON_BUSINESS`; `audienceBidAdjustments` at most 1 (the merged model says 10); `creativeBidAdjustments` at most 2. Sponsored Brands v1: `bidStrategy` `MANUAL` or `SALES_UP_AND_DOWN`; placements `TOP_OF_SEARCH`, `REST_OF_SEARCH`, `PRODUCT_PAGE`, `HOME_PAGE`, at most 4; one audience; ad groups carry no bid (Sponsored Brands bids are on targets). Sponsored Display v1: see L.
- The 207 body is `{"success": [ {index, target} ], "partialSuccess": [ … ], "error": [ {index, errors: [ {code, message, fieldLocation} ]} ]}`. To complete this action, create the resource manually"). v1 has no bid-specific error code named like v3's `BID_GT_BUDGET`. The model's `ErrorsIndex.index` is bounded 0–19 while success indexes run to 999.

## What practitioners add

Each item is [opinion] unless marked. Practitioners and vendors sell bid tools; their data are observational unless they say otherwise.

### On the auction and what an advertiser pays

- Ad Badger (The Badger), on the FTC case: the FTC's figure (advertisers paying their own bid about 80% of the time in 2024) is about price paid, while Amazon's (92% of winners were not the highest bidder) is about who wins; "both sets of numbers can be true": "'Not the highest bid' is about who wins ... 'Paying your own bid' is about what you are charged after you win." It suggests that ratios of CPC to bid "clustering at 0.90–1.00 mean you are effectively in a first-price auction". *Amazon Says Its Ad Auction Was Second-Price. The FTC Says You Paid Your Own Bid Anyway.*, Ad Badger blog, 2026-09-10, https://www.adbadger.com/blog/amazon-says-its-ad-auction-was-second-price-the-ftc-says-you-paid-your-own-bid-anyway/ (fetched by the verifier on 2026-11-09).
- The "second price plus one cent" rule. Ad Badger's FTC post describes the historical pitch as "if you win, you pay one cent more than the next-highest bid"; vendor pages still teach it (seen in search-result summaries of vendor guides, 2026-11-09). [seat] Amazon's own description (A) makes this out of date: the soft reserve can lift the price above the runner-up, up to the winner's maximum.
- Ad Badger (The Badger, 2018-02-21) teaches the bid as revenue per click times target ACOS: "average order value times your conversion rate, divided by one over your Target ACoS"; $19 × 10% = $1.90 revenue per click, × 30% = $0.57. The article treats bid and cost per click as the same number. *Calculating a Bid Strategy for Amazon Ad Campaigns*, https://www.adbadger.com/blog/calculating-amazon-bid-strategy/
- Trellis (Geoffrey Martlin) gives the same ceiling per placement: "maximum CPC = price x conversion rate in that placement x target ACoS", and the placement adjustment ceiling as maxCPC ÷ base bid − 1 (its illustration: a $30 product, 30% target ACOS and $0.60 base bid; at 10% top-of-search conversion, $30 × 0.10 × 0.30 = $0.90, a +50% ceiling; at 14%, +110%; at 8%, +20%; at 5%, no ceiling because $0.45 is below the base bid). It advises reading the placement report first, moving one placement at a time, and treating the computed number "as a ceiling, not a target". It states the up-and-down ceiling as 100% for top of search and 50% elsewhere "according to a third-party summary of Amazon's announcement". *Amazon Placement Bid Adjustments*, Trellis blog, 2025-05-13, updated 2026-11-04, https://gotrellis.com/resources/blog/amazon-ad-placements
  - [seat] Both formulas compute a maximum the seller is willing to pay per click. Under Amazon's auction (A) the bid is a maximum, and with soft reserves the price paid has drifted toward it (FTC figures), so the formula's output is closer to a cost estimate now than it was under a pure second-price rule. Economics of target ACOS belong to `beyond/goals-and-economics.md`.
- On the suggested bid: Amalyze calls Amazon's figures "a backwards-looking summary of winning bids on auctions Amazon judged comparable" and states "The bid range is the 25th–75th percentile of recent winning bids ... The suggested bid is roughly the median" (*Amazon PPC bid range explained*, Amalyze advertising guide, https://amalyze.com/resources/guides/advertising/amazon-ppc-bid-range-explained , fetched 2026-11-09). Seller Metrics, through search-result summaries only (its pages returned no text to a plain GET), says suggested bids skew high and suggests starting 20–30% below them. [seat] Amazon's own pages say only "bids for ads similar to yours that have won impressions over the past 7 days"; the percentile reading is a vendor's inference, not Amazon's statement.

### On bidding strategies

- BidX (Lena Einschütz), *Case study on the dynamic bidding strategies of Amazon 2024*, 2024-04-17, https://www.bidx.io/blog/dynamic-bidding-strategies-amazon-2024 : about 130,000 campaigns in February 2024. Down-only campaigns showed better conversion rates and the lowest ACOS despite higher CPCs. BidX states the up-and-down ceiling as 100% for top of search and 50% elsewhere, and notes that a 900% top-of-search adjustment with up and down can in theory reach 20× the base bid (€1.50 → €30). Within the down-only campaigns, a first-page (top of search) adjustment above 0% went with the best CTR and conversion but about €0.30 higher CPC and "almost 5 percentage points" higher ACOS; a product-page adjustment above 0% went with the worst ACOS (21.65%). BidX recommends down only "if you are mainly aiming for profitability" and up and down for a product launch, and for manual keyword campaigns suggests halving the bid and allowing about +104% for first-page placements. The data are observational: advertisers chose their strategies, so the comparison carries selection effects (BidX's own caveat is weaker than this; the seat's reading).
- Perpetua (Yasser Sakan), *Amazon Bidding: The Ultimate Guide to Dynamic Bidding Strategies and "Adjust Bids by Placement"*, 2019-02-27, https://perpetua.io/blog-amazon-ppc-advertising-dynamic-bidding-bid-strategy/ : applies the placement modifier first and the dynamic factor second (1 USD with +900% top of search under up and down gives 10 USD and a range up to 20 USD; product pages +400% give 5 USD and up to 7.50 USD under the 50% rule). It advises down only early, up and down for well-optimised campaigns, fixed for launches, and relays what it calls "Amazon’s word of caution": against comparing a new up-and-down campaign with an older down-only one, or copying a campaign to test a strategy ("When you copy a campaign, the sales history data is not transferred"), and against running down-only and up-and-down campaigns side by side to compare ("They will be bidding against each other in the same auction"). Dated 2019; the 50% figure is the older ceiling.
- Novadata (Matthieu, CTO at Nova Analytics), *Amazon Tests ROAS Auto-Bidding for Sponsored Products*, 2026-02-18, https://novadata.io/resources/news/amazon-sponsored-products-roas-auto-bidding-2026 : reports a beta "Automatically optimizing ROAS within budget" option "rolling out to select US accounts": "You set a daily budget only. No base bid, no ROAS target." Its listed sources are "Hunter's Insights" and Amazon's Bid Controls API page; the latter (legacy v1 reference, read here) does not describe it. Unverified. A separate single-source item (Pulse, June 2026, read only as a search-result summary) reports a "Smart Bid V2" rollout to US accounts; no Amazon page read here mentions either.

### On placements and top of search

- Perpetua (Stephen Bench-Capon), *Maximizing the impact of top-of-search Amazon ad placements*, 2023-05-08, https://perpetua.io/blog-maximizing-the-impact-of-top-of-search-amazon-ad-placements/ : from Perpetua's Q1 2023 North American benchmark (for human readers only), top of search CTR 4.41%, conversion 13.8%, ACOS 27.0%; rest of search 0.79%, 8.7%, 31.8%; product pages 0.13%, 6.7%, 40.9%. Perpetua attributes the conversion gap partly to advertisers putting their best ASINs there and to shoppers being nearer a decision. It advises isolating keywords before applying large top-of-search multipliers and backing those campaigns with budget, and warns of cannibalization of organic sales for ASINs with a high organic rank: "If you see ACOS going down, but TACOS not following suit, this could mean your ads are negatively impacting your organic sales."
  - [seat] The table compares placements across advertisers who chose to bid up where they convert best; it is not a causal effect of the placement. The research section (Narayanan & Kalyanam; Agarwal, Hosanagar & Smith) says naive position comparisons overstate the effect.
- Perpetua help (read through search summaries): Perpetua computes a per-target share as top-of-search impressions for the keyword-ASIN pair over top-of-search impressions for the query, and targets with no clicks do not sync. Intentwise (blog, through search summaries) suggests roughly 10–15% top-of-search share as a rough figure for non-branded terms, "benchmarks vary by category and budget". Pacvue (a post titled "Three Reasons Winning Top of Search Doesn't Matter", through a search summary; the page returned 403) says a moderate base bid with a moderate multiplier tends to serve more often than a very low bid with a high multiplier.
- Intentwise (Michael Waters), *New controls for offsite Sponsored Products placements*, 2025-04-16, https://www.intentwise.com/blog/new-controls-for-offsite-sponsored-products-placements : the controls are "Maximize reach" and "Minimize spend"; the placement report has an "Off Amazon" category with no breakdown by site.
- Ad Badger, *Your Sponsored Products ads are now running inside creator reviews and nobody asked you first*, 2026-08-20, https://www.adbadger.com/blog/your-sponsored-products-ads-are-now-running-inside-creator-reviews-and-nobody-asked-you-first/ : creator (Influencer) placements began 2026-08-10 with campaigns enrolled by default; the console options are "Increase reach" (default) and "Limit off-Amazon spend"; top-of-search and product-page adjustments do not apply to creator placements; off-Amazon clicks share the campaign budget. Amazon's help page (F) confirms the last three points in its own words, under different option labels ("Increase reach off-Amazon (default)", "Limit reach to Amazon"). Ad Badger adds that "Only your base bid governs delivery on this inventory"; Amazon's page says instead that "Dynamic bidding and rule-based bidding still apply" off Amazon.

### On dayparting

- Perpetua (Stephen Bench-Capon), *Study: Intraday Optimization*, 2022-06-30, https://perpetua.io/blog-a-mini-study-intraday-optimization/ : US Sponsored Products, six weeks in spring 2022 (for human readers only). Average CPC fell from $1.13 to $0.84 over the day (Perpetua's hypothesis: competitors' budgets run out); conversion peaked around 20% between 6 and 10am Pacific against 6–8% overnight; ACOS sat at 20–23% from 6am to 6pm and reached 41% at 11pm. Perpetua recommends hourly bid multipliers.
- Ad Badger (The Badger), *Amazon PPC Dayparting: The Complete Guide (2026)*, 2025-07-02 (modified 2026-08-12), https://www.adbadger.com/blog/how-to-use-dayparting-to-improve-your-amazon-ppc/ : answers "Does Amazon Ads support dayparting natively?" with "No ... Sellers use third-party tools like Ad Badger, or manual bid rules" (Amazon's schedule bid rules, H, exist but only raise bids), advises "Avoid turning ads off/on every hour", skipping dayparting during launches and high-traffic events, and lowering bids "instead of turning ads off entirely".
  - [seat] The practitioner case for dayparting rests on hour-of-day differences in conversion and CPC. Amazon's down-only and up-and-down strategies already adjust each bid by predicted conversion, which includes the time of the auction as far as Amazon's model sees it; no source read here measures dayparting on top of dynamic bidding against dynamic bidding alone.

## Published research

Each item is [research]. Most of these studies are of Google, Yahoo, Bing, eBay or other marketplaces, not Amazon, and none uses Amazon's soft reserve as described in 2026. They describe mechanisms and measured effects; they do not set numbers for a skill. Where the seat read only an abstract or a search-result summary, the item says so.

### Position effects in sponsored search

- Agarwal, Hosanagar & Smith, "Location, Location, Location: An Analysis of Profitability of Position in Online Advertising Markets", *Journal of Marketing Research* 48(6), 2011, 1057–1073, doi:10.1509/jmr.08.0468 (bibliographic record checked through Crossref). From a field experiment with a retailer's search ads: click-through rate falls with position, but conversion rate does not fall the same way (it can rise lower down), and top positions cost more, so profit can be higher below the top. (Abstract and secondary summaries; the verbatim abstract was not retrieved.)
- Narayanan & Kalyanam, "Position Effects in Search Advertising and their Moderators: A Regression Discontinuity Approach", *Marketing Science* 34(3), 2015, 388–407, doi 10.1287/mksc.2014.0893. Using the discontinuity at the rank boundaries of the auction: naive estimates overstate position effects; effects exist at some positions and not others; they are larger for smaller advertisers and for consumers with less prior experience of the keyword. (Abstract.)
- Ghose & Yang, "An Empirical Analysis of Search Engine Advertising: Sponsored Search in Electronic Markets", *Management Science* 55(10), 2009, 1605–1622, doi 10.1287/mnsc.1090.1054. A six-month panel of a US retailer's Google keywords: conversion rates are highest at the top and fall with rank, so the value of a click differs by position; but "profits are often lower in the top positions than those in the middle positions due to the aggressive nature of bidding". Higher landing-page quality goes with higher conversion and lower CPC. (Abstract and NYU Stern press summary through search.)
- Jerath, Ma, Park & Srinivasan, "A 'Position Paradox' in Sponsored Search Auctions", *Marketing Science* 30(4), 2011, 612–627, doi 10.1287/mksc.1110.0645. A theory with Korean search-engine data: a superior firm may bid below an inferior one, take a lower slot and still get more clicks, because consumers seek it out; the paradox appears in a large share of observed auctions. (Abstract through search.)
- Ursu, "The Power of Rankings: Quantifying the Effect of Rankings on Online Consumer Search and Purchase Decisions", *Marketing Science* 37(4), 2018, 530–552, doi 10.1287/mksc.2017.1072. With randomised rankings at Expedia: rankings affect what consumers search, but conditional on search do not affect what they buy; the estimated position effect is smaller than non-experimental estimates. (Abstract and INFORMS release through search.)
- Joo, Shi & Abhishek, "Do Sellers Benefit from Sponsored Product Listings? Evidence from an Online Marketplace", *Marketing Science* 43(4), 2024, 817–839, doi 10.1287/mksc.2021.0293. A large field study on a mobile marketplace: in the top slots shoppers favour organic over sponsored listings of the same product (a credibility effect), and are roughly indifferent lower down; a sponsored listing that displaces the seller's own top organic listing can hurt, while swapping a low organic listing for a top sponsored one can pay. (Abstract and university release through search.) The organic interaction is `areas/ads-organic-and-total.md`'s.
- Blake, Nosko & Tadelis, "Consumer Heterogeneity and Paid Search Effectiveness: A Large-Scale Field Experiment", *Econometrica* 83(1), 2015, 155–174 (NBER Working Paper 20171, 2014), https://www.nber.org/papers/w20171 . eBay's experiments: "brand-keyword ads have no measurable short-term benefits"; for non-brand keywords "new and infrequent users are positively influenced by ads", while frequent buyers, who would have bought anyway, take most of the spend, so average returns were negative. Measured returns were a fraction of non-experimental estimates. (NBER abstract page.)
- Rutz, Bucklin & Sonnier, "A Latent Instrumental Variables Approach to Modeling Keyword Conversion in Paid Search Advertising", *Journal of Marketing Research* 49(3), 2012, 306–319, doi 10.1509/jmr.10.0354 (abstract read through Crossref, 2026-11-09). Keyword-level paid-search data of a major lodging chain, with position treated as endogenous ("Position endogeneity in paid search data can arise from both omitted variables and measurement error") and corrected by a latent instrumental variable: "higher positions increase both the click-through and conversion rates. When advertisements are served in higher positions, approximately one-third of new conversions is due to increased click-through while approximately two-thirds are due to increased conversion rates."

### Conversion rate by position: why the studies disagree

The studies above give three different answers about conversion rate (not click-through rate, on which they agree) as an ad moves up. Abstracts re-read through Crossref on 2026-11-09:

- Ghose & Yang (2009, a retailer's Google keywords, six months, hierarchical Bayesian simultaneous equations): "conversion rates are highest at the top and decrease with rank".
- Rutz, Bucklin & Sonnier (2012, a lodging chain, latent instrumental variables for endogenous position): "higher positions increase both the click-through and conversion rates".
- Agarwal, Hosanagar & Smith (2011, a field experiment on several keywords of an online retailer): "while click-through rate decreases with position, conversion rate increases with position and is even higher for more specific keywords"; in their usage a larger position number is lower on the page, so conversion rate rises lower down, and "the topmost position is not necessarily the revenue-or profit-maximizing position".
- Narayanan & Kalyanam (2015, a regression discontinuity at the auction's rank boundaries, with competitors' data): "Because positions are determined through an auction, there are significant selection issues in measuring position effects. A simple mean comparison of outcomes at two positions is likely to be biased", and "experimentation is rendered difficult in this situation by competitors’ bidding behavior, which induces selection biases that cannot be eliminated by randomizing the bids for the focal advertiser"; their causal effects on click-through and sales orders are "much smaller, and exist only in some of the positions", stronger for smaller advertisers and less experienced consumers, weaker for brand- or product-specific keywords, exact-match copy and weekends.
- Ursu (2018, randomised rankings at Expedia): position changes what is searched, but conditional on search it does not change what is bought.

[seat] The disagreement is about method and data, not arithmetic. Two of the conversion findings (Ghose & Yang; Rutz et al.) come from observational panels with modelled endogeneity, one (Agarwal et al.) from a focal advertiser's own bid experiment, which Narayanan & Kalyanam argue cannot remove the selection that rivals' bids create; the one design built for that selection finds small position effects at some positions only, and the randomised-ranking study finds none on purchase given search. None is an Amazon study, all are text ads on web search or travel listings, and none has Amazon's top-of-search row of up to 5 (desktop) or 4 (mobile) product ads. What transfers is the warning: the conversion rate a seller's `campaignPlacement` report shows for top of search mixes the placement's effect with which shoppers and queries reach it and which rivals are beside it, and the direction of the true effect on conversion is not settled in the literature.

### Position bias in click data and placement comparisons

- Craswell, Zoeter, Taylor & Ramsey, "An experimental comparison of click position-bias models", WSDM 2008, 87–94, doi 10.1145/1341531.1341545 (Microsoft Research publication page read 2026-11-09). "A key source of bias is presentation order: the probability of click is influenced by a document’s position in the results page." They perturbed the ranking of a major search engine and tested four hypotheses: "The data are not well explained by simple position models, where some users click indiscriminately on rank 1 or there is a simple decay of attention over ranks. A ‘cascade’ model, where users view results from top to bottom and leave as soon as they see a worthwhile document, is our best explanation for position bias in early ranks." https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/an-experimental-comparison-of-click-position-bias-models/
- Joachims, Swaminathan & Schnabel, "Unbiased Learning-to-Rank with Biased Feedback", WSDM 2017, doi 10.1145/3018661.3018699 (arXiv 1608.04468, abstract read 2026-11-09; an IJCAI 2018 summary version is doi 10.24963/ijcai.2018/738). "position bias in search rankings strongly influences how many clicks a result receives, so that directly using click data as a training signal in Learning-to-Rank (LTR) methods yields sub-optimal results"; they give a counterfactual framework with a propensity-weighted ranking SVM "where click models take the role of the propensity estimator", tested on an operational search engine. https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.04468
- Reiley, Li & Lewis, "Northern Exposure: A Field Experiment Measuring Externalities between Search Advertisements", ACM EC 2010, 297–304, doi 10.1145/1807342.1807390 (SSRN 2190189 abstract read through Crossref, 2026-11-09). A controlled experiment on the "north" ads above the organic results: "additional rival ads in the north tend to increase rather than decrease the click through rate (CTR) of the top sponsored listing". Neither the SSRN abstract nor the abstract of Lewis's MIT thesis (DSpace@MIT 1721.1/62403, read 2026-11-09), whose search chapter covers the same experiment, names the search engine; the brief that raised this gap calls it a Yahoo! experiment, which this seat did not confirm from a page read.
- Xue, Liu, Du, Korlimarla & Wen, "Practical Lessons on Optimizing Sponsored Products in eCommerce", arXiv 2304.09107, 2023 (abstract page and PDF read 2026-11-09). The PDF's affiliation line is "Walmart eCommerce" (San Bruno, California; walmart.com addresses): this is Walmart's sponsored-products system, not Amazon's. "Based on our beforehand observation from our online shopping site, as the position increases, the CTR drops significantly. Such observation indicates that more clicks on an ad item is not necessarily equal to the preference of this item from users, it may be just because this ad item is in a good position." They de-bias by weighting training instances with functions of position ("polynomial, logarithmic, and inverse propensity weighting functions") and predict with position set aside. The follow-on PACC paper (Wang, Xue, Liu, Wen, Zhao, Guo & Yu, arXiv 2307.16060, SIGIR) by University of Illinois Chicago, Walmart eCommerce and Indeed authors de-biases click and conversion prediction jointly "on the E-commerce sponsored product search dataset".
- Karra, Zhao, Murray & Pellegrini, "Nudging neural click prediction models to pay attention to position", CIKM 2023, listed on amazon.science (publication page read 2026-11-09): "users are more likely to interact with items that were shown prominently on a website. CTR models often overestimate the value of such items and show them more often, at the expense of items of higher quality that were previously shown at less prominent positions. This self-reinforcing position bias effect reduces both the immediate and long-term quality of recommendations"; they propose training that pushes networks to use position "(or other confounding variables) as much as possible to explain the training data" and a metric for bias. https://www.amazon.science/publications/nudging-neural-click-prediction-models-to-pay-attention-to-position
  - [seat] The page names no Amazon product, so it shows Amazon researchers treating position as a confounder in click prediction, not how Amazon's sponsored-products ranker is built.
- [seat] What this means for placement multipliers. The placement report compares a placement's CTR and CVR with another's, but each placement differs in where it sits on the page (position bias, cascade reading), in which shoppers and queries reach it (selection by Amazon's own relevance ranking and by the seller's bids), and in which rival ads are beside it (Reiley et al. find rivals can raise a top listing's CTR). The click-model and de-biasing literature corrects for position by modelling it or by randomising it; an advertiser's report does neither, and a seller cannot randomise Amazon's ranking. A multiplier judged on raw placement CTR or CVR rests on a biased comparison of unknown sign; the comparison that does identify a multiplier's effect is a change in the multiplier itself, read as `method/change-measurement.md` describes.

### Auction design: second price, reserves and soft floors

- Edelman, Ostrovsky & Schwarz, "Internet Advertising and the Generalized Second-Price Auction: Selling Billions of Dollars Worth of Keywords", *American Economic Review* 97(1), 2007, 242–259. The generalized second-price auction has no dominant strategy of truthful bidding; in its "locally envy-free" equilibria revenue is at least that of the VCG auction, with one such equilibrium matching VCG's positions and payments. (Abstract and summaries through search; drafts on the authors' sites.)
- Ostrovsky & Schwarz, "Reserve Prices in Internet Advertising Auctions: A Field Experiment", *Journal of Political Economy* 131(12), 2023, 3352–3376, doi 10.1086/725702. On Yahoo, theory-based reserve prices raised revenue, most for keywords with high volume, high estimated reserves and few bidders. (Abstract through search; a secondary blog puts the average gain near 3%.)
- Zeithammer, "Soft Floors in Auctions", *Management Science* 65(9), 2019, 4204–4221, doi 10.1287/mnsc.2018.3164. With symmetric bidders, soft floors in a second-price auction change neither allocation nor revenue (revenue equivalence); with asymmetric bidders revenue can go either way; profitable conditions are narrow, and a soft floor high enough effectively turns the auction into first price. (Abstract fragments and UCLA Anderson brief through search.)
- Chen (Outreach Inc.), Nabi (Amazon Ads) & Siniscalchi (Amazon Ads and Northwestern University), "Advancing Ad Auction Realism: Practical Insights & Modeling Implications", AdKDD workshop at KDD 2023, arXiv 2307.11732; PDF at https://cdn.amazon.science/a5/3f/f4a65055483ba954a1bc476a17fc/advancing-ad-auction-realism-practical-insights-modeling-implications.pdf (full text read). They model advertisers as bandit learners in auctions where values vary by query, competitors are unobserved, feedback is partial, and "Pricing mechanisms are often incompletely specified. For instance, ad services may indicate that reserve prices are used, but not what they are, or how they are computed." Their soft floor: if the second bid is at or above the floor the price is the second bid; if the first is above the floor and the second below, the price is the floor; and "Crucially, if 𝑠 > 𝑏(1), then the high bidder still wins the auction ... and 𝑝 = 𝑏(1)". In a symmetric single-query setting soft floors had "virtually no impact on revenues"; in a multi-query setting where bidders choose which queries to target, formats gave different revenues, and "Soft-floor reserve prices can impact revenues; thus, our simulations provide some support to this common industry practice", with the ranking of first-price, second-price and soft-floor formats depending on the learning algorithm.
  - [seat] The paper's soft-floor rule matches Amazon's 2026 statement in the case that matters most (a winner below the soft reserve still wins and pays its own bid). They differ in wording for a winner above the floor: the paper prices at the larger of the second bid and the floor, Amazon's statement says the winner "pay[s] the soft reserve". The paper is a simulation, two of whose three authors are at Amazon Ads, and says nothing about Amazon's production parameters.

### Bid optimization and autobidding

- Aggarwal, Badanidiyuru & Mehta, "Autobidding with Constraints", WINE 2019, LNCS 11920, 17–30. For an advertiser maximising value under spend or return constraints, uniform bidding (each bid a constant multiple of value) is optimal when the auction is truthful, and not in general otherwise. (Through search and later papers' summaries.)
- Balseiro, Deng, Mao, Mirrokni & Zuo, "Robust Auction Design in the Auto-bidding World", arXiv 2111.02468, 2021 (abstract read on arXiv). With value-maximizing bidders under return-on-spend constraints, "by choosing reserve prices appropriately the auctioneer can improve not only the total revenue but also the total welfare"; the results generalize to VCG, GSP and first-price auctions, and additive boosts improve welfare further; an empirical study on online advertising auction data accompanies the theory.
  - [seat] Amazon's rule-based bidding (a ROAS guardrail, bids up to 5× the adjusted bid) is a value-maximiser under a return-on-spend constraint in exactly this sense, and its up-and-down strategy scales bids by predicted conversion, which is a form of value-proportional bidding. The papers say such bidding is optimal in truthful auctions; a GSP with soft reserves is not truthful.
- Jeunen, Murphy & Allison (Amazon), "Off-Policy Learning-to-Bid with AuctionGym", KDD 2023, doi 10.1145/3580305.3599877 (an earlier version, "Learning to Bid with AuctionGym", won the AdKDD 2022 best paper). Bid data logged from one's own past policy carry unobserved confounding, so naive learning from them is biased; the paper proposes off-policy methods and a simulator. The simulator, https://github.com/amzn/auction-gym (README read), simulates second-price auctions and first-price auctions with bid shading. The repository makes no claim about Amazon's production auction. (PDF at https://cdn.amazon.science/4f/3c/7f9a5c6c4181894d5e64c684c0d7/learning-to-bid-with-auctiongym.pdf was not extracted.)
- Jeunen, Stavrogiannis, Sayedi & Allison (Amazon), "A probabilistic framework to learn auction mechanisms via gradient descent", AAAI 2023 Workshop on AI for Web Advertising, https://www.amazon.science/publications/a-probabilistic-framework-to-learn-auction-mechanisms-via-gradient-descent (publication page read). Learns auction rules by gradient descent over a probabilistic relaxation of allocation and pricing.
  - [seat] The confounding point in AuctionGym is the research form of a practical warning: a seller's own history of "bid X gave Y" was produced by the seller's earlier bidding choices, so it does not by itself identify what a different bid would do. `method/change-measurement.md` owns how cx reads a change.

## Stable enough for a skill

These are mechanisms Amazon states on more than one current page, or states once in a specification that a write would hit. Figures inside them are still read live.

1. A bid is a maximum. The price of a click (or of 1,000 viewable impressions) never exceeds the maximum effective bid, which is the base bid after adjustments and strategy. Exception: Sponsored Brands and display cost control set a target average, not a maximum. Sources: *Understand Amazon Ads auctions*; *Understand bidding on Sponsored ads*; *What is cost per click (CPC)?*; *Understand Cost controls for sponsored ads*.
2. Amazon's auction ranks by relevance and bid and prices as a generalized second-price auction with a hard reserve (entry threshold) and soft reserves (which can raise the price above the runner-up, up to the winner's maximum). Sources: *Understand Amazon Ads auctions*; *Amazon's response to the FTC's lawsuit regarding Sponsored Ads*; *Understand auctions for Sponsored Products ads across retailers*.
3. A higher bid does not guarantee a win: lower bids with higher relevance win. Sources: *Understand Amazon Ads auctions*; *Amazon's response to the FTC's lawsuit*.
4. Sponsored Products has four strategies: down only (`LEGACY_FOR_SALES`, the API default), up and down (`AUTO_FOR_SALES`), fixed (`MANUAL`) and rule-based (`RULE_BASED`). Sources: *Bidding strategies for Sponsored Products*; Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI.
5. Down only can lower a bid by up to 100%; fixed never moves it; up and down can raise it by up to 100% (current help, guide and model say for all placements). Sources: *Bidding strategies for Sponsored Products*; *Dynamic bidding for Sponsored Products*; Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI.
6. Sponsored Products placement adjustments are increases from 0% to 900% for top of search, rest of search, product pages and Amazon Business, and dynamic bidding then applies to the placement-adjusted bid. Sources: *Understand bid adjustments in Sponsored ads*; *Adjust Sponsored Products bids*; Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI; *Sponsored Products best practices*.
7. Placement, audience and video adjustments multiply with each other; among several audiences only the highest boost applies. Sources: *Adjust Sponsored Products bids*; *Understand bid adjustments in Sponsored ads*; Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI (`shopperCohortBidding`).
8. Off-Amazon placements use the same bid and budget; top-of-search, rest-of-search and product-page adjustments do not apply there; dynamic and rule-based bidding do. Source: *Understand Sponsored Products off-Amazon advertising*.
9. Rule-based bidding moves base bids under a ROAS guardrail, up to 5× the adjusted bid, with no guarantee; it excludes schedule bid rules on the same campaign. Sources: *Bidding rules for Sponsored Products*; *Sponsored Products Rule-based bidding overview*.
10. Schedule bid rules only raise bids (1–100% of base), add to each other, add to the placement adjustment, take effect within 24 hours, and are limited to 20 per campaign. Sources: *Bidding rules for Sponsored Products*; *Scheduled bid rules* (API guide).
11. Suggested bids and ranges are computed from recent winning bids of similar ads (7 days on the help page), updated daily, and are not forecasts. Sources: *Understand bidding on Sponsored ads*; *Theme-based bid suggestions - Quick-start guide*; *Performance Estimate (beta)*; Sponsored Display 3.0 OpenAPI.
12. Sponsored Products has no separate placement report in the v3 API; placement is a grouping of the campaign report. Top-of-search impression share is campaign-level in v1 and also target-level in v3. Sources: *Placement reports*; *Reporting columns (version 3)*; v1 *Search (Impression share)* page.
13. Sponsored Brands adjusts placements only for top of search and rest of search (other than top of search); automated bidding optimizes placements below top of search; negative placement adjustments are no longer accepted from 2026-06-15. Sources: *Understand bid adjustments in Sponsored ads*; *Create a Sponsored Brands campaign*; Sponsored Brands 4.0 OpenAPI; Ads API release notes.
14. Sponsored Brands "drive page visits" and display ads can raise a bid by up to 300% for an auction. Sources: *Understand bidding on Sponsored ads*; *Optimization strategies for Display*; *Amazon Ads API for Sponsored Display overview*.
15. A display optimization strategy, a Sponsored Brands goal and a site restriction cannot be changed after creation. Sources: *Optimization strategies for Display*; *Campaign goals for Sponsored Brands*; *Sites selection for Sponsored Ads campaigns*; Sponsored Products 3.0 and Sponsored Brands 4.0 OpenAPI.
16. Click data arrive within about 12 hours, clicks can be invalidated for 72 hours, and Sponsored Products conversions can be restated for 60 days. Sources: *Limits, constraints, and quotas*; *Sponsored Products performance datasets*.
17. Bid writes are batch writes answered item by item: Sponsored Products `PUT /sp/keywords`, `/sp/targets`, `/sp/adGroups`, `/sp/campaigns` (versioned vendor media types); v1 `POST /adsApi/v1/update/{targets,adGroups,campaigns}` (`application/json`); Sponsored Brands `PUT /sb/keywords`, `/sb/targets`, `/sb/v4/campaigns`; Sponsored Display `PUT /sd/targets`, `/sd/adGroups`. A refused item comes back with its request index and a typed reason, and for out-of-range bids the limits. Sources: Sponsored Products 3.0, v1 merged and per-product, Sponsored Brands 3.0 and 4.0, Sponsored Display 3.0 OpenAPI.
18. Under an enabled optimization rule (Sponsored Brands campaign, display ad group), a target's or keyword's bid is not the bid the auction uses. Sources: Sponsored Brands 3.0 OpenAPI; Sponsored Display 3.0 OpenAPI; *Understand Cost controls for sponsored ads*.
19. Existing sponsored ads campaigns serve in Alexa for Shopping (Rufus and Alexa+ merged, May 2026), as prompts, and in Alexa+ Conversational Ads without set-up; Amazon publishes no rule on which bid adjustments apply there. Sources: *What agentic shopping means for advertising*; *Meet Alexa for Shopping*; *Understand prompts on Sponsored Products and Sponsored Brands*.

## Positions

**P-bids-and-placements-1.** On Amazon a bid is the maximum the advertiser authorizes per click (or per 1,000 viewable impressions); Amazon states it never charges more than the maximum effective bid, which includes bid adjustments and the bidding strategy. Sources: [official] *Understand Amazon Ads auctions* (help GEXGZNBBWPZZK7P8); [official] *Understand bidding on Sponsored ads* (help GDS2E64R9GQ35768); [official] *What is cost per click (CPC)?*; all read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-2.** Amazon describes its sponsored-ads auction as "a form of generalized “second-price” auction" with a hard reserve that a bid must exceed to enter, and soft reserves; a winner above both pays the soft reserve, and a winner above the hard but below the soft reserve still wins and pays its own bid. Sources: [official] *Amazon's response to the FTC's lawsuit regarding Sponsored Ads* (aboutamazon.com); [official] *Understand Amazon Ads auctions*; read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-3.** Amazon states that "the price charged to you may exceed the runner-up bid", and that reserves move with factors including predicted sale likelihood, predicted return on ad spend, competing bids, placement, context and peak periods. Sources: [official] *Understand Amazon Ads auctions*; [official] *Understand auctions for Sponsored Products ads across retailers* (help GW3CP75ZUTET4QEF); read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-4.** Amazon ranks ads by relevance and bid, and states that ads with lower bids and higher relevance "can and do win"; it says that in 2024 about 92% of selected Sponsored Products ads were not the highest bid (figure for human readers only). Sources: [official] *Understand Amazon Ads auctions*; [official] *Amazon's response to the FTC's lawsuit*; read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-5.** The FTC and 22 states allege (complaint of 2026-08-31, untested) that Sponsored Products advertisers paid their own bid 30–40% of the time in 2021, 70% in 2022 and about 80% in 2024 because of an undisclosed soft reserve (figures for human readers only). Sources: [official] *Amazon's response to the FTC's lawsuit regarding Sponsored Ads* (disputes the characterisation), read 2026-11-09; [opinion: FTC, regulator] *FTC, States Sue Amazon Over Secret Ad Surcharge Scheme*, read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-6.** Sponsored Products offers four bidding strategies, named in the 3.0 API `LEGACY_FOR_SALES` (dynamic bids, down only), `AUTO_FOR_SALES` (up and down), `MANUAL` (fixed) and `RULE_BASED`; a campaign created through the API without a strategy gets `LEGACY_FOR_SALES`. Sources: [official] Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI; [official] *Bidding strategies for Sponsored Products* (help GCU2BUWJH2W3A8Z7); read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-7.** Under "Dynamic bids - down only" Amazon may lower a bid by up to 100% for clicks it judges less likely to convert, and never raises it. Sources: [official] *Bidding strategies for Sponsored Products*; [official] Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI; read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-8.** Under "Dynamic bids - up and down" Amazon's current help, library guide and Sponsored Products 3.0 and v1 API models say it may raise or lower a bid by up to 100% for all placements ($1.00 up to $2.00), while Amazon's author's guide still says up to 100% for top of the first page and up to 50% for other placements. Sources: [official] *Bidding strategies for Sponsored Products*; [official] *Dynamic bidding for Sponsored Products*; [official] Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI; [official] Amazon Ads API v1 merged OpenAPI; [official] *An author's guide to Sponsored Products*; read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-9.** Under fixed bids Amazon uses the exact bid plus any placement adjustments and does not apply dynamic bidding; Amazon says fixed bids may give more impressions but fewer conversions for the spend. Sources: [official] *Bidding strategies for Sponsored Products*; [official] Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI (`MANUAL`); read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-10.** Sponsored Products placement adjustments are increases of 0% to 900% for top of search (first page: up to the first 5 positions of the top row on desktop, 4 on mobile), rest of search, product pages and Amazon Business placements. Sources: [official] *Understand bid adjustments in Sponsored ads* (help GQ4R4FR4H56JL6V8); [official] *Adjust Sponsored Products bids* (help GYYZVM7LGSRYGWV5); [official] Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI; read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-11.** Dynamic bidding applies to the placement-adjusted bid: Amazon's example is a $1 bid with +50% top of search giving $1.50, which up and down "can further increase ... up to $3". Sources: [official] *Understand bid adjustments in Sponsored ads*; [official] *Adjust Sponsored Products bids*; read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-12.** In Sponsored Products, adjustments of different kinds multiply (placement × audience × video), and when a shopper is in several boosted audiences only the highest audience boost applies, with ties attributed to one audience at random. Sources: [official] *Adjust Sponsored Products bids*; [official] *Understand bid adjustments in Sponsored ads*; [official] Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI (`shopperCohortBidding`); read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-13.** Sponsored Products allows up to 10 boosted audiences per campaign (Amazon audiences or AMC custom audiences), each 0% to 900%; outside the US an advertiser can select only 1 audience. Sources: [official] *Bidding strategies for Sponsored Products*; [official] *Set bids in a Sponsored Products campaign* (help GTMXQWASBRZTHLF2); [official] Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI; read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-14.** Sponsored Products off-Amazon placements use the campaign's own bid and budget; top-of-search, rest-of-search and product-page adjustments do not apply to them, while dynamic and rule-based bidding do; the API switch is `offAmazonBudgetControlStrategy` `MAXIMIZE_REACH` (default in the console) or `MINIMIZE_SPEND`. Sources: [official] *Understand Sponsored Products off-Amazon advertising* (help GYTD2Z3SYMAAMVXA); [official] Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI; read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-15.** Off Amazon, Amazon adjusts the bid on third-party sites by predicted click-through and conversion and submits it to the publisher, who decides which ads show; for creator content Amazon sets the CPC from the maximum bid, expected performance and the sales the ad generates. Sources: [official] *Understand Sponsored Products off-Amazon advertising*; read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-16.** A site restriction (Amazon Business only, or Amazon Haul in the US) cannot be changed after the campaign is created, and such a campaign cannot use audience (shopper-cohort) bid adjustments or the Amazon Business placement adjustment. Sources: [official] *Sites selection for Sponsored Ads campaigns* (help GJGYD2FKNF3MUWBQ); [official] Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI; read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-17.** Rule-based bidding adjusts base bids per impression toward a ROAS goal, can raise bids up to 5 times the adjusted bid, and carries no guarantee of the goal; Amazon's help says that without a ROAS target Amazon sets one. Sources: [official] *Bidding rules for Sponsored Products* (help GDLQ5D2BNFCAU3TW); [official] *Sponsored Products Rule-based bidding overview*; read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-18.** Amazon's API guide states that a rule-based bidding rule is switched off automatically when 20-day ROAS falls below 30% of ROAS over the 20-to-40-day window, and the campaign's earlier strategy and bids return. Sources: [official] *Sponsored Products Rule-based bidding overview*; read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-19.** Schedule bid rules cannot be applied to a campaign with rule-based bidding active, and go on hold if the campaign switches to rule-based bidding. Sources: [official] *Bidding rules for Sponsored Products*; read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-20.** Schedule bid rules are Sponsored Products only and only increase bids, by 1% to 100% of the base bid, for an hour window, weekday, date range or event; several rules add together, and the total adds to the placement adjustment (Amazon's example: $1.00, top of search +10%, Monday rule +20% gives $1.30). Sources: [official] *Bidding rules for Sponsored Products*; [official] *Scheduled bid rules* (API guide); read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-21.** Amazon states that a schedule bid rule change takes effect within 24 hours and that a campaign may carry at most 20 schedule bid rules. Sources: [official] *Scheduled bid rules* (API guide); read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-22.** Amazon says suggested bids and bid ranges are calculated from bids of similar ads that won impressions over the past 7 days and are updated daily; for events in the next 45 days the console shows a separate event suggestion. Sources: [official] *Understand bidding on Sponsored ads*; [official] *Set bids in a Sponsored Products campaign*; [official] Sponsored Display 3.0 OpenAPI; read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-23.** Amazon states that Performance Estimate figures are not a forecast of campaign performance and do not consider budget constraints; the quick-start guide says the same of the clicks-and-orders impact metrics ("not estimates of expected performance", computed assuming the campaign does not run out of budget), which belonged to the v3 theme-based content type shut off on 2025-05-15 and were removed in v4. Sources: [official] *Performance Estimate (beta)* (help GHFYQ2D4LQC5ABPW); [official] *Theme-based bid suggestions - Quick-start guide*; [official] *Amazon Ads advanced tools release notes* (2024-08-22) and *Deprecations*; read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-24.** `POST /sp/targets/bid/recommendations` returns up to 3 bid values per target by theme (`CONVERSION_OPPORTUNITIES`, `PRIME_DAY`, `FALL_PRIME_DEAL_EVENT`, `BFCM_HOLIDAY`); the v5 content type adds estimated impressions for the suggested bid and, with `includeAnalysis`, impression estimates per placement for eight bid values, which Amazon says is currently available only for existing ad group requests in the US; an unsupported marketplace or target returns 422. Sources: [official] Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI; read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-25.** Amazon defines top-of-search impression share as top-of-search impressions earned over the top-of-search impressions the campaign was eligible for; for Sponsored Products it covers the first page. Sources: [official] *Reporting columns (version 3)* (reporting v3); [official] *Top-of-search impression share metric* (what's new, 2021-09-22); read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-26.** In the v3 reporting API, top-of-search impression share is listed for `spCampaigns`, `sbCampaigns` and `sbTargeting` on the columns page, for `spTargeting` and `sbTargeting` base metrics on the targeting report page, and for `spCampaigns` grouped by `campaign` or `campaignPlacement` on the campaign report page; in the v1 reporting API it is compatible with campaign-level dimensions only, not with target, search term, ad group, hour or placement. Sources: [official] *Reporting columns (version 3)*, *Targeting reports* and *Campaign reports* (v3); [official] v1 *Search (Impression share)* metrics page; read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-27.** Amazon's v3 reporting has no separate Sponsored Products placement report: placement performance comes from `spCampaigns` grouped by `campaignPlacement`, and off-Amazon and creator traffic appear there as "Off Amazon" or "Other – On Amazon". Sources: [official] *Placement reports* (v3); [official] *Understand Sponsored Products off-Amazon advertising*; read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-28.** In Sponsored Brands only top of search and rest of search (placements other than top of search) can be adjusted; automated bidding lets Amazon optimize placements below top of search, and in the 4.0 API `bidOptimization: true` makes Amazon ignore `bidAdjustmentsByPlacement`. Sources: [official] *Understand bid adjustments in Sponsored ads*; [official] *Create a Sponsored Brands campaign* (help GF86HBCNDJUAC5WN); [official] Sponsored Brands 4.0 OpenAPI; read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-29.** Amazon's release notes state that new negative Sponsored Brands placement adjustments are not accepted from 2026-06-15 (first announced for 2026-02-02, extended on 2026-01-27), while existing ones keep running with their current settings; positive adjustments go up to 900% for top of search and rest of search (announced 2025-06-19; rest of search was limited to 99% before). Sources: [official] *Amazon Ads advanced tools release notes* (2025-06-19, 2025-12-01, 2026-01-27); read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-30.** For Sponsored Brands campaigns with the goal "drive page visits", Amazon may raise a bid by up to 300% for an auction and lower it where conversion looks less likely. Sources: [official] *Understand bidding on Sponsored ads*; [official] Ads API release notes (2023-10-05); read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-31.** Sponsored Brands and display cost control set a target average CPC, not a maximum: Amazon states the average CPC may be higher than the amount entered and that with placement or audience adjustments the highest possible CPC exceeds the target. In Sponsored Brands cost control is on automatically for the goal "drive page visits" and only available there, and turning it off means entering bids; in display, bids are set automatically and cannot be edited by hand, and suggested bids are not shown. Sources: [official] *Understand Cost controls for sponsored ads* (help GNJDAT6AEYD3XJA8); [official] *Sponsored Brands bids by placement and audiences* (help GC8K522SMNCPH99Z); read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-32.** Sponsored Brands allows one audience bid adjustment per campaign (Amazon-built or AMC, not both), and a campaign's goal cannot be changed after creation. Sources: [official] *Sponsored Brands bids by placement and audiences*; [official] *Campaign goals for Sponsored Brands* (help GXXPFXLSVX9VD3U9); [official] Sponsored Brands 4.0 OpenAPI; read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-33.** Reserve share of voice gives Sponsored Brands a fixed, upfront price for top-of-search placement on 5 to 1,000 branded keywords, outside the auction, and Amazon says it strives for 95% share of voice; Amazon's pages give different flights (30–92 days on *Campaign goals for Sponsored Brands*, 15–92 on *Reserve keywords in a Sponsored Brands campaign*, "e.g., 14–90" on the library guide), different US minimums ($3,000 or $6K) and different marketplace lists (two help pages exclude Spain; the library guide lists 20 marketplaces including Ireland), as `beyond/brand-defense-and-competition.md` records in full (figures for human readers only). Sources: [official] *Campaign goals for Sponsored Brands* (help GXXPFXLSVX9VD3U9); [official] *Reserve keywords in a Sponsored Brands campaign* (help G86SD7HK6NHHRB9B); [official] *How to set up Amazon Sponsored Brands reserve share of voice*; [official] *Reserve share of voice* (Sponsored Brands API guide); read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-34.** In display ads (formerly Sponsored Display) Amazon may raise a bid by up to 300% for an auction under any optimization strategy ($1 to $4); the strategy (reach on vCPM; page visits, conversions or leads on CPC) cannot be changed after creation. Sources: [official] *Optimization strategies for Display* (help GDRFG6U7TRD72EFW); [official] *Amazon Ads API for Sponsored Display overview* (API guide); read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-35.** For vCPM, the bid is deducted per 1,000 viewable impressions, a view following MRC (at least 50% of the ad in view for at least 1 second); Amazon publishes per-country vCPM minimums, and its display help warns that a vCPM bid below $3 "may not lead to viewable impressions" (figure for human readers only). Sources: [official] *Understand bidding on Sponsored ads*; [official] *Limits, constraints, and quotas*; [official] *Optimization strategies for Display* (help GDRFG6U7TRD72EFW); read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-36.** Amazon states that click data reach the API within about 12 hours, that clicks can be invalidated for 72 hours afterwards, and that Sponsored Products conversions in the Marketing Stream v1 dataset can be revised for up to sixty days after the click; Stream Version 1 stops delivering data on 2027-06-30, and the Stream v2 `ads-performance-v1` dataset states a 70-day restatement window. Sources: [official] *Limits, constraints, and quotas*; [official] *Sponsored Products performance datasets* (Marketing Stream); [official] *Deprecations*; [official] *Ads performance* (Stream v2); read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-37.** BidX reports, from about 130,000 campaigns in February 2024, that down-only campaigns had the lowest ACOS and better conversion than up-and-down despite higher CPCs, and that within down-only campaigns a product-page adjustment above 0% went with the worst ACOS; the data are observational. Sources: [opinion: BidX, Lena Einschütz] *Case study on the dynamic bidding strategies of Amazon 2024*, read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-38.** Perpetua reports, from six weeks of US Sponsored Products data in spring 2022, average CPC falling from $1.13 to $0.84 over the day and ACOS rising from 20–23% in the daytime to 41% at 11pm (figures for human readers only). Sources: [opinion: Perpetua, Stephen Bench-Capon] *Study: Intraday Optimization*, read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-39.** Narayanan and Kalyanam find, with a regression-discontinuity design, that naive estimates overstate position effects in search advertising and that the effects are larger for smaller advertisers and less experienced consumers; Agarwal, Hosanagar and Smith find that profit can be higher below the top position. Sources: [research] Narayanan & Kalyanam, *Marketing Science* 2015; [research] Agarwal, Hosanagar & Smith, *JMR* 2011; abstracts read 2026-11-09.

**P-bids-and-placements-40.** Researchers at Amazon Ads and Outreach describe a soft floor under which a bidder below the floor still wins and pays its own bid, and find in simulation that soft floors barely change revenue with symmetric single-query bidders but can change it when bidders choose which queries to target. ### Seat's positions (enter no skill)

- [seat] The full chain of an adjusted Sponsored Products bid, assembled from separate Amazon examples, is base × (1 + placement) × (1 + top audience) × (1 + video), then the strategy (down only to zero, up and down to ×2, rule-based to ×5), with schedule rules adding to the placement percentage. No single Amazon page states the chain.
- [seat] With soft reserves, the price paid sits nearer the bid than the "second price plus a cent" folklore suggests, so a bid set by revenue per click × target ACOS behaves closer to a cost than to a ceiling.
- [seat] Placement benchmarks that show top of search converting best (Perpetua, BidX) are selected comparisons; the research says naive position comparisons overstate the effect.
- [seat] Amazon's native schedule surface can only add to bids, so a practitioner's "lower bids at night" is a direct bid write on a clock, not an Amazon feature.
- [seat] Raw CTR and CVR by placement in the `campaignPlacement` report confound the placement with position bias, with the shoppers and queries Amazon's ranking sends there, and with the rival ads beside it; a placement multiplier judged on them is a biased comparison of unknown sign.
- [seat] A bid write's 207 success shows the item was accepted, not that the auction uses the number: under an enabled optimization rule, or with an SD v1 write of the kind developers report as silently ignored, only a read-back and the next data show the effect.
