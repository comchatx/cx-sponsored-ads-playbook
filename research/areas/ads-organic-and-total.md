## What this covers

**The part of a seller's ad work.** Every reading that sets what ads did against what the whole account did: ad-attributed sales beside total ordered sales; TACoS and its relatives; the halo the ads earn on other SKUs and other products of the brand; new-to-brand shoppers; what share of a query's search-page funnel the brand holds paid and organic together (Brand Analytics Search Query Performance, SQP); whether ad sales move organic rank; whether an ad sale is a sale the seller would have had anyway (cannibalisation) or one the ad caused (incrementality); and the tools Amazon offers for that question in 2026 (Amazon Marketing Cloud, multi-touch attribution, Brand Lift, long-term sales, Marketing Mix Modeling data, Amazon Attribution for off-Amazon traffic). It ends with what a total-account reading can and cannot conclude.

**Why a senior Amazon advertising lead cares.** Attributed ROAS is the number in every console, and it is not the number that pays the bills. Three gaps open between them on every account:

1. **Definitions.** Amazon's attributed sales are counted on the date of the ad click, over a 7- or 14-day window, without tax or cart discounts, and for sellers they include any product the seller sells, not only the advertised one; the Sales and Traffic report counts ordered product sales on the order date. A reading that divides one by the other without aligning them is wrong before any judgement starts.
2. **Causation.** An attributed sale is a sale that followed an ad interaction under Amazon's last-touch rules. Field experiments on search ads (eBay, Bing, an unnamed marketplace) show attributed and incremental sales can differ by a large factor, and on brand terms the ad can be close to pure substitution for the organic listing. Other experiments (mobile app stores, Flipkart clothing) show paid exposure raising organic outcomes. The sign is not known in advance.
3. **The organic channel's own drivers.** Price, deals, stock, the Featured Offer, reviews, seasonality and competitors move organic sales whatever the ads do. A falling TACoS can mean ads lifted organic sales, or that a competitor went out of stock.

**Lane.** Metric and attribution definitions in general are `beyond/metrics-and-attribution.md`'s; this file uses them only where the ads-versus-total reading needs them. Report mechanics (lifecycle, retention, polling) are `method/reporting.md`'s. How to measure one ad change (before/after, holdouts, switchbacks as a method) is `method/change-measurement.md`'s; this file states what the evidence says about incrementality and what Amazon's tools offer, and cross-references that file for test design. Brand-term defence as a competitive matter is `beyond/brand-defense-and-competition.md`'s; this file covers the brand-term cannibalisation evidence. Unit economics (break-even ACoS, margin, TACoS targets as goals) are `beyond/goals-and-economics.md`'s. Why spend or sales moved, as a diagnosis, is `areas/diagnosis.md`'s.

## Amazon's own rules and guidance

RULE marks something Amazon enforces or a shape its API or report fixes (a definition, a field, a window, a limit, a date). GUIDANCE marks a recommendation or a marketing statement.

### A. What an "attributed sale" is, per Amazon

A1. RULE. Amazon's help page *Ad campaign attribution* defines the frame: "Attribution measures conversion events that occur after shoppers interact with ads." Conversions "are reflected on the date of the shopper's ad interaction, which may be different from the conversion date. Attribution metrics for a given report date remain incomplete until the lookback window has ended." "Sales is the total value of attributed purchases and is used to calculate return on ad spend (ROAS). Attributed sales may not always align with retail reports because they're calculated differently." [official] (Ad campaign attribution, https://advertising.amazon.com/help/GX7KDKHMWQYMJ385, read 2026-11-09; the page body is served inside the page's own HTML, decoded by the seat)

A2. RULE. The same page's table by campaign type, verbatim in the load-bearing cells:

| Campaign type, advertiser | Lookback | Eligible products | Interactions | Model |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sponsored Products, Seller | 7 days | "Products featured in the ads as well as any other products sold by the Seller Central merchant." | Clicks only | "Last click" |
| Sponsored Products, Vendors and Authors | 14 days | "Products featured in ads as well as any other products from the same brands ("brand halo"). Only products sold by Amazon are attributed. Book products don't have a brand halo." | Clicks only | "'Last touch' attribution that prioritizes product relevance and clicks over views. Competes with other Amazon Ads products." |
| Sponsored Brands and Display, all advertisers ("must be registered brand owners") | 14 days | "Products on the landing page as well as any other products from the same brands ("brand halo"). Attributed purchases may include sales by Amazon or third-party sellers, in New or Used condition." | "Clicks (CPC) or clicks and views (vCPM)" | as above |

[official] (Ad campaign attribution, above). The windows and eligibility are figures a skill reads from the page; the 7/14-day split is **(human readers only)** as a number.

A3. RULE. Why attributed sales differ from retail sales, in Amazon's list ("Differences between attribution and retail sales"):
- "In campaign reports, sales are reported on the ad interaction date, which may differ from the actual sale date. Sales reported on a given date may have occurred at any time between that date and the end of the lookback window."
- "Attributed sales don't reflect any discounts applied in the cart."
- "Attributed sales don't include tax. Inclusive tax such as VAT is subtracted from sales totals."
- "Depending on the campaign type, attributed sales may include items sold by you, Amazon, or another seller. They can also be items sold in Used condition at a different price."
- "Depending on the campaign type, attributed sales may include products that weren't promoted in the campaign but that are from the same brands as products that were promoted."
- "Payment failures and purchases that are cancelled within 72 hours of order creation will be deducted from attribution metrics. These deductions may take up to 7 days to process after the cancellation."
- "Ads that feature a specific product may have conversions attributed to them for different products. In reports grouped by "Advertised ASIN," the ASIN is the product featured in the ad. Attributed sales for the ad may include other ASINs."

[official] (Ad campaign attribution, above)

A4. RULE. One sale, one credit: "Amazon will attribute a conversion to no more than 1 ad interaction across all campaigns and all advertisers. A sale won't, for example, be attributed to 2 campaigns in both a promoted and brand halo context, or as both a view-through and click-through conversion." The ranking Amazon uses to choose the ad is "Promoted ASIN ad clicks", then "Brand halo ad clicks", then "Promoted ASIN ad views", then "Brand halo ad views", where brand halo for this ranking means "Products within the same brand and subcategory as a promoted ASIN." "Sponsored Products for sellers is a merchant-based ad program that doesn't normally compete for attribution with brands. However, if the same advertiser wins attribution as both a merchant and a brand, attribution will choose the more relevant ad, ensuring the same conversion is attributed only once per advertiser." [official] (Ad campaign attribution, above)

A5. RULE. Views: "View attribution is enabled for: All Display, Video, and Audio campaigns … All Streaming TV campaigns … Sponsored Brands and Display campaigns using vCPM billing." "Beginning January 1, 2026, view attribution for some placements will incorporate additional signals, such as shopping behavior patterns, when determining which ad views are eligible for attribution." [official] (Ad campaign attribution, above). The launch page for that change, dated January 01, 2026: "We are introducing a shopping-signal enhanced last-touch attribution model on January 1, 2026 … giving credit to ad views that reach shoppers during early discovery moments such as exploratory browsing or general category searches, and applies a shorter attribution window"; it changes conversion metrics for "Sponsored Brands, Sponsored Display campaigns billed on a viewable impression (vCPM) basis, including branded keyword campaigns" and "Amazon DSP campaigns that serve ads in the Store"; "Conversion reporting for all other campaign types and inventory types remains unchanged. Click-based attribution remains unchanged."; the earlier model stays available as "'all views' metrics (e.g., Purchases (all views))". [official] (View Attribution Updates for Amazon Store ads, https://advertising.amazon.com/resources/whats-new/view-attribution-updates-for-amazon-store-ads, read 2026-11-09). A total-account series that spans January 1, 2026 and includes Sponsored Brands or vCPM Sponsored Display conversions crosses a definitional step; Sponsored Products click attribution is not affected. [seat] The v1 overview: "Campaigns that do not measure view-through attribution (e.g., Sponsored Products or Sponsored Brands CPC) will continue to report 0 for conversions from views." [official] (Overview of the Reporting v1 API, https://d3a0d0y2hgofx6.cloudfront.net/en-us/guides/reporting/ads-v1/overview.md, read 2026-11-09)

A6. RULE. Freshness and restatement, which a total-sales join inherits: "Initial impression and click data is available using the API within 12 hours." Clicks can change "up to three days after the initial click date due to the traffic validation process." "Restatements of conversion data occur 1, 7, and 28 days after the conversion event. Because conversions are reported on the date of the ad interaction, restatement includes all dates in your attribution window. For example, if your attribution window is 14 days, restatement can update attribution up to 42 days (14 plus 28) after the report date." [official] (Sponsored ads reporting FAQ (version 3), https://d3a0d0y2hgofx6.cloudfront.net/en-us/guides/reporting/v3/faq.md, read 2026-11-09). GUIDANCE: "We recommend waiting at least 5 days for the most accurate click data"; conversion data "is subject to change for up to 60 days back from the current date. We recommend re-retrieving reports monthly". [official] (Amazon Ads reporting overview, https://d3a0d0y2hgofx6.cloudfront.net/en-us/guides/reporting/overview.md, read 2026-11-09). All figures in A6 **(human readers only)**; detail is `method/reporting.md`'s.

### D. The total-sales side: SP-API reads and their keys

D1. RULE. `GET_SALES_AND_TRAFFIC_REPORT` has two sections: `salesAndTrafficByDate`, "aggregated by date (across the seller's entire catalog of items)", and `salesAndTrafficByAsin`, "aggregated by ASIN" over the range. `dateGranularity` takes `DAY`, `WEEK`, `MONTH` (default `DAY`); `asinGranularity` takes `PARENT`, `CHILD`, `SKU` (default `PARENT`). `childAsin` "is only present when ASIN aggregation is CHILD or SKU"; `sku` "only present when ASIN aggregation is SKU". ### TACoS: the shared definition and what each author reads into it

- **Jungle Scout** (Brian Connolly, "What Is Amazon TACoS (Total Advertising Cost of Sale)?", May 24, 2023, https://www.junglescout.com/blog/amazon-tacos/): "TACoS = (Advertising Spend / Total Revenue) x 100". A falling TACoS signals more cost-effective ads "and/or" rising organic sales; as ad sales rise, "the higher your Amazon Best Sellers Rank and organic keyword ranking will be", offered as reasoning ("Think of it like this"), with no data. The article's second worked example divides by $16,600 where its own total is $17,000 (the seat's check).
- **Perpetua** (Martin Saunders, "Amazon Total ACoS (TACoS): Introduction to a Key Ad Metric", July 25, 2024, https://perpetua.io/blog-amazon-tacos): "advertising spend divided by total sales multiplied by 100"; "An increasing TACoS indicates that total ad profitability is decreasing", though not always bad during a launch; the premise "ad sales drive organic sales", in positive and negative cycles, stated without data; the decision rule "IF TACoS < organic profit margin, THEN your overall business is profitable after advertising"; TACoS "varies widely depending on the marketplace, advertising goals, ad types used, seasonality, competition, product price, and other factors."
- **Helium 10** (Bradley Sutton, "Why does your Amazon ACoS look great but you're still losing money?", April 16, 2026, https://www.helium10.com/blog/why-does-your-amazon-acos-look-great-but-youre-still-losing-money/): "TACoS measures total business impact (ad spend ÷ total sales)"; benchmarks "typically range from 8-15%", with 8–12% for mature products, 5–8% for established ones with strong organic presence, 15–20% as an upper limit for growth and 20–30% for 60–90 days of launch; "optimize TACoS first, then improve ACoS within your TACoS constraints"; ACoS "doesn't capture the value of defensive advertising". The figures carry no data source. **(human readers only)**
- **PPC Land** (Luis Rijo, "TACoS", October 18, 2026, https://ppc.land/tacos/): TACoS is "a seller's advertising spend divided by its total sales over the same period, expressed as a percentage"; "Amazon's ACOS guide and its campaign-reporting page, checked in October 2026, list advertising cost of sale, ROAS, new-to-brand, brand halo, long-term sales and Subscribe and Save metrics, but neither mentions total sales or TACoS"; the caveats, verbatim: "TACoS falls when organic sales rise for any reason: a price cut, a competitor stocking out, seasonal demand, a social media mention or off-Amazon traffic. It rises when a product goes out of stock."; "A stable TACoS can conceal a shift of sales from free to paid placement."; "Vendors publish target ranges without data."; "Without a platform standard, two tools can report different TACoS for the same account." It also says "Helium 10 and SellerApp decline to name a figure", which Helium 10's own April 2026 post (above) contradicts. It cites a 2023 working paper by Tim O'Reilly, Ilan Strauss and Mariana Mazzucato as finding "that ads lifted products the algorithm ranked lower organically by a median of 17 positions". Checked on 2026-11-09 against the paper that carries the figure, Rock, Strauss, O'Reilly & Mazzucato, "Behind the Clicks: Can Amazon allocate user attention as it pleases?" (IIPP Working Paper 2023/11; published in *Information Economics and Policy* 69, 2024): its sentence is "The median advertisement appears 17 search ranks above its organic duplicate", measured on first-page results for products that appear both as an ad and organically. PPC Land's wording matches the figure; the paper that states it lists Rufus Rock as first author, and PPC Land's attribution to "O'Reilly, Strauss and Mazzucato" fits their companion paper "Amazon's Algorithmic Rents" (IIPP WP 2023-12, unreachable), so PPC Land may have merged the two (§Published research, audits). The seat did not open the Amazon pages PPC Land summarises; the claim about them is PPC Land's, and it is now contradicted by two Amazon pages that do name TACOS (§What practitioners add, TACoS, and P-6).
- **Where they disagree.** All four agree on the ratio. They disagree on what moves it and what a good value is: Helium 10 gives bands, Perpetua refuses a universal target, PPC Land says the bands are unsupported. Jungle Scout and Perpetua read a falling TACoS as ads feeding organic sales; PPC Land reads it as uninformative about cause. None of them names which "total sales" (ordered or shipped, gross or net of refunds, including B2B, which marketplaces, which time zone) or which "ad spend" (SP only or all ad products; DSP; off-Amazon) enters the ratio. Amazon's two pages that name TACOS (below) define it in one sentence each and name no sales field either (§A, §D).
- **Amazon's own words on TACOS** [official]. *The author's guide to Sponsored Products*, under "How to measure ad success": "Monitor your Total Advertising Cost of Sales (TACOS) to understand how efficiently your advertising investment is working. TACOS shows your ad spend as a percentage of total sales." (https://advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/authors-guide-to-sponsored-products, read 2026-11-09; page metadata 2023-09-07 and 2026-10-09; written for book authors, pointing them to "the Reports + Marketing tab within Author Central"). *Holiday advertising myths: What experts want you to know* (URL slug `common-holiday-advertising-myths`): "ACOS only measures the efficiency of ad-attributed sales, not the full picture. That's where total advertising cost of sales (TACOS) comes in. TACOS reveals whether your total sales (organic and paid) are growing in relation to your ad spend." It then quotes Adam Mellott of BTR Media: "When ACOS rises during high-traffic events, we don't immediately pull back. Instead, we look at TACOS—and often, we find it dropping. This signals improved overall efficiency, as ads are driving both paid and organic sales through increased visibility." (https://advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/common-holiday-advertising-myths, read 2026-11-09; page metadata 2025-09-26 and 2026-10-09) [official for Amazon's sentences; opinion for Mellott's, quoted on Amazon's page]. Neither page is a help page, an API field or a report column; no Ads API metric named TACOS was found in the v1 metric pages or v3 columns read (`beyond/goals-and-economics.md` §J holds the same reading). ### Cannibalisation and incrementality on Amazon

- **Perpetua, the SQP test** (Martin Saunders, "Amazon Search Query Performance Report: tapping into the superpowers of search query data", 2024-10-16, as quoted by the prior research; not re-opened by this seat): if your sales share holds after you cut spend on a keyword, "your Sponsored Products ad was likely cannibalizing your organic sales"; test one keyword at a time. *Not re-verified here; the next item is the same author group re-read.*
- **Perpetua** (Apoorva Ashok Kumar, "How to identify incremental opportunities on Amazon", October 23, 2024, https://perpetua.io/blog-incrementality-amazon-ads-optimization-strategies/): incrementality means using ads "to drive sales that wouldn't have happened"; branded terms are more likely to cannibalise because shoppers already seek the brand, but not winning one's own branded bids risks losing sales to competitors; reduce spend on terms where the product already ranks highly. Measurement is click share across the top three ASINs and total ACoS; no holdout, no data.
- **m19** (Xuan Xie, "Does Amazon PPC Sales Cannibalize your Organic Sales?", April 10, 2024, https://www.m19.com/blog/does-amazon-ppc-sales-really-cannibalize-your-organic-sales): "Organic sales can not be cannibalized by your PPC strategy most of the time"; it happens "only when one of your products ranks very high organically" and the seller still bids; "Normally PPC campaigns on Amazon can have a significant impact on your Amazon organic ranking"; a drop after pausing ads may mean "your competitors have increased their advertising on those keywords". No data; m19 sells a top-of-search rank optimiser.
- **Intentwise and Nectar** (webinar "The brand guide to incrementality: Boost profits with AMC and Brand Analytics", Sreenath Reddy and Jason Landro, February 18, 2026, https://www.intentwise.com/webinars/the-brand-guide-to-incrementality-boost-profits-with-amc-and-brand-analytics): "correlating ad spend against purchase share at a keyword level", "comparing impression share to purchase share", "overlaying ad spend data with Brand Analytics data"; ROAS-driven strategies "often lead to overspending on branded search terms, which may not be incremental"; "A healthy brand sees an increase in branded searches over time." No figures.
- **Skai** (Ana Alicia Santella, "Unlocking incrementality on Amazon with Amazon Marketing Cloud via Skai", June 8, 2023, https://skai.io/blog/unlocking-incrementality-on-amazon-with-amazon-marketing-cloud-via-skai/): AMC lets you "unpack the incremental value of each ad"; its case evidence is a path-to-purchase frequency (a sub-brand with about 5% of sales in 14.3% of paths), which is not a lift measurement. Its own survey: 37% of advertisers struggle to prove incrementality.
- **A seller's switchback** (Jonathan Hershaff, "Estimating Amazon Ad Incrementality for Sellers: How to Combine Switchback Tests with MMM", February 13, 2026, https://whatstheimpact.substack.com/p/estimating-amazon-ad-incrementality): ads alternated "On" and "Off" daily "for about a month (post-Christmas late Dec 2025 – Jan 2026)" by a seller with "$20k annual ad spend"; outcome total sales; platform ROAS about 2.35, naive switchback incremental ROAS 1.25, adstock-adjusted 1.54 after a Google Meridian MMM estimated adstock decay of about 0.35 ("~35% of ad pressure bleeds into the next day"); the MMM's own ROAS had median 1.42, mean 1.79 and a 90% credible interval of 0.27–4.57. He writes "Amazon Attribution is greedy" — meaning Amazon's ad attribution in Seller Central, not the Amazon Attribution product of §H. ## Published research

Each item: what was measured, on what data, and how far it transfers to a seller on Amazon. Abstracts were read from RePEc/EconPapers, NBER, arXiv's API, SUFE's journal listing and Semantic Scholar on 2026-11-09; bibliographic details from Crossref the same day.

### Attributed is not incremental: search-ad field experiments

- **Blake, Nosko & Tadelis, "Consumer Heterogeneity and Paid Search Effectiveness: A Large-Scale Field Experiment"**, *Econometrica* 83(1), 2015, 155–174 (NBER WP 20171, May 2014). eBay experiments on paid search. "Because search clicks and purchase behavior are correlated, we show that returns from paid search are a fraction of conventional non-experimental estimates." "As an extreme case, we show that brand-keyword ads have no measurable short-term benefits." "For non-brand keywords we find that new and infrequent users are positively influenced by ads but that more frequent users whose purchasing behavior is not influenced by ads account for most of the advertising expenses, resulting in average returns that are negative." Transfer: the mechanism (people who would buy anyway click the ad) applies to Amazon brand and repeat-buyer terms; the size does not transfer (Google/Bing, a retailer buying search ads, 2012). [research]
- **Simonov, Nosko & Rao, "Competition and Crowd-Out for Brand Keywords in Sponsored Search"**, *Marketing Science* 37(2), 2018, 200–215. Bing experiments, thousands of brands. "When no competitors are present, we find a positive, statistically significant impact of brand ads of 1%–4%, with larger brands having a smaller causal effect." "When the focal brand ad is present, competitors in paid positions 2–4 can "steal" 1%–5% of the focal brand's clicks"; for brands that do not advertise, "competitors "steal" 18%–42% of clicks, suggesting a strong causal effect of position. Under such position effects, we find the return on investment on defensive advertising to be strongly positive." Transfer: on a brand query the own ad adds little when no rival bids, and much when rivals would otherwise take the top slot; on Amazon whether rivals bid on the brand term is observable (search-term impression share, SB/SP ads on the term). [research]
- **Simonov & Hill, "Competitive Advertising on Brand Search: Traffic Stealing and Click Quality"**, *Marketing Science* 40(5), 2021, 923–945. Bing quasi-experiment: competitors steal "6%–15% instead of 1%–2% of traffic" if the focal brand's link is removed from the top paid slot; "the probability of an immediate conversion on these "stolen" clicks is low, with around 20%–47% of consumers returning to Bing in less than 30 seconds". Transfer: stolen brand traffic converts worse than the brand's own; the value of defence is less than the click share suggests. [research]
- **Golden & Horton, "The Effects of Search Advertising on Competitors: An Experiment Before a Merger"**, *Management Science* 67(1), 2021, 342–362. A firm suspended Google ads in random US markets: "By shutting off its ads, Firm Vary lost customers, but only 63% as many as a nonexperimental estimate would have suggested"; spillovers to its close competitor "were surprisingly small". Transfer: a geographic holdout recovered a smaller effect than observational estimates; Amazon offers sellers no geographic targeting of Sponsored Products, so the design does not carry over directly. [research; the last clause is the seat's]
- **Moshary, "Does Sponsored Search Advertising Augment Organic Search? Evidence from an E-Commerce Platform"**, *Management Science* 71(11), 2025, 9687–9709. "I leverage a field experiment that blocks all sponsored search advertising for 3% of site visitors. The experiment shows that sponsored search advertising both steals business from organic listings and also reduces the total volume of transactions intermediated by the platform. However, in this case study, I find that sponsored search is nonetheless profitable for the platform because ad revenue more than compensates for the reduction in commissions". Transfer: the closest marketplace setting found, an unnamed platform; it measures the platform's totals with all ads off, not one seller's ads on or off. It shows that sponsored listings can take sales from organic ones at the market level. [research]

### When paid exposure helps organic outcomes

- **Ju, Zhao & Aral, "Advertising Spillovers in Mobile Apps: Evidence from Ad Shutoffs and Store Rankings"**, arXiv 2504.16151 (v1 April 22, 2025; v2 July 10, 2026; preprint). A global app-install ad shutoff at a US mobile game developer: "the shutoff decreased organic installs by 20-30%"; "every $100 spent is associated with 32 paid installs and 2.2 organic installs"; "paid installs boost app store category rankings, thereby increasing organic visibility"; "the relationship between organic installs and ad spend disappears once these rankings are factored in". Transfer: this is the mechanism Amazon practitioners claim (paid sales → rank → organic sales) measured in another store whose rankings respond to sales; it is not Amazon evidence, and Amazon has not said its search rank responds to ad sales (§F). [research]
- **Yang & Ghose, "Analyzing the Relationship Between Organic and Sponsored Search Advertising: Positive, Negative, or Zero Interdependence?"**, *Marketing Science* 29(4), 2010, 602–623. Google, a national retailer, several hundred keywords, three months: "click-throughs on organic listings have a positive interdependence with click-throughs on paid listings, and vice versa", asymmetric ("the impact of organic clicks on increases in utility from paid clicks is 3.5 times stronger"), worth 4.2%–6.15% of expected profit in counterfactuals. Transfer: on web search, showing both listings can beat showing one; whether this holds on Amazon is untested here. [research]
- **Abhishek, Jerath & Sharma, "The Impact of "Retail Media" on Online Marketplaces: Insights from a Field Experiment"**, *Information Systems Research* 36(1), 2025, 456–473. Flipkart field experiment: in electronics "the sponsored listings perform worse (in terms of clicks and conversions) than the organic listings that they replace, whereas the organic listings in the neighborhood of the sponsored listings perform better"; in clothing and footwear the effects reverse, "suggesting that sponsored listings might help the platform identify new high-relevance products and improve search rankings for these categories"; at search level, ~10 points more sponsored share "does not affect clicks and conversions in any product category." (These are the EconPapers abstract's words; the abstract Crossref and Semantic Scholar serve for the same DOI is shorter, names clicks only for electronics, "the clothing category" only, and says the sponsored share "does not affect the performance in any product category"; the findings agree.) Transfer: a large marketplace, randomised; the sign varies by category, which is the strongest published reason not to carry one answer across a seller's catalogue. [research]

### Paid slot versus organic slot

- **Joo, Shi & Abhishek, "Do Sellers Benefit from Sponsored Product Listings? Evidence from an Online Marketplace"**, *Marketing Science* 43(4), 2024, 817–839. Field study with experimental and natural variation on a mobile-app marketplace: "consumers prefer organic listings in the top-ranked positions to sponsored listings of the same product/position. Consumers become indifferent between sponsored and organic listings in the lower-ranked positions." "top-ranked organic listings are perceived as more credible than seller-subsidized sponsored listings." Yet "an advertising seller may benefit financially if a lower-ranked organic listing can be replaced with a top-ranked sponsored listing." Transfer: a top sponsored slot is worth less than a top organic slot of the same product, but more than a low organic slot. [research]
- **Yu, "The Welfare Effects of Sponsored Product Advertising"** (Chuan Yu, Harvard Business School, working paper, April 2024; AEA 2025 conference program). Data: "web scraping for several thousand high-traffic keywords six times a day over a two-month period in 2022" on Amazon, "over one million searches", about one million products with daily prices, sales ranks and reviews, rank-to-quantity conversion validated on "exact daily quantities for several hundred products over two years obtained from an Amazon seller". Findings: "moving up one position higher in the search results for a keyword increases a product's sales quantity by 0.36%"; "A product can appear as both organic and sponsored"; "top sponsored products have prices that are 16% higher than their organic counterparts"; sponsored products "exhibit lower average utility than their organic counterparts in similar locations"; advertising "tends to be more beneficial in markets with fewer established products and greater product differentiation". It also reports "According to Amazon's data, 81% of clicks happen on the first page" (cited, not Amazon's page). The model holds organic rankings fixed in its baseline counterfactual ("I assume that the distribution of organic ranks remains fixed in all counterfactual exercises"). Its Appendix F.3 ("Endogenous Organic Rankings") estimates an organic ranking score from the product's previous-week price and sales with product fixed effects and reports "a higher price is associated with a lower average score, while a larger sales volume is associated with a higher average score"; the welfare results with that ranking rule are "all qualitatively similar". This is the one Amazon-data estimate read that ties a product's organic rank to its own recent sales; it does not separate ad-driven from other sales, and it is a within-product correlation in scraped data, not an experiment. Transfer: Amazon data; position matters, and sponsored exposure helps less-established products most. [research]
- **Ursu, "The Power of Rankings: Quantifying the Effect of Rankings on Online Consumer Search and Purchase Decisions"**, *Marketing Science* 37(4), 2018, 530–552. Expedia randomised rankings: "rankings affect what consumers search, but conditional on search, do not affect purchases"; "an average position effect of $1.92, which is lower than literature estimates obtained without experimental variation." (published abstract, Crossref and EconPapers) Transfer: position effects are real and smaller than observational estimates; not Amazon. [research]
- **Dash, Ghosh, Mukherjee, Chakraborty & Gummadi, "Sponsored is the New Organic: Implications of Sponsored Results on Quality of Search Results in the Amazon Marketplace"**, AIES 2024 (Proceedings 7, 316–327; arXiv 2407.19099). 4,800 simulated searches in four countries, 2M organic and 638K sponsored results: "items with poor organic ranks (beyond 100th position) appear as sponsored results even before the top organic results on the first page"; top sponsored results are mostly "costlier and are of poorer quality than the top organic results". Transfer: paid exposure for a query can be unrelated to organic rank for it; audit data, no causal claim on rank. [research]
- **Long, Jerath & Sarvary, "Designing an Online Retail Marketplace: Leveraging Information from Sponsored Advertising"**, *Marketing Science* 41(1), 2022, 115–138. Theory: "sellers' bids in ad auctions … can reveal the sellers' private information to the platform ("information effect"), which it can optimally combine with information that it has about consumers to improve the placement of organic results, a practice we call "strategic listing."" Under some conditions "the platform must also reduce the degree of strategic listing (i.e., commit to limiting the influence of the sponsored ad auction outcomes on the placement of organic results)." Transfer: a model of why a platform might or might not let ad outcomes inform organic rank; it says nothing about what Amazon does. [research]

### Measuring incrementality at all

- **Gordon, Zettelmeyer, Bhargava & Chapsky, "A Comparison of Approaches to Advertising Measurement: Evidence from Big Field Experiments at Facebook"**, *Marketing Science* 38(2), 2019, 193–225. 15 US experiments, 500 million user-experiment observations: "The observational methods often fail to produce the same effects as the randomized experiments, even after conditioning on extensive demographic and behavioral variables." [research]
- **Gordon, Moakler & Zettelmeyer, "Close Enough? A Large-Scale Exploration of Non-Experimental Approaches to Advertising Measurement"**, *Marketing Science* 42(4), 2023, 768–793. 663 Facebook experiments, over 5,000 user features: "The median RCT lifts are 29%, 18%, and 5% for the upper, middle, and lower funnel outcomes, respectively. Using DML (SPSM), the median lift by funnel is 83% (173%), 58% (176%), and 24% (64%)"; "we are unable to reliably estimate an ad campaign's causal effect." Transfer: an AMC exposed-versus-unexposed comparison (G3) is the kind of observational estimate these papers found biased upward, even with far richer features than AMC exposes. [research; transfer is the seat's]
- **Gordon, Moakler & Zettelmeyer, "Predicted Incrementality by Experimentation (PIE) for Ad Measurement"**, arXiv 2304.06828 (v2 April 1, 2026). 2,226 Meta experiments: a model trained on RCTs predicts incrementality with "out-of-sample R² = 0.88 for incremental conversions per dollar, compared to R² = 0.19 for industry-standard 7-day last-click attribution." Transfer: last-click attributed conversions, Amazon's standard (§A2), are a weak predictor of incremental conversions in the one large dataset that measured both. [research]
- **Lewis, Zettelmeyer, Gordon, Garib, Hermle, Perry, Romero & Schnaidt, "Amazon Ads Multi-Touch Attribution"**, arXiv 2508.08209, August 11, 2025 (Amazon authors; correspondence causal@amazon.com). Illustration: if an RCT shows 900 incremental conversions where 7-day last-touch attribution shows 1,000, the calibration factor implies "10% fewer incremental conversions than what we think when using a last-touch attributed metric"; Amazon's system holds "a database of hundreds of thousands of RCTs across our ad products that spans most of our advertisers and campaign types"; it quotes Gordon et al. 2023 as showing observational errors "ranging from 488% to 948%" and the IAB/MRC 2024 retail media guidelines as "highly recommends using RCTs". The 900/1,000 is an illustration, not a measured Amazon ratio. Transfer: Amazon runs incrementality experiments internally and uses them to calibrate MTA credit; it publishes no per-advertiser incrementality readout for Sponsored Products (G6). [research]
- **Lewis & Wong, "Incrementality Bidding and Attribution"**, arXiv 2208.12809, 2022: "The causal effect of showing an ad to a potential customer versus not, commonly referred to as "incrementality", is the fundamental question of advertising effectiveness." A methods proposal. [research]

### Read from abstracts by the verifier (Crossref, 2026-11-09)

- **Lewis & Rao, "The Unfavorable Economics of Measuring the Returns to Advertising"**, *QJE* 130(4), 2015, 1941–1973. "Twenty-five large field experiments with major U.S. retailers and brokerages"; "The median confidence interval on return on investment is over 100 percentage points wide"; "informative advertising experiments can easily require more than 10 million person-weeks"; "selection bias, due to the targeted nature of advertising, is a crippling concern for widely employed observational methods." Transfer: a single seller's own on/off test is noisy by construction; wide intervals such as Hershaff's are the expected outcome, not a flaw of one test. [research; transfer is the seat's]
- **Johnson, Lewis & Nubbemeyer, "Ghost Ads: Improving the Economics of Measuring Online Ad Effectiveness"**, *JMR* 54(6), 2017, 867–884. A method that identifies "the control group counterparts of the exposed consumers in a randomized experiment"; in a retailer's display retargeting campaign "the ads lifted website visits by 17.2% and purchases by 10.5%". Transfer: the design needs the ad platform's cooperation; no Amazon page read offers it to Sponsored Products sellers (§G6). [research]
- **Sahni, "Advertising Spillovers: Evidence from Online Field Experiments and Implications for Returns on Advertising"**, *JMR* 53(4), 2016, 459–478. Randomized experiments on a restaurant-search website: "ads increase the chances of sales for nonadvertised restaurants significantly", concentrated on similar, highly rated rivals, and "The spillovers are largest when the intensity (frequency) of advertising is low." Transfer: an ad can send demand to competitors' similar listings; on Amazon the analogue is a shopper reached by an ad who buys a rival's product, which no seller-side report shows. [research]

### Audits of Amazon's search page: paid position against organic position

- **Rock, Strauss, O'Reilly & Mazzucato, "Behind the clicks: Can Amazon allocate user attention as it pleases?"**, *Information Economics and Policy* 69, December 2024, article 101115 (doi 10.1016/j.infoecopol.2024.101115; abstract via OpenAlex; full text read as UCL IIPP Working Paper 2023/11, the submitted version on UCL Discovery). Data: Amazon.com search results "scraped … between 11-19th June 2023", "154,172 product listings" for "2,250 of the most searched queries", first page only ("since more than 70% of Amazon shoppers do not click past it (FTC 2023)"), each query scraped once, joined to "the three most-clicked products from Amazon's Seller Central Search Term Report". Definitions: "Organic rank is an index telling us where a product appears in a list of Amazon's organic search results for a specific search query, when advertising is not included"; "Search rank is the indexed result on the screen of a product amongst all search results shown, including both organic and advertised results." Findings: "One-quarter of product search results on the first page are adverts. This leads to 48.3% of advertised results having at least one duplicate organic result on the first page, and 93.6% of top-3 most clicked ads being duplicated"; "The median advertisement appears 17 search ranks above its organic duplicate. However, top-3 most clicked advertisements tend to receive smaller boosts from sponsorship"; "The top-3 most clicked advertised products are around 17% more expensive than organic ones ($19.3 vs. $16.5) and one-third less relevant (organic rank of 4 vs. 3)"; abstract: "Among the top five search results, where typically four are advertisements, we find that neither decreased relevancy nor increased price significantly sways user decisions." Their own caveat: "Advertising may indirectly influence a product's organic ranking to the extent that sales volume is used by Amazon as a ranking factor (Turton 2023)." Transfer: on 2023 US data, a product that buys an ad on a query it already ranks for organically typically appears much higher on the page as the ad than as the organic listing, and the clicks follow prominence; this measures the paid slot's lift over the product's own organic slot on one page view, not any effect of ads on later organic rank. [research]
- **Farronato, Fradkin & MacKay, "Self-Preferencing at Amazon: Evidence from Search Rankings"**, *AEA Papers and Proceedings* 113, 2023, 239–243 (doi 10.1257/pandp.20231068; read as NBER Working Paper 30894, January 2023). A browser-extension panel: "228,281 search results– including both sponsored and organic results—in 3,019 unique searches conducted by 184 users"; "In 72.1% of searches consumers do not click past the first results page, and, based on scroll position data, only half of the products on a full page of results are actually seen by consumers"; "within a search spell, sponsored products have lower rank, i.e., they are closer to the top of the page. In particular, being sponsored pushes a product up by 7 positions on average, an 18% increase in prominence"; "The prominence given to Amazon brands is 30% to 60% of the prominence granted to sponsored products." They note the sponsored flag is captured "with some noise" for products in carousels. Transfer: a second, independent measurement that sponsorship moves a product's page position up by several places, on logged-in shoppers' own searches rather than a scraper's. [research]
- **Reimers & Waldfogel, "A Framework for Detection, Measurement, and Welfare Analysis of Platform Bias"**, NBER Working Paper 31766, October 2023. Defines a platform's ranks as "biased if they deliver outcomes that lie below the frontier that maximizes a weighted sum of seller and consumer surplus", and contrasts conditioning-on-observables tests (regressing rank on a platform dummy and controls, "the approach of Jürgensmeier and Skiera (2023) and Farronato et al. (2023)") with outcome-based tests. Its Amazon application is the Kindle Daily Deal page, not search, with quantities "inferred from sales ranks rather than directly observed" (Bookstat): Amazon-published ebooks get ranks "23.6 percent better (lower) than their non-platform counterparts, conditional on observable characteristics", and "conditional on the rank the platform assigns to them, Amazon books sell 50 percent less", which the authors read as bias in favour of Amazon titles; "the magnitudes are very different for Amazon" between the two tests. Transfer: the paper's point for a seller's reading is methodological: a ranking that correlates with observables can look biased or unbiased depending on the test, and sales-rank-inferred quantities carry their own error. [research]

### The "Sponsored" label itself: a randomised experiment

- **Sahni & Nair, "Sponsorship Disclosure and Consumer Deception: Experimental Evidence from Native Advertising in Mobile Search"**, *Marketing Science* 39(1), 2020, 5–32 (doi 10.1287/mksc.2018.1125; abstract via EconPapers). "We experimentally vary the format of paid-search advertising, the extent to which ads are disclosed to over 200,000 users, and track their anonymized browsing behavior including clicks and conversions" on "a mobile restaurant-search platform". Findings: "native advertising benefits advertisers and detect no evidence of deception under typically used formats of disclosure currently used in the paid-search marketplace"; "the incremental conversions due to advertising are not driven by users clicking on the native ads. Rather, the benefits from advertising are driven by users seeing the ads and later clicking on the advertiser's "organic" listings"; "mere exposure seems sufficient to produce most of the incremental effect of advertising." Transfer: in a randomised setting, an ad's measured value ran mostly through the advertiser's organic listing rather than the ad click, which is exactly the path Amazon's click-based Sponsored Products attribution (§A2, "Clicks only") does not credit; it is a restaurant-search app, not Amazon. Read beside Joo, Shi & Abhishek (shoppers prefer a top organic listing to a top sponsored one) the two studies measure different things: preference between the two slots, and where the ad's effect lands. [research; transfer is the seat's]

### What a sales rank means in units

- **Chevalier & Goolsbee, "Measuring Prices and Price Competition Online: Amazon.com and BarnesandNoble.com"**, *Quantitative Marketing and Economics* 1(2), 2003, 203–222 (doi 10.1023/A:1024634613982; read as NBER Working Paper 9085, 2002). "we use publicly available data on the sales ranks of about 20,000 books to derive quantity proxies". Method: "A standard distributional assumption for this type of rank data is a Pareto distribution (i.e., a power law)", giving ln(Rank) = c − θ ln(Sales); θ calibrated from a publisher's data (estimates "0.9 and 1.3"), their own purchase experiment (a book at rank 14,468 selling about two a day rose to 2,854 after six purchases, implying θ = 1.17) and an author's experiment; "all of these experiments suggest fairly consistent estimates of θ in the relatively tight range of 0.9 to 1.3 … We will use 1.2 as the basic estimate." Transfer: under a power law, equal proportional moves in rank mean equal proportional moves in sales, so halving a rank (20,000 to 10,000, or 200 to 100) multiplies the sales rate by 2^(1/θ), about 1.7 to 2.2 across their 0.9–1.3 range; the 2001 book-market slope does not carry over to another category or year. [research; transfer is the seat's]
- **Brynjolfsson, Hu & Smith, "Consumer Surplus in the Digital Economy: Estimating the Value of Increased Product Variety at Online Booksellers"**, *Management Science* 49(11), 2003, 1580–1596 (doi 10.1287/mnsc.49.11.1580.20580; abstract via Crossref and Semantic Scholar). The abstract reports the welfare result ("increased product variety of online bookstores enhanced consumer welfare by $731 million to $1.03 billion in the year 2000") and not the rank-to-quantity calibration; the full text (MIT DSpace) answered HTTP 429 to this seat, so no slope is taken from it (Unreachable). Yu (below) cites the later "Brynjolfsson et al. 2011" with Chevalier & Goolsbee for the stable rank-quantity relationship. [research, abstract only]
- **Yu, "The Welfare Effects of Sponsored Product Advertising"** (already above), Appendix B.2: "Amazon uses the BSR system to report a product's sales performance relative to that of other products within the same category over a recent period of time"; "The BSR is defined within one of approximately 25 product categories"; "While Amazon maintains confidentiality regarding the exact algorithm employed to calculate the BSR, it is generally believed that the BSR takes into account both recent and historical sales, with more recent sales carrying significantly higher weights"; "Prior studies have documented that when the number of products is sufficiently large, there exists a relatively stable and predictable relationship between a product's sales rank and its sales quantities (e.g., Chevalier and Goolsbee 2003; Brynjolfsson et al. 2011). This relationship is often modeled using functional forms such as power functions or splines." Yu used category mappings fitted by Jungle Scout ("I obtained these mappings estimated by Jungle Scout on May 16, 2022"; figures plot Log10 daily sales against Log10 sales rank) and validated them against "actual daily sales data obtained in collaboration with an Amazon seller, Forum Brands" for "several hundred products … over a two-year period": "For most products, the two series align remarkably well, both in the cross-section and for high-frequency variation". Transfer: BSR is a relative, category-specific, recency-weighted measure; a product's rank can improve because category rivals sold less (a stock-out, a season ending) with no change in its own sales, and the mapping to units differs by category and is a vendor's fit. [research; transfer is the seat's]

### How long an ad's effect lasts, on Amazon's evidence

- **Qin, "Lengthen your attribution window: which digital ads have the most long-term impact?"**, *Applied Marketing Analytics* 9(1), 2023, 73–83 (an Amazon Ads author; PDF on cdn.amazon.science, read 2026-11-09). "For 15 US brands across three verticals (Hardlines, Softlines and Consumables) on Amazon, it was found that within the two-week attribution window, upper/middle-funnel ad products only materialise 30–50 per cent of the total effects, compared to lower-funnel at 60–90 per cent." On Sponsored Products: "In contrast, SP does not show significant carry-over effects beyond three weeks for any brand. This is intuitive because SP is a lower-funnel ad product that aims at immediate returns"; "most of lower-funnel ads' (eg Sponsored Products) effects materialise within the attribution window"; appendix: "some ad products, such as SPs, see all their effects dissipate after three weeks. In contrast, other products, such as DSP display, show a large part of their impact beyond the fourth week." Category differences: "carry-over effects beyond two weeks are vertical specific, with the smallest for CPG (30 per cent) and the largest for Softlines (70 per cent)" (for detail page views). Transfer: Amazon's own published time-series estimate puts most of a Sponsored Products effect inside the attribution window. [research, Amazon-authored]
- Set beside the other figures in this file: Lily Tong, "a director of measurement at Amazon Ads", quoted by Digiday ("What we actually find is that roughly 20% of the sales value for a brand shows up over time and isn't captured in that immediate attribution window because that's just a cutoff date for measurement"; Seb Joseph, "Amazon wants to own the long game in ad measurement", October 1, 2026, https://digiday.com/media-buying/amazon-wants-to-own-the-long-game-in-ad-measurement/, read 2026-11-09) [opinion, press report of an Amazon statement], states the figure for "a brand", not for Sponsored Products, and next to the long-term-sales measures of §C4; Hershaff's Meridian adstock of about 0.35 per day (§What practitioners add) is one seller's all-ads estimate and decays within days; Ju, Zhao & Aral's app-store effect runs through a store ranking, a different path from adstock. The three Amazon-side figures agree that Sponsored Products' direct effect is short; none measures a rank-mediated organic effect on Amazon. Window design is `method/change-measurement.md`'s, which owns this question; metrics-and-attribution carries Qin as P-30. [seat]

### What the research adds up to [seat]

On search ads generally and on two marketplaces, paid exposure has been measured as substituting for organic sales (eBay brand terms; Moshary's platform; Flipkart electronics), complementing them (Yang & Ghose; Flipkart clothing), and feeding a sales-responsive store ranking (Ju et al.). On brand terms the own ad adds little when no rival bids and protects a lot when rivals would take the slot (Simonov et al.). Observational lift estimates run high against experiments (Gordon et al.; Golden & Horton; Lewis & Rao). On Amazon data, a product's organic rank score moves with its own previous-week sales (Yu, Appendix F.3), which is consistent with, but does not demonstrate, an ad-sales-to-rank path. None of these studies is a seller's Sponsored Products on Amazon; the evidence fixes the questions, not the answers. Two audits of Amazon's own result pages (Rock et al.; Farronato et al.) measure that a sponsored listing sits several to seventeen places above where the same product sits organically, so on a single page view the ad buys position the organic listing does not have. A randomised disclosure experiment (Sahni & Nair) found an ad's incremental effect arriving mostly through the advertiser's organic listing, a path click attribution does not credit. Amazon's own published time-series estimate (Qin) finds little Sponsored Products carry-over past three weeks. The organic outcome most readings use, Best Sellers Rank, is a relative, recency-weighted, category-specific rank whose mapping to units is a fitted power law (Chevalier & Goolsbee; Yu).

## Stable enough for a skill

Each item is a fact that held on every page read for it and that a skill can state without carrying a figure. Figures named inside an item are read at run time.

1. **An ad-attributed sale and an ordered sale are different measurements of different events.** Ads count a purchase on the date of the ad interaction, inside a lookback window, in the campaign's time zone, without tax or cart discounts, net of cancellations within 72 hours; the Sales and Traffic report counts ordered product sales as price times units on the period's date. Sources: Ad campaign attribution; Overview of the Reporting v1 API; Dates and historical data retention; sellerSalesAndTrafficReport.json.
2. **For a seller's Sponsored Products, attributed sales cover the advertised product and any other product the same merchant sells.** v3 splits them as `SameSku` and `OtherSku`; v1 as Promoted and Halo, with `productRelevance.value` and `convertedProduct.id` naming the product bought. Sources: Ad campaign attribution; Reporting columns (version 3); Purchased product reports; Overview of the Reporting v1 API; Product relevance and Converted product dimension pages.
3. **v1 base conversion metrics include halo.** `metric.purchases` and `metric.sales` are promoted plus halo; the promoted-only figure is the `…Promoted` field. A v3-to-v1 migration that maps `sales14d` to `metric.sales` without reading the qualifier changes what is counted. Sources: Overview of the Reporting v1 API; Purchases (v1 metric page); All releases (Stream v2 and Reporting v1 GA).
4. **Reporting v3 is deprecated on the date on its own banner.** Sources: Purchased product reports; Reporting columns (version 3).
5. **One conversion is credited to at most one ad interaction across all campaigns and advertisers**, so attributed sales summed over campaigns do not double count within Amazon's attribution, and a sale credited to one campaign is not credited to another. Source: Ad campaign attribution.
6. **Attributed figures for recent days are incomplete and are restated.** The window has to close and restatements follow. Sources: Ad campaign attribution; Sponsored ads reporting FAQ (version 3); Amazon Ads reporting overview.
7. **Amazon names TACOS in two library pages as ad spend as a percentage of total sales, and fixes neither field.** No Ads API metric, report column or help page read defines it; which ad products and costs form the numerator, and which sales field (ordered or shipped, gross or net, which marketplaces, which day boundary) forms the denominator, are the reader's choice. Sources: The author's guide to Sponsored Products; Holiday advertising myths: What experts want you to know; Reporting columns (version 3) and the v1 metric pages (no such field); practitioners (Jungle Scout, Perpetua, Helium 10, PPC Land) agree on the same form.
8. **No Amazon page read says ad sales are, or are not, an input to organic search rank.** Amazon's own marketing words stop at "can help offers rise through search results" and "improve product visibility". Sources: Amazon SEO blog; Sponsored Products product page; Amazon Science ranking talk; the Seller Forums threads read.
9. **Search Query Performance counts the ASIN's search-results-page funnel per query and does not split paid from organic.** Source: sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json. (Amazon's help page saying it includes "organic and Sponsored Products" was not re-opened here; see Unreachable.)
10. **New-to-brand means no purchase from the brand in the previous 12 months, and Amazon's help page says it is not available for Sponsored Products.** v3 reports it for Sponsored Brands, Display and TV only; the v1 metric page lists Sponsored Products as eligible, so the two Amazon pages differ and a v1 report answers for a given account. Sources: Ad campaign attribution; Sponsored ads new-to-brand metrics; Purchases (new to brand); Reporting columns (version 3).
11. **Long-term sales are ad-attributed estimates, not measured incremental sales, and exclude Sponsored Products in v1.** Sources: Long-term sales (v1 metric page); All releases (June 5, 2025; September 25, 2026).
12. **No Amazon page read offers a self-serve incrementality measurement for Sponsored Products.** Brand Lift, a survey study, excludes Sponsored Products in Amazon's words; multi-touch attribution splits credit and is labelled beta, US; AMC's exposed-versus-unexposed split is an observational comparison; MMM supplies data to a model the advertiser runs. Sources: Amazon Brand Lift product page; Brand Lift launch page; Multi-touch attribution launch page; Conversions all table; MMM overview.
13. **AMC compares ad-exposed and non-exposed conversions and holds the whole Amazon retail purchase record for the advertiser's products**, subject to aggregation floors, UTC by default, with paid-feature tables. Sources: Conversions all table; Amazon Retail Purchases in AMC; Data aggregation thresholds in AMC; AMC data sources overview; Paid features overview.
14. **Off-Amazon traffic to the listing is measured by Amazon Attribution tags; Brand Referral Bonus pays on that traffic and not on Amazon ads.** Sources: Amazon Attribution API overview; Amazon Attribution product page; Brand Referral Bonus blog.
15. **Attributed and incremental sales can differ by a large factor, and the sign of paid-on-organic spillover varies with setting and category.** Sources: Blake, Nosko & Tadelis; Moshary; Abhishek, Jerath & Sharma; Ju, Zhao & Aral; Yang & Ghose; Gordon et al. 2019, 2023; Lewis et al. 2025.
16. **On a brand query, an own ad adds little when no rival bids, and recovers traffic rivals would take when they do.** Sources: Simonov, Nosko & Rao; Simonov & Hill; Blake, Nosko & Tadelis.
17. **In one marketplace field study, shoppers preferred a top organic listing to a top sponsored listing of the same product, were indifferent lower down, and a seller could still gain by replacing a low organic listing with a top sponsored one.** On Amazon data, sponsored products showed lower average utility than organic ones in similar positions, and poorly ranked organic items appeared as top sponsored results. Sources: Joo, Shi & Abhishek; Yu; Dash et al.
18. **A vendor's total-sales side is Vendor Retail Analytics, not Sales and Traffic.** `GET_VENDOR_SALES_REPORT` gives `orderedRevenue` and `orderedUnits` (only under `distributorView` `MANUFACTURING`), `shippedRevenue`, `shippedCogs`, `shippedUnits` and `customerReturns`, aggregate and by ASIN; `MANUFACTURING` covers ASINs the vendor manufactures whoever sourced them, `SOURCING` the ASINs the vendor group sourced; all Vendor Retail Analytics data is in PST whatever the marketplace. Sources: Analytics Reports (SP-API); vendorSalesReport.json; vendorTrafficReport.json; vendorInventoryReport.json.
19. **AMC's Amazon Retail Purchases matches Business Reports "By ASIN Detail" for sellers and Retail Analytics Sales with Distributor View = Manufacturing for vendors.** Source: Amazon Retail Purchases in AMC.
20. **No Amazon API or report returns a product's organic search position.** Amazon exposes Best Sellers Rank (Catalog Items `salesRanks`), advertisers' paid impression share and rank per search term (`metric.impressionShare`, `metric.impressionShareRank`), and search-funnel shares (SQP, Search Catalog Performance, Top Search Terms), none of which is a position. Sources: catalogItems_2022-04-01.json; Search (Impression share); sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json; sellingPartnerSearchCatalogPerformanceReport.json; sellingPartnerSearchTermsReport.json.
21. **Search Catalog Performance counts sponsored results in its impressions.** Its `impressionCount` "Impressions include sponsored search results"; it, like SQP, has no field that separates paid from organic. Source: sellingPartnerSearchCatalogPerformanceReport.json.
22. **Third-party rank trackers sample one search context per check, at a cadence and depth they choose; Helium 10 and SmartScout say the rank seen varies with location (and, for Helium 10, browser, time and login state).** Sources: Helium 10 Keyword Tracker blog and masterclass; DataDive, Jungle Scout and Scale Insights help pages; SmartScout Keyword Ranking Map.
23. **Best Sellers Rank is relative to a category's other products, is "generally believed" (Yu) to weight recent sales more, and maps to units by a fitted power law.** A product's rank can move with no change in its own sales. Sources: Chevalier & Goolsbee 2003; Yu (Appendix B.2); Catalog Items `ItemClassificationSalesRank`.
24. **On Amazon's search pages, sponsorship places a product well above its own organic position.** Sources: Rock, Strauss, O'Reilly & Mazzucato 2024 (median 17 ranks above its organic duplicate, 2023 data); Farronato, Fradkin & MacKay 2023 (7 positions on average).
25. **Amazon's own published estimate finds little Sponsored Products carry-over past three weeks.** Source: Qin 2023 (Amazon Ads author).
26. **The AMC instructional-query library is in the AMC console; the IQ names Amazon publishes outside it are few and named on the playbook and release-note pages.** Sources: Manage a workflow; Working with AMC sandbox; Customer journey analytics and Customer long-term value playbooks; All releases.

## Positions

Each position is a fact, worded as one, with its source in the sections above.

**P-ads-organic-and-total-1.** Amazon credits an ad-attributed purchase to the date of the ad interaction, in the campaign's time zone, after a lookback window, without tax or cart discounts and net of cancellations within 72 hours; the Sales and Traffic report counts ordered product sales as price times units for the period's date. The two series measure different things on different days (Ad campaign attribution; Overview of the Reporting v1 API; Dates and historical data retention; sellerSalesAndTrafficReport.json; §A1–A3, §B6, §D1–D2, §D6) [official].

**P-ads-organic-and-total-2.** For a seller's Sponsored Products, attributed sales include the advertised product and any other product the same merchant sells; the advertised-product split is in v3 `SameSku`/`OtherSku` and `purchasedAsin`, and in v1 the Promoted and Halo qualifiers with `productRelevance.value` and `convertedProduct.id` (Ad campaign attribution; Reporting columns (version 3); Purchased product reports; Purchases (Amazon retail conversions); Product relevance and Converted product dimension pages; §A2, §B1–B2, §B5, §B7) [official].

**P-ads-organic-and-total-3.** Reporting v1's base conversion metrics include halo conversions; the promoted-only figure is the field with the Promoted qualifier (Overview of the Reporting v1 API; Purchases (Amazon retail conversions); §B4–B5) [official].

**P-ads-organic-and-total-4.** Amazon attributes each conversion to at most one ad interaction across all campaigns and advertisers (Ad campaign attribution; §A4) [official].

**P-ads-organic-and-total-5.** Attributed conversions for a report date are incomplete until the lookback window closes, and Amazon restates conversion data 1, 7 and 28 days after the conversion event, so a 14-day window can be updated up to 42 days after the report date (Ad campaign attribution; Sponsored ads reporting FAQ (version 3); §A1, §A6) [official].

**P-ads-organic-and-total-6.** Amazon names TACOS on two of its guidance pages as ad spend as a percentage of total sales, total sales being "organic and paid"; no Amazon Ads API metric, report column or help page read defines it, and neither page names the sales field or the ad products counted, so the numerator and denominator are each reader's choice; the benchmark ranges read (Helium 10) cite no data source, a point PPC Land makes in general ("Vendors publish target ranges without data") (The author's guide to Sponsored Products; Holiday advertising myths: What experts want you to know; Reporting columns (version 3); §What practitioners add, TACoS) [official; opinion for the benchmark ranges].

**P-ads-organic-and-total-7.** No Amazon help page, API document, policy or staff reply read on 2026-11-09 states whether ad-attributed sales are an input to organic search rank; Amazon's own words stop at Sponsored Products ads "can help offers rise through search results" and "improve product visibility" (Amazon SEO: 7 ways to improve your product's search rankings; Sponsored Products product page; Seller Forums threads read; §F) [official for the quoted words; the absence is as searched].

**P-ads-organic-and-total-8.** The Search Query Performance report gives an ASIN's impressions, clicks, cart adds and purchases from the search results page per query, with shares of the query total, and carries no field that separates sponsored from organic (sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json; §E1) [official].

**P-ads-organic-and-total-9.** New-to-brand is a purchase by a shopper with no purchase from the brand in the previous 12 months; Amazon's new-to-brand help page says the data "isn't available for Sponsored Products" and v3 reports it for Sponsored Brands, Display and TV only, while the v1 metric page lists Sponsored Products as eligible for most NTB fields, so two Amazon pages differ on Sponsored Products; AMC's new-to-brand columns include modeled conversions from November 2, 2026 (Ad campaign attribution; Sponsored ads new-to-brand metrics; Purchases (new to brand); Reporting columns (version 3); All releases, November 2, 2026; §C1–C2, §C6–C7) [official].

**P-ads-organic-and-total-10.** Long-term sales is an ad-attributed estimate of a year's value of new-to-brand engagements plus attributed sales, offered for Sponsored Brands, Display, TV and DSP and not for Sponsored Products in v1 (Long-term sales (Amazon retail conversions); All releases, June 5, 2025 and September 25, 2026; §C3–C4) [official].

**P-ads-organic-and-total-11.** No Amazon page read on 2026-11-09 offers sellers a self-serve incrementality measurement of Sponsored Products: Amazon's Brand Lift page says sponsored ads are eligible "except for Sponsored Products"; multi-touch attribution divides credit and is labelled beta in the US; AMC's ad-exposed versus non-exposed conversions are an observational split; MMM data feeds a model the advertiser runs (Amazon Brand Lift; Uncover ad value across the funnel with multi-touch attribution; Conversions all table; Marketing Mix Modeling overview; §G) [official for each page's words; the absence is as searched].

**P-ads-organic-and-total-12.** AMC holds, for an advertiser's own instance, ad traffic events with search term and placement, ad-exposed and non-exposed conversions, and all Amazon store purchases of the advertiser's products, in UTC by default, with output limited to aggregates above a distinct-user floor (Sponsored ads traffic; Conversions all table; Amazon Retail Purchases in AMC; AMC data sources overview; Data aggregation thresholds in AMC; §G3) [official].

**P-ads-organic-and-total-13.** `getOrderMetrics` takes an IANA time zone for the day boundary, so seller-side daily ordered sales can be cut on the ads profile's time zone; no Amazon page read states the time zone of a Sales and Traffic report day (sales.json; sellerSalesAndTrafficReport.json; §D4, §D7) [official; the absence is as searched].

**P-ads-organic-and-total-14.** Data Kiosk Economics returns, per MSKU and day, the seller's ad charges by ad type beside ordered and net product sales; those charges are spend, not attributed sales (analytics_economics_2024_03_15.graphql; §D5) [official].

**P-ads-organic-and-total-15.** Off-Amazon traffic to Amazon listings is measured by Amazon Attribution tags, and the Brand Referral Bonus is paid on Attribution-tagged sales and not on Amazon advertising (Amazon Attribution API overview; Brand Referral Bonus; §H) [official].

**P-ads-organic-and-total-16.** In published field experiments, observational or attributed estimates of advertising returns exceeded the experimentally measured ones: on eBay's paid search, brand-keyword ads had "no measurable short-term benefits" and returns were "a fraction of conventional non-experimental estimates"; a geographic Google-ads shutoff lost "only 63% as many" customers as a non-experimental estimate implied; on Facebook, observational methods failed to recover experimental lifts (Blake, Nosko & Tadelis 2015; Golden & Horton 2021; Gordon et al. 2019, 2023) [research].

**P-ads-organic-and-total-17.** In published field experiments on marketplaces and app stores, the effect of sponsored listings on organic outcomes was negative in some settings (Moshary's platform; Flipkart electronics) and positive in others (Flipkart clothing and footwear; a mobile game whose paid installs raised store rank); no published experiment read measures a seller's Sponsored Products against its Amazon organic sales (Moshary 2025; Abhishek, Jerath & Sharma 2025; Ju, Zhao & Aral 2025) [research].

**P-ads-organic-and-total-18.** On a brand search, the measured incremental effect of the brand's own ad was small when no competitor advertised and larger when competitors would otherwise take the top paid positions (Simonov, Nosko & Rao 2018; Simonov & Hill 2021; Bing experiments) [research].

**P-ads-organic-and-total-19.** On Amazon, one study measured that moving one position up a keyword's results raised a product's sales quantity by about a third of a percent, and that a product can appear both organically and as sponsored on the same results page (Yu, working paper April 2024, 2022 data) [research]. The figure is **(human readers only)**.

**P-ads-organic-and-total-20.** A total-account series (ad spend, attributed sales, ordered sales, their ratio) establishes how the quantities moved together over the dates read; price, deals, stock, Featured Offer share, reviews, seasonality and competitors' actions move ordered sales over the same dates, and the series alone does not separate their effect from the ads' (Amazon SEO: 7 ways to improve your product's search rankings, on price and listing quality moving rank [official]; Gordon et al. 2019, 2023 and Lewis & Rao 2015, on observational estimates [research]; PPC Land, on TACoS moving with price cuts, stock-outs and seasonality [opinion]).

**P-ads-organic-and-total-21.** A vendor's total-sales side is Vendor Retail Analytics: `GET_VENDOR_SALES_REPORT` returns ordered revenue and units (under the `MANUFACTURING` view only), shipped revenue, shipped COGS, shipped units and customer returns, for a `distributorView` of `MANUFACTURING` (ASINs the vendor manufactures, whoever sourced them) or `SOURCING` (ASINs the vendor group sourced) and a `sellingProgram`; traffic is `glanceViews` and inventory includes sellable on-hand units and a procurable out-of-stock rate (Analytics Reports; vendorSalesReport.json; vendorTrafficReport.json; vendorInventoryReport.json; §D8–D9) [official].

**P-ads-organic-and-total-22.** All Vendor Retail Analytics data is reported in Pacific Standard Time whatever the marketplace, and the Analytics Reports page states no time zone for the seller Sales and Traffic report (Analytics Reports; §D7, §D10) [official].

**P-ads-organic-and-total-23.** AMC's Amazon Retail Purchases matches Business Reports "By ASIN Detail" for sellers and Retail Analytics Sales with Distributor View = Manufacturing for vendors (Amazon Retail Purchases in AMC; §D11) [official].

**P-ads-organic-and-total-24.** No Amazon API or report read returns a product's organic search position: Catalog Items returns Best Sellers Rank, the Ads API returns an advertiser's paid impression share and rank per search term, and Brand Analytics returns search-funnel counts and shares; the console's Search Term Impression Share report is listed as not available in the API (catalogItems_2022-04-01.json; Search (Impression share); Advertising console vs. API reports; the Brand Analytics report models; §E5–E7) [official; the absence is as searched].

**P-ads-organic-and-total-25.** Search Catalog Performance's impressions "include sponsored search results", and neither it nor SQP has a field separating paid from organic (sellingPartnerSearchCatalogPerformanceReport.json; sellingPartnerSearchQueryPerformanceReport.json; §E5) [official].

**P-ads-organic-and-total-26.** Every organic-rank series is a third-party sample: Helium 10 checks once a day (hourly on Boost) to 306 positions and says rank varies with location, browser, time and login state; SmartScout samples per state with a zip code and says a national number "buries the spread"; DataDive and Jungle Scout publish a daily cadence and no sampling frame; none read publishes a validation against Amazon data (Helium 10 Keyword Tracker blog and masterclass; SmartScout Keyword Ranking Map; DataDive and Jungle Scout help pages; §What practitioners add, Rank trackers) [opinion].

**P-ads-organic-and-total-27.** Amazon's AMC instructional-query library sits in the AMC console's Use Cases tab, behind sign-in; the IQ names Amazon publishes outside it include "Path to conversion by campaign group", "Path to conversion by campaign category", "Customer Value - Average Spending by Exposure Group", "Tentpole Phase Overlap" and "How to query Sponsored Display traffic", and none read is named for Sponsored Products overlap with organic purchases (Manage a workflow; Working with AMC sandbox; Customer journey analytics and Customer long-term value playbooks; Traffic events API data sources; §G7) [official; the absence is as searched].

**P-ads-organic-and-total-28.** On 2023 Amazon.com first-page results, the median advertisement appeared 17 search ranks above the same product's organic listing, with 48.3% of ads having an organic duplicate on the first page; on a shopper panel, being sponsored moved a product up 7 positions on average (Rock, Strauss, O'Reilly & Mazzucato 2024; Farronato, Fradkin & MacKay 2023; §Published research, audits) [research].

**P-ads-organic-and-total-29.** Whether a ranking is biased depends on the test: on the Kindle Daily Deal page, Amazon-published ebooks got ranks 23.6% better conditional on observables and sold 50% less conditional on rank, and the quantities were inferred from sales ranks (Reimers & Waldfogel 2023; §Published research, audits) [research].

**P-ads-organic-and-total-30.** In a randomised experiment on a mobile restaurant-search platform with over 200,000 users, the incremental conversions from advertising came mostly from users who saw the ad and later clicked the advertiser's organic listing, not from ad clicks (Sahni & Nair 2020; §Published research, the "Sponsored" label) [research].

**P-ads-organic-and-total-31.** Best Sellers Rank reports sales relative to other products in the same category, and the rank-to-quantity mapping is modelled as a power law: Chevalier & Goolsbee estimated the slope θ at 0.9 to 1.3 on 2001 book data, and Yu validated category mappings fitted by Jungle Scout against a seller's daily sales (Chevalier & Goolsbee 2003; Yu, Appendix B.2; catalogItems_2022-04-01.json; §Published research, sales rank) [research; official for the BSR definition].

**P-ads-organic-and-total-32.** Amazon's "bought in past month" label, as Keepa exposes it in `monthlySold`, is a floor bucket that most ASINs lack and that is variation-specific; it moves only when a product crosses a bucket boundary (Keepa `Product.java` [vendor doc]; PMG; Marketplace Pulse [opinion]; §What practitioners add, Bought in past month).

**P-ads-organic-and-total-33.** In its complaint, the FTC alleges that Amazon more than doubled top-of-page ads on desktop queries in one year, increased irrelevant ads its staff called "defects", rejected relevance "guardrails" on senior executives' "clear guidance", and that an internal study found higher ad loads "decreases purchase rates and increases search abandonment"; these are allegations, not findings, and trial is set for March 29, 2027 (FTC v. Amazon, Document 114, ¶¶230–238; PYMNTS, October 11, 2026; §F6) [allegation; opinion for the trial date].

### Seat's positions (enter no skill)

- [seat] The most defensible incrementality reading a single seller can make without Amazon's help is a time-alternating on/off design read against total ordered sales on a matched day boundary, with carryover accounted for; Hershaff's one-account example shows the method and how wide the answer stays. Design belongs to `method/change-measurement.md`.
- [seat] TACoS is most useful as a ratio of two series the reader has aligned (same days, same marketplace, same currency, a named sales field), and least useful as a target; the gap between attributed ROAS and a total-sales reading is where the cannibalisation question sits, not its answer.
- [seat] Brand-term Sponsored Products spend is where the research most strongly predicts low incrementality and where competitor presence changes the answer most; the evidence for competitor presence on a brand term is in the seller's own search-term and impression-share data (`beyond/brand-defense-and-competition.md`).
- [seat] The claim that ad sales lift organic rank is plausible by analogy with app stores and with Amazon's matching models trained on purchases, and is unconfirmed by Amazon; a skill that states it as fact would be stating a practitioner belief.
- [seat] AMC's free 2026 window for `conversions_all` and ARP makes 2026 the year a seller with an instance can see all-store purchases beside ad exposure for its own products at no table cost; after the window the same reading depends on a paid subscription.
