## What this covers

This file covers money on the sponsored-ads side of an Amazon advertising account. It covers how much a campaign may spend, how Amazon spends it across a day and a month, how cx can tell that a campaign or account has stopped serving for money reasons, and what Amazon offers for raising, capping, sharing and recommending budgets. The scope:

- the daily budget of Sponsored Products (SP), Sponsored Brands (SB) and Sponsored Display (SD): monthly averaging, the 25% / 100% over-delivery setting, the month cap and the invoice adjustment, and mid-day and mid-month changes;
- lifetime budgets (SB) and the daily-or-lifetime choice;
- the seller-only SP account daily budget cap;
- detecting out-of-budget: serving status and delivery reasons, the budget usage API, the budget recommendations and missed-opportunity APIs (SP, SB, SD), the initial budget recommendation for new SP campaigns, and the console budget report;
- budget rules: schedule-based, performance-based, recommended events, hours of day, stacking, evaluation timing, and how a rule-raised budget is read back;
- portfolios: date-range and monthly-recurring caps, and unspent-budget sharing;
- campaign budgets in Ads API v1 (`budgets[]`, `recurrenceTimePeriod`, per-marketplace budgets and currency on global campaigns);
- budget limits by marketplace;
- invalid traffic (IVT) and the credits it produces;
- billing as far as it changes spend: payment methods, credit limits and invoice thresholds, payment failure as a cause of suspension, promotional credits, the billing API and its notifications;
- intraday pacing and allocation as practitioners describe them;
- plan-level pacing (added 2026-11-09): monthly spend plans per marketplace and currency, what Amazon offers for them (portfolio caps per profile, global campaigns' per-marketplace budgets) and the third-party plan tools;
- what the missed-opportunity estimates measure and do not (added 2026-11-09);
- research on budget-constrained bidding and pacing.

## Amazon's own rules and guidance

Each item is marked RULE (Amazon states it as a requirement or a mechanism, or the API enforces it) or GUIDANCE (Amazon recommends it). Amazon's budget material is spread over four hosts:

- Amazon Ads help pages;
- library guides and "what's new" posts on advertising.amazon.com;
- the Ads API guides and release notes;
- the OpenAPI specifications.

The hosts disagree on several figures. The disagreements are recorded where they occur, with the page that is newer or more specific named.

### A. What a daily budget is and how Amazon spends it

- RULE: "A bid is how much you'll spend per click, and your budget is how much you're willing to spend on clicks per day. Each time a shopper clicks your ad, the bid amount will be deducted from the daily budget. Budgets are spent as quickly as possible and not paced throughout the duration of your campaign." [official] *Understand budgets in Sponsored ads* (help GTGPQGUXNCTHE2DS), https://advertising.amazon.com/help/GTGPQGUXNCTHE2DS
  - [seat] "the bid amount will be deducted" is the page's simplification. The auction page says the charge "may exceed the runner-up bid but will never exceed the maximum bid you authorize" (next item). What is deducted is the price charged, not the bid.
- RULE: "the price charged to you may exceed the runner-up bid but will never exceed the maximum bid you authorize". [official] *Understand Amazon Ads auctions* (help GEXGZNBBWPZZK7P8), https://advertising.amazon.com/help/GEXGZNBBWPZZK7P8
- RULE: "A daily budget is the maximum amount that you're willing to spend, on average, each day over a calendar month. You won't necessarily spend all of your daily budget each day. If you do reach your daily budget within a day, your campaign will pause until the following day when your daily budget resets." [official] *Understand budgets in Sponsored ads* (help GTGPQGUXNCTHE2DS)
- RULE: "Daily budgets are spent as quickly as possible, because all available impressions for your ads to show to relevant shoppers are taken. This means that a smaller budget could be spent in a few minutes". When a campaign is out of budget, "your ads won't be eligible to show until the next day, at midnight when the daily budget resets". [official] *Sponsored Products budget basics and best practices*, https://advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/sponsored-products-budget-best-practices (the older address https://advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/how-to-budget-sponsored-products 301-redirects to this page)
- RULE (SD API): the SD v3 campaign object carries `deliveryProfile` with the single enum value `as_soon_as_possible`. [official] *Sponsored Display v3* OpenAPI (`Campaign` schema)
- RULE: on vCPM-priced sponsored ads, "when your sponsored ad gets 1,000 viewable impressions, your bid amount is deducted from your daily or lifetime budget". The page's illustration is "Daily budget ($20) ÷ winning bid ($1) = number of impressions per day (20,000)". [official] *Understand bidding on Sponsored ads* (help GDS2E64R9GQ35768), https://advertising.amazon.com/help/GDS2E64R9GQ35768
- RULE (contrast, not sponsored ads): Display, Video, and Audio campaigns (the programmatic buying formerly sold as Amazon DSP, now one of the three campaign types inside the Amazon Ads Agent platform; section O) are paced. The page says it "breaks your total budget into smaller delivery targets - daily and then into shorter intervals of just a few minutes - and continuously adjusts bidding behavior to hit those targets"; the system "recalculates how competitively your campaign bids every few minutes". [official] *Understand delivery and pacing* (help GCELVBWV7S8S9AJA), https://advertising.amazon.com/help/GCELVBWV7S8S9AJA
  - In the v1 API the DSP pacing choice is `Pacing.deliveryProfile` EVEN | PACE_AHEAD | ASAP. [official] *Amazon Ads API v1* OpenAPI (`AmazonAdsAPIALL_prod_3p.json`)
  - Conflict (for human readers only): the v1 spec says `PACE_AHEAD` "can deliver up to 25% more than the daily Even pace targets"; the help page says Pace ahead "starts with a more aggressive (15%) front-loaded delivery pattern". [official] both pages above.
  - [seat] So "pacing" means two different things on Amazon. Sponsored ads have no intraday pacer: the budget is spent as auctions are won until it is gone. Display, Video, and Audio line items are paced against a flight. Practitioner talk of "pacing" on sponsored ads means the advertiser's own spreading of spend (bids, schedules, rules), not an Amazon mechanism.

### B. Over-delivery within a day, the monthly cap and the 25% / 100% setting

- RULE (monthly averaging and cap): "The daily budget amount is averaged over the course of a calendar month. … At the end of the month, you will not spend more than the daily budget you have set, multiplied by the number of days in that month. Your invoice will be adjusted for any over-delivery, so that you will not be charged for any amount in excess of your monthly charging limit." The worked example: a $100 budget with $90 of clicks on day one "could receive up to $110 worth of clicks on the second day". [official] Ads API guide *Average daily budget user guide*, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/account-management/average-daily-budget
- RULE (the help page's wording): "we use leftover funds from low traffic days to increase your budget by up to 100%. At the end of the month, you won't spend more than the daily budget you've set, multiplied by the number of days in that month." And: "By default, we'll increase your budget up to 100% on high-traffic days. If you'd like us to only increase your daily budget by 25%, you can change this in your campaign settings. (Sponsored ads > Campaigns > Settings)." The page's arithmetic is "Daily Budget ($10) x Days in the month (30) = Monthly budget ($300)". [official] *Understand budgets in Sponsored ads* (help GTGPQGUXNCTHE2DS)
- RULE (the setting, and its scope): "You can set the average daily budget increase amount at either 25% or 100% of campaign budget. You select your choice at the profile level, so all sponsored ads campaigns are set at the same amount. By default, all campaigns have a 100% average daily budget increase amount." Opting out to 25% is `POST /accountBudgets/featureFlags` with `{"featureFlags":{"isOptedOutForAverageDailyBudgetIncrease": true}}`. Setting it back to 100% is the same call with `false`. The current value is read with `GET /accountBudgets/featureFlags`. [official] *Average daily budget user guide* (as above); *Account budgets* API reference (media type `application/vnd.accountBudgetFeatureFlags.v1+json`), read in the API docs search index
- RULE (history): "We have updated our daily budgeting policy for Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands, and Sponsored Display, allowing you to spend up to 100% more than the average daily budget on any given day." [official] Ads API release note *New sponsored ads daily budgeting policy and options*, 2023-03-28, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/release-notes/index. Then: "We have now updated the default rollover value for all accounts to be 100%. This change only impacts accounts that have not yet selected a default … If you have already chosen either 25% or 100%, you will not experience any change in your settings." [official] release note *Updated: Sponsored ads daily budgeting policy and options*, 2023-06-20 (same page). The console announcement, titled *New sponsored ads daily budgeting policy and options* and dated 2023-05-09, says the change is "effective worldwide for sponsored ads". [official] *New sponsored ads daily budgeting policy and options* (what's new), https://advertising.amazon.com/resources/whats-new/sponsored-ads-daily-budgeting-policy-and-options
- Conflict (stale library guide): the library guide read today still gives 25%: "less than or up to 25% more than your average daily budget on any given day", with the example "$12.50 (25% higher)" on a $10 budget and "$300" for the month. [official] *Sponsored Products budget basics and best practices*, https://advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/sponsored-products-budget-best-practices. (The research seat listed this as two guides; the second address, `library/guides/how-to-budget-sponsored-products`, 301-redirects to the same page, so it is one page.)
  - [seat] The release notes, the API guide and the help page agree that 100% is the default since 2023 and 25% is an opt-out. The library guide predates the change. A skill that quotes "25%" from the library is quoting a superseded figure. The live value for a profile is `isOptedOutForAverageDailyBudgetIncrease`.
- RULE (an Amazon moderator's statement, forum): "Your daily budget amount is averaged over the course of a calendar month. On any given day you could spend less than your daily budget, or up to 100% more than your average daily budget. … However, at the end of the month, you won't spend more than the daily budget you've set, multiplied by the number of days in that month." This was Veronica_Amazon (Amazon moderator) answering a seller whose $10 auto campaign spent $20.83 in a day. The page shows "3 years ago" with no exact date. [official, forum moderator] Seller Forums (CA) thread, https://sellercentral.amazon.ca/seller-forums/discussions/t/06995973-7cba-4289-9b83-03df691d326a. The same moderator gave a similar answer, without a percentage ("you could spend less than or more than your average daily budget, on any given day. However, by the end of the month, you will not spend more than your daily budget"), to a seller who reported spend "about 100%" over a $100 cap on a thread tagged Sponsored display. [official, forum moderator] Seller Forums (EU) thread, https://sellercentral-europe.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/d89a0bd7-549e-4216-991f-88a27d0547c3
- RULE (credit on the invoice): "If a campaign receives clicks that exceed the overall monthly budget, the difference between your ad cost and your monthly budget may be credited back to your account." The invoice line is called "Over-delivery credits". [official] *View and understand your invoice* (help GDWV9R4RA7FCHSTE), https://advertising.amazon.com/help/GDWV9R4RA7FCHSTE
  - [seat] Campaign-manager Cost and report `cost` show gross delivered cost. The month cap is enforced on the invoice, so a month's report cost can exceed budget × days while the bill does not. Reconciling report cost to the bill belongs to section M and `beyond/metrics-and-attribution.md`.

### C. Changing a budget, and daily versus lifetime

- RULE (mid-month change): after a mid-month change, the rest of the month is capped at the new daily budget × the days remaining. The worked example: a $50 daily budget with $800 spent by the 20th, then changed to $10, allows at most $900 for the month. [official] *Edit your Sponsored ads campaign budget* (help GVYUKBJQFPH7ZQ2L), https://advertising.amazon.com/help/GVYUKBJQFPH7ZQ2L
- RULE (several changes in one day): the highest budget set during the day applies to that day, and the last budget set applies from the next day. [official] *Edit your Sponsored ads campaign budget* (help GVYUKBJQFPH7ZQ2L)
- RULE (cut below today's spend): "I reduced my daily budget, but I already exceeded the new budget earlier today." — "Your spending will be capped for the rest of the day. Your new budget will take effect tomorrow." A cut that stays above today's spend "will take effect today". Daily budget changes otherwise "take effect immediately". [official] *Edit your Sponsored ads campaign budget* (help GVYUKBJQFPH7ZQ2L)
- RULE (lifetime): "If you're using a lifetime budget, you can only increase it once your campaign is live." And: "you can't switch from a daily budget to a lifetime budget once a campaign is live. If you want to do this, pause your current campaign, copy it to a new one, and choose lifetime budget." [official] *Edit your Sponsored ads campaign budget* (help GVYUKBJQFPH7ZQ2L)
- RULE (status latency): after a budget change on an out-of-budget campaign, it is "typically 1-2 hours" before the status changes back to Delivering. The same page advises "Avoid frequent budget changes within the same day". [official] *Troubleshoot your campaign status* (help GEC8BVH43CU9UNYH), https://advertising.amazon.com/help/GEC8BVH43CU9UNYH. The "avoid" line is GUIDANCE.
- RULE (lifetime budget, SB): "In Sponsored Brands campaigns, you can choose between a daily budget and a lifetime budget. A lifetime budget is the total amount that you're willing to spend on a single campaign for as long as it runs. It requires an end date and a minimum $100 budget." [official] *Understand budgets in Sponsored ads* (help GTGPQGUXNCTHE2DS). The $100 figure is for human readers only; the API's lifetime minimum by marketplace is in section K.
- RULE (SB API): `budgetType` is `DAILY` | `LIFETIME`, and "For the lifetime budget type, `startDate` and `endDate` must be specified". [official] *Sponsored Brands v4* OpenAPI, https://d3a0d0y2hgofx6.cloudfront.net/openapi/en-us/sponsored-brands/4-0/openapi.json
- RULE (SB lifetime, out of budget): "If your budget depletes before end of the day or before the campaign end date (in the case of lifetime budget), your campaign ads will no longer be eligible for impressions." [official] *Troubleshoot your campaign for impressions* (help G8WCYU3FQKFVSMR4), https://advertising.amazon.com/help/G8WCYU3FQKFVSMR4
- RULE (SP v3 create and update): `budget.budgetType` is `DAILY` | `OTHER` on read, and create and update accept `DAILY` only. [official] *Sponsored Products v3* OpenAPI (`SponsoredProducts_prod_3p.json`)
- RULE (SD): SD campaigns carry a daily budget. "If you don't specify a `budget`, it will be set as the default budget for your region". [official] *Sponsored Display v3* OpenAPI, https://d3a0d0y2hgofx6.cloudfront.net/openapi/en-us/sponsored-display/3-0/openapi.yaml. The defaults table gives US 100, UK 100, DE 100, IN 1000, JP 10000, AE 400, SA 400, SG 300, TR 300, MX 150, BR 150 and EG 500, in local currency. [official] Ads API reference *Limits, constraints, and quotas* (section *Default budgets*), https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/reference/concepts/limits. These figures are for human readers only.
  - [seat] An SD campaign created through the API without a budget therefore spends up to a regional default the seller never typed. A JP or IN default is large in local currency.
- GUIDANCE: "you can change your daily budget at any time", and "At least once every two weeks, check in on how well your current budget is serving your campaigns." [official] *Sponsored Products budget basics and best practices*
- GUIDANCE: "a minimum of $10 (or the equivalent for the country in which you are selling) per campaign" for many advertisers; "you should always choose a budget that you're able to pay". [official] *Sponsored Products budget basics and best practices*. The $10 is for human readers only.

### D. Detecting out-of-budget: serving status and delivery reasons

There are four places where Amazon says a campaign has stopped for money:

- the per-campaign serving status in the version-specific APIs;
- the v1 `deliveryReasons`;
- the portfolio serving status;
- the billing status and billing notifications (section M).

- RULE (SP v3): `extendedData.servingStatus` on a campaign includes `CAMPAIGN_OUT_OF_BUDGET`, `PORTFOLIO_OUT_OF_BUDGET`, `ACCOUNT_OUT_OF_BUDGET`, `ADVERTISER_OUT_OF_BUDGET`, `ADVERTISER_EXCEED_SPENDS_LIMIT` and `ADVERTISER_PAYMENT_FAILURE`, with `servingStatusDetails[] {name, message, helpUrl}`. `extendedData` is returned when the list request sets `includeExtendedDataFields: true` ("Whether to get entity with extended data fields such as creationDate, lastUpdateDate, servingStatus"). [official] *Sponsored Products v3* OpenAPI. Extended-data list calls "hold 5x the weight of standard calls towards throttling limits". [official] Ads API reference *Rate limiting*, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/reference/concepts/rate-limiting, read in the docs search index
- RULE (meanings): the computed-status reference gives the meanings.
  - `CAMPAIGN_OUT_OF_BUDGET`: "Campaign daily budget has been exceeded."
  - `PORTFOLIO_OUT_OF_BUDGET`: "The maximum budget cap at the portfolio level has been reached."
  - `ACCOUNT_OUT_OF_BUDGET`: "Total ad-spend for the day across all of your campaigns has reached your Account Daily Budget Cap threshold. Your account level budget governs how much you spend per day across all of your campaigns."
  - `ADVERTISER_PAYMENT_FAILURE`: "Advertiser payment was unsuccessful."

  On Sponsored Brands (the page's *Sponsored Brands Campaign Status* table), a campaign status `OUT_OF_BUDGET` ("The campaign has run out of budget") clears when "The next calendar day has started, causing the daily spent budget to refresh" or when the budget is increased "(for daily budget only) through a `PUT` request". The same SB table has `BILLING_ERROR` ("The campaign has been stopped because of billing error"), cleared by "Fix billing error(s)". [official] Ads API reference *Computed status and campaign status*, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/reference/concepts/computed-status. (The research seat had attributed the `OUT_OF_BUDGET` transitions to SD; the page places them under Sponsored Brands.)
- RULE (v1): a v1 campaign has `status{deliveryStatus: DELIVERING | NOT_DELIVERING | UNAVAILABLE | LIMITED, deliveryReasons[]}`. The money reasons include:
  - `CAMPAIGN_OUT_OF_BUDGET`;
  - `PORTFOLIO_OUT_OF_BUDGET`;
  - `ADVERTISER_OUT_OF_BUDGET`, described as "out of budget for Sponsored Products campaigns for sellers", which is the account daily cap;
  - `ADVERTISER_OUT_OF_POSTPAY_CREDIT_LIMIT`, `ADVERTISER_OUT_OF_POSTPAY_MONTHLY_BUDGET` and `ADVERTISER_OUT_OF_PREPAY_BALANCE`;
  - `ADVERTISER_PAYMENT_FAILURE`, `SPEND_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` and `OUT_OF_REWARD_BUDGET`.

  [official] *Amazon Ads API v1* OpenAPI; Ads API reference *Common models: enums*, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/reference/common-models/enums. The enums page maps the names across APIs:

  | v1 | SP | SB | SD |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `ADVERTISER_OUT_OF_BUDGET` | `ADVERTISER_OUT_OF_BUDGET` AND `ACCOUNT_OUT_OF_BUDGET` | `ADVERTISER_ACCOUNT_OUT_OF_BUDGET` | `ACCOUNT_OUT_OF_BUDGET` |
  | `CAMPAIGN_OUT_OF_BUDGET` | `CAMPAIGN_OUT_OF_BUDGET` | `CAMPAIGN_OUT_OF_BUDGET` | `CAMPAIGN_OUT_OF_BUDGET` |
  | `PORTFOLIO_OUT_OF_BUDGET` | `PORTFOLIO_OUT_OF_BUDGET` | (none) | (none) |

  The enums page lists `deliveryStatus` as `DELIVERING`, `NOT_DELIVERING` and `UNAVAILABLE`; the v1 OpenAPI adds `LIMITED` ("partial delivery status, applicable to global resources that have different delivery status across marketplaces"). The postpay, prepay, `SPEND_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` and `OUT_OF_REWARD_BUDGET` reasons are in the v1 OpenAPI enum and not on the enums page; `SPEND_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` and `OUT_OF_REWARD_BUDGET` carry no description in the spec.
  - Conflict (gap fill 2026-11-09): the enums page's SB cell for `PORTFOLIO_OUT_OF_BUDGET` is empty, but the *Sponsored Brands v4* OpenAPI's `CampaignServingStatus` (and its ad-group and ad serving-status enums) carries `PORTFOLIO_OUT_OF_BUDGET` beside `CAMPAIGN_OUT_OF_BUDGET` and `ADVERTISER_ACCOUNT_OUT_OF_BUDGET`. [official] *Sponsored Brands v4* OpenAPI, read 2026-11-09. [seat] The spec is the contract the API answers with, so an SB campaign can come back as `PORTFOLIO_OUT_OF_BUDGET` whatever the mapping table shows.
- RULE (portfolio): `extendedData.servingStatus` on a portfolio takes `PortfolioServingStatus` values:
  - account and payment states: `ADVERTISER_ACCOUNT_OUT_OF_BUDGET`, `ADVERTISER_ARCHIVED`, `ADVERTISER_EXCEED_SPENDS_LIMIT`, `ADVERTISER_OUT_OF_BUDGET`, `ADVERTISER_OUT_OF_PREPAY_BALANCE`, `ADVERTISER_PAUSED`, `ADVERTISER_PAYMENT_FAILURE`;
  - portfolio states: `PORTFOLIO_ENDED`, `PORTFOLIO_OUT_OF_BUDGET`, `PORTFOLIO_PENDING_START_DATE`, `PORTFOLIO_STATUS_ENABLED`.

  A portfolio also carries `inBudget` (boolean). [official] *Portfolios v3* OpenAPI (`Portfolios_prod_3p.json`)
- RULE (console troubleshooting): the status page lists out of budget, portfolio budget cap reached and the SP daily budget cap as causes of a campaign not delivering. While out of budget, ads are not eligible "until midnight when the daily budget is reset". [official] *Troubleshoot your campaign status* (help GEC8BVH43CU9UNYH)
  - [seat] "Midnight" is not defined on the page by timezone. The SP v3 and reporting docs work in the profile's timezone (the `timezone` of `GET /v2/profiles`). The seat did not find a page that states the budget reset uses the profile timezone; see Open questions.
- RULE (billing as a delivery cause): "If your credit or debit card is declined, we may automatically suspend all your campaigns." [official] *Troubleshoot your campaign for impressions* (help G8WCYU3FQKFVSMR4)
- RULE (change history as a record of out-of-budget): `POST /history` returns campaign change events including `IN_BUDGET` and `BUDGET_AMOUNT`, and on campaigns also `RULE_BASED_BUDGET_VALUE`, `APPLICABLE_RULE_ID`, `APPLICABLE_RULE_NAME` and `PORTFOLIO_ID`. Event metadata carries `campaignBudgetType` `DAILY` | `LIFETIME_BUDGET`. History covers SP and SB only ("Changes for Sponsored Display campaigns are not returned"), with "Max 90 days of history", a count of 50–200 per page and times in UTC epoch milliseconds. It "doesn't return information about who made the change". [official] *Change history* OpenAPI (`Changehistory_prod_3p.json`), https://d1y2lf8k3vrkfu.cloudfront.net/openapi/en-us/dest/Changehistory_prod_3p.json
  - [seat] `IN_BUDGET` events are the only Amazon-provided timeline of when a campaign went out of budget and came back. The usage API gives the current percentage only, and the budget recommendation gives a 7-day `percentTimeInBudget` aggregate. Whether `IN_BUDGET` events record each out-of-budget transition for SP was not tested by this seat; reading them needs a seller's data.

### E. The budget usage API: how much of today's budget is gone

- RULE (what it is): "The budget usage API retrieves the budget usage percent for your campaigns and portfolios to provide near real-time information about your budget usage. You can request the budget usage percent which is retrieved in near real-time for any active, paused, or expired campaigns or portfolios." [official] Ads API guide *Budget usage overview*, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/budgets/usage/overview
- RULE (the denominator): "Budget usage percent is the current campaign ad spend for the day divided by the specified daily budget. This includes changes in budget due to budget rules and increases from average daily budget calculations." In Amazon's example, a $100 budget with a +50% rule and $80 spent gives "$80.00/$150.00 = 53.3%". [official] *Budget usage overview* (as above)
  - [seat] So 100% usage means the day's effective budget is gone. That budget is the set budget plus rule increases plus the average-daily-budget lift. It does not mean the typed budget is gone. A campaign can show more than 100% of its typed budget spent and still be delivering.
- RULE (operations): one POST per ad product, each with `{"campaignIds": [...]}` or `{"portfolioIds": [...]}`, at most 100 ids per call:
  - `POST /sp/campaigns/budget/usage` (`application/vnd.spcampaignbudgetusage.v1+json`);
  - `POST /sb/campaigns/budget/usage` (`application/vnd.sbcampaignbudgetusage.v1+json`);
  - `POST /sd/campaigns/budget/usage`;
  - `POST /portfolios/budget/usage` (`application/vnd.portfoliobudgetusage.v1+json`).

  The reply is a multi-status of `success[]` and `error[]`, each carrying the `index` of the request item. A success item carries `campaignId` or `portfolioId`, `budget`, `budgetUsagePercent` and `usageUpdatedTimestamp`. [official] *Sponsored Products v3*, *Sponsored Brands v4*, *Sponsored Display v3* and *Portfolios v3* OpenAPI specs; Ads API guide *Getting started with the budget usage API*, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/budgets/usage/getting-started

#### F2. What the missed-opportunity figures measure (gap fill 2026-11-09)

The figures above are the most quoted argument for a budget raise. Amazon's own pages say how they are built, and what they are built from limits what they can show.

- RULE (how missed sales are derived, verbatim): "Based on similar campaigns (with similar products and targeting) in the same marketplace which did not go out of budget, we used machine learning to estimate missed clicks. Then using the historical CTR and historical sales together with the estimated missed clicks, we can also calculate the estimated missed sales and missed impressions. Like any predictive model, these metrics are estimated for reference only and are not guaranteed." And: "Same as missed opportunities, the recommended budget is also based on the machine-learning model's estimated missed clicks. We also estimate the CPC based on your campaign's historical CPCs and taking into account other factors including seasonality. Then, we calculate the recommended budget as estimated missed clicks * estimated CPC." [official] *Sponsored Products budget basics and best practices*, read 2026-11-09.
- RULE (the report's column definitions): Estimated Missed Clicks is "based on factors that may include historical performance of similar campaigns that didn't run out of budget"; Estimated Missed Sales is "based on factors that may include estimated missed clicks and historical sales"; Estimated Missed Impressions is "based on factors that may include estimated missed clicks and historical click-through rate (CTR)". Each is "an estimate, not a guarantee", and "Some campaigns may not have enough data to provide an estimate." [official] *Budget report for Sponsored Products* (help GMSBDCQF5ZW5P53N), read 2026-11-09.
- RULE (the API's wording): `estimatedMissedSalesLower` is the "Lower bound estimate of the additional sales the campaign might have generated if it had not run out of budget during the {startDate} to {endDate} period. Provided in local currency"; the click and impression bounds are worded the same way. The guide adds that these are "estimates based on previous website traffic and historical campaign performance, not a guarantee of actual impressions, clicks, and sales." [official] *Sponsored Products v3* OpenAPI (`SevenDaysMissedOpportunities`); *Budget recommendations and missed opportunities*, both read 2026-11-09.
- RULE (what "sales" is on Sponsored Products): the reporting columns define `sales7d` as "Total value of sales occurring within 7 days of an ad click". [official] Ads API guide *Reporting columns (version 3)*, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/reporting/v3/columns, read 2026-11-09. Ad sales are click-attributed sales, not sales caused by the ad; that distinction is owned by `beyond/metrics-and-attribution.md` and `areas/ads-organic-and-total.md`.
- Conflict (the samples): the SP spec describes `percentTimeInBudget` as "a decimal ranging from 0 to 1", while the same spec's response examples carry `65.2` and `80`, and one example carries `suggestedBudget: "-1"` as a string. The guide's Scenario 1 sample returns 3–5 missed impressions against 8–12 missed clicks, more clicks than impressions. [official] *Sponsored Products v3* OpenAPI (`BudgetRecommendationResponse` examples); *Budget recommendations and missed opportunities*, both read 2026-11-09. [seat] The samples are illustrations, but they show the fields were not drawn from one consistent case; the scale of `percentTimeInBudget` is best judged from the value itself (a value above 1 is a percentage).
- [seat] What follows from Amazon's own description:
  - The missed-sales figure is a model output stacked on a model output: modelled missed clicks (from other campaigns that stayed in budget), multiplied by this campaign's own historical CTR and sales. It is not an observation of what the campaign would have sold.
  - The "historical sales" it uses are attributed sales (`sales7d` for sellers), so the estimate is of attributed sales, not of sales the raise would cause. Attributed and incremental differ most on brand terms (Blake, Nosko and Tadelis 2015, in *Published research*).
  - The campaign's own historical rates come from the hours it was in budget. The hours a raise buys are the hours after exhaustion, usually later in the day, and practitioner hourly data show conversion rate and CPC differ by hour (P8 below). Amazon does not say whether its model conditions on hour of day.
  - The day-level metrics of a day the campaign went out of budget cover only the in-budget hours. A daily ACOS, CVR or ROAS on such a day is a partial-day figure, and a 7-day average that mixes full and truncated days is weighted toward the hours that were served.
  - The seat found no published validation by Amazon or anyone else of the missed-opportunity estimates against what campaigns did after a raise (searches of amazon.science, arXiv and Amazon's help and API pages on 2026-11-09). Ad Badger (2021) remarks of the console report's ranges that "the difference between both values is great" (P8).
  - The direct read of what a raise did is a before/after of the campaign's own spend, clicks, orders and hourly cost once the budget changed, which is `method/change-measurement.md`'s.

- RULE (shape, Portfolios v3, released 2024-07-18): `POST /portfolios`, `PUT /portfolios` and `POST /portfolios/list` take media type `application/vnd.spPortfolio.v3+json`; list filters by `portfolioIdFilter` and others.   - `budgetControls.campaignUnspentBudgetSharing.featureState` `ENABLED` | `DISABLED`;
  - `extendedData.servingStatus` (section D).

  Removing a cap means setting `policy: NO_CAP` with the amount and dates nulled. [official] *Portfolios v3* OpenAPI (`Portfolios_prod_3p.json`), https://d1y2lf8k3vrkfu.cloudfront.net/openapi/en-us/dest/Portfolios_prod_3p.json
- RULE (budget validation errors in SP and portfolios): a write can fail with:
  - `BUDGET_TOO_LOW`, `BUDGET_TOO_HIGH` or `BUDGET_OUT_OF_MARKET_PLACE_RANGE`, and on portfolios only `BUDGET_AMOUNT_INVALID` (it is in `PortfolioBudgetErrorReason`, not in `SponsoredProductsBudgetErrorReason`);
  - `BUDGET_CURRENCY_DOES_NOT_MATCH_MARKETPLACE_SETTINGS`;
  - `BUDGET_LT_DEFAULT_BIDS`, `BUDGET_LT_KEYWORD_BIDS` or `BUDGET_LT_PREDEFINED_TARGET_BIDS`;
  - `BUDGETING_POLICY_INVALID`, `MISSING_BUDGETING_POLICY` or `MISSING_IN_BUDGET_FLAG`.

  [official] *Portfolios v3* (`PortfolioBudgetErrorReason`) and *Sponsored Products v3* (`SponsoredProductsBudgetErrorReason`) OpenAPI specs. Portfolio writes can also fail on billing (`PortfolioBillingErrorReason`: `ADVERTISER_SUSPENDED`, `BILLING_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `EXPIRED_PAYMENT_METHOD`, `PAYMENT_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND`, `VETTING_FAILURE`). [official] *Portfolios v3* OpenAPI
  - [seat] The `BUDGET_LT_*_BIDS` reasons mean Amazon refuses a daily budget below the campaign's own bids.
- RULE (unspent-budget sharing): "campaigns that have already spent their full daily budget can automatically use unspent campaign budget from other campaigns from previous days in the month, allowing them to spend up to 100% more than their average daily budget on any given day. … At the end of the month, the campaigns in your portfolio will not spend more in total than the sum of the daily budget for each campaign, multiplied by the number of days in that month. Your invoice will be adjusted for any over-delivery". The worked example: three $100 campaigns spend $70, $50 and $100, which leaves "$80 in unspent leftovers"; the next day the third campaign "can still spend the additional $80". The pool is reset each month: "at the beginning of the next month the leftover amount is reset to 0". A campaign first uses its own leftovers through the average daily budget, and sharing is "offered to help campaigns that still run out of budget, even after spending their own leftovers". [official] Ads API guide *Sharing leftover budget in a portfolio*, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/portfolios/sharing-leftover
- RULE (default on): "By default, all portfolios created after 6/20/2025 have unspent campaign budget sharing turned on." It is turned off per portfolio with `PUT /portfolios` and `budgetControls.campaignUnspentBudgetSharing.featureState: DISABLED`. [official] *Sharing leftover budget in a portfolio*; release notes 2025-07-07 and 2025-07-19 ("allows high-performing campaigns to spend up to twice their daily budget by tapping into unspent campaign budget … staying within your total monthly portfolio budget").
  - [seat] With sharing on, the monthly bound is the portfolio's sum of daily budgets × days, not each campaign's. One campaign can therefore spend more than its own budget × days in a month. A lower portfolio cap still binds, with status `PORTFOLIO_OUT_OF_BUDGET`.
- RULE (billing by portfolio): "Billing details will reflect the portfolio mapping at the time the invoice is issued. If you don't group your campaigns into portfolios, the invoices will be a list of campaign-level spend." [official] help GPEJN2E6R52G7C2T. Invoice lines in the billing API carry `portfolioId`. [official] *Advertising Billing* OpenAPI
- RULE (bulk): portfolios, including budget policy and amounts, are supported in bulksheets for all campaign types (since 2022-01-01). [official] Ads API guide *Bulksheets: portfolios*, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/no-code-tools/bulksheets/bulksheets-portfolios; release notes 2022-01-01.

### J. Campaign budgets in Ads API v1, global campaigns and currency

- RULE (shape): a v1 campaign carries `budgets[]` (at most 2 items). Each item is `{budgetType: MONETARY, recurrenceTimePeriod: DAILY | LIFETIME | MONTHLY, budgetValue: {monetaryBudgetValue: {monetaryBudget: {value, currencyCode, ruleValue}, marketplaceSettings[] {marketplace, monetaryBudget}}}}`. `marketplaceSettings` takes at most 30 items. `ruleValue` is "The monetary amount of the budget when a budget rule is applied." [official] *Amazon Ads API v1* OpenAPI (`AmazonAdsAPIALL_prod_3p.json`, campaign schemas), https://d1y2lf8k3vrkfu.cloudfront.net/openapi/en-us/dest/AmazonAdsAPIALL_prod_3p.json; Ads API reference *Common models: campaigns*, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/reference/common-models/campaigns
  - Conflict: the v1 OpenAPI `Recurrence` enum is `DAILY`, `LIFETIME`, `MONTHLY`; the *Common models: enums* page lists `recurrenceTimePeriod` as `DAILY`, `LIFETIME`, `OTHER`. [official] both pages.
  - [seat] Which `recurrenceTimePeriod` values a given ad product accepts follows the product: SP daily, SB daily or lifetime, DSP flights. `MONTHLY` exists in the spec's shared model. The seat found no sponsored-ads guide that offers a monthly campaign budget, and the Stream campaign dataset lists `budgets.recurrence_time_period` `DAILY`, `MONTHLY` and `LIFETIME` without saying which products use which.
- RULE (operations): v1 campaign operations are all POST: `/adsApi/v1/create/campaigns`, `/adsApi/v1/query/campaigns`, `/adsApi/v1/update/campaigns` and `/adsApi/v1/delete/campaigns`. [official] *Amazon Ads API v1* OpenAPI. The door's own reference gives the SP v1 budget shape `budgets: [{ budgetType: "MONETARY", budgetValue: { monetaryBudgetValue: { monetaryBudget: { value } } }, recurrenceTimePeriod: "DAILY" }]`. [seat, from the plugin] `cx-amazon-ads/skills/amazon-ads/references/door.md`
- RULE (budget settings): `optimizations.budgetSettings` carries `budgetAllocation` (`AUTO` | `MANUAL`, DSP), `flightBudgetRolloverStrategy` (DSP) and `offAmazonBudgetControlStrategy` (SP). [official] *Amazon Ads API v1* OpenAPI. On SP v3 the same SP setting is `offAmazonSettings.offAmazonBudgetControlStrategy` `MAXIMIZE_REACH` | `MINIMIZE_SPEND`, and global campaigns carry `marketplaceBudgetAllocation` `AUTO` | `MANUAL`. [official] *Sponsored Products v3* OpenAPI
  - RULE (what the setting means): SP v3 `MAXIMIZE_REACH` "Allow ad placements both on and off Amazon"; `MINIMIZE_SPEND` "Restrict ad placements to Amazon owned-and-operated properties only." The v1 spec words them "Maximize the reach of off-Amazon inventory within the budget" and "Minimize spend on off-Amazon inventory while maintaining delivery." [official] *Sponsored Products v3* and *Amazon Ads API v1* OpenAPI specs. The status page says: "For potential off-site Cost (formerly known as Spend on sponsored ads), account for additional budget monitor this using a Placement Report as these metrics are not visible in Campaign Manager." [official] help GEC8BVH43CU9UNYH
  - [seat] Read together ("within the budget"), off-Amazon SP cost is spent from the campaign's budget; the help page's "account for additional budget" is ambiguous on that point. The placement mechanics are owned by `bids-and-placements.md`.
- RULE (global SP campaigns): a global campaign is created once and runs in several marketplaces, each with its own budget in local currency. Amazon's example is "The budgets are $100, £80, €60" for US, GB and DE, set through `marketplaceSettings`. The request uses the `Amazon-Ads-AccountId` header instead of a profile scope. The supported marketplaces are AE, AU, BE, BR, CA, DE, EG, ES, FR, GB, IN, IT, JP, MX, NL, PL, SA, SE, SG, TR and US. Values for other marketplaces can be filled with the localization APIs. "Only the `spCampaigns` report surfaces global campaigns." [official] Ads API guide *Global campaigns for Sponsored Products*, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/campaign-management/global-campaigns. The reporting page at `guides/reporting/ads-v1/report-types/sp-campaigns` is titled *Migrating from: spCampaigns* and maps the v3 `spCampaigns` columns to v1 fields (`campaign.budgetAmount`, `campaign.budgetType`, `campaign.currencyCode`, `campaign.deliveryStatus`); it does not itself discuss global campaigns. [official] https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/reporting/ads-v1/report-types/sp-campaigns
- RULE (setting the per-marketplace budgets of a global campaign; gap fill 2026-11-09):
  - Each marketplace's budget is a `marketplaceSettings[] {marketplace, monetaryBudget {value}}` entry inside `budgets[].budgetValue.monetaryBudgetValue`; the read returns each with its `currencyCode` (USD, GBP, EUR in Amazon's example). `marketplaceSettings` is "List of Monetary Budget values selectively applied at the given marketplace level", at most 30. [official] *Global campaigns for Sponsored Products*; *Amazon Ads API v1* OpenAPI (`MonetaryBudgetValue`), both read 2026-11-09.
  - The per-marketplace overrides (`marketplaceConfigurations[].overrides`) cover `state`, `name`, `startDateTime`, `endDateTime`, `optimizations` and `tags`; budget is not among them. "Bid strategy is campaign-level; only bid values differ per marketplace." [official] *Global campaigns for Sponsored Products* (*Key fields*, FAQ).
  - Adding a marketplace: "Update `marketplaces` *and include the budget for the new marketplace in the same call* — a `marketplaces`-only update is rejected with `FIELD_VALUE_IS_NULL`." A campaign can also "auto-expand to new marketplaces as they become available (`autoScaleGlobalCampaign: AUTO`)". [official] *Global campaigns for Sponsored Products* (Scenario 4, step 2; section *Global campaign APIs*).
  - Values for other marketplaces "can either be entered directly by you, or you can provide the values for one marketplace and receive the localized values for all other marketplaces using the localization APIs". [official] same page.
  - Allocation mode: SP v3 `SponsoredProductsMarketplaceBudgetAllocation` reads "`AUTO` | Auto distribute global budget to marketplaces in global campaign. Budget updates for marketplaces are not allowed for AUTO campaigns. The budget can only be updated in global campaign for AUTO campaigns. | `MANUAL` | Manually distribute global budget to marketplaces in global campaign." Its list filter "is not functional yet" and "By default, only MANUAL campaigns are returned". [official] *Sponsored Products v3* OpenAPI, read 2026-11-09. In v1, `marketplaceBudgetAllocation` appears in the read model `BudgetSettings` only; `CreateBudgetSettings` and `UpdateBudgetSettings` do not carry it. [official] *Amazon Ads API v1* OpenAPI, read 2026-11-09.
  - Status per marketplace: "Query responses include a `status` object with a global `deliveryStatus` plus per-market `marketplaceSettings`", with `deliveryStatus` `DELIVERING`, `LIMITED`, `NOT_DELIVERING` or `UNAVAILABLE` and `deliveryReasons` including `CAMPAIGN_OUT_OF_BUDGET`; "In `status.marketplaceSettings[]`, the API returns the marketplace ID (for example, `A1F83G8C2ARO7P` for the UK) rather than the country code." [official] *Global campaigns for Sponsored Products* (*Delivery status*). [seat] A global campaign that is out of budget in one marketplace only reads `LIMITED` at the top; the marketplace that stopped is in `status.marketplaceSettings[]`, keyed by marketplace ID.
  - [seat] So on a `MANUAL` global campaign each marketplace's daily budget is a separate local-currency number that cx sets; on an `AUTO` one only the global figure can be written, and Amazon splits it. Neither page says whether the monthly-averaging cap (section B) applies per marketplace or to the global figure; see Open questions.
- RULE (portfolios are per marketplace): Portfolios v3 is called with a profile scope, and a profile is one marketplace; the v1 spec has no portfolio path (only a `CampaignPortfolioIdFilter`). A portfolio budget's `currencyCode` must match the marketplace (`BUDGET_CURRENCY_DOES_NOT_MATCH_MARKETPLACE_SETTINGS`). [official] *Portfolios v3* OpenAPI; *Amazon Ads API v1* OpenAPI, both read 2026-11-09. [seat] A monthly plan "per client per marketplace" therefore has a native Amazon counterpart only per profile: a `MONTHLY_RECURRING` portfolio cap in local currency, one per portfolio, with no Amazon object that caps several marketplaces together or converts between their currencies.
- RULE (currency): a budget is always in the marketplace's currency. A portfolio budget whose `currencyCode` does not match fails with `BUDGET_CURRENCY_DOES_NOT_MATCH_MARKETPLACE_SETTINGS`. [official] *Portfolios v3* OpenAPI. In reports, v3 campaign reports carry `campaignBudgetAmount`, `campaignBudgetType` and `campaignBudgetCurrencyCode`. v1 reports carry `campaign.budgetAmount` ("The budget allocated to the campaign") and the `budgetCurrency.value` dimension, and "The `metric.sales` field requires the `budgetCurrency.value` field". [official] *Campaign reports (v3)*; Ads API guides *Helpful concepts* (reporting v1), https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/reporting/ads-v1/helpful-concepts, and *Budget currency* dimension, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/reporting/ads-v1/dimensions/currency/budget-currency
  - RULE (converted currency in v1 reports): v1 reporting has a `convertedCurrency.value` dimension ("The currency selected by the customer to view metrics", SP, SB, SD, Sponsored TV, DSP, Full-Funnel) and converted metrics such as `metric.totalCostConverted` ("Values are displayed in the selected currency"). The v1 cost metric is `metric.totalCost` ("The total amount spent on running the campaign"; v3 Sponsored Ads name `spend`), which requires `budgetCurrency.value`. [official] Ads API guides *Converted currency (Currency)*, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/reporting/ads-v1/dimensions/currency/converted-currency, and *Costs* metric family, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/reporting/ads-v1/metrics/costs-and-fees/costs
  - [seat] So spend can be read in one currency across marketplaces from v1 reports. No converted field was found for budget amounts on campaign entities; adding typed budgets across marketplaces still needs a rate the entity APIs do not supply. (The research seat had written that the API supplies no conversion at all.)
- RULE (exports): the exports campaign entity carries `budgetCaps.budgetType`, `budgetCaps.recurrenceTimePeriod`, `budgetCaps.budgetValue.monetaryBudget.currencyCode`, `.amount` and `.ruleAmount`, with a mapping table to the SP, SB, SD and DSP field names. [official] *Exports: campaigns*

### K. Sponsored Brands and Sponsored Display budgets, and budget limits by marketplace

- RULE (SB): daily or lifetime, chosen at create and fixed thereafter (sections C and G). SB budget rules support ROAS, IS and NTB. SB budget recommendations are under `/sb/` (section F). [official] *Sponsored Brands v4* OpenAPI; help GVYUKBJQFPH7ZQ2L
- RULE (SD): daily budget only, with a regional default when none is given (section C). SD optimization rules (a separate feature from budget rules, beta since 2023-09-18) require the campaign budget to be at least "5x the value of any COST_PER_ORDER threshold", "10x the value of any COST_PER_THOUSAND_VIEWABLE_IMPRESSIONS threshold" and 20× a CPC threshold. [official] *Sponsored Display v3* OpenAPI (`POST /sd/campaigns`, `POST /sd/optimizationRules`); release notes 2023-09-18. The per-marketplace minimum thresholds (e.g. US cost per order 5, cost per click 0.5) are in *Limits, constraints, and quotas* (section *Metric minimum threshold values*). These figures are for human readers only.
- RULE (writing an SB budget; gap fill 2026-11-09): `PUT /sb/v4/campaigns` with media type `application/vnd.sbcampaignresource.v4+json` takes `{"campaigns": [UpdateCampaign]}`, 1 to 10 items. `UpdateCampaign` requires only `campaignId`; its budget field is `budget` ("The budget of the campaign", a double), with `startDate` ("can only be changed if the current startDate is in the future") and a nullable `endDate` ("If null is explicitly provided in a campaign update request, any existing endDate for the campaign will be removed"). `UpdateCampaign` has no `budgetType`: `budgetType` (`DAILY` | `LIFETIME`, "For the lifetime budget type, `startDate` and `endDate` must be specified") is in `CreateCampaign` only. The reply is `campaigns.success[] {index, campaignId, campaign}` and `campaigns.error[]`, each at most 10. [official] *Sponsored Brands v4* OpenAPI, read 2026-11-09. With the help page's rules ("If you're using a lifetime budget, you can only increase it once your campaign is live"; no switch from daily to lifetime once live; section C), the budget type of a live SB campaign is fixed and only the amount (and, for a lifetime budget, upward only) is written.
- RULE (writing an SD budget, v3): `PUT /sd/campaigns` takes a JSON array of `UpdateCampaign` (media type `application/json`); "The mutable fields are `name`, `state`, `budget`, `startDate`, and `endDate`. Maximum length of the array is 100 objects." `budgetType` is in the shared `BaseCampaign` with the single value `daily` (lower case) and `budget` is "The amount of the budget". The 207 reply is an array of `{code, description, campaignId}`, without the campaign's new values. [official] *Sponsored Display v3* OpenAPI, read 2026-11-09. The read-back is `GET /sd/campaigns/{campaignId}` or `GET /sd/campaigns/extended/{campaignId}` (same spec).
- RULE (writing an SD budget, v1): the SD v1 model's `SDCampaignUpdate.budgets` (exactly 1 item) references `SDCreateBudget`, which has `budgetType` (`MONETARY`) and `budgetValue.monetaryBudgetValue.monetaryBudget.value` and no `recurrenceTimePeriod`; the read model `SDBudget` requires `budgetType`, `budgetValue` and `recurrenceTimePeriod`. [official] *Amazon Ads API v1, Sponsored Display* OpenAPI (`AmazonAdsAPISD_prod_3p.json`), https://d1y2lf8k3vrkfu.cloudfront.net/openapi/en-us/dest/AmazonAdsAPISD_prod_3p.json, read 2026-11-09.
  - [opinion] `zhangwg4apollo` (GitHub issue #475 on `amzn/ads-advanced-tools-docs`, *SDCreateBudget schema is missing recurrenceTimePeriod — budget updates silently ignored*, opened 2026-07-21, open with no reply on 2026-11-09) reports that an SD v1 budget update built to that schema returns HTTP 207 with no error while the server ignores the budget object, so a change from 5.0 to 1.0 never takes effect; the reporter proposes adding `recurrenceTimePeriod` as the SP and SB create-budget schemas have it. https://github.com/amzn/ads-advanced-tools-docs/issues/475. The full surface record is `method/ads-api-surface.md`'s.
  - [seat] If the report holds, a 207 with no error on an SD v1 budget write does not show the budget changed; only a read of the campaign afterwards does. Sending `recurrenceTimePeriod: "DAILY"` follows the read model, though the update schema does not list it. The SD v3 write's reply also omits the new values, so on both SD paths the budget is confirmed by reading it back.
- RULE (SD auto-pause): "Sponsored Display campaigns with no delivery could be automatically paused", announced 2023-04-28. The pausing applied to campaigns with no delivery for 6 months, from 2023-09-30. [official] release notes 2023-04-28.
- RULE (limits table, for human readers only): budget constraints by marketplace, in local currency:
  - SP min / max daily: US, CA, UK, DE, FR, IT, ES, NL, BE, IE 1 / 1,000,000; IN 50 / 21,000,000; JP 100 / 21,000,000; AU 1.4 / 1,500,000; MX 1 / 21,000,000; UAE and SA 4 / 3,700,000; BR 1.32 / 5,300,000; SG 1.39 / 1,300,000; TR 2 / 2,500,000; PL 2 / 2,000,000; SE 9 / 9,300,000; EG 7 / 7,400,000; ZA 20 / 7,000,000.

The SB BR row (4 / 3,700,000 daily) differs from the SP and SD BR rows (1.32 / 5,300,000), and the SB JP maximum lifetime (21,000,000) equals its maximum daily. ### L. Invalid traffic and the credits it produces

- RULE (two stages): "IVT is filtered at two stages. Pre-bid filtering blocks IVT before an ad is served. … Most post-impression IVT is invalidated in real time. Some takes longer to detect, so we monitor traffic for up to 30 days after it occurs. IVT detected in this window is automatically removed from invoices and advertiser reports". "If an investigation identifies previously undetected IVT, affected metrics and invoices may be restated." [official] *Traffic validation* (help GBYPYH79NGJJ5JPS), https://advertising.amazon.com/help/GBYPYH79NGJJ5JPS
- RULE (the signal Amazon names): "if you notice a significant drop in IVR, with an anomalous increase in select campaign metrics like click-through rate (CTR) or Impressions or Clicks, it could suggest undetected IVT was billed to your campaign. For Sponsored Ads campaigns, a drop in Conversion Rate (CVR) along with the above changes in metrics, is a higher confidence indicator for undetected IVT." And: "IVR level on its own doesn't indicate undetected IVT." [official] help GBYPYH79NGJJ5JPS
- RULE (timing, cost): "While 90% of detected invalid traffic (IVT) is removed in real time, traffic quality systems may take up to 72 hours to remove 100% of IVT. For this reason, you may see a discrepancy between the campaign manager and your invoice if you're looking at the cost from the last 3 days." "Cost … can fluctuate for up to 72 hours after a click due to traffic validation." [official] *Understand campaign spend* (help GUJY2CG5DTCPDCVQ), https://advertising.amazon.com/help/GUJY2CG5DTCPDCVQ
- RULE (SP methodology, MRC-accredited, figures for human readers only): "Amazon Ads prebid techniques block approximately 20-25% of ad requests WW for Sponsored Products" (since 2022-08-15). "Amazon Ads' General IVT post-impression detection techniques filter approximately 3-8% of Clicks WW for Sponsored Products." Data "is restated within a 30-day timeframe, in which data is considered official and final. Rare occurrences of quality issues due to irregularity may result in data revisions within a lookback period of 90 days." "Amazon Ads doesn't bill advertisers for invalid clicks that are identified as a result of filtration methods. Metrics are reported and billed net of General and Sophisticated IVT, defined for MRC purposes as 'Total Net.'" [official] *Sponsored Products description of methodology* (help G8KL93RTVG44RZAQ), https://advertising.amazon.com/help/G8KL93RTVG44RZAQ
- RULE (credit on the invoice): "If we detect invalid clicks on your ads, you'll receive a credit. If we later validate clicks that were initially considered invalid, you may receive a debit to your account." [official] *View and understand your invoice* (help GDWV9R4RA7FCHSTE)
- RULE (the report): report type ids `spGrossAndInvalids`, `sbGrossAndInvalids` and `sdGrossAndInvalids` (reporting v3, GA 2023-11-16). They are grouped by campaign, with time unit `SUMMARY` or `DAILY` and a 365-day range and retention. The columns are `grossImpressions`, `grossClickThroughs`, `invalidImpressions`, `invalidClickThroughs`, `invalidImpressionRate`, `invalidClickThroughRate`, `impressions`, `clicks`, `campaignName`, `campaignStatus`, `date`, `startDate` and `endDate`. [official] Ads API guide *Gross and invalid traffic reports*, https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/reporting/v3/report-types/gross-and-invalid-traffic; release notes 2023-11-16. The page carries the warning "Reporting API v3 will be deprecated on June 30, 2027." [official] same page.
- RULE (IVT in v1 reporting): v1 reporting carries `metric.grossClicks` ("can include valid as well as invalid clicks"), `metric.invalidClicks` and `metric.invalidClickRate` ("invalid clicks / gross clicks"), for SP, SB, SD, Sponsored TV, DSP and Full-Funnel; `metric.clicks` is "gross clicks - invalid clicks". [official] Ads API guide *Clicks* metric family (reporting v1), https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/reporting/ads-v1/metrics/delivery/clicks
- RULE (budget and IVT): "Due to the Amazon traffic validation process, it is possible that you may receive the same notification twice. For example, if a click is invalidated, that may reduce your budget usage under the 5% threshold." [official] *Sponsored ads budget usage dataset*
  - [seat] The page does not say whether an invalidated click gives budget back for the same day. Two things can be read from Amazon's pages. ## Published research

All items are [research]. "Read" means the seat opened the abstract page. "Record" means only a bibliographic or search record was read, and the claim is limited to what that record states.

- Balseiro, S. and Gur, Y. (2019). *Learning in Repeated Auctions with Budgets: Regret Minimization and Equilibrium*. Management Science 65(9): 3952–3968, doi 10.1287/mnsc.2018.3174. [research, record]
  - Budget-constrained advertisers who do not know their value distribution or competitors' bids use "adaptive pacing" strategies, which adjust bids through the campaign according to the advertiser's own expenditure path.
  - The authors prove these strategies are asymptotically optimal against i.i.d. or arbitrary competing bids, and that they form an approximate Nash equilibrium in a regime motivated by online advertising.
- Balseiro, S., Kim, A., Mahdian, M. and Mirrokni, V. (2021). *Budget-Management Strategies in Repeated Auctions*. Operations Research 69(3): 859–876, doi 10.1287/opre.2020.2073; an earlier version appeared at WWW 2017. [research, record]
  - The paper compares six budget-management methods applied by all budget-constrained advertisers at once: probabilistic throttling, thresholding, bid shading, reserve pricing and two forms of multiplicative boosting.
  - Each method admits a system equilibrium, and each leads to a different one. The equilibria are compared on real sponsored-search auction data.
  - The search record lists one author as at Amazon; the seat did not verify the affiliation.
- Conitzer, V., Kroer, C., Sodomka, E. and Stier-Moses, N. (2022). *Multiplicative Pacing Equilibria in Auction Markets*. Operations Research 70(2): 963–989, doi 10.1287/opre.2021.2167; arXiv 1706.07151. [research, read]
  - Pacing equilibria always exist and refine competitive equilibria. They can be multiple, "with large variations in several natural objectives such as revenue and social welfare", though the authors' mixed-integer experiments find that multiplicity "occurs very rarely across several families of random instances".
  - Finding a welfare- or revenue-maximising one is NP-hard.
- Agarwal, D., Ghosh, S., Wei, K. and You, S. (2014). *Budget Pacing for Targeted Online Advertisements at LinkedIn*. KDD 2014, pp. 1613–1619, doi 10.1145/2623330.2623366. [research, record]
  - Greedy allocation, where an ad serves until its budget is gone, is suboptimal for advertisers and publishers when budgets are finite.
  - The authors propose a pacing component that knows global supply patterns, tested in experiments on LinkedIn ads.
- Xu, J., Lee, K., Li, W., Qi, H. and Lu, Q. (2015). *Smart Pacing for Effective Online Ad Campaign Optimization*. KDD 2015; arXiv 1506.05851. [research, read]
  - Advertisers want smooth spend and performance at once, which is hard when bid landscapes move fast.
  - The method learns each campaign's delivery pace from offline and online data and was implemented in a production DSP. The abstract reports improvement without figures.
- Hajiaghayi, M. and Springer, M. (2022). *Analysis of a Learning Based Algorithm for Budget Pacing*. arXiv 2205.13330. [research, read]
  - A learning algorithm learns a good average bid early in a campaign and paces the budget uniformly. It is robust to changes in the auction mechanism.
  - Its aim is to spend nearly the whole budget while pacing "to prevent early exit from the campaign and a loss of the opportunity to bid on potentially lucrative impressions later in the period". It is evaluated on open-source campaign data.
- Bergemann, D., Bonatti, A. and Wu, N. (2025). *Bidding with Budgets: Algorithmic and Data-Driven Bids in Digital Advertising*. Cowles Foundation Discussion Paper 2429; journal version *Bidding with Budgets: Data-Driven Bid Algorithms in Digital Advertising*, International Journal of Industrial Organization (June 2025). [research, read]
  - Abstract: "In digital advertising, the allocation of sponsored search, sponsored product, or display advertisements is mediated by auctions. The generation of bids in these auctions for attention is increasingly supported by auto-bidding algorithms and platform-provided data. We analyze the equilibrium properties of a sequence of increasingly sophisticated auto-bidding algorithms. First, we consider the equilibrium bidding behavior of an individual advertiser who controls the auto-bidding algorithm through the choice of their budget. …"
- Liu, M., Mao, J. and Kang, K. (2021). *Trustworthy and Powerful Online Marketplace Experimentation with Budget-split Design* (KDD 2021 title; the arXiv 2012.08724 title is *Trustworthy Online Marketplace Experimentation with Budget-split Design*); LinkedIn engineering blog *Budget-split testing* (2021). [research, record]
  - Splitting each campaign's budget into two identical sub-campaigns gives an unbiased marketplace A/B test that avoids budget cannibalisation.
  - LinkedIn reports up to 10× sensitivity, with tests shortened from weeks to 1–3 days.
  - The test-design use is owned by `method/change-measurement.md`. It is listed here because the bias it removes comes from shared budgets.
- Jeunen, O., Murphy, S. and Allison, B. (2022/2023). *Learning to Bid with AuctionGym* (AdKDD 2022 best paper), later *Off-Policy Learning-to-Bid with AuctionGym* (KDD 2023). Amazon; https://www.amazon.science/publications/learning-to-bid-with-auctiongym; code at github.com/amzn/auction-gym. [research, record] An open simulation environment for bandit and RL bid learning in ad auctions. The record does not say whether budgets are modelled.
- Chen, M., Nabi, S. and Siniscalchi, M. (2023/2024). *Advancing Ad Auction Realism: Practical Insights & Modeling Implications*. arXiv 2307.11732; the PDF is also hosted on cdn.amazon.science. [research, read] Real ad auctions have query-dependent values, unknown competitors, aggregated feedback and partly known payment rules. Advertisers are modelled as adversarial-bandit learners. The abstract does not treat budgets.
- Özlük, Ö. and Cholette, S. (Journal of Optimization Theory and Applications; reported by phys.org, 2012). [research, record] The authors argue that a strict daily budget on pay-per-click search "could be greatly harming" sales. The seat read only the news write-up.
- Heymann, B. (2025). *Counterfactual simulations for large scale systems with burnout variables*. arXiv 2509.04038 (submitted 2025-09-04). [research, read; gap fill 2026-11-09]
  - Budgets are "burnout variables - state variables that start active, shape dynamics, and irreversibly deactivate once certain conditions are met". Simulating "what-if scenarios in such systems is computationally demanding", and "This challenge arises in settings like online advertising, because of campaigns budgets, complicating counterfactual analysis despite rich data availability." The paper proposes "uncertainty relaxation" for parallel counterfactual estimation.
  - Transfer [seat]: a counterfactual of "what if the campaign had not run out" is a simulation of a path the campaign never took, because the budget cap ends the observed path; Amazon's missed-opportunity figures (F2) are an estimate of that kind, built from other campaigns.
- Pathak, N., Jeunen, O. and Lambert, E. (2026). *Auditing Marketing Budget Allocation with Hindsight Regret*. arXiv 2604.25977 (v2 2026-04-30). [research, read; gap fill 2026-11-09]
  - Spend-response functions are estimated "from historical logs", and "moderate feasible reallocations often capture most measurable gain, while larger shifts move into weak-support regions with higher uncertainty."
  - Transfer [seat]: a response estimated from the spend a campaign has had says little about spend levels it has never had; a recommended budget far above a campaign's history sits in that weak-support region.
- Blake, T., Nosko, C. and Tadelis, S. (2015). *Consumer Heterogeneity and Paid Search Effectiveness: A Large Scale Field Experiment*. NBER Working Paper 20171 (2014); *Econometrica* 83 (2015). https://www.nber.org/papers/w20171. [research, read; gap fill 2026-11-09]
  - eBay field experiments: "Because search clicks and purchase behavior are correlated, we show that returns from paid search are a fraction of conventional non-experimental estimates. As an extreme case, we show that brand-keyword ads have no measurable short-term benefits."
  - Transfer [seat]: missed "sales" on Amazon are click-attributed sales (F2); on brand terms in particular they can be sales the seller would have had without the ad. The full treatment is `beyond/metrics-and-attribution.md`'s and `areas/ads-organic-and-total.md`'s.
- [seat] Two searches found no Amazon Science paper on sponsored-ads budget pacing. Amazon's "spent as quickly as possible and not paced" is the greedy regime Agarwal et al. call suboptimal, and Amazon's smoothing is monthly (the daily lift and the month cap), not intraday. None of the research tests Amazon's mechanism.

## Stable enough for a skill

These hold across the pages read, or Amazon's API enforces them. The figures named are read live; the list states mechanisms.

1. A sponsored-ads daily budget is a monthly average. Spend in a calendar month does not exceed daily budget × days in the month, and any over-delivery is credited on the invoice. — *Average daily budget user guide*; *Understand budgets in Sponsored ads*; *View and understand your invoice*.
2. On a single day a campaign may spend more than its daily budget, up to a profile-level ceiling of +100% (the default) or +25% (opt-out), read from `isOptedOutForAverageDailyBudgetIncrease`. — *Average daily budget user guide*; release notes 2023-03-28 and 2023-06-20; *Understand budgets in Sponsored ads*.
3. Sponsored ads are not paced within the day. Budget is spent as auctions are won, and an out-of-budget campaign stops until the daily reset. — *Understand budgets in Sponsored ads*; *Sponsored Products budget basics and best practices*; *Troubleshoot your campaign status*.
4. A mid-month change caps the rest of the month at the new budget × the remaining days. Several changes in one day apply the day's highest budget today and the latest budget tomorrow. A cut below today's spend stops today's spend. — *Edit your Sponsored ads campaign budget*.
5. SB offers daily or lifetime budgets. A live campaign cannot be switched from daily to lifetime (the help page's route is a copy into a new campaign), a live lifetime budget can only rise, and a lifetime budget needs start and end dates. SP and SD are daily. — *Edit your Sponsored ads campaign budget*; *Sponsored Brands v4* OpenAPI; *Sponsored Products v3* OpenAPI.
6. Out-of-budget and money stops are machine-readable as serving status:
   - SP `CAMPAIGN_OUT_OF_BUDGET`, `PORTFOLIO_OUT_OF_BUDGET`, `ACCOUNT_OUT_OF_BUDGET`, `ADVERTISER_PAYMENT_FAILURE` and others;
   - v1 `deliveryReasons`;
   - the portfolio `servingStatus`.

   — *Computed status and campaign status*; *Common models: enums*; *Sponsored Products v3*, *Amazon Ads API v1* and *Portfolios v3* OpenAPI specs.
7. Budget usage percent is today's spend divided by today's effective budget. The effective budget includes rule increases and the average-daily-budget lift. It is a POST per ad product and for portfolios, at most 100 ids. — *Budget usage overview*; the SP, SB, SD and Portfolios OpenAPI specs.
8. Budget recommendations return a suggested daily budget, a 7-day share of time in budget (a 0–1 decimal in the SP spec; the SD example reads as a percentage), and lower and upper bounds of missed impressions, clicks and sales, per campaign, for SP, SB and SD. They are estimates and need history (Amazon suggests 9 days). — *Budget recommendations and missed opportunities*; SP, SB and SD OpenAPI specs.
9. Budget rules only raise the budget. They are schedule-based (date range, event, or weekly recurrence on chosen days, with an optional hour window in US, CA, UK, IN and JP) or performance-based (ACOS, CTR, CVR or ROAS on SP and SD; ROAS, IS or NTB on SB; 7-day seller and 14-day vendor lookback). Increases from rules that meet their conditions add together. A campaign can have up to 250 rules. — *Budget rule evaluation*; *Budget Rules overview*; *Schedule-based rules*; *Performance-based rules*; release notes 2023-01-31, 2023-03-20, 2023-04-06 and 2023-11-16.
10. A rule-raised budget is read back as SP `budget.effectiveBudget`, SB and SD `ruleBasedBudget.value`, or v1 `monetaryBudget.ruleValue`. `applicableRuleId` can be incomplete, so the active rules are read from the campaign's rule list. — *Sponsored Products v3*, *Sponsored Brands v4* and *Sponsored Display v3* OpenAPI specs; *Viewing changes to daily budgets for campaigns with budget rules*; *Amazon Ads API v1* OpenAPI.
11. A portfolio cap is date-range, monthly-recurring or none. A campaign is in at most one portfolio. Hitting the cap stops all its campaigns with `PORTFOLIO_OUT_OF_BUDGET`. — *Get started with Portfolios*; *Portfolios v3* OpenAPI; *Computed status and campaign status*.
12. In a portfolio with unspent-budget sharing on (the default for portfolios created after 2025-06-20), a campaign that has spent its budget can draw on other campaigns' leftovers from earlier days in the month. The monthly bound is the portfolio's sum of daily budgets × days, and the pool resets each month. — *Sharing leftover budget in a portfolio*; release notes 2025-07-07 and 2025-07-19.
13. The SP account daily budget cap is seller-only and console-set. Hitting it stops all SP campaigns with `ACCOUNT_OUT_OF_BUDGET` (v1 `ADVERTISER_OUT_OF_BUDGET`). — *Edit your Sponsored ads campaign budget*; *Troubleshoot your campaign status*; *Computed status and campaign status*; *Amazon Ads API v1* OpenAPI.
14. v1 campaign budgets are a list of monetary budgets with a recurrence period. Global SP campaigns carry one budget per marketplace in local currency. — *Amazon Ads API v1* OpenAPI; *Global campaigns for Sponsored Products*.
15. Budgets are in marketplace currency, and Amazon refuses a budget outside the marketplace range or below the campaign's bids (`BUDGET_TOO_LOW`, `BUDGET_OUT_OF_MARKET_PLACE_RANGE`, `BUDGET_LT_*_BIDS`, `BUDGET_CURRENCY_DOES_NOT_MATCH_MARKETPLACE_SETTINGS`). — *Portfolios v3* and *Sponsored Products v3* OpenAPI specs.
16. Cost is net of detected invalid traffic. Most IVT is removed in real time, cost can still move for 72 hours, data is restated within 30 days, and invoices carry IVT credits and debits. The gross and invalid report gives per-campaign IVT. — *Traffic validation*; *Understand campaign spend*; *Sponsored Products description of methodology*; *Gross and invalid traffic reports*.
17. A payment failure pauses campaigns. On seller-balance payment, Amazon retries at a fixed frequency and reinstates the account when a charge succeeds. A backup card is charged the full invoice when the primary fails. — *Payment methods (Sponsored ads)*; *Resolve a payment failure*; *Add a backup payment method*.
18. The invoice differs from campaign cost by IVT adjustments, over-delivery credits, promotional credits, taxes, regulatory fees and month-boundary delays. — *Understand campaign spend*; *View and understand your invoice*; *Regulatory Advertising Fees*.
19. Change history (SP and SB, 90 days) records budget edits and in-budget changes, without who made them. — *Change history* OpenAPI.
20. Hourly cost is available in v1 reports through `hour.value`, for the last 14 days. — *Dates and historical data retention*; *Hour (Time)*.
21. Amazon builds its missed sales from modelled missed clicks (from similar campaigns that stayed in budget) and the campaign's own historical CTR and sales, and calls the figures estimates "for reference only". The sales are click-attributed sales. — *Sponsored Products budget basics and best practices*; *Budget report for Sponsored Products*; *Sponsored Products v3* OpenAPI; *Reporting columns (version 3)*.
22. An SB budget is written with `PUT /sb/v4/campaigns` (`budget`, 1–10 campaigns); the budget type is set at create only. An SD v3 budget is written with `PUT /sd/campaigns` (`budget`, at most 100), whose reply does not echo the new value. — *Sponsored Brands v4* and *Sponsored Display v3* OpenAPI specs; *Edit your Sponsored ads campaign budget*.
23. The SD v1 update-budget schema lacks `recurrenceTimePeriod`, which its read model requires, and a developer reports such updates silently ignored with a 207. — *Amazon Ads API v1, Sponsored Display* OpenAPI; issue #475.
24. A global SP campaign's budgets are per marketplace in local currency; adding a marketplace needs its budget in the same update, and an `AUTO`-allocated global campaign accepts only the global budget. Status per marketplace is in `status.marketplaceSettings[]`. — *Global campaigns for Sponsored Products*; *Sponsored Products v3* and *Amazon Ads API v1* OpenAPI specs.
25. Portfolios, and so the only monthly cap an advertiser sets as an amount (`MONTHLY_RECURRING`), are per profile (one marketplace) in that marketplace's currency. — *Portfolios v3* OpenAPI; *Get started with Portfolios*.

## Open questions

1. Which timezone defines "midnight" for the daily budget reset, and the calendar month for the cap? The budget pages say "midnight" and "calendar month" without a zone. The nearest statements are on the reporting side: v1 reports aggregate "based on each campaign timezone" (`timeZone.value`), and the SP budget recommendation marks its dates "Local time". No budget page states the reset zone.
2. Does the month cap apply per campaign, per portfolio with sharing on, or per profile on the invoice? The average-daily-budget guide states it per campaign. The sharing guide states it per portfolio. The over-delivery credit on the invoice is described without a scope.
3. Is there any API operation that reads or sets the seller SP account daily budget cap? None was found. Only its effect (`ACCOUNT_OUT_OF_BUDGET` / `ADVERTISER_OUT_OF_BUDGET`) is visible.
4. Is the console's two-year budget report (average time in budget, missed-opportunity ranges by day, week, month or quarter) available as an Ads API report type? The help page says the data can be accessed "through the Advertising API", but neither seat found a report type with its columns. The API's missed-opportunity read found is a single 7-day window.
5. Does `POST /history` with the `IN_BUDGET` filter record every out-of-budget transition of an SP campaign, with a timestamp precise enough to give the hour it ran out? This needs a live read on a seller's account.
6. When clicks on an out-of-budget campaign are invalidated within the same day, does the campaign re-enter auctions that day? The Stream dataset says a duplicate usage notification may follow an invalidation, which suggests the usage figure drops. The serving effect is not stated.

## Positions

**P-budgets-and-pacing-1.** A sponsored-ads daily budget is averaged over the calendar month: spend in the month does not exceed the daily budget × the days in that month, and the invoice is adjusted for any over-delivery. [official] *Average daily budget user guide*; *Understand budgets in Sponsored ads* (help GTGPQGUXNCTHE2DS).

**P-budgets-and-pacing-2.** On a single day Amazon may spend up to 100% more than a campaign's daily budget (the default), or up to 25% more if the profile has opted out. The setting is one per profile, read as `isOptedOutForAverageDailyBudgetIncrease` on `GET /accountBudgets/featureFlags`. [official] *Average daily budget user guide*; release notes 2023-03-28 and 2023-06-20; *Understand budgets in Sponsored ads*.

**P-budgets-and-pacing-3.** Amazon's library guide *Sponsored Products budget basics and best practices* still gives a 25% daily ceiling and the "$12.50 on a $10 budget" example as of 2026-11-09, while the API guide, the release notes (2023-03-28, 2023-06-20) and the help page give 100% as the default and 25% as an opt-out. [official] *Sponsored Products budget basics and best practices*; *Average daily budget user guide*; release note 2023-06-20.

**P-budgets-and-pacing-4.** Amazon states that sponsored-ads budgets "are spent as quickly as possible and not paced", and that an out-of-budget campaign is not eligible to serve until the daily reset. [official] *Understand budgets in Sponsored ads*; *Sponsored Products budget basics and best practices*; *Troubleshoot your campaign status* (help GEC8BVH43CU9UNYH).

**P-budgets-and-pacing-5.** Display, Video, and Audio campaigns (the buying formerly sold as Amazon DSP, now a campaign type in the Amazon Ads Agent platform) are paced: a flight budget is split into daily and minute-level targets, and bids are adjusted every few minutes to meet them. Sponsored ads in the same platform are not paced (P-budgets-and-pacing-4). [official] *Understand delivery and pacing* (help GCELVBWV7S8S9AJA); *Amazon Ads API v1* OpenAPI (`deliveryProfile` EVEN, PACE_AHEAD, ASAP).

**P-budgets-and-pacing-6.** After a mid-month budget change, spend for the rest of the month is capped at the new daily budget × the remaining days. Amazon's example is $50/day with $800 spent by the 20th, then $10/day, for at most $900 in the month. [official] *Edit your Sponsored ads campaign budget* (help GVYUKBJQFPH7ZQ2L).

**P-budgets-and-pacing-7.** When a budget is changed several times in a day, the day's highest budget applies that day and the last one applies from the next day. A budget cut below what has already been spent today stops spend for the rest of the day. [official] *Edit your Sponsored ads campaign budget*.

**P-budgets-and-pacing-8.** Amazon says a budget change on an out-of-budget campaign "typically" takes 1–2 hours to show as Delivering, and it advises against frequent budget changes within one day. [official] *Troubleshoot your campaign status*.

**P-budgets-and-pacing-9.** Sponsored Brands campaigns have a daily or a lifetime budget. A live campaign cannot be switched from a daily to a lifetime budget (Amazon's route is to copy it into a new campaign), a live lifetime budget can only be increased, and a lifetime budget requires start and end dates. SP campaigns take daily budgets only. [official] *Edit your Sponsored ads campaign budget*; *Sponsored Brands v4* OpenAPI; *Sponsored Products v3* OpenAPI.

**P-budgets-and-pacing-10.** A Sponsored Display campaign created through the API without a budget gets a regional default budget in local currency, for example 100 in the US and 10,000 in Japan as of 2026-11-09. [official] *Sponsored Display v3* OpenAPI; *Limits, constraints, and quotas* (Default budgets).

**P-budgets-and-pacing-11.** The SP serving status names each money stop:
- `CAMPAIGN_OUT_OF_BUDGET`: the campaign's daily budget is spent;
- `PORTFOLIO_OUT_OF_BUDGET`: the portfolio cap is reached;
- `ACCOUNT_OUT_OF_BUDGET`: the seller's account daily cap is reached;
- `ADVERTISER_PAYMENT_FAILURE`: a payment failed.

The status is returned with `includeExtendedDataFields: true` on `POST /sp/campaigns/list`. [official] *Computed status and campaign status*; *Sponsored Products v3* OpenAPI.

**P-budgets-and-pacing-12.** In Ads API v1 a campaign's `status.deliveryReasons[]` carries the money reasons: `CAMPAIGN_OUT_OF_BUDGET`, `PORTFOLIO_OUT_OF_BUDGET`, `ADVERTISER_OUT_OF_BUDGET` (the seller SP account cap), postpay and prepay limits, `ADVERTISER_PAYMENT_FAILURE` and `SPEND_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. [official] *Amazon Ads API v1* OpenAPI; *Common models: enums*.

**P-budgets-and-pacing-13.** Budget usage percent is the day's spend divided by the day's effective budget, which includes budget-rule increases and the average-daily-budget lift. It is read per campaign or portfolio with `POST /sp|sb|sd/campaigns/budget/usage` or `POST /portfolios/budget/usage`, at most 100 ids per call. [official] *Budget usage overview*; SP, SB, SD and Portfolios OpenAPI specs.

**P-budgets-and-pacing-14.** Amazon's push feed of budget usage (Stream v1 `budget-usage`, every 5% consumed) requires an SQS or Firehose destination and shuts off on 2027-06-30. The Stream v2 dataset list has no budget-usage dataset, and Amazon's deprecation notice says budget consumption notifications "are not carried forward in Stream v2" and points to the Campaign Management API instead. [official] *Sponsored ads budget usage dataset*; *Stream v2 datasets overview*; *Reporting overview*; *Deprecations* (September 2026 entry).

**P-budgets-and-pacing-15.** The SP budget recommendation returns a suggested daily budget, the share of the last 7 days the campaign was in budget (a decimal from 0 to 1 in the SP spec), and lower and upper estimates of missed impressions, clicks and sales. Amazon calls these estimates "not a guarantee" and suggests waiting until a campaign has been active nine days. [official] *Budget recommendations and missed opportunities*; *Sponsored Products v3* OpenAPI.

**P-budgets-and-pacing-16.** Budget recommendations with missed-opportunity estimates exist for SB (`/sb/campaigns/budgetRecommendations`) and SD (`/sd/campaigns/budgetRecommendations`) as well as SP. When it launched them, Amazon said roughly ten percent of SB campaigns went out of budget daily (2023). [official] *Sponsored Brands v4* and *Sponsored Display v3* OpenAPI specs; release notes 2023-01-31 and 2023-07-20.

**P-budgets-and-pacing-17.** Amazon describes the console's recommended budget as estimated missed clicks × estimated CPC, from a machine-learning model of similar campaigns that stayed in budget. The suggested budget at creation is based on campaigns with similar ASINs by category, price, bids, strategy and targeting. [official] *Sponsored Products budget basics and best practices*; *Understand budgets in Sponsored ads*.

**P-budgets-and-pacing-18.** Budget rules only raise a campaign's daily budget, by a percentage. Schedule-based rules cover a date range, a recommended event or a weekly recurrence on chosen days, with an optional hour window in US, CA, UK, IN and JP only. Performance-based rules apply when a metric meets a threshold. [official] *Understand budget rules in Sponsored ads* (help GNSMLANWNF344YBE); *Schedule-based rules*; *Create budget rules for sponsored ads campaigns* (help G3HZWVWYE23DBR2V).

**P-budgets-and-pacing-19.** The metrics available to performance-based rules are ACOS, CTR, CVR and ROAS on SP and SD, and ROAS, top-of-search impression share and new-to-brand on SB. They are computed over the previous 7 days for sellers and 14 days for vendors. [official] *Budget Rules overview*; *Performance-based rules*; release note 2023-04-06.

**P-budgets-and-pacing-20.** When several budget rules meet their conditions, their increases add. Amazon's example is +40% and +100%, which apply as +140% on the day. A campaign can carry up to 250 rules. [official] *Budget rule evaluation*; release notes 2023-01-31 and 2023-03-20.

**P-budgets-and-pacing-21.** The API guide says budget rules are evaluated at the start of the day, and again whenever a rule is created or updated or the campaign's daily budget changes. The help page describes a daily review that sets the next day's budget. [official] *Budget rule evaluation*; *Understand budget rules in Sponsored ads*.

**P-budgets-and-pacing-22.** A performance-based rule does not apply below a minimum daily budget and shows the status "Budget threshold not met". The published figure is $10, stated on the current help page and in the 2021 announcement; no figure in another currency was found. [official] *Budget rule evaluation*; *Understand budget rules in Sponsored ads* (help GNSMLANWNF344YBE); *Budget rules now available in Amazon advertising console globally* (what's new, 2021-08-12); *Performance-based rules*.

**P-budgets-and-pacing-23.** A rule-raised budget is read back as SP `budget.effectiveBudget`, SB and SD `ruleBasedBudget.value` or v1 `monetaryBudget.ruleValue`. Amazon warns that `applicableRuleId` can be incomplete when several rules are active, and the campaign's rule list is the full record. [official] *Sponsored Products v3*, *Sponsored Brands v4* and *Sponsored Display v3* OpenAPI specs; *Viewing changes to daily budgets for campaigns with budget rules*; *Amazon Ads API v1* OpenAPI.

**P-budgets-and-pacing-24.** Amazon returns recommended events for budget rules (SP and SB), each with an event id, dates and a suggested percentage increase. A rule tied to an event whose dates are not yet announced takes the dates once Amazon announces them. [official] *Schedule-based rules*; *Sponsored Products v3* OpenAPI; SB v3 OpenAPI.

**P-budgets-and-pacing-25.** A portfolio budget cap is a date range (`DATE_RANGE`), a monthly renewing amount (`MONTHLY_RECURRING`) or none (`NO_CAP`). A campaign belongs to at most one portfolio. Reaching the cap stops the portfolio's campaigns with `PORTFOLIO_OUT_OF_BUDGET`. [official] *Get started with Portfolios*; *Portfolios v3* OpenAPI; *Computed status and campaign status*.

**P-budgets-and-pacing-26.** With unspent-budget sharing on, a portfolio's campaigns that have spent their own daily budget, including their own leftovers, can use other campaigns' leftovers from earlier days in the month, up to 100% over daily budget. The portfolio's month stays within the sum of daily budgets × days. Sharing is on by default for portfolios created after 2025-06-20 and is set per portfolio. [official] *Sharing leftover budget in a portfolio*; release notes 2025-07-07 and 2025-07-19.

**P-budgets-and-pacing-27.** The Sponsored Products account daily budget cap is a seller-only console setting. It caps the combined daily spend of all SP campaigns in the account, and no Ads API operation for setting it was found. [official] *Edit your Sponsored ads campaign budget*; *Troubleshoot your campaign status*; *Computed status and campaign status*.

**P-budgets-and-pacing-28.** In Ads API v1 a campaign's budgets are `budgets[]` of `MONETARY` budgets with `recurrenceTimePeriod` `DAILY`, `LIFETIME` or `MONTHLY`. A global SP campaign carries a separate budget per marketplace in local currency in `marketplaceSettings`. [official] *Amazon Ads API v1* OpenAPI; *Global campaigns for Sponsored Products*.

**P-budgets-and-pacing-29.** Budgets are set in the marketplace's currency. Amazon refuses a budget outside the marketplace's range, below the campaign's bids, or in a mismatched currency, and names the reason in the error. [official] *Sponsored Products v3* and *Portfolios v3* OpenAPI specs; *Limits, constraints, and quotas*.

**P-budgets-and-pacing-30.** Amazon filters invalid traffic before the bid and after the impression, and bills net of it. Most of it is removed in real time. Cost can change for up to 72 hours, data is restated within 30 days, and invoices carry credits and debits for invalidated or revalidated clicks. [official] *Traffic validation* (help GBYPYH79NGJJ5JPS); *Understand campaign spend* (help GUJY2CG5DTCPDCVQ); *Sponsored Products description of methodology* (help G8KL93RTVG44RZAQ); *View and understand your invoice*.

**P-budgets-and-pacing-31.** Amazon names a fall in IVR together with a rise in CTR, impressions or clicks and a fall in CVR as a higher-confidence sign of undetected invalid traffic on sponsored ads. The per-campaign figures are in the `spGrossAndInvalids`, `sbGrossAndInvalids` and `sdGrossAndInvalids` reports. [official] *Traffic validation*; *Gross and invalid traffic reports*.

**P-budgets-and-pacing-32.** A failed advertising payment pauses an account's campaigns. On seller-balance payment Amazon retries at a fixed frequency and reinstates the account when a charge succeeds, and a backup card is charged the full invoice when the primary method fails. [official] *Payment methods (Sponsored ads)* (help GMELNQQNEEVLX456); *Resolve a payment failure* (help G2SQRLBPB8TY587E); *Add a backup payment method* (help GPGZ28EK9WM4LZ2E).

**P-budgets-and-pacing-33.** Sponsored-ads charges are invoiced each time accumulated cost reaches the account's credit limit, and at least monthly on the second day of the next month. The credit limit rises with successful payments. [official] *Understand Amazon Ads billing cycles* (help GY3YNTWRZYVPLADL); *View and understand your invoice*.

**P-budgets-and-pacing-34.** An invoice differs from campaign cost by invalid-traffic adjustments, over-delivery credits, promotional credits, taxes, regulatory advertising fees and charges carried across a month boundary. Promotional credits lower the invoice and do not raise any budget. [official] *Understand campaign spend*; *View and understand your invoice*; *Understand advertising promotions* (help GFAN744AATXXDQBB); *Regulatory Advertising Fees* (help GRQM2HSUXGUWFJ3H).

**P-budgets-and-pacing-35.** The billing API reports billing status, billing notifications (including whether campaigns are suspended and the suspension date), invoices with campaign and portfolio lines, and payment methods. Amazon documents the billing-profiles API as supporting vendor accounts and not seller or ADSP accounts; the other billing guides state no seller limit. [official] *Advertising Billing* OpenAPI; *Billing profiles*; *Billing: getting started*; *Payments*.

**P-budgets-and-pacing-36.** Change history (`POST /history`) returns up to 90 days of SP and SB campaign changes, including budget amounts and in-budget changes, without who made them. [official] *Change history* OpenAPI.

**P-budgets-and-pacing-37.** Hourly cost per campaign is available from Ads API v1 reports with the `hour.value` dimension, kept for 14 days with a 14-day maximum range. [official] *Dates and historical data retention*; *Hour (Time)*; *Helpful concepts* (reporting v1).

**P-budgets-and-pacing-38.** Sponsored Products off-Amazon placements are governed by `offAmazonBudgetControlStrategy`, which the v1 spec describes as maximising off-Amazon reach "within the budget" (`MAXIMIZE_REACH`) or minimising off-Amazon spend (`MINIMIZE_SPEND`, on SP v3 "Restrict ad placements to Amazon owned-and-operated properties only"). Amazon's status page says off-site cost is monitored in a placement report, "as these metrics are not visible in Campaign Manager". [official] *Amazon Ads API v1* and *Sponsored Products v3* OpenAPI specs; *Troubleshoot your campaign status*.

**P-budgets-and-pacing-39.** Trellis (Mike Lepine, 2026) advises recording the time of day each campaign ran out of budget, and Intentwise (Michael Waters, 2024) observes that advertisers commonly exhaust budgets by the evening and that CPCs are lower at night. [opinion] Trellis, *Amazon Ads Budget Pacing SOP*; Intentwise, *Amazon PPC Dayparting: The Expert's Guide*.

[opinion] Clear Ads Agency, *Does Amazon Dayparting Save Money or Destroy Campaigns?*.

- [seat] On sponsored ads, "pacing" is the advertiser's own work, done with bids, schedule rules and budget changes. Amazon has no intraday pacer there, and its smoothing is monthly (the daily lift and the month cap).
- [seat] An out-of-budget day is visible in three Amazon reads of different reach:
  - the current `budgetUsagePercent`;
  - the 7-day `percentTimeInBudget`;
  - `IN_BUDGET` change events and `hour.value` cost, which can date the stop to the hour within 14 days.
- [seat] Because the effective budget includes rule increases and the daily lift, a report showing cost above the typed budget is not by itself an overspend. The month cap is enforced on the invoice.
- [seat] A seller using an optimiser that rewrites budgets daily will see any other budget write overwritten. Change history does not name the writer, so the daily-edit pattern is the only clue.
- [seat] A payment failure (`ADVERTISER_PAYMENT_FAILURE`, or a billing notification with `impact: CAMPAIGNS_SUSPENDED`) stops all delivery whatever the budgets, so no budget figure explains such a stop.
