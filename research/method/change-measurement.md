# Measuring an ad change

Read 2026-11-09 by the research seat. Tags: [official] Amazon's own words (Ads API docs and OpenAPI models, Amazon Ads library and launch pages, Sell on Amazon pages, Seller Forums); [opinion] a named practitioner, agency or paid tool; [research] published research; [seat] the seat's own reasoning, allowed in this file and never a position. Figures that Amazon may change are quoted with their page and date, and are marked "for human readers only": a skill reads them where they live, at run time.

## What this covers

**Which part of a seller's ad work.** Every ad change a seller or cx makes is a bet: a bid raised, a budget cut, a keyword added, a negative added, a campaign split, a placement adjustment, a bidding strategy switched, a Sponsored Brands creative swapped, a campaign paused. This file is about the step after the bet: how to tell whether it paid. That means the change record (what was there before, what was sent, what Amazon answered, what Amazon's own history says, and what was expected), the windows (baseline, lag, observation, and the date the observation window's figures stop moving), the confounders that move ad and total figures on their own, the experiment tools Amazon offers for ads as of 2026-11-09 and what they do not cover, the on-off (pause) test and its research basis, the statistics of small ad samples, and the three verdicts (*held*, *not held*, *cannot tell*) that cx-listing-playbook's `effect-review` uses for listing changes.

**Lanes.** What each ad metric means and how attribution windows work in depth is `beyond/metrics-and-attribution.md`. How reports are created, polled and read is `method/reporting.md`. The Ads API operations themselves are `method/ads-api-surface.md`. Why spend or sales moved without a planned change is `areas/diagnosis.md`. Ads against organic and total sales, including how much of an ad sale is incremental, is `areas/ads-organic-and-total.md`. Season and event calendars are `areas/seasons-and-deals.md`. Amazon's automatic bidding is `areas/bids-and-placements.md`; budget rules and pacing are `areas/budgets-and-pacing.md`; Amazon's own agents and tools are `beyond/automation-and-amazon-tools.md`. This file takes from them only what a verdict on one change needs.

**Why a senior Amazon advertising lead cares.** Three things make ad changes harder to judge than listing changes, and an assistant that does not know them will report false wins and false losses:

1. *The figures for the newest days are not final.* Conversions are dated on the day of the ad click and keep arriving for the length of the look-back window; Amazon restates conversions afterwards and invalidates clicks for up to 30 days. A bid raised on Monday and "judged" on Thursday is judged on clicks that have not had time to convert.
2. *Amazon changes things inside the window.* Dynamic bidding moves bids on every auction, rule-based bidding moves base bids and can switch itself off, budget rules raise budgets on event days, Sponsored Brands can rewrite headlines, new paid placements are auto-enrolled, recommendations are applied in one click from the console, and an ad stops serving the moment the product loses the Featured Offer or goes out of stock. Amazon's change history does not say who made a change.
3. *Ad sales are attributed, not incremental.* An ad-attributed sale on a brand term can be a sale that would have come through the organic result anyway (Blake, Nosko & Tadelis 2015). A change that "improves ACOS" can lower total sales. The only clean answer is a randomized holdout, and Amazon gives sellers none for Sponsored Products.

The method in cx-listing-playbook's `research/method/effect-measurement.md` (read 2026-10-14, re-read for this file on 2026-11-09) carries over: one change per window where attribution is wanted, windows fixed before the change, whole weeks with matching weekdays, a control series where one exists, a confounder check, no verdict during a major sale, counts and not only percentages, and three verdicts. This file adds what is specific to ads.

## Amazon's own rules and guidance

### A. The change history API (RULE: the API contract)

The Ads API has one operation for the history of entity changes. It is described in Amazon's OpenAPI model `Changehistory_prod_3p.json` (title "Change history", version "3.0"), served from the docs site's model host at `https://d1y2lf8k3vrkfu.cloudfront.net/openapi/en-us/dest/Changehistory_prod_3p.json` and read 2026-11-09. No guide page for it was found on the docs host (the paths tried are listed under Unreachable). [official]

- **Operation.** `POST /history`, `operationId` `getHistory`, summary "History of entity changes." Permissions: `["advertiser_campaign_edit","advertiser_campaign_view"]`. [official]
- **Scope, verbatim.** "Returns history of changes for provided event sources that match the filters and time ranges specified. Only events that belong to the authenticated Advertiser can be queried. All times will be in UTC Epoch format." … "This API only returns change history for Sponsored Products and Sponsored Brands campaigns. Changes for Sponsored Display campaigns are not returned. Also, it doesn't return information about who made the change." [official]
- **IDs.** "This API accepts identifiers in either the alphamumeric format (default), or the numeric format. If numeric IDs are supplied, then numeric IDs will be returned otherwise, alphanumeric IDs are returned." (Amazon's spelling.) [official]
- **Versioning.** "Use `Accept: application/vnd.historyresponse.v1.1+json` to access version 1.1 features. **THEME event type requires version 1.1 or higher.** Without the proper Accept header, THEME requests will return a 400 error." The model's 400 example: `"Invalid request: Error: Key in HistoryEventTypes: THEME is not supported."` [official]
- **Request body** (required: `eventTypes`, `fromDate`, `toDate`):
  - `fromDate`, `toDate`: integers, epoch milliseconds in the model's example (`"fromDate": "1704195640000"`). `fromDate`'s description: "Max 90 days of history." (figure for human readers only)
  - `count`: "Requested number of results. Default 100. Minimum 50. Maximum 200." (figures for human readers only)
  - `nextToken`, or `pageOffset`: "Mutually exclusive with 'nextToken'. Max results with pageOffset is 10000. Use nextToken instead for more results." `totalResults` is "Only provided for pageOffset queries and capped at 10000."
  - `sort`: `{ "key": "DATE", "direction": "ASC" | "DESC" }`, default `DESC`.
  - `eventTypes`: an object keyed by `AD`, `AD_GROUP`, `CAMPAIGN`, `KEYWORD`, `NEGATIVE_KEYWORD`, `PRODUCT_TARGETING`, `THEME`. Each holds `eventTypeIds` ("max of 10 event types. IDs here belong to the EventType. For example, if requesting CAMPAGIN as the eventType, these are campaignIds."), `filters`, and `parents` ("maximum of 10 parents", each `campaignId`, `adGroupId`, `useProfileIdAdvertiser`: "If true, retrieves events which belong to the associated advertiser").
  - `filters` enum: `BID_AMOUNT`, `BUDGET_AMOUNT`, `END_DATE`, `IN_BUDGET`, `NAME`, `PLACEMENT_GROUP`, `SMART_BIDDING_STRATEGY`, `START_DATE`, `STATUS`; the description maps them: `BUDGET_AMOUNT`, `IN_BUDGET`, `END_DATE`, `START_DATE`, `PLACEMENT_GROUP`, `SMART_BIDDING_STRATEGY` to CAMPAIGN; `STATUS` to CAMPAIGN, AD_GROUP, AD, KEYWORD, PRODUCT_TARGET, NEGATIVE_KEYWORD; `BID_AMOUNT` to AD_GROUP; `NAME` to CAMPAIGN, AD_GROUP. [official]
- **Response.** `events[]`, `nextToken`, `totalResults`. Each event: `entityType` (`AD`, `AD_GROUP`, `CAMPAIGN`, `KEYWORD`, `NEGATIVE_KEYWORD`, `PRODUCT_TARGETING`, `THEME`), `entityId`, `changeType`, `previousValue`, `newValue`, `timestamp` ("unix time"), and `metadata` (`campaignId`, `adGroupId`, `campaignBudgetType` `DAILY`/`LIFETIME_BUDGET`, `keyword`, `keywordType` `KEYWORD_BROAD`/`KEYWORD_EXACT`/`KEYWORD_PHRASE`, `negativeTargetingType` `NEGATIVE_KEYWORD_BROAD`/`_EXACT`/`_PHRASE`, `placementGroupPosition` `DETAIL_PAGE`/`TOP_OF_SEARCH`, `predefinedTarget` `CLOSE-MATCH`/`COMPLEMENTS`/`LOOSE-MATCH`/`SUBSTITUTES`, `productTargetingType` `EXPRESSION`/`PREDEFINED`, `targetingExpression`, `targetingType` and `theme` for THEME). [official]
- **Change types by entity**, from the `changeType` description table: CAMPAIGN "STATUS, IN_BUDGET, PORTFOLIO, BUDGET_AMOUNT, NAME, START_DATE, END_DATE, SMART_BIDDING_STRATEGY, PLACEMENT_GROUP, CREATED, IS_PREMIUM_BID_ENABLED, PORTFOLIO_ID, BUDGET_DOCUMENT_VERSION, IS_PROCESSING, RULE_BASED_BUDGET_VALUE, APPLICABLE_RULE_NAME, APPLICABLE_RULE_ID, CREATION_DATE, AUTO_TARGETED"; AD_GROUP "STATUS, NAME, DEFAULT_BID_AMOUNT"; AD "STATUS"; KEYWORD "STATUS, BID_AMOUNT"; PRODUCT_TARGETING "STATUS, BID_AMOUNT"; NEGATIVE_KEYWORD "STATUS". [official]
- **The model disagrees with itself.** The `changeType` enum lists only `BID_AMOUNT`, `BUDGET_AMOUNT`, `DEFAULT_BID_AMOUNT`, `END_DATE`, `IN_BUDGET`, `NAME`, `PLACEMENT_GROUP`, `PORTFOLIO`, `SMART_BIDDING_STRATEGY`, `START_DATE`, `STATUS`. The description table names more for CAMPAIGN (`RULE_BASED_BUDGET_VALUE`, `APPLICABLE_RULE_NAME`, `APPLICABLE_RULE_ID`, `IS_PREMIUM_BID_ENABLED`, `AUTO_TARGETED`, and others). Which of these a reply actually carries is read from the reply. [official; the reconciliation is seat's]
- **Value forms**, from the `previousValue`/`newValue` table: STATUS "CREATED, ENABLED, PAUSED, ARCHIVED"; IN_BUDGET "true or false"; PORTFOLIO "portfolioId"; BUDGET_AMOUNT and BID_AMOUNT and DEFAULT_BID_AMOUNT "amount in marketplace currency"; START_DATE and END_DATE "unix time"; SMART_BIDDING_STRATEGY "OPTIMIZE_FOR_SALES, LEGACY, MANUAL"; PLACEMENT_GROUP "number as percent". Both fields are typed `string`. [official]
- **Gaps against the campaign model** [seat, from comparing the two models read 2026-11-09]:
  - The history's bidding-strategy values (`OPTIMIZE_FOR_SALES`, `LEGACY`, `MANUAL`) are not the Sponsored Products 3.0 names (`AUTO_FOR_SALES` "Dynamic bids - up and down", `LEGACY_FOR_SALES` "Dynamic bids - down only", `MANUAL` "Fixed bid", `RULE_BASED`, `OTHER`). No `RULE_BASED` value is listed in the history table.
  - `placementGroupPosition` lists `DETAIL_PAGE` and `TOP_OF_SEARCH`; the SP 3.0 placement enum has four (`PLACEMENT_TOP`, `PLACEMENT_PRODUCT_PAGE`, `PLACEMENT_REST_OF_SEARCH`, `SITE_AMAZON_BUSINESS`). A rest-of-search or Amazon Business adjustment may not appear in the history.
  - No event type for negative product targets, campaign-level negative targets, audience or shopper-cohort bid adjustments, ad creative edits (Sponsored Brands headline, image or video), budget-rule association, or optimization rules was found in the model.
  - No Sponsored Display, Sponsored TV or DSP events.
- **The console view.** A trade-press article reports a History tab for Sponsored Products and Sponsored Brands campaigns, and for Sponsored Products ad groups, giving "a summary of all changes made to campaign settings in the past 90 days", and says "The history feature is available in Amazon DSP." Amazon's own announcement URL (`advertising.amazon.com/blog/history-now-available-for-sponsored-products-and-sponsored-brands`) now lands on a general Sponsored ads page. [opinion, trade press: Luis Rijo, PPC Land, "Amazon rolls out a new history feature for Sponsored Products and Sponsored Brands campaigns", byline date 2020-01-20 as shown, read 2026-11-09]
- **Other history-shaped reads.** The budget-rule history endpoints (`GET /sp|sb|sd/campaigns/{campaignId}/budgetRules/budgetHistory`) were announced for deprecation on August 31, 2023 [official] (All releases, "Awareness: Budget rules history endpoints to be deprecated on August 31, 2023", read 2026-11-09). The Sponsored Products 3.0 model read 2026-11-09 has no `budgetHistory` path.

### B. The write's own receipt (RULE: the API contract)

The change history is one witness. The other is the reply to the write itself, which only exists if it is kept when it arrives.

- **Ads API v1 writes answer 207.** `POST /adsApi/v1/update/campaigns` (`operationId` `UpdateCampaign`) answers `207` with `CampaignMultiStatusResponseWithPartialErrors`: `success[]` (each `{ index, campaign }`, `index` 0–999), `partialSuccess[]` (`{ index, campaign, errors }`) and `error[]` (`{ index, errors }`, where the model's `ErrorsIndex.index` reads 0–19), each array capped at 1,000 entries. The returned `Campaign` carries `lastUpdatedDateTime` ("The date time that the campaign was last updated."), `creationDateTime`, `state`, `status` and `budgets`. Ad groups, ads and targets have the same multi-status shape (`AdGroupMultiStatusResponseWithPartialErrors`, `AdMultiStatusResponseWithPartialErrors`, `TargetMultiStatusResponseWithPartialErrors`). "Batch size limits are specific to each ad product." [official] (Amazon Ads API v1 merged OpenAPI, `AmazonAdsAPIALLMerged_prod_3p.json`, read 2026-11-09)
- **Sponsored Products 3.0 objects carry their own timestamps.** Every SP 3.0 `extendedData` (campaign, ad group, product ad, keyword, negative keyword, targeting clause, negative targeting clause, campaign negatives) has `creationDateTime`, `lastUpdateDateTime` ("Last updated date in ISO 8601."), `servingStatus` and `servingStatusDetails`. [official] (Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI, `SponsoredProducts_prod_3p.json`, read 2026-11-09). Note the spelling: `lastUpdateDateTime` in SP 3.0, `lastUpdatedDateTime` in v1.
  - The object is returned when the list request sets `includeExtendedDataFields: true`. The model's description of that flag names the fields differently from the object it returns: "Whether to get entity with extended data fields such as creationDate, lastUpdateDate, servingStatus", while the returned schemas (for example `SponsoredProductsCampaignExtendedData`) hold `creationDateTime`, `lastUpdateDateTime`, `servingStatus` and `servingStatusDetails`. The field names a reply carries are the schema's. [official] (SP 3.0 OpenAPI, re-read 2026-11-09 for the gap fill; `method/ads-api-surface.md` §H owns the request shape and records the flag's own wording)
- **Labels on objects.** v1 campaigns and targets have `tags`, "Open ended labels with a key value pair applied to the campaign" (and "…target"); the v1 Campaign entity guide marks `tags.Tag[].key` and `.value` as supported for Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands and DSP. A labels API also exists (`/adsApi/v1/query/labels`, `/adsApi/v1/query/labelObjectAssociations`; `LabelObjectAssociation` with `labelObjectId` "The identifier of the advertising object this label is applied to (e.g., a deal ID, ad group ID)"). [official] (v1 merged OpenAPI; Campaign entity guide, `https://advertising.amazon.com/API/docs/en-us/guides/campaign-management/entities/campaign`, read 2026-11-09 from its markdown source)
- **A lost reply.** cx-amazon-ads' door reads every `POST` as a change; a `POST` whose reply was lost reads `sent, outcome unknown`, and a redirect is followed nowhere. [door.md, ComChatX's own behaviour, read 2026-11-09] Whether the change landed is then read from the object (its `lastUpdatedDateTime` / `lastUpdateDateTime` and the field itself) or from `POST /history`. [seat]
- **What no Amazon read holds.** Neither the history nor the write reply holds the expected effect, the windows, the reason, the metric to be judged, or who asked. Those exist only if they are written down before the change. [seat]

### C. When a window's figures arrive, and when they stop moving (RULE about processing; GUIDANCE on waiting; figures for human readers only)

Three Amazon pages give three different horizons for "final". All three were read 2026-11-09 from the docs site's markdown host.

- **Sponsored ads reporting FAQ (version 3)** (`guides/reporting/v3/faq`):
  - "Initial impression and click data is available using the API within 12 hours. Note that small changes to impression and click data may happen up to three days after the initial click date due to the traffic validation process. We recommend retrieving click and impression data again after three days to ensure you have the latest data."
  - "Initial conversion data is available within 24 hours, but may change as part of the restatement process. Restatements of conversion data occur 1, 7, and 28 days after the conversion event. Because conversions are reported on the date of the ad interaction, restatement includes all dates in your attribution window. For example, if your attribution window is 14 days, restatement can update attribution up to 42 days (14 plus 28) after the report date."
  - "We currently recommend 1-2 report requests, per advertiser, per day, for each report type. This is based on data refresh rates."
  - The page opens: "**Reporting API v3 will be deprecated on June 30, 2027.**" [official]
- **Amazon Ads reporting overview** (`guides/reporting/overview`):
  - "The invalid traffic detection system monitors traffic data for up to 30 days after the fact to recognize invalid traffic patterns. We recommend waiting at least 5 days for the most accurate click data to be available before evaluating campaign performance."
  - "Conversion data is refreshed and validated at daily, weekly, and monthly intervals and is subject to change for up to 60 days back from the current date. We recommend re-retrieving reports monthly to help ensure your conversion metrics are up to date."
  - "Amazon Marketing Stream v2 delivers data updates and automated backfill up to 70 days on subscription creation, so your data self-corrects as attribution validation completes." [official]
- **Amazon Marketing Stream v2** (datasets overview and `ads-performance` dataset page):
  - "Records can be restated after first delivery as attribution data matures. A restated record arrives as a new version of the same key. Each dataset defines a restatement window — the period during which a record can still change — in its dataset specifications. Treat a record as final only after its window has passed."
  - `ads-performance-v1`: grain "Hourly", "Restatement window | 70 days". [official] (Overview [Stream v2 datasets], `guides/amazon-marketing-stream/v2/datasets/overview`; Ads performance, `guides/amazon-marketing-stream/v2/datasets/ads-performance`; both read 2026-11-09)
- **Amazon Ads help, Traffic validation** (GBYPYH79NGJJ5JPS, `lastModified` 2026-11-02; read 2026-11-09 for the gap fill from the article JSON embedded in the page, after the earlier pass found only a script shell): "Most post-impression IVT is invalidated in real time. Some takes longer to detect, so we monitor traffic for up to 30 days after it occurs. IVT detected in this window is automatically removed from invoices and advertiser reports". "If an investigation identifies previously undetected IVT, affected metrics and invoices may be restated." "IVT metrics are available in report center and in Sponsored Ads Gross and Invalid Traffic report", as click and impression invalidation rates per campaign per day. [official] (Traffic validation, `https://advertising.amazon.com/help/GBYPYH79NGJJ5JPS`). So a click count is not final for 30 days, and an investigation can restate it later still.
- **The horizons do not agree.** 3 days (clicks, FAQ), 30 days (invalid traffic, overview), up to 42 days for a 14-day window (FAQ), 60 days (overview) and 70 days (Stream v2). The FAQ's 42 days is an example built on a 14-day window; with a 7-day window the same arithmetic gives 35 days [seat]. The Stream page is the only one that states the rule as "final only after its window has passed". [official, with the comparison seat's]
- **Dating.** "All conversion metrics are now reported on 'traffic date,' meaning conversions are reported on the date of the ad interaction rather than the date the conversion occurred." Example: "If a shopper clicked an ad on Oct 1 and purchased on Oct 3, the purchase conversion is now reported on Oct 1". [official] (Overview of the Reporting v1 API, `guides/reporting/ads-v1/overview`, read 2026-11-09)
- **Look-back.** "**Sponsored Products:** Base metrics automatically align with campaign defaults—7-day look-back for sellers and 14-day look-back for vendors—consistent with the advertising console and downloadable reports." "**Other campaign types:** The standard look-back is 14 days". v3 columns keep the window in the name: `purchases7d` "Number of attributed conversion events occurring within 7 days of an ad click"; `purchasesSameSku7d` "…where the purchased `SKU` was the same as the `SKU` advertised"; `salesOtherSku7d` "…where the purchased `SKU` was different from the `SKU` advertised". [official] (Overview of the Reporting v1 API; Reporting columns (version 3), `guides/reporting/v3/columns`; both read 2026-11-09)
- **Days are campaign-timezone days.** "By default, performance is measured and data is aggregated based on each campaign timezone, which can also be included in the report via the `timeZone.value` dimension field." "Future releases will include support for UTC time zone normalization". The change history's timestamps are "UTC Epoch". [official] (Dates and historical data retention, `guides/reporting/ads-v1/dates`; Changehistory model; both read 2026-11-09)
- **Retention** (figures for human readers only): v1 `hour.value` 14 days (max range 14 days), `date.value` 24 months (max range 120 days), `week.value` and `day.value` 24 months (15 months), `month.value` 6 years (25 months); benchmark dimensions "Available from 6/1/2025". v3 campaign reports: 95 days for SP, and 60, 65, 95, 95 days for the other columns of the page's table. The change history: 90 days. [official] (Dates and historical data retention; Campaign reports (v3), `guides/reporting/v3/report-types/campaign`; Changehistory model; all read 2026-11-09) A baseline older than 90 days has its performance data but no change history from `POST /history`. [seat]
- **Search terms below a threshold disappear.** The v3 FAQ: "The API search term report only contains impressions that also had at least one click." Stream v2's dataset page: "In this dataset, `searchTerm.value` is subject to reporting thresholds"; the datasets overview says values under the threshold are "grouped into a row with value `(Other)`" and "Totals stay complete: the sum of all rows at a grain, including `(Other)`, matches the totals at coarser grains." [official] (Sponsored ads reporting FAQ (version 3); Ads performance [Stream v2]; Overview [Stream v2 datasets]; all read 2026-11-09)
- **Ratios are aggregated, then divided.** "When you compute a derived metric such as ROAS (sales ÷ total cost), aggregate the numerator and the denominator across all rows at your grain before dividing, because the components can sit on different rows." "The dataset does not include calculated metrics such as ROAS or CTR." [official] (Ads performance [Stream v2], read 2026-11-09)
- **Promoted and halo.** "All conversion metrics now include Promoted and Halo qualified versions"; base metrics such as `metric.purchases` "now include *all* conversions—both promoted and halo", and, in a warning addressed to DSP integrations, "To report attribution for promoted products only, **use metrics with the Promoted qualifier**". [official] (Overview of the Reporting v1 API, read 2026-11-09). Detail is `beyond/metrics-and-attribution.md`'s.
- **Warnings on a v1 report.** Since 2026-10-05 a completed v1 report may carry `warnings[]`, each `{ field, warningCodes[] }`, for example `NO_PERMISSION` (values redacted) or `PENDING_DEPRECATION`; "Treat `warningCodes` as an open-ended list". [official] (All releases, "Warning signals now returned on new Reporting API reports"; New features [Reporting v1], `guides/reporting/ads-v1/new-features`; both read 2026-11-09)

### D. What Amazon changes inside a window (RULE where it is a documented mechanism; GUIDANCE where it is advice)

These are documented mechanisms by which a campaign's bids, budgets, placements, creative or serving change with no write from the seller or cx.

- **Dynamic bidding.** SP 3.0 `SponsoredProductsBiddingStrategy`: `AUTO_FOR_SALES` "Dynamic bids - up and down": "Increases or decreases your bids in real time by a maximum of 100%."; `LEGACY_FOR_SALES` "Dynamic bids - down only": "Lowers your bids in real time when your ad may be less likely to convert to a sale."; `MANUAL` "Fixed bid": "Uses your exact bid and any placement adjustments you set, and is not subject to dynamic bidding."; `RULE_BASED`. [official] (Sponsored Products 3.0 OpenAPI, read 2026-11-09)
  - The ceiling by placement. Amazon's current help page and library guide say 100% for all placements; one Amazon page, the author's guide, still says 50% outside top of search. The help page *Bidding strategies for Sponsored Products* (GCU2BUWJH2W3A8Z7, `lastModified` 2026-09-28): "We may increase or decrease your bids by up to 100% for all placements based on performance", with "Example: Amazon can adjust your bid of $1.00 up to $2.00 for all placements"; for down only, "We may decrease your bids by up to 100% for all placements based on performance." The up-and-down library guide: "We will increase or decrease your bids by up to 100% for all placements based on performance", and "Currently, we can increase this bid by up to 100%, so that would be a maximum of $2.00 for all placements opportunities". The author's guide (written for book authors): "Bids will be increased by up to 100% for placements at the top of the first page of shopping results and by up to 50% for all other placements." [official] (Bidding strategies for Sponsored Products, `https://advertising.amazon.com/help/GCU2BUWJH2W3A8Z7`; Guide to dynamic bidding - up and down with Sponsored Products, `https://advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/dynamic-bidding-sponsored-products`; The author's guide to Sponsored Products, `https://advertising.amazon.com/library/guides/authors-guide-to-sponsored-products`; all re-read 2026-11-09). `areas/bids-and-placements.md` owns this question. Its P-bids-and-placements-8 counts four current Amazon sources for "100% for all placements" (help, library guide, SP 3.0 and v1 models) against the author's guide, and notes that no Amazon page dates a change from 100/50. That file records practitioners still repeating 100/50: BidX and Perpetua, and Trellis "according to a third-party summary of Amazon's announcement". This file follows it. [seat]
  - Placement adjustments stack: "a 50% Top of Search adjustment on a $1.00 bid would increase the bid to $1.50 … A further 100% Amazon Business adjustment would increase the bid to $3.00 for the Amazon Business Top of Search placement and to $2.00 for all other Amazon Business placements." [official] (SP 3.0 OpenAPI, `SponsoredProductsPlacement`)
- **Rule-based bidding** moves base bids and can undo itself: "Amazon Ads may then adjust your base bids up and down to increase conversions while maintaining your guardrails." "if your campaign is not meeting the guardrail and the return on ad spend (ROAS) of the campaign for the last 20 days drops to less than 30% of your ROAS over a 20-to-40-day period, we will disable the rule on your behalf and will enable your previous bidding strategy and targeting level bids." Eligibility: "running for at least 10 days prior to setting a rule and have a minimum of 10 conversions in the last 30 days", plus a minimum daily budget per marketplace (US 10, JP 600, IN 300, … local currency; figures for human readers only). "the ROAS guardrail is based on the previous 21 days." [official] (Sponsored Products rule-based bidding overview, `guides/rules/bidding-rules/overview`, read 2026-11-09)
- **Budget rules** raise budgets without a write on the day: "Schedule-based rules … temporarily increases campaign budgets by the percentage specified in the rule." "Performance-based rules … increase campaign budgets when your campaigns meet a certain performance threshold … calculated using data from the previous 7 days for sellers and 14 days for vendors." [official] (Sponsored ads budget rules overview, `guides/rules/budget-rules/overview`, read 2026-11-09). v3 `spCampaigns` carries `campaignRuleBasedBudgetAmount` ("The value of the rule-based budget for a campaign."), `campaignApplicableBudgetRuleId` and `campaignApplicableBudgetRuleName`. [official] (Reporting columns (version 3))
- **Out of budget** is a serving state, not a write: SP 3.0 campaign `servingStatus` values include `CAMPAIGN_OUT_OF_BUDGET`, `PORTFOLIO_OUT_OF_BUDGET`, `ACCOUNT_OUT_OF_BUDGET`, `ADVERTISER_OUT_OF_BUDGET`; `POST /sp/campaigns/budget/usage` returns `budgetUsagePercent` ("Budget usage percentage (spend / available budget) for the given budget policy.") and `usageUpdatedTimestamp` ("Last evaluation time for budget usage"). The history records `IN_BUDGET` "true or false" on CAMPAIGN. [official] (SP 3.0 OpenAPI; Changehistory model)
- **The Featured Offer and stock gate.** "Sponsored Products only appear when your advertised items are in stock"; "Your products need to be in stock to be the featured offer and eligible for advertising." ### F. What a seller cannot randomize on Amazon (RULE where the API says so)

- **No geography for Sponsored Products or Sponsored Brands.** The v1 Target entity guide's support table marks `targetDetails.locationTarget` and `LocationTarget.locationId` for the common model and Amazon DSP only; the Sponsored Products and Sponsored Brands columns are empty. The same holds for `dayPartTarget`, `deviceTarget`, `audienceTarget` and the other DSP-only target types. Sponsored Products targets are `keywordTarget`, `productTarget`, `productCategoryTarget` and `themeTarget`. [official] (Target entity guide, `guides/campaign-management/entities/target`, read 2026-11-09 from its markdown source). A geo holdout of the Blake et al. kind (ads off in a random set of regions) cannot be run on Sponsored Products through Amazon's targeting. [seat, from the table]
- **No shopper split.** No Ads API object read assigns shoppers to arms for a Sponsored Products bid, budget, target or negative. MYE splits shoppers, but only for listing content (§E). [official, by absence on the pages and models read; seat's reading]
- **Time is the lever left, and the seller can randomize it.** A seller can switch a change on and off by date (an on-off or "pause" test) or run two campaigns side by side. Which days or weeks are "on" can be decided by a coin flip per period, which is what makes a switchback randomized (Bojinov, Simchi-Levi & Zhao 2023; Published research). A fixed alternation, or a before/after, is not randomized. [seat]
- **Duplicate targets: what Amazon states.** The match-type help page: "If you set multiple match types with different bids for the same keyword, then the match type with the highest bid will be used for any matching shopping query." The targeting guide: "And don't worry, you won't be bidding against yourself by adding different match types on the same keyword." On several campaigns for different products: "if you have multiple campaigns where you're bidding on category keywords that are relevant to multiple advertised products, set bids based on your priority for each product; this will give your top priority product the best opportunity to get top ad placements, while still giving your lower priority product(s) a chance to also appear on the search results page." [official] (Understand keyword match types, `https://advertising.amazon.com/help/GHTRFDZRJPW6764R`, `lastModified` 2026-07-29; A guide to targeting with Sponsored Products; both re-read 2026-11-09). The first sentence covers one keyword in several match types. No page read says whether it reaches across ad groups or campaigns, which of two copies of one keyword in the same match type enters an auction, or whether the bid compared is the base bid or the placement-adjusted one. [seat; `areas/keyword-and-product-targeting.md` and `areas/campaign-structure.md` P-56 own the rule and record the same limits]
- **Same-keyword arms are not a shopper split.** The A/B designs practitioners recommend put the same keyword in two ad groups (ClearAds) or two campaigns (Sagum, Marin) for the same product. Within the scope of Amazon's sentence, the arm whose match type carries the higher bid "will be used for any matching shopping query". Outside it, Amazon states no rule. Either way, Amazon, not a coin, decides which arm serves a given search, and the arms meet in the same auctions and, in ClearAds' design, under one campaign budget (Basse et al. 2016). Arms that differ only in a creative or a landing page, for different targets or different products, are not covered by the match-type sentence. [seat, from the quotes above and Published research]
- **Budget-constrained arms interfere.** When a test arm and a control arm draw on budgets or share an auction, research shows the measured effect can be badly biased (Basse, Soufiani & Lambert 2016; Liu, Mao & Kang 2020; see Published research). [research]

**Time-alternating (switchback) designs**

- **Bojinov, Simchi-Levi & Zhao, "Design and Analysis of Switchback Experiments", *Management Science* 69(7) (2023) 3759–3777, DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2022.4583** [research] (arXiv 2009.00148 abstract and full text, read 2026-11-09; journal record via Crossref).
  - *Setting.* "a firm sequentially exposes an experimental unit to random treatments". The carryover effect is "the length of time a treatment persists in impacting the outcome", of order *m* periods.
  - *Design.* Under the paper's assumptions, "any optimal design of experiment (T,Q) must satisfy q0 = q1 = ... = qK = 1/2" (fair coin flips at the randomization points). When *T* = *n·m* (n ≥ 4), the optimal randomization points are "T∗ = {1, 2m+1, 3m+1, ..., (n−2)m+1}". So the first and last blocks are 2*m* long and the rest *m*. The worked example is *T* = 12 and *m* = 2, with points {1, 5, 7, 9}.
  - *Estimation.* A Horvitz-Thompson estimator compares periods that follow *p*+1 consecutive treatments with periods that follow *p*+1 consecutive controls, weighted by their known assignment probabilities. It is unbiased for "the average lag-p causal effect" when *p* ≥ *m*. "when p<m, the Horvitz-Thompson estimator (4) will be biased for the causal estimand", although exact randomization-based p-values stay valid.
  - *Practical guidance, verbatim.* "we suggest that each period's length be smaller than the carryover effect duration"; "the firm should set p = m"; choosing *n* "resembles a typical power analysis". Identifying *m* from data needs "experimental resources at the scale of T/m>100 to distinguish two candidate values".
  - *Transfer.* A seller's daily or weekly on/off test of a campaign is a switchback with one unit. Three things decide whether it estimates the effect: whether the switch times are randomized, whether the period is long enough against the carryover, and whether the analysis drops or models the periods right after a switch. Hershaff's daily alternation (What practitioners add) met a measured daily decay of about 0.35, so a one-day carryover is not negligible. Qin's Sponsored Products carry-over reached up to three weeks in weekly data. Those two figures differ by more than an order of magnitude, measured on different outcomes and data. [seat]

- **de Chaisemartin & D'Haultfœuille, "Two-Way Fixed Effects Estimators with Heterogeneous Treatment Effects", *American Economic Review* 110(9) (2020) 2964–96** [research] (abstract via IDEAS, read 2026-11-09). Such regressions "estimate weighted sums of the average treatment effects (ATE) in each group and period, with weights that may be negative. Due to the negative weights, the linear regression coefficient may for instance be negative while all the ATEs are positive."
- **Callaway & Sant'Anna, "Difference-in-Differences with multiple time periods", *Journal of Econometrics* 225(2) (2021) 200–230** [research] (abstract via IDEAS, read 2026-11-09). It identifies group-time effects "with (i) multiple time periods, (ii) variation in treatment timing, and (iii) when the "parallel trends assumption" holds potentially only after conditioning on observed covariates". It proposes aggregations "to highlight treatment effect heterogeneity" and a bootstrap for "simultaneous (instead of pointwise) inference".
- **Sun & Abraham, "Estimating dynamic treatment effects in event studies with heterogeneous treatment effects", *Journal of Econometrics* 225(2) (2021) 175–199** [research] (abstract via IDEAS, read 2026-11-09). "in settings with variation in treatment timing across units, the coefficient on a given lead or lag can be contaminated by effects from other periods, and apparent pretrends can arise solely from treatment effects heterogeneity."
- *Transfer.* cx changes many targets and campaigns on different dates. Pooling their before/after differences in one regression with target and date effects is the two-way fixed-effects estimator these papers analyse. When effects differ across targets or grow over time, the pooled coefficient can carry negative weights and even the wrong sign, and a pre-trend test on such a regression can mislead. The papers' remedies compare each changed unit only with units not yet changed, or never changed, at the same dates, then aggregate. Parallel trends is an assumption about the untreated paths, checked, not proved, on the pre-period. [seat]

**Synthetic control**

- **Abadie, Diamond & Hainmueller, "Synthetic Control Methods for Comparative Case Studies: Estimating the Effect of California's Tobacco Control Program", *JASA* 105(490) (2010) 493–505** [research] (NBER Working Paper 12831 abstract, read 2026-11-09). The method builds "a comparable synthetic control region" as a weighted combination of untreated units. It is meant for interventions that "affect a small number of aggregate units", and "produce[s] informative inference regardless of the number of available comparison units".
- **Abadie, "Using Synthetic Controls: Feasibility, Data Requirements, and Methodological Aspects", *Journal of Economic Literature* 59(2) (2021) 391–425** [research] (NBER Summer Institute copy of the published article, `https://conference.nber.org/confer/2021/SI2021/Abadie_2021.pdf`, text extracted and read 2026-11-09). Its feasibility conditions, by heading and verbatim where quoted:
  - *Size of the effect and volatility of the outcome.* "small effects will be indistinguishable from other shocks to the outcome of the affected unit, especially if the outcome variable of interest is highly volatile."
  - *Availability of a comparison group.* "it is important that not all units adopt interventions similar to the one under investigation during the period of the study".
  - *No anticipation*, *no interference* ("units' outcomes are invariant to other units' treatments").
  - *Convex hull condition.* "it should be checked that the differences in the characteristics of the affected unit and the synthetic control are small".
  - *Time horizon.* Use "surrogate outcomes or leading indicators" when effects take time.
  - On donor pools: "each of the units in the donor pool have to be chosen judiciously". Including unsuitable units risks "large interpolation biases".
  - Inference is by placebo runs: "iteratively reassigning the treatment to the units in the donor pool and estimating "placebo effects"". Abadie, Diamond & Hainmueller's ratio of post- to pre-intervention RMSPE serves as the test statistic.
- **Arkhangelsky, Athey, Hirshberg, Imbens & Wager, "Synthetic Difference-in-Differences", *American Economic Review* 111(12) (2021) 4088–4118** [research] (abstract via IDEAS, read 2026-11-09). "a new estimator for causal effects with panel data that builds on insights behind the widely used difference-in-differences and synthetic control methods" with "desirable robustness properties".
- *Transfer.* The control series in S5 (the brand's unadvertised ASINs, the same ASIN in another marketplace, the category benchmark CPC, untouched campaigns) are a donor pool. Abadie's conditions map onto ad work. The pre-period fit must be close. The donors must not have had a similar change in the window, which is Abadie's comparison-group condition applied to cx's other changes. The donors must not be moved by the change: a competitor's ASIN fails this (Johari et al.), and a sibling ASIN that shares the shopper may fail too. A single ASIN's daily orders are often volatile against the effect sought. [seat]

**Variance reduction and trustworthy-experiment checks**

- **Deng, Xu, Kohavi & Walker, "Improving the Sensitivity of Online Controlled Experiments by Utilizing Pre-Experiment Data" (CUPED), WSDM 2013** [research] (PDF on Microsoft's ExP site, `https://exp-platform.com/Documents/2013-02-CUPED-ImprovingSensitivityOfControlledExperiments.pdf`, read 2026-11-09). With a pre-period covariate *X* and θ = cov(*Y*,*X*)/var(*X*), "var(Ycv) = var(Y)(1−ρ²)". On Bing "we can reduce variance by about 50%, effectively achieving the same statistical power with only half of the users, or half the duration". "Using the metric measured in the pre-period as the covariate typically provides the best variance reduction." "Using a pre-experiment period of 1-2 weeks works well for variance reduction. Too short a period will lead to poor matching, whereas too long a period will reduce correlation with the outcome metric during the experiment period." "Never use covariates that could be affected by the treatment, as this could bias the results." *Transfer:* CUPED reduces variance in a comparison between randomized arms, for example targets assigned at random to a change against targets left alone. There each target's own pre-period clicks or orders are the covariate. In a single before/after with no control arm it has nothing to adjust. The gain depends on ρ, the correlation of a target's pre- and post-period values. That correlation is low for sparse targets whose weekly orders are mostly 0 or 1. [seat]
- **Kohavi, Tang & Xu, *Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments: A Practical Guide to A/B Testing*, Cambridge University Press (2020)** [research, book] (bibliographic record via Open Library, read 2026-11-09). The publisher's pages and excerpt PDFs answered HTTP 403 or 503, so the book's chapters were not read, and its content is cited here only through the authors' papers read below.
- **Dmitriev, Gupta, Kim & Vaz, "A Dirty Dozen: Twelve Common Metric Interpretation Pitfalls in Online Controlled Experiments", KDD 2017** [research] (ExP PDF, `https://exp-platform.com/Documents/2017-08%20KDDMetricInterpretationPitfalls.pdf`, read 2026-11-09). If the ratio of units between arms "is different than the expected (a chi-square test can be used), it means something is wrong with the sampling process. We call this situation Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). Most of the time SRM implies a severe selection bias, enough to render the experiment results invalid". The paper also has a section on "Novelty and Primacy Effects".
- **Kohavi, Deng, Frasca, Longbotham, Walker & Xu, "Trustworthy online controlled experiments: five puzzling outcomes explained", KDD 2012** [research] (ExP PDF, `https://exp-platform.com/Documents/puzzlingOutcomesInControlledExperiments.pdf`, read 2026-11-09). An A/A test "can be used to (i) collect data and assess its variability for power calculations, and (ii) test the experimentation system".
- *Transfer.* An ad analogue of SRM is a change set whose "treated" targets did not all receive the change, for example partial failures in a `207` reply (§B), or targets the history shows as changed again by someone else. **Identifying a target's response to its own bid**

- **Wager & Xu, "Experimenting in Equilibrium", *Management Science* 67(11) (2021) 6694–6715, DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2020.3844** [research] (arXiv 1903.02124 abstract and full text, read 2026-11-09). - *Transfer.* The platform in the paper sets one parameter for many suppliers. A single seller is the opposite case: one small bidder whose own bid barely moves the market equilibrium. The paper's direct regression, the marginal response "against a fixed ambient market equilibrium", is then the quantity the seller wants: how clicks, cost and orders on a target respond to a small change in its own bid. ### Worked statistics for small ad samples [seat; arithmetic by the seat on 2026-11-09, using the formulas above]

These are illustrations of the size of chance in ad data, not thresholds.

- **A conversion rate from 100 clicks.** 10 orders from 100 clicks is a CVR of 10%; its Wilson 95% interval is about 5.5% to 17.4% (Jeffreys: 5.3% to 17.0%). 3 orders from 30 clicks: 3.5% to 25.6%. 0 orders from 20 clicks: 0% to 16.1% (Wilson), 0% to 11.7% (Jeffreys), 15% by the rule of three. 40 from 400: 7.4% to 13.3%. 100 from 1,000: 8.3% to 12.0%.
- **Telling 10% from 12%.** With the n = 16σ²/Δ² rule (σ² = p(1−p)), detecting a CVR move from 10% to 12% at 95% confidence and 80% power needs about 3,600 clicks per period; 10% to 11% needs about 14,400; 5% to 6% about 7,600. A keyword with 30 clicks a week would need years. Only large moves on high-volume terms, or totals pooled across many terms, can be told from chance in a few weeks.
- **A Bayesian reading.** With uniform Beta(1,1) priors, 12/100 before and 18/100 after gives a posterior probability of about 0.88 that the after-rate is higher; 30/300 against 40/300 gives about 0.90. Both are "probably higher", neither is near certainty, and neither says by how much. A probability that one period beat the other is not the probability that the change caused it, because the periods differ in more than the change.
- **ACOS from few orders.** If orders are roughly Poisson, the relative standard error of an order count k is about 1/√k: about 32% at 10 orders, 22% at 20, 10% at 100. ACOS (cost over sales) inherits at least that relative error from its denominator, plus the spread of order values. An ACOS that moves from 30% to 24% on 10 orders a week is inside chance.
- **Zero conversions.** A keyword with 20 clicks and no order has a 95% upper bound on its CVR of about 11–16% by the intervals above; Amazon's targeting guide recommends judging a negative after "at least 20 clicks" (§G). The guide's 20 clicks rules out only very high conversion rates. [seat]
- **Clicks are not independent trials.** One shopper can click several times; clicks cluster by day and by query. The binomial intervals above are therefore too narrow, not too wide (Deng, Knoblich & Lu 2018 on aggregation levels).

(Changehistory_prod_3p.json [official])
3. **P-change-measurement-3.** The history model's bidding-strategy values (`OPTIMIZE_FOR_SALES`, `LEGACY`, `MANUAL`) and placement positions (`DETAIL_PAGE`, `TOP_OF_SEARCH`) differ from Sponsored Products 3.0's (`AUTO_FOR_SALES`, `LEGACY_FOR_SALES`, `MANUAL`, `RULE_BASED`, `OTHER`; `PLACEMENT_TOP`, `PLACEMENT_PRODUCT_PAGE`, `PLACEMENT_REST_OF_SEARCH`, `SITE_AMAZON_BUSINESS`), and the model has no event type for negative product targets, audience or shopper bid adjustments, ad creative or budget-rule associations. She found that "SP does not show significant carry-over effects beyond three weeks for any brand", and that lower-funnel products realise 60–90% of their total effect within two weeks. The paper states the sample is "only … brands that asked for a long-term effects assessment" and that the estimate nets out "company feedback effects and competitive response". (Qin 2023 [research, Amazon-authored])
57. **P-change-measurement-57.** Lily Tong of Amazon Ads, as reported by Relevant Audience from Digiday, said that across "more than 2 million campaigns" in June, "roughly 20% of the sales value for a brand shows up over time and isn't captured in that immediate attribution window". The figure covers all ad products and is not split out for Sponsored Products. (Relevant Audience, Antonio Fernandez, 2026-10-02; Digiday, Seb Joseph, 2026-10-01, as read in `beyond/metrics-and-attribution.md` [opinion, trade press quoting Amazon])
58. **P-change-measurement-58.** Jonathan Hershaff reports a daily on/off switchback for about a month, by a seller with $20k annual ad spend. Platform ROAS was about 2.35 and incremental ROAS from total sales 1.25, rising to 1.54 after an MMM-estimated adstock of about 0.35 per day. He calls the 1.25 a "floor" because "A daily switchback assumes marketing has zero memory". (Hershaff, Substack, 2026-02-13 [opinion, seller-analyst])
59. **P-change-measurement-59.** In Bojinov, Simchi-Levi & Zhao's switchback design, under carryover of order *m*, optimal assignment probabilities are 1/2 at randomization points {1, 2*m*+1, 3*m*+1, …, (*n*−2)*m*+1}. The Horvitz-Thompson estimator is unbiased for the lag-*p* effect when *p* ≥ *m* and biased when *p* < *m*. The authors suggest "each period's length be smaller than the carryover effect duration". (Bojinov, Simchi-Levi & Zhao 2023 [research])
60. **P-change-measurement-60.** Amazon's pricing scientists cannot split shoppers for a price test, so they use switchback experiments. Their "random-days" design "can reduce the standard error … by 60%". They name carryover through recommendations, which "could artificially boost the product's sales even during control periods". (Cooprider & Nassiri, amazon.science, 2023-04-14 [research, Amazon-authored])
61. **P-change-measurement-61.** With treatment at different times, the two-way fixed-effects estimator "equals a weighted average of all possible two-group/two-period DD estimators". Its weights "may be negative", so "the linear regression coefficient may for instance be negative while all the ATEs are positive". In event studies, "apparent pretrends can arise solely from treatment effects heterogeneity". (Goodman-Bacon 2021; de Chaisemartin & D'Haultfœuille 2020; Sun & Abraham 2021 [research])
62. **P-change-measurement-62.** Abadie's feasibility conditions for a synthetic control are these: an effect large against the outcome's volatility; a donor pool that did not adopt similar interventions; no anticipation; no interference; a close pre-period fit (the convex hull condition); and donors "chosen judiciously" to avoid "large interpolation biases". Inference is by placebo reassignment across donors. (Abadie 2021 [research])
63. **P-change-measurement-63.** CUPED cuts the variance of a treatment-control difference to var(Y)(1−ρ²) using a pre-period covariate, about 50% on Bing. The same metric in a pre-period of 1–2 weeks "works well", and "Never use covariates that could be affected by the treatment". (Deng, Xu, Kohavi & Walker 2013 [research])
64. **P-change-measurement-64.** A sample ratio mismatch between arms "implies a severe selection bias, enough to render the experiment results invalid" most of the time. A chi-square test detects it. Carryover from earlier experiments on the same units lasted more than three weeks in Bing's case and was detected with A/A tests. (Dmitriev, Gupta, Kim & Vaz 2017; Kohavi et al. 2012 [research])
65. **P-change-measurement-65.** In an Airbnb pricing meta-experiment, "at least 20% of the TATE estimate" from individual-level randomization was interference bias, which cluster randomization removed. In simulation, blocked cluster randomization cut the naive estimator's bias "by as much as 62%" at the cost of higher variance. (Holtz, Lobel, Lobel, Liskovich & Aral 2024; Holtz & Aral 2020 [research])
66. **P-change-measurement-66.** Wager & Xu estimate marginal responses to system parameters "by combining unobstrusive randomization with lightweight modeling, all while remaining in equilibrium". They use ±1 shocks per unit, and the regression on them recovers the response "against a fixed ambient market equilibrium". (Wager & Xu 2021 [research])
67. **P-change-measurement-67.** Amazon's AuctionGym authors state that "an advertiser does not observe counterfactual outcomes of bid amounts that were not submitted", and that adaptation by competitors complicates "evaluation of bidding strategies based on logged data alone". (Jeunen, Murphy & Allison, AuctionGym [research, Amazon-authored])
68. **P-change-measurement-68.** The false discovery rate is "the expected proportion of falsely rejected hypotheses", and a step-up procedure controls it for independent tests. Multilevel models instead shrink estimates toward each other. Winners chosen from a group of experiments carry a selection bias, for which Lee & Shen give a correction. (Benjamini & Hochberg 1995; Gelman, Hill & Yajima 2012; Lee & Shen 2018 [research])

Azevedo et al. find that with fat-tailed effects "a "lean" strategy of trying more ideas, each with possibly smaller sample sizes, is preferred". ### Seat's positions (enter no skill)

- **S1. The change record.** A record that makes a later verdict a test rather than a story holds, written before the write: the entity ids and the profile; the field's value read just before (from the object, not memory); the request as sent (method, path, body); Amazon's reply whole (the `207` body, or the door's `sent, outcome unknown`); the object read just after; the metric the change is judged on, with its Amazon field name and look-back; the expected direction and the smallest move that would count; the baseline and observation windows by date in the campaign's time zone; the date the observation window's figures are expected to be final under the horizon chosen from §C; the control series; and every other change in the same campaign, ad group or ASIN during both windows, from `POST /history` and the seller's word. This follows cx-listing-playbook's P-effect-measurement-6 with the ad-specific fields added.
- **S2. Windows for an ad change.** Whole weeks, matching weekdays. The observation window starts the day after the change lands (its `lastUpdatedDateTime`). A verdict on conversion metrics waits until the observation window's last day is past the chosen restatement horizon; a verdict on impressions, clicks and spend can come after Amazon's click-validation period. Exposure (impressions, top-of-search impression share, clicks, CPC) answers within days for a bid or budget change; conversion and ACOS answer later and more noisily.
- **S3. Amazon's automatic responses are part of the effect.** Under dynamic or rule-based bidding, what is measured is the change plus Amazon's response to it. A manual change by anyone else in the window is a confounder; an automatic bid move by Amazon is not. A rule's switch-off, a budget rule's event day, an applied recommendation and an Amazon Ads Status incident are confounders, because they would have happened without the change or are separate changes.
- **S4. The on-off test on Amazon.** The design Blake et al. used for brand terms (ads off on some platforms, on in another) has two Amazon analogues a seller can run: alternating whole weeks on and off for the same term or campaign, and switching off in one marketplace while another runs. As usually run, neither is randomized. The first meets seasonality and competitor moves between weeks, and the second compares different markets. The first becomes a randomized switchback when a coin decides each period's arm (Bojinov, Simchi-Levi & Zhao 2023; Amazon's own random-days price tests). Under their design, the period is set against the carryover (Qin's up to three weeks for Sponsored Products in weekly brand data, against Hershaff's daily decay of about 0.35), and the periods right after a switch are dropped or modelled. The second is a donor comparison and needs Abadie's conditions (pre-period fit, no similar change in the donor, no spillover). In either, Amazon's competitors respond within days (Amazon's FTC response: rival bids fell about 83% when one advertiser stopped), so the test measures the pause together with the market's answer to it. The quantity to read is total sales of the advertised ASIN and brand (SP-API), not attributed ad sales, and organic signals (Search Query Performance share, Keepa rank), because the question is substitution.
- **S5. Control series an ad change does not touch.** The brand's unadvertised ASINs, the same ASIN in another marketplace, the category benchmark CPC/CTR series, Search Query Performance query volume for the changed terms, and the seller's own other campaigns not sharing the budget, auctions or ASIN. Competitors' series are partly caused by the change (Johari et al. 2020).
- **S6. Verdicts adapted to ads.** *Held* needs the change visible in its first-order metric (bid → CPC or impression share; budget → hours or days out of budget; negative → spend on the negated term; new target → impressions on it) and the judged metric past the logged smallest move once restatement has settled. *Not held* needs a window large enough, by the counts in the worked statistics, to have seen the logged move. *Cannot tell* covers a change that did not land, a change Amazon overrode (rule switch-off, out-of-budget days, Featured Offer loss, stock-out), an incident, a sale event, another change in the same entity, or too few events. For most single keywords, a conversion-rate or ACOS verdict will be *cannot tell* for weeks; exposure and spend verdicts will not.
- **S7. Several changes at once.** When a seller makes a change set (a restructure, a harvest of twenty terms), the verdict is about the set, measured on totals of the ASINs it touches, and the record says so.
- **S8. Peeking.** A heartbeat that reads a campaign daily reports progress and gives no verdict before the logged end date and the restatement horizon.
- **S9. Many verdicts.** A heartbeat that issues verdicts on many changes is running many tests. The published corrections apply to it: false-discovery control across the batch, shrinkage of each estimate toward the seller's own history of similar changes, and a discount on kept winners (Benjamini & Hochberg; Gelman, Hill & Yajima; Lee & Shen; Deng 2015). The procedure's false-alarm rate on a given seller's data can be read by running it on windows with no change, the A/A analogue (Kohavi et al. 2012).
- **S10. The unit is the change sent.** The contrast that stays valid is between everything assigned the change and everything not assigned, counting targets whose raise won nothing and changes that partly failed (Johnson, Lewis & Nubbemeyer's ITT). Restricting to targets that gained impressions conditions on Amazon's allocation.
- **S11. Pooled before/after across staggered changes.** One regression over many targets changed on different dates, with target and date effects, is the two-way fixed-effects estimator. When effects differ, its coefficient can carry negative weights. The published estimators compare each changed unit only with not-yet-changed or never-changed units at the same dates, then aggregate (Callaway & Sant'Anna; Sun & Abraham).
- **S12. Small randomized bid perturbations.** For a seller whose bid barely moves the market, small ± changes to base bids assigned at random across targets or days identify the local response of clicks and cost to the target's own bid (Wager & Xu's marginal response at a fixed equilibrium). The seller's logged bid history cannot identify it cleanly, because the seller chose those bids (AuctionGym). Under dynamic bidding the perturbation is on the base bid, and Amazon's adjustment sits on top of it.
