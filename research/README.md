# Amazon advertising playbook: research

This directory is the research base for a skill pack that sits over `cx-amazon-ads`, ComChatX's open door to the Amazon Ads API (`cx raw amazon-ads`). The pack's name is not yet ruled; the candidates are below. Eighteen research files, each written by a research seat and then checked by a verifier seat, cover a senior Amazon advertising lead's work in three directories: the areas of the work itself (`areas/`), what lies past opening and tuning campaigns (`beyond/`, Jack's "more than running ads"), and how cx reaches, reads and judges an account through the door (`method/`). Every page was read on 2026-11-09. Sources rank in one order: Amazon's own pages, models and staff statements **[official]**, then named practitioners, vendors and trade press **[opinion]**, then published research **[research]**; a news report the vendor file keeps apart from opinion is tagged **[press]**. A **[seat]** line in a file is the writing seat's own reading; it is never a position here. What was kept out, by rule: no Ads API host (`advertising-api*.amazon.com`), no Login with Amazon (`api.amazon.com/auth`), no Amazon Ads MCP endpoint, no Selling Partner API host, no Seller Central help-hub `GetHelpTopic` content call and no page needing sign-in was called, and no seller's data appears anywhere in the files.

## How to read this

- **This page is the entry point.** It says what each file covers, what the pack would hold, every position the skills will take, where the files disagreed, what is still open, and what the door package lacks. The files hold the evidence: quotes, paths, field names, figures and the pages behind them.
- **Positions are facts, not instructions.** Each one states what Amazon, a practitioner or a study says or does, with the P-ids it merges and the pages it rests on, Amazon's first. Under decision D233 none of them tells cx when to ask the seller, in what order to work, what number to treat as a rule, what not to do, or how to talk: the model judges those from the facts. A practitioner's threshold appears as that practitioner's practice. Writes to a seller's ads carry no switch of ours; their risk is the seller's.
- **P-ids** are the files' own: `P-launch-4` is position 4 of `areas/launch.md`. The source names after each position are copied, shortened, from those position lines; the full citation, the URL and the read date sit in the file.
- **Figures that move** (limits, fees, dates, benchmark values) are stated as they read on 2026-11-09. The live value is read at run time from the operation or page each file's run-time section names; a figure here is for knowing what to look for.
- **What this synthesis read.** Every file's *Positions*, *Open questions*, verification record and *Pages read* in full, plus the run-time, door and report-reader sections of `method/`. It did not reopen any page. No position was removed by a verifier; corrections were made in place, and the positions below are the corrected ones.

## The pack this research proposes

Eight skills, each a directory holding a `SKILL.md` of at most 371 words and a `references/` directory where the facts below land; seven templates in `prompts/`, one line each; and one script, because `method/reporting.md` shows that `cx-amazon-ads` names no way to land a report. The parts carry one letter each: **A** review, **L** launch, **T** structure and targets, **S** search terms, **B** bids and budgets, **E** seasons, **G** economics, **C** changes, **M** metrics, **P** products and policy, **R** reports and the surface. A, L, T, S, B, E, G and C are skills; M and P are shared references that live in one skill and are linked from the others; R is the script with its reference.

### Skills

- **`ads-review` (A).** Reading an account as it stands: which profiles and global accounts the grant reaches, whether each product can serve (Featured Offer, stock, listing state, price checks), the serving status and delivery reasons that name every stop, payment and billing stops, Amazon's status page, and the identities and methods that split a move in spend or sales into its parts. Its references hold the serving enums, the SP-API reads for the Featured Offer and price, and the decomposition and change-detection methods. Fed by `areas/diagnosis.md`, `method/ads-api-surface.md`, `beyond/brand-defense-and-competition.md` (the Featured Offer held by another seller) and `areas/budgets-and-pacing.md` (money stops) and `beyond/vendors-and-hybrid.md` (a vendor's profile, purchase orders, supply fields and product-cost stops, and the rule that binds each of a hybrid brand's ads to its own offer).
- **`ads-launch` (L).** A product, a seller or a brand new to advertising: the gates, the programs and credits for new sellers, Vine and coupons around a launch, Amazon's own guidance for a first campaign, what newness does and does not do in Amazon's words and in research, and the create order of a campaign through the API, and which vendors may advertise and how a Vendor Central account is linked. Fed by `areas/launch.md`, `beyond/ad-products-and-eligibility.md`, `method/ads-api-surface.md` and `beyond/vendors-and-hybrid.md`.
- **`ads-structure-and-targets` (T).** The shape of the account and what each target matches: levels and the settings that live on them, what is fixed at creation, archive, portfolios, limits, global campaigns and localization, SB v4; match types, auto groups, product, category, theme and audience targets; share metrics; brand defence, conquesting and reserve share of voice; a vendor's ASIN-keyed ads and custom landing pages. Fed by `areas/campaign-structure.md`, `areas/keyword-and-product-targeting.md`, `beyond/brand-defense-and-competition.md` and `beyond/vendors-and-hybrid.md`.
- **`ads-search-terms` (S).** What the search term report is in each surface, harvesting through targets or Amazon's target promotion groups, negatives at each level and in each ad product, and the statistics and practitioner practice for judging a term. Fed by `areas/search-terms-and-negatives.md`, with `areas/keyword-and-product-targeting.md` and `method/reporting.md`.
- **`ads-bids-and-budgets` (B).** The auction and its reserves; strategies, placements, audiences and the order they multiply; rule-based bidding, schedule rules and budget rules; budgets, pacing, out-of-budget and usage; portfolios; what Amazon automates on its own switches; third-party bid tools; the write paths for bids and budgets. ### The report reader script (R)

`scripts/ads-report.sh`, with `references/reports.md` beside it, takes the shape of `cx-listing-playbook`'s `scripts/report.sh` and maps it to Ads v3 and Ads v1, as `method/reporting.md` sets out: an ask lands in `<tree>/reports/<reportType>/<ask>/<n>/` with a `receipt.json`; a second run carries on an unlanded ask instead of filing a new one; `--wait` sets a ceiling and `--every` the poll interval; the pre-signed link is used once and stripped from the receipt; there is no limiter and no retry; exit codes 0, 1, 2 and 3 keep report.sh's meanings. What v3 and v1 change: v3 asks are named by their report id and v1 asks by a checksum of the request; v1 lands up to 100 parts; v1's retrieve is a `POST`, which the door reads as a change, so a lost reply reads "sent, outcome unknown"; receipts keep v1 warnings; and a landed report is a vintage, since Amazon restates figures after it. The download is the box's own fetch of Amazon's link, outside the door. The reference also carries the surface facts the script and the skills need: deprecation dates, report types, the v3-to-v1 map, Stream, the MCP server. Fed by `method/reporting.md` and `method/ads-api-surface.md`.

### Templates

One line each in `prompts/`. They overlap with the door's own three (`spend-this-week`, `wasted-keywords`, `out-of-budget`), which rest on Reporting v3; see the follow-ups below.

- `/ads-review`: read this account's sponsored ads over the last weeks and say what moved and why.
- `/ads-launch`: get this new ASIN advertising, from whether it can serve to its first campaigns.
- `/ads-harvest`: work through the search terms since the last pass and move or negate what the figures support.
- `/ads-season`: get this account ready for the next Amazon event in its marketplaces.
- `/ads-margin`: work out what an ad sale is worth on these SKUs and what that makes the account's targets.
- `/ads-brand`: show who advertises on this brand's searches and pages and what holding them costs.
- `/ads-change-review`: tell whether the ad changes made on a given day held.

### A. Reading the account (`ads-review`)

- **A1.** The Ads API is served from three regional hosts, NA (`advertising-api.amazon.com`), EU (`-eu`) and FE (`-fe`), each with its own marketplace list; Ireland is a country code that the Profiles model tables under region EU, while Amazon's endpoint tables omit it. — `P-ads-api-surface-1`, `P-ads-api-surface-2`, `P-ad-products-and-eligibility-13`. [official] *API overview*; *Well-Architected Framework: Fundamental component*; *Profiles* model; *Limits, constraints, and quotas*; Release notes index. [opinion] `salton4`, issue 391.
- **A2.** Login with Amazon tokens are valid in every region; an access token lives 60 minutes, and a refresh token for advertising scopes issued on or after 2026-07-30 expires 365 days after the advertiser's consent. — `P-ads-api-surface-3`, `P-ads-api-surface-4`. [official] *Authorization grants*; *Access tokens*; *Refresh tokens*; *Release notes* (2026-05-26). [opinion] Airbyte.
- **A3.** The `advertising::campaign_management` scope lets an application act with whatever advertising permissions the authorising user holds; each operation names the permissions it requires, and too little permission answers 401. — `P-ads-api-surface-5`, `P-ads-api-surface-6`. [official] *Permissions*; *Authorization grants*; *Sponsored Products* model; *Manager accounts*. [opinion] `jerirose` (Amazon Ads docs contributor), issue 406.
- **A4.** A profile is one advertiser account in one marketplace; `GET /v2/profiles` takes no Scope header, lists only the calling region's profiles, returns nothing for an account that has never created a campaign, and types each as `seller`, `vendor` or `agency` (with `KDP_AUTHOR` as a sub-type). — `P-campaign-structure-1`, `P-ads-api-surface-7`, `P-ads-api-surface-8`, `P-ads-api-surface-9`, `P-ad-products-and-eligibility-5`. [official] *Profiles*; *Profiles* model; Profiles contract; Profiles guide.
- **A5.** Ads API v1 reporting, v1 recommendations and Stream v2 identify the advertiser by a global account id (`amzn1.ads-account.g.*`), not by profile, and `POST /adsApi/v1/query/advertiserAccounts` maps each global account to its per-marketplace `profileId`s, paging by `nextToken` with pages that can be empty while a `nextToken` remains; Amazon names it the replacement for the legacy `/adsAccounts*` endpoints, which answer `Warning: 299` from July 2026, block new client integrations (existing callers continue "through an allowlist") and answer `404` from July 2027. Stream v2 takes no manager account id, Vendor Group id or Seller Central merchant id. — `P-campaign-structure-2`, `P-ads-api-surface-10`, `P-ads-api-surface-11`, `P-ads-api-surface-66`, `P-reporting-46`, `P-campaign-structure-49`, `P-ads-api-surface-41`, `P-reporting-45`, `P-vendors-and-hybrid-60`. [official] *Global campaigns for Sponsored Products*; *QueryAdvertiserAccount API developer guide*; *Query advertiser accounts*; *Stream v2: Create a subscription*; *Recommendations API: How to*; and 5 more.
- **A6.** The United Kingdom is `UK` in the Profiles model and `GB` in the v1 `Marketplace` and `CountryCode` enums. — `P-ads-api-surface-67`. [official] *Profiles* model; *Amazon Ads API v1* model.
- **A7.** Amazon defines click-through rate as clicks over impressions and cost per click as cost over clicks, so on a cost-per-click row spend equals impressions × CTR × CPC; Optmyzr describes the metrics as a chain from impressions through CTR, clicks, conversion rate and orders to sales and ACoS, names three root causes of an ACoS rise (traffic, conversion rate, demand or market shift) and calls a conversion-rate drop "the most misdiagnosed cause of ACoS spikes"; Chuck Kessler (Canopy) orders a conversion drop's causes from the Featured Offer first, and Bob Norberg (eComEngine) puts availability first and ads last. — `P-diagnosis-1`, `P-diagnosis-40`, `P-diagnosis-41`, `P-diagnosis-80`. [official] v1 "Clicks (Delivery)" metric page; "Placement report for Sponsored Products". [opinion] Optmyzr, "Why Amazon Performance Drops Feel Random"; Optmyzr, "Your Amazon ACoS Spiked"; Canopy, "Amazon Conversion Rate"; eComEngine, "'Why Are Sales Down?'".
- **A8.** A Sponsored Products or display ad serves only while the advertised product holds the Featured Offer and is in stock, judged at the moment the shopper views it; an advertised product that goes out of stock is paused within its campaign, and a product without the Featured Offer gets zero impressions even when the campaign runs. — `P-launch-1`, `P-diagnosis-4`, `P-diagnosis-5`, `P-ad-products-and-eligibility-6`, `P-brand-defense-and-competition-40`, `P-brand-defense-and-competition-41`, `P-goals-and-economics-30`, `P-change-measurement-15`, `P-diagnosis-62`, `P-vendors-and-hybrid-15`. ### B. Bids, budgets and Amazon's automation (`ads-bids-and-budgets`)

## Open questions

### For Jack's word (Jack, or counsel through him)

1. **The package name.** `cx-ads-playbook` is recommended above; the alternatives are `cx-amazon-ads-playbook` and `cx-ppc-playbook`.
2. **Stream v2.** Hourly data and Amazon's push feeds need an S3 bucket and SQS queue that belong to the application that first registers them, which for the door is ComChatX's client application, at most five per application. Whether ComChatX runs that infrastructure is a deployment ruling.
3. **The MCP server and Amazon's skills.** Whether cx may call `advertising-ai*.amazon.com`, and whether it may fetch `GET /skills` on the Ads API origin and reuse what it returns, under Amazon's terms.
4. **The Ads API Policies' eligibility line.** The Ads API is "made available only to companies that do not operate a significant ecommerce business, a demand-side ad platform, or an ad network". Whether ComChatX's client application stands under that line is counsel's.
5. **Combining data.** The Partner Network Policies forbid combining Amazon data with other third-party sources without Amazon's written approval. Several positions (G20, G23, A11) set Ads data beside SP-API data for one seller. Whether that join on the seller's own box is "combining" under the policy is counsel's.
6. **One client id, many sellers.** The licence keeps confidential information to the participant it relates to; ComChatX's single client id serves many sellers, and Reporting v1 limits may be shared across them (R9). Both the legal reading and whether a second client id or quota plan is wanted are Jack's.
7. **Credentials.** Policy 5.1 forbids collecting or storing end users' authentication credentials; the door has the seller save a refresh token in the plugin's settings. Whether a token issued to the application by the seller's own consent is such a credential is counsel's.
8. **Landed data.** The Ads API Data Protection Policy page could not be read (a JavaScript shell); the SP-API policy requires incident notice within 24 hours, deletion within 30 days of a trigger and origin-tagged storage. What retention, encryption and deletion rules apply to reports landed by the script is Jack's.
9. **Disclosure of AI.** The SP-API Acceptable Use Policy requires providers to be explicit about calculations and the use of AI, their accuracy and freshness. How cx's outputs meet it, where SP-API data is involved, is counsel's.
10. **AMC and Brand Analytics.** AMC needs its terms accepted once per region in its own interface; Brand Analytics data (SQP, Top Search Terms) carries its own terms and role. Whether the pack reads them, and keeps their results on the box, is Jack's.
11. **Trademark law.** The pages read cover US case law and one EU study; whether the conquesting positions (T35, T45) need a counsel line for other jurisdictions is Jack's.
12. **SP-API for a vendor.** Every vendor retail-analytics read (Vendor Sales, Traffic, Inventory, Forecasting and Net PPM reports, Data Kiosk, the Vendor Orders API, the promotion and coupon reports, Brand Analytics) sits on SP-API hosts under a vendor authorisation, outside the door. Whether cx reaches them is Jack's (gap 17 below).

## What `cx-amazon-ads` lacks

Read against that package's `SKILL.md` and `references/door.md` (whose Amazon guides were read 2026-11-03). Each item is offered to that package's owners; none is done here, and none changes what this pack's positions say.

1. **A report reader.** The door names the v3 three-call flow and leaves the download to the box; there is no script that lands a report, keeps a receipt, carries on an unlanded ask and strips the pre-signed link. The R part proposes one for this pack; the door could carry it instead.
2. **Reporting v1.** `door.md` documents v3 only, with its 2027-06-30 stop. v1 names the advertiser by global account id in the body, lands up to 100 parts, answers 207, and its retrieve is a `POST`.
3. **The global account id.** `POST /adsApi/v1/query/advertiserAccounts` and the `Amazon-Ads-AccountId` header are not named, though v1 reporting, v1 recommendations and Stream v2 need them; the legacy `/adsAccounts*` endpoints answer `Warning: 299` and are due to stop.
4. **Reads sent as `POST`.** v1 queries, SP v3 and SB v4 lists, exports, budget usage, eligibility, product metadata, change history, recommendations and the v1 report retrieve are `POST`s that change nothing, and the gate reads each as a change, so a lost reply reads "sent, outcome unknown". The opposite also holds: `POST /recommendations/apply` and v1 creates are real changes with no idempotency key.
5. **Families not named.** Manager accounts, exports, change history, budget usage, eligibility, product metadata, moderation, portfolios, budget and optimization rules, recommendations of each kind, audiences, brands and Stores, localization, reserve share of voice, promotion offers and rewards, Events and measurement. The door carries them; it does not name their media types or account headers.
6. **`Content-Type` per operation.** The door composes none, while SP v3, SB v4, product metadata, moderation and change history name versioned media types, change history's theme events need the v1.1 `Accept`, and extended-data list calls weigh five times a standard call.
7. **Header names.** The door attaches `Amazon-Advertising-API-ClientId`; several v1 and MCP pages use `Amazon-Ads-ClientId`. Whether every v1 family takes the older name is not stated.
8. **No wait on 429, and no 425.** The door adds no wait; Amazon's guidance is backoff on `Retry-After`, and field reports show 429s without it. v3's `425` duplicate answer is not named.
9. **Ireland.** The origin table has no IE row, though IE profiles exist under region EU.
10. **Hosts outside the door.** The MCP server (`advertising-ai*`), the asset library's 15-minute S3 upload URL (so a new image or video creative cannot be made end to end), export and report downloads, AMC download URLs and Stream v2's S3 files are outside the door's origins. Whether `--body` carries the binary multipart that SB `POST /stores/assets` takes is not stated.
11. **Stream v2.** It needs an AWS account and destinations the door does not have.
12. **A fresh token per call.** The door trades the refresh token at Login with Amazon on every call, so each call is two requests; LwA's own limits are not documented on the pages read.
13. **Test accounts and AMC.** Test accounts need a separate scope and have no seller type; AMC needs its own headers and a per-region terms step in its interface.
14. **Unlisted models.** Targetable Entities, AMC Administration and `SponsoredBrands_prod_3p.json` are served but absent from `contracts.json`, so a reader mapping the surface from the index misses them.
15. **The door's own prompts.** `spend-this-week`, `wasted-keywords` and `out-of-budget` rest on Reporting v3, which stops on 2027-06-30.
16. **Terms under one client id.** The licence, the Ads API Policies and the Partner Network Policies bind the client application the door calls under; `door.md` says nothing of what is done with landed data afterwards (see the open questions for Jack).
17. **SP-API for vendors.** The door reaches Ads API origins only and names no SP-API route, so a vendor's retail-analytics reads (Vendor Sales, Traffic, Inventory, Forecasting and Net PPM reports, Data Kiosk `analytics_vendorAnalytics_2024_09_30`, the Vendor Orders API, the promotion and coupon reports, Brand Analytics and their notifications) are outside it, and the Eligibility read on a vendor's ASINs, a `POST`, passes the gate as a change (item 4).
